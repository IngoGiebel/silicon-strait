## §2 — India substrate: anchor, coupling, ceiling, drift

### §2.1 The 1949-04-01 anchor and the 23-year pre-1972-wave lead

India established formal diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China on **1949-04-01** [Source #1], making it one of the first non-bloc countries to extend recognition to the PRC after the 1949-10-01 founding. The recognition was unilateral, Nehru-led, and explicitly tied to the Panchsheel principles (1954 codification) of non-interference and mutual respect for territorial sovereignty. There is no parallel recognition of the Republic of China (Taiwan) at the state level since 1949; the India–ROC tie effectively ended with the PRC recognition.

The structural significance of the 1949 anchor for the substrate corpus is the **23-year lead over the 1972-wave**. The A.3 substrates (JPN 1972-09, AUS 1972-12, CAN 1970-10, EU member-state pattern 1971-79) all recognise the PRC in the wake of UN General Assembly Resolution 2758 (1971-10-25), under the multilateral pressure of the US-PRC opening (Nixon visit 1972-02). India's recognition is **pre-resolution-2758 by 22 years** and **pre-Nixon-visit by 23 years**. It is the only corpus substrate where the anchor is independent of the UN-codified Cold-War alignment pivot.

This pre-dating effect has two analytic consequences:
1. India's anchor is **not derivative** of any multilateral or alliance-derived framework. The A.3 substrates inherit the 1972-wave language (some variant of "one China" with varying textual strength); India's 1949 anchor uses sui generis Nehruvian language that pre-dates the canonical phrasing.
2. India's posture-stability across 75+ years is **bilaterally negotiated**, not multilaterally-pinned. This makes the anchor more sensitive to bilateral PRC-India relations (Galwan 2020, Tawang 2022) than to the broader US-PRC relationship that pins the A.2/A.3 anchors.

Anchor classification: **A.1 self-anchored, sub-class A.1_self**. This is a singleton in the current corpus and the basis for the H22.1 architectural-fourth-quadrant test.

### §2.2 Industrial coupling: Tata-PSMC Dholera and HCL-Foxconn Jewar

Despite the formal-anchor absence of any India-ROC state-level recognition, India's industrial substrate exhibits **two load-bearing Taiwan-firm couplings** that emerged under the India Semiconductor Mission (ISM) launched 2021-12-15 (revised Cabinet sanction 2022-09-21).

**Tata Electronics × PSMC Dholera (the primary industrial coupling)** [Source #2, #3]:
- Joint venture between Tata Electronics (TEPL) and Powerchip Semiconductor Manufacturing Corporation (PSMC, Taiwan, ROC-incorporated, distinct from TSMC) announced 2024-02-29 with Cabinet sanction the same day.
- Investment: **Rs 91,000 crore (~USD 11 billion)** for a 28nm-110nm fab in Dholera, Gujarat. Capacity: **50,000 wafers per month** at full ramp.
- ISM subsidy share: 50% of fixed capex per the December-2021 Cabinet sanction scheme. Operational since groundbreaking 2024-03-13.
- Structurally significant because: (a) it routes Taiwan-firm semiconductor technology transfer directly into Indian-territory industrial substrate, (b) it represents an Indian-government-subsidised industrial coupling with a Taiwan firm at a scale comparable to ISM's MoU with Micron at Sanand (USD 2.75B), (c) it operationalises Indo-Taiwan industrial coupling without requiring any state-level recognition change.

**HCL × Foxconn Jewar (the secondary industrial coupling)** [Source #4]:
- Joint venture for OSAT (outsourced semiconductor assembly and test) facility at Jewar, UP, announced 2025-05-14 with ISM Cabinet sanction.
- Investment: Rs 3,706 crore (~USD 450M), capacity 36 million units/day (display drivers).
- Foxconn (Hon Hai Precision Industry Co., Ltd., Taiwan-listed, ROC-incorporated) is the Taiwan partner; HCL provides the Indian side equity + management.

**Micron Sanand (adjacent but US-firm-anchored)**:
- 2023-06-22 announcement, ISM-sanctioned, Rs 22,500 crore (~USD 2.75B), Sanand Gujarat, ATMP (assembly, test, mark, pack) facility. Micron is US-incorporated, so this is **not** a Taiwan coupling but it is a US-coupling-with-Taiwan-semi-supply-chain-adjacency. Classification: ISM-anchored, US-firm-tied, Taiwan-stress-permeable but not Taiwan-stress-direct.

The aggregate ISM picture reads as: **two Taiwan-firm couplings load-bearing, one US-firm coupling Taiwan-adjacent, supply-chain depth at ~USD 14B aggregate**.

### §2.3 Bilateral trade $10.6B 2024 (+29.3% YoY)

India-Taiwan bilateral trade reached **USD 10.6 billion** in 2024, up from **USD 8.2 billion** in 2023 — a **+29.3% year-over-year increase** [Source #5]. This is the strongest YoY growth in any India-Taiwan trade decade since the bilateral statistical series began in 1995.

Breakdown:
- Indian exports to Taiwan: USD 2.1B (mineral fuels, organic chemicals, plastics)
- Indian imports from Taiwan: USD 8.5B (electrical machinery, semiconductors at HSN 8542, plastics, organic chemicals)

The imports composition is **semiconductor-dominant**: HSN 8542 (electronic integrated circuits) accounts for roughly USD 2.3B of the 2024 import side, more than tripling from USD 700M in 2022. This is the trade-level corroboration of the industrial coupling identified in §2.2 — the Tata-PSMC and HCL-Foxconn relationships are flowing operational supply along with the joint-venture announcements.

Numerical-anchor methodology: matches Day-21 §3.2 ROK trade pattern (Samsung-supply-chain corroboration via HSN 8542 import dominance). India-Taiwan trade growth rate **exceeds** the India-mainland-China trade growth rate over 2023–2024 (PRC-India trade was USD 118.4B in 2024, +3.1% YoY per RBI commerce ministry tables; India-Taiwan trade growth is **9.5×** the India-mainland-China growth rate over the same window). This is a substrate-finding: India's industrial-substrate Taiwan coupling is **growing faster than India's industrial-substrate PRC coupling** despite the absence of state-level recognition.

### §2.4 Lai–Modi June-2024 reciprocal head-of-government congratulations

On 2024-06-05, Taiwan President Lai Ching-te publicly congratulated Indian PM Narendra Modi on his BJP-led NDA's third-term electoral victory; Modi reciprocated on 2024-06-06 with a thanks message acknowledging the congratulations and naming Lai by name [Source #6, #7]. The exchange was conducted in English on X (formerly Twitter), the platforms of both heads of government's official accounts.

Structurally significant because:
- This is the **first head-of-government-level Taiwan-India direct exchange since 1949** that is not mediated through track-2, parliamentary delegation, or ministerial cover.
- Modi naming Lai by name, in his official account, is operationally equivalent to a state-level acknowledgement, while remaining textually within India's standing "one China" posture (Modi did not use the phrase "President of Taiwan").
- The exchange triggered the MOFA-PRC Mao-Ning protest of 2024-06-06 (§5.1), making it the proximate cause of the only PRC-government direct protest at India-Taiwan substrate behaviour in the 2020–2026 window.

The exchange is **substrate-novel** across the corpus: no prior substrate (US/JPN/EU/ROK/PHL/AUS/CAN) registered a comparable head-of-government direct social-media exchange with Taiwan's head of state in the Day-7-to-Day-21 study window. It is an India-substrate-specific operational drift event.
