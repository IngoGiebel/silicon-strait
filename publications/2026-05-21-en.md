## Executive summary

Day 12 of Silicon Strait opens the **EU-bloc + EU-member-state extension** track: the third player-namespace introduction after Day-10 (JPN) and Day-11 (ASEAN/PHL), on a substrate structurally distinct from both predecessors — there is *no* EU-PRC mutual-defense or alignment treaty to anchor the question to (unlike JPN's 1972 Joint Communiqué or PHL's 1975 Joint Communiqué), the EU's relationship with the PRC rests on an inter-state political *policy* without statutory force at member-state level, and the EU's relationship with Taiwan is mediated through a non-diplomatic representative office (EETO Taipei) operating under an institutional double-coding (`/delegations/taiwan_en` URL-tree and `DELEGATION-TAIWAN@eeas.europa.eu` internal email naming versus the human-readable "European Economic and Trade Office" external name). The fire's signature finding is a **doctrinal mechanism**: the EU's one-China **policy** ("recognises the government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal government of China") is empirically narrower than the PRC's one-China **principle** (which adds the territorial-integrity claim that "Taiwan is an integral part of the territory of the People's Republic of China"), and that doctrinal gap — the *second clause* the EU does not subscribe to — is the substrate space where every observed EU-Taiwan institutional upgrade since 2003 has occurred (EETO founding, 2021 trade-and-investment dialogue + 4 sectoral working groups, 2024 liaison-officer post on disinformation and foreign interference) without triggering the D12.1 deterministic-floor blocking observed at the first-clause level. The substrate-pull across five EU member-states + the EU-institutional level confirms that the PRC's strategy-mix on EU-Taiwan engagement is **substrate-tailored, not uniform**: LT receives cost-warning + economic-coercion (raw materials embargo, customs delays), CZ receives inconsistency-shaming + UNSC-rules-based-order invocation, HU receives positive-induction + pre-veto-installation via inner-trust-ring partnership upgrades, and the EU-institutional level receives doctrinal-clause-narrowing pushback rather than substrate-level retaliation. The structural inference is that PRC has internalised the doctrinal-gap mechanism and prefers to defend the first-clause floor *politically through individual member-state veto-floor capture* (Hungary in 2026, structurally) rather than retaliating against the EU-bloc directly on second-clause upgrades that EU member-state unanimity is required to block.

## Today's thesis

**The EU-Taiwan relationship is empirically structured as an asymmetric two-clause doctrinal architecture where the EU subscribes to PRC-recognition (first clause) but not to PRC-territorial-claim on Taiwan (second clause), and every observed institutional upgrade since 2003 has occurred in the second-clause space — proving that the PRC deterministic floor (D12.1) is doctrinal-clause-specific rather than blanket: the floor caps recognition-tier moves at the first clause but does not cap engagement-tier moves in the second-clause space, even when (as with the 2024 EETO liaison-officer post on disinformation and foreign interference) those moves carry political substance the PRC opposes.** Five member-state substrate-pulls confirm that the PRC strategy-mix is substrate-tailored: a divergent substrate (LT, doctrinal opposition + EU-bloc loyal cooperation operationally) receives cost-warning + economic-coercion; a convergent substrate (CZ, doctrinal opposition + EU-bloc-aligned operationally + UNSC-rules-based-order anchor) receives inconsistency-shaming + UN-instrument invocation; a monolithic-anti-pole substrate (HU, doctrinal alignment + EU-bloc deviation + PRC inner-trust-ring partnership) receives positive-induction + pre-veto-installation. ### §3.1 — EU-bloc doctrinal architecture + EETO institutional substrate (anchored on q-B1 + q-B5)

The EU's formal relationship with the PRC and the EU's de-facto relationship with Taiwan rest on a single doctrinal foundation captured in the EEAS's own published policy-position language: **"the EU pursues its 'One China' policy and recognises the government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal government of China"** [Source TBD #B1 — EEAS, "The European Union and Taiwan" overview page, eeas.europa.eu/delegations/taiwan/european-union-and-taiwan_en, accessed 2026-05-21]. The verbatim phrasing carries a *structural* property critical to every observation Day-12 documents: **the EU formulation recognises PRC as "the sole legal government of China" and stops there**; it does *not* state — verbatim — that "Taiwan is an integral part of the territory of the People's Republic of China," which is the PRC's own canonical formulation captured in the 1972 PRC-USA Shanghai Communiqué, the 1979 PRC-USA Joint Communiqué on the Establishment of Diplomatic Relations, the PRC's standard speech-act register on the Strait, and reaffirmed in the PRC's 2022 White Paper on the Taiwan Question. The EU's doctrinal floor on Taiwan is *one clause shallower* than the PRC's doctrinal claim. The mechanism is consequential: every load-bearing EU institutional move on Taiwan since 2003 has operated *in the gap between the two clauses*.

The institutional anchor for that mechanism is the **European Economic and Trade Office (EETO) Taipei**, established 2003 [Source TBD #B5-1 — EEAS, "Who we are," eeas.europa.eu/delegations/taiwan/who-we-are_en, accessed 2026-05-21]. Three structural features of the EETO substrate carry forward to every downstream observation in Day-12:

**Feature 1 — institutional double-coding.** The EETO's public-facing name avoids the term "Delegation" (which the EEAS uses since the 2009 Lisbon Treaty for its formally-categorised diplomatic missions abroad under Council Decision 2010/427/EU Art. 5), but the EETO's URL-tree on the EEAS portal is `/delegations/taiwan_en` and its internal email-naming is `DELEGATION-TAIWAN@eeas.europa.eu` (verbatim observation from q-B5 source-pull). The office is **Delegation-internally on systems, Office-externally on public-facing communication** — a deliberate institutional asymmetry that gives the office functional Delegation-class capacity without legal Delegation-class status. The EETO's internal structure (Political and Press/Information Section + Trade Section + Administration Section) mirrors the standard three-section EEAS Delegation layout. This double-coding is itself the sixth deterministic-floor type in the Day-12 D12.2 taxonomy: it *caps* upgrade-trajectory at the public-naming boundary while *expanding* operational capacity below that boundary.

**Feature 2 — no treaty basis, WTO-membership-symmetry anchor.** The EU-Taiwan relationship rests on no treaty instrument [Source TBD #B5-2 — EEAS, "European Union and Taiwan," verbatim: "two like-minded WTO members"]. The legal-status workaround that permits EU institutions to interact with Taiwan without violating one-China policy is *Taiwan's 2002 WTO accession as "Separate Customs Territory of Taiwan, Penghu, Kinmen and Matsu"* (the formal name; "Chinese Taipei" is the short form). The substrate that follows is institutionally rich (7 bilateral dialogue formats — annual trade consultations, mid-term review, industrial-policy dialogue, digital-economy dialogue, and four-plus sectoral working groups on investment, IPR, SPS, TBT, pharmaceuticals-cosmetics) but politically constrained to the WTO-economic register. There is **no political dialogue** with Taiwan at EU-institutional level, **no security dialogue**, **no human-rights dialogue** named in the EEAS public-facing rendering — though EPRS PE 760.357 (March 2024) [Source TBD #B5-4] confirms annual human-rights consultations exist separately. The substrate is configured to maximise functional engagement while remaining textually consistent with the EU one-China policy first clause.

**Feature 3 — EP-as-leading-indicator vs Council-as-trailing-actor.** The EP-2021-10-21 resolution (TA-9-2021-0431, **580 in favour / 26 against / 66 abstentions** vote — Inanna DE-pass verified) proposed renaming the EETO to "European Union Office in Taiwan." The DE-language verbatim text of the renaming clause is **"den Namen des Europäischen Wirtschafts- und Handelsbüros in Taiwan in „Büro der Europäischen Union in Taiwan" abzuändern, um dem breiten Spektrum unserer Beziehungen Rechnung zu tragen"** [Source TBD #B-INANNA-1 — Inanna DE-pass commit `d8e56de`, EP TA-9-2021-0431 verbatim quotation]. **The Council has not implemented this rename in the 4.5 years since.** The EP-vs-Council asymmetry is structurally informative: the EP is doctrinally further from the PRC position than the Council/HR-VP, and the EP's resolutions (TA-9-2021-0431 + RC-B10-0134/2024) sit at a doctrinal tier the Council has not adopted. The substrate evidence falsifies any picture of "the EU" as a single doctrinal actor on Taiwan: there is an **EP-Council doctrinal split**, and the EP leads while the Council trails. The 2024-10-24 EP resolution (RC-B10-0134/2024 → TA-10-2024-0030, **432 in favour / 60 against / 71 abstentions** vote — Inanna DE-pass verified) welcomed the EETO liaison-officer post on disinformation and foreign interference; the DE-language verbatim welcoming clause is **"begrüßt die Entsendung eines Verbindungsbeamten an das Europäische Wirtschafts- und Handelsbüro in Taiwan"** [Source TBD #B-INANNA-2 — Inanna DE-pass commit `d8e56de`]. Note the DE-language term: **"Verbindungsbeamten"** is a civil/diplomatic liaison official, *not* the militarised **"Verbindungsoffizier"** — the EP's terminology choice reinforces the office's non-diplomatic, non-security register and structurally aligns with the EETO's six-domain cooperation list (economy / investment / semiconductor supply chains / disaster management / civil protection / countering disinformation and foreign interference) where security and recognition-elevation domains are deliberately absent. The 2024 liaison-officer post is a 2024 institutional upgrade that operationalised second-clause-space engagement without first-clause perturbation, structurally confirming Feature 1's double-coding mechanism.

**Founding-name page-error documented for this publication.** The EEAS "Who we are" page (eeas.europa.eu/delegations/taiwan/who-we-are_en) states the EETO was "established in 2003 as the EU Office in Taiwan." Inanna's DE-pass cross-check against **European Commission Press Release IP/03/347 (2003)** verifies this is *retroactively erroneous*: the office was established in 2003 *exactly as* the "European Economic and Trade Office," and the EP's 2021 push was the proposal to rename it *to* "EU Office in Taiwan" — a rename the Council has not implemented. The Day-12 publication therefore documents the EEAS-side page-error rather than reproducing it, and Feature 3's empirical claim — that the EP leads doctrinally while the Council trails — is consistent with the EEAS even mis-stating its own founding-name in the direction the EP has been pushing toward since 2021.

**Mechanism summary for §3.1.** The EU-Taiwan substrate is configured as an asymmetric two-clause doctrinal architecture where the EU subscribes to PRC-recognition (first clause) but withholds PRC-territorial-claim (second clause), and the EETO institutional substrate operationalises that asymmetry through (1) public-vs-internal double-coding, (2) WTO-membership-symmetry as legal-status workaround, and (3) EP-Council doctrinal split that lets the EP push the second-clause edge while the Council holds the first-clause floor. The PRC strategy on this substrate is not to retaliate against the EU-bloc directly on second-clause upgrades (which require Council unanimity to authorise and would surface the EP-Council split publicly), but to capture individual member-state veto-floor positions inside the EU machinery — which Day-12 §3.4 documents structurally via Hungary's 2024-05 ALL-WEATHER COMPREHENSIVE STRATEGIC PARTNERSHIP upgrade with the PRC (Pakistan/Russia/Belarus-class tier; first EU member at that tier) and the 2026-H2 Hungary EU Council presidency that the PRC explicitly named as a strategic instrument in the 2024-05-09 Xi-Orbán bilateral readout. The bloc-vs-member-state mechanism observed here is structurally distinct from the Day-10 (JPN, 1972-Communiqué bilateral) and Day-11 (PHL, 1975-Communiqué bilateral + MDT/EDCA alliance) substrates: there is no analogous bilateral PRC-EU treaty floor to anchor the question to, and the EU's response surface is mediated by a 27-member-state unanimity rule that creates structural incentives for PRC to capture-by-veto-floor rather than coerce-by-bilateral-pressure.

**Numerical anchor for §3.1 — Nisaba EETO trade-and-investment audit verbatim** [Source TBD #N-NISABA-1 — Nisaba audit commit `97ab40a`]. The 2021 trade-and-investment-dialogue upgrade is the **only** statistically-detectable institutional-period break in the EU-Taiwan bilateral trade series 2002-2024 (Eurostat `ext_lt_maineu` + `bop_its6_det`). On goods-only 2002-2024 the fixed-2021 Mann-Whitney test returns **U = 0.0, p = 0.000226** and the Chow trend-break test returns **F = 24.886, p = 4.93 × 10⁻⁶**; on goods+services 2010-2024 the fixed-2021 Mann-Whitney returns **U = 0.0, p = 0.00147** and Chow **F = 12.670, p = 0.00140**. The EU-Taiwan goods+services bilateral mean shifts from **€47.5b (2010-2020) to €92.4b (2021-2024)**, a +94.5% mean lift across the 2021 institutional milestone. By contrast, the 2003 EETO-founding date is **not** statistically testable (only one pre-period annual observation in Eurostat's EU27_2020 vintage), and the 2024 liaison-officer date has **only one post-treatment year** — the EETO-founding and liaison-officer milestones therefore *cannot* be empirically anchored to a quantitative trade-break, only the 2021 trade-and-investment-dialogue can. This is consequential for the doctrinal-clause-specific D12.1 finding: the 2021 institutional upgrade — which operationalised the second-clause-space engagement *substantively* through 4+ new sectoral working groups (investment, IPR, SPS, TBT, pharmaceuticals-cosmetics) — is also the upgrade that produced the empirically observable trade-magnitude jump, structurally confirming that second-clause-space institutional moves carry real economic substance the PRC has elected not to retaliate against at bloc-level.

**FDI substrate-bind asymmetry — Nisaba verbatim** [Source TBD #N-NISABA-2 — Nisaba audit, §2]. The EU-bloc Taiwan-linked anchor projects (TSMC Dresden €3.5b + ProLogium Dunkirk €5.2b primary = €8.5-8.7b) sum to less than the Hungary-PRC-linked anchor projects (CATL Debrecen €7.34b primary + BYD Szeged ≈€4b = €11.3b). The ratio Taiwan-EU-bind : Hungary-PRC-bind = **0.75-0.77**, i.e. the Hungary-PRC bind is **roughly 30% larger** than the EU-bloc Taiwan bind. The asymmetry direction matches the political-substrate finding: the PRC-side substrate-bind via Hungary is the larger of the two industrial-investment anchors, consistent with the inner-trust-ring partnership mechanism §3.4 documents. Caveats: BYD €4b and isolated TSMC €3.5b component figures are derived from EPRS PE 760.357 secondary rather than company-primary filings (BYD Szeged primary confirms the plant but omits the amount; TSMC's company page was Cloudflare-blocked in the audit pass); the ratio direction is robust to the €5.0b vs €5.2b ProLogium primary-vs-secondary treatment.

**EU FDI in Taiwan — current Eurostat vintage update** [Source TBD #N-NISABA-3 — Nisaba audit, §3]. EPRS PE 760.357 (March 2024) reports EU direct-investment stock in Taiwan rose from €6.8b in 2013 to €28.4b in 2022 (4.17×, 17.2% CAGR). Nisaba's current Eurostat `bop_fdi6_geo` pull gives **€6.803b (2013) → €31.957b (2022) = 4.70× / 18.75% CAGR** over the same start/end years — i.e. the EPRS figure understates the lift by approximately one full multiplier-of-base, with both versions being year-end FDI stock positions rather than annual-flow averages. The Day-12 publication therefore uses Nisaba's current-vintage **€32.0b / 4.70× / 18.75%** figure rather than EPRS's archived figure. The substantive implication: EU FDI in Taiwan has more than doubled-then-some across the 2013-2022 window, with the deepening rate higher than EPRS's archived figure suggests, and the deepening has continued post-2022 at a slower rate (€31.957b → €30.350b across 2022-2024, a moderate post-pandemic plateau rather than withdrawal).

**EP cohesion as deterministic-floor variable — Nisaba verbatim** [Source TBD #N-NISABA-4 — Nisaba audit, §4]. The 2021 rename resolution (TA-9-2021-0431) passed **580 in favour / 26 against / 66 abstentions = 86.3% yes-share, 22.3:1 yes:no ratio, 9.8% abstain-share**. The 2024 UN-2758 resolution (RC-B10-0134/2024 → TA-10-2024-0030) passed **432 / 60 / 71 = 76.7% yes-share, 7.2:1 yes:no ratio, 12.6% abstain-share**. The 2024 Ukraine financial-and-military-support resolution (RC-B10-0028/2024) — a deliberately conservative comparator since Ukraine support is generally high-consensus in the EP — passed **425 / 131 / 63 = 68.7% yes-share, 3.2:1 yes:no ratio, 10.2% abstain-share**. Both Taiwan resolutions therefore exceed the Ukraine-support comparator on both yes-share and yes:no cohesion: **EP positioning on Taiwan is a supermajority floor, not a marginal preference.** The 2021→2024 cohesion-decline (86.3% → 76.7%) is informative but does not approach the threshold where EP positioning on Taiwan could be characterised as contested at the bloc-doctrinal level. The substrate evidence reinforces Feature 3's EP-as-leading-indicator finding: the EP has not merely *resolved* on Taiwan, the EP has resolved on Taiwan with cohesion strictly above its own high-salience foreign-policy baseline.

**Hungary share of PRC FDI in Europe — partial Nisaba finding** [Source TBD #N-NISABA-5 — Nisaba audit, §5; Rhodium/MERICS 2024]. Hungary captured **31% of all Chinese FDI in Europe in 2024**, the highest share of any country, on a 2024 PRC FDI in EU+UK total of €10b. This is project-announcement / new-greenfield data, not stock-level country-origin matrix data, and therefore confirms only the marginal-flow substrate-bind claim for Hungary; the stronger claim that PRC-origin dominates Hungary's *total* inward FDI stock requires MNB/OECD/Eurostat bilateral FDI positions by ultimate investor (not pulled in the 600s audit budget). The 31% figure is nevertheless empirically anchored to the Day-12 substrate-bind asymmetry thesis: Hungary is the single largest PRC-FDI capture-point in Europe in 2024.

### §3.2 — Lithuanian substrate: divergent doctrinal opposition + EU-bloc loyal cooperation operationally (q-B2)

**Founding act and the naming asymmetry.** The Lithuanian Ministry of Foreign Affairs announced on 2021-07-20 [Source TBD #B2-1 — urm.lt/en/news/928/taiwan-to-open-its-representative-office-in-lithuania:34701] that it had "received a notification from Taipei" of a forthcoming "Taiwanese representative office" in Vilnius, anchoring the substrate in "economic relations and cultural exchanges" and "people-to-people contacts." The clause structure is *notification-received*, not *agreement-concluded* — the LT-MFA built the institutional vehicle (a trade office reciprocity arrangement) without itself naming the office. The actual naming choice — "Taiwanese" rather than "Taipei" — was Taipei's, and Taipei would later claim it was the product of "mutual agreement of both governments after a series of bilateral consultations" [Source TBD #B2-2 — LRT 2024-05-07, Taipei Representative Office statement to LRT]. The two registers do not match: LT-MFA's 2021-07-20 announcement does not mention the office's intended title, does not reference cross-strait military or political-stability framing, and does not reference PRC reaction expectations. The substrate-record contains contradictory bilateral framings of the same institutional act, and the §3.2 prose surfaces the contradiction rather than picking one side. This naming asymmetry is the seed for the entire LT-PRC retaliation cascade: PRC contention was with the *naming*, not with the office's existence (analogous offices under the "Taipei" name exist in 18 EU member states without precipitating a PRC ambassador-recall).

**WTO DS610 procedural arc and silent termination.** The PRC retaliation following the 2021-11-18 office opening — customs IT system deletion, *de facto* embargo on Lithuanian goods, embargo widened to third-country products containing Lithuanian components — drew the EU into a WTO dispute, DS610, filed 2022-01-27. The dispute attracted an unprecedented eighteen third-party joiners (Australia, Brazil, Canada, Colombia, India, Indonesia, Japan, Korea, New Zealand, Norway, Russia, Switzerland, Chinese Taipei, Thailand, Türkiye, UK, US, Viet Nam), the second-most third-party-rich EU-China WTO dispute on record after the 2012 rare-earths case [Source TBD #B2-3 — WTO DS610 case page, wto.org/english/tratop_e/dispu_e/cases_e/ds610_e.htm, snapshot 2026-02-12; chinaobservers.eu (CHOICE), 2023-11 Matěj Hulička]. The case's procedural trajectory was: consultations 2022-01-27 → DSB panel established 2023-01-27 → Director-General composed the panel 2023-04-18 → MPIA arbitration framework agreed by EU+PRC 2023-07-04 → final report estimated 2024-H2 (per Chair's 2023-10-13 letter) → **panel work suspended at EU request 2024-01-25** (Art. 12.12 DSU, "for an indeterminate period of time") → **2025-12-01: EU notified the DSB (WT/DS610/14) that "the key objectives behind the dispute had been met and relevant trade had resumed," and that the dispute is terminated.** No panel ruling on the merits ever issued. The EU's chosen termination language is bilateral-normalisation-fit ("trade has resumed"), not enforcement-fit ("we won and China was held to account") — and Lithuania was never the complainant. Under CCP and TFEU Art. 207 the Commission represented Lithuania throughout: **even at the height of LT's bilateral conflict with the PRC, Lithuania could not bring its own WTO case**. **The doctrinal-gap mechanism re-stated at the institutional level.** The three nested doctrinal formulations from §3.1 — PRC "one China principle" (maximalist), EU "one China policy" (medium), EP positions (narrowest) — operate inside the EETO substrate as a *gap-mechanism* permitting second-clause-space engagement without first-clause-perturbation. The PRC formulation requires both (a) PRC-as-sole-legal-government of China AND (b) Taiwan-as-part-of-China territorial-integrity claim AND (c) no third-state may have official or unofficial ties suggesting otherwise. The EU formulation includes only (a) and adds a *non-binding* "no official ties" register on (b). The EP formulation explicitly rejects (b) and adopts "neither Taiwan nor China is subordinate to the other" + "China's territorial claims have no basis in international law" (the EP Feb-2024 RC-B10-0134 register language extracted from EPRS PE 760.357). Taiwan's MOFA on 2024-10-25 [Source TBD #B5-5 — en.mofa.gov.tw/News_Content.aspx?n=1329&s=118344, Press Release No. 365] **rebuts the PRC formulation publicly** ("MOFA urges the global community to take concrete action to oppose China's misrepresentation of UNGA Resolution 2758 and to refute China's false claim that there is an international consensus on its so-called 'one China principle'") while **accepting the EU formulation** ("Taiwan is a key, like-minded partner of the EU in the Indo-Pacific" — MOFA quoting and embracing the EP Feb-2024 resolution register). This is the doctrinal asymmetry that lets EU-Taiwan ties deepen without triggering Taiwan to claim recognition: **Taiwan attacks the PRC formulation, accepts the EU formulation, builds bilateral substance on the gap between the two.**

**The 2021 institutional milestone and the 2024 liaison-officer probe.** Two empirical anchors mark the second-clause-space engagement trajectory. In 2021, the EP passed TA-9-2021-0431 on 2021-10-21 with a **580-26-66** vote (Inanna DE-pass verified), proposing the rename and substantively endorsing the trade-and-investment-dialogue upgrade and the four-plus sectoral working groups (investment, IPR, SPS, TBT, pharmaceuticals-cosmetics) that operationalise the second-clause-space. The DE-language verbatim of the rename clause is **"den Namen des Europäischen Wirtschafts- und Handelsbüros in Taiwan in „Büro der Europäischen Union in Taiwan" abzuändern, um dem breiten Spektrum unserer Beziehungen Rechnung zu tragen"** [Source TBD #B-INANNA-1 — Inanna DE-pass commit `d8e56de`]. In 2024, the EP passed RC-B10-0134/2024 → TA-10-2024-0030 on 2024-10-24 with a **432-60-71** vote, welcoming the EETO's new liaison-officer post on disinformation and foreign interference. The DE-language verbatim welcoming clause is **"begrüßt die Entsendung eines Verbindungsbeamten an das Europäische Wirtschafts- und Handelsbüro in Taiwan"** [Source TBD #B-INANNA-2 — Inanna DE-pass commit `d8e56de`]. The 2024 liaison-officer post is therefore a 2024 institutional upgrade that operationalised second-clause-space engagement substantively without first-clause perturbation — *structurally confirming* the double-coding's intended function.

**Founding-name page-error documented for the record.** The EEAS "Who we are" page reads: "The European Economic and Trade Office, established in 2003 as the EU Office in Taiwan, is the official representation of the European Union in Taiwan." **Nisaba's numerical anchor on the 2021 milestone.** The 2021 trade-and-investment-dialogue upgrade is the **only** statistically-detectable institutional-period break in the EU-Taiwan bilateral trade series 2002-2024 [Source TBD #N-NISABA-1 — Nisaba audit commit `97ab40a`; Eurostat `ext_lt_maineu` + `bop_its6_det`]. On goods-only 2002-2024 the fixed-2021 Mann-Whitney test returns **U = 0.0, p = 0.000226** and the Chow trend-break test returns **F = 24.886, p = 4.93 × 10⁻⁶**; on goods+services 2010-2024 the fixed-2021 Mann-Whitney returns **U = 0.0, p = 0.00147** and Chow **F = 12.670, p = 0.00140**. The EU-Taiwan goods+services bilateral mean shifts from **€47.5b (2010-2020) to €92.4b (2021-2024)**, a +94.5% mean lift across the 2021 institutional milestone. The 2003 EETO-founding date is *not* statistically testable in annual data (only one pre-period annual observation in Eurostat's EU27_2020 vintage), and the 2024 liaison-officer date has *only one* post-treatment year — the EETO-founding and liaison-officer milestones therefore *cannot* be empirically anchored to a quantitative trade-break, only the 2021 trade-and-investment-dialogue can. The substantive implication is structurally consequential: the 2021 institutional upgrade — which operationalised the second-clause-space engagement *substantively* through 4+ new sectoral working groups (investment, IPR, SPS, TBT, pharmaceuticals-cosmetics) — is also the upgrade that produced the empirically observable trade-magnitude jump, confirming that second-clause-space institutional moves carry real economic substance the PRC has elected not to retaliate against at bloc-level (the PRC retaliated against EP-level rhetorical positions and against individual member-state acts on LT and CZ substrates, but not against the bloc-level engagement-deepening).

**EP cohesion on Taiwan as a supermajority-floor.** Nisaba's cohesion test [Source TBD #N-NISABA-4 — Nisaba audit, §4] benchmarks EP voting on Taiwan against the EP's own high-salience baseline. The 2021 rename resolution (TA-9-2021-0431) passed **86.3% yes-share, 22.3:1 yes:no ratio, 9.8% abstain-share**. The 2024 UN-2758 resolution passed **76.7% yes-share, 7.2:1 yes:no ratio, 12.6% abstain-share**. The 2024 Ukraine financial-and-military-support resolution (RC-B10-0028/2024) — a deliberately conservative comparator — passed **68.7% yes-share, 3.2:1 yes:no ratio, 10.2% abstain-share**. Both Taiwan resolutions therefore exceed the Ukraine-support comparator on both yes-share and yes:no cohesion: **EP positioning on Taiwan is a supermajority floor, not a marginal preference.** The 2021→2024 cohesion-decline (86.3% → 76.7%) is informative but does not approach the threshold where EP positioning on Taiwan could be characterised as contested at the bloc-doctrinal level. The empirical finding: the EP-Council asymmetry §3.1 documents is not a thin-margin majority straining at its own consensus — the EP's leading position on Taiwan is built on a yes-share floor materially above its own foreign-policy convention.
