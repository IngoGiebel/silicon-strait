## Research findings

### §1 — Substrate-selection rationale (US as sixth and final in the five-power arc)

The five-power state-extension arc (Days 13-16: EU, JPN, PHL, UK, AUS, CAN) was designed to test P28 portability across substrates with increasing institutional distance from each other. The US was reserved for last because its instrument-set architecture is structurally unique:

- **Three communiqués** (1972, 1979, 1982) rather than a single recognition instrument
- **A domestic-law counterweight** (TRA, 1979) with no parallel in any other substrate
- **Private diplomatic assurances** (Six Assurances, 1982/declassified 2020) operating as interpretive constraints on the Third Communiqué
- **The most legislatively active Taiwan-related register** in any substrate (Taiwan Travel Act 2018, TAIPEI Act 2020, Taiwan Assurance Act 2021, NDAA provisions FY22-26)

Testing the US last allows the classification framework refined across six prior substrates (Class A collapsed, Class B preserved, architectural-absence) to be applied to a substrate that may require extending the framework itself.

### §2 — The Three Communiqués: lexical floor architecture

#### §2.1 — Shanghai Communiqué (1972-02-28): 认识到 — the weakest floor in the sample

The 1972 Shanghai Communiqué established the foundational Taiwan-status formula in the US-PRC bilateral relationship. The full EN and ZH texts are verified from Wikisource primary archives [Source #1, #2]:

**EN (para 12):**
> "The US side declared: The United States **acknowledges** that all Chinese on either side of the Taiwan Strait maintain there is but one China and that Taiwan is a part of China. The United States Government does not challenge that position."

**ZH:**
> "美国方面声明：美国**认识到**，在台湾海峡两边的所有中国人都认为只有一个中国，台湾是中国的一部分。美国政府对这一立场不提出异议。"

The Chinese verb **认识到** (rènshí dào) means "comes to understand" or "comes to realize." In the diplomatic-register hierarchy established across Days 13-16, it sits below both tested alternatives:

| Verb | Pinyin | Meaning | Substrates | Class |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 承认 | chéngrèn | "recognize/admit" (performative) | AUS 1972, UK 1972, US 1979 | A (collapsed) |
| 注意到 | zhùyì dào | "take note of" (epistemic, minimal) | CAN 1970 | B (preserved) |
| **认识到** | **rènshí dào** | **"come to understand" (cognitive, non-endorsing)** | **US 1972** | **C (unique)** |

认识到 is weaker than 注意到 because it frames the US position as a *cognitive act* ("we have come to understand that Chinese on both sides hold this view") rather than even the minimal epistemic acknowledgment of 注意到 ("we note this position"). The US did not "take note of" PRC's claim; it noted that *both sides' Chinese people* hold this view — a structurally different claim that locates the one-China position in the political views of Chinese people rather than in PRC's sovereign assertion.

The scholarly controversy over this translation is itself load-bearing evidence. 黄安年's 2002 academic paper "究竟是'承认'还是'认识到'?" (Exactly "recognize" or "come to understand"?) in 学术界 (Academia) directly debates whether 认识到 in the 1972 text should be read as equivalent to 承认 [Source #4]. The fact that a Chinese academic journal published a peer-reviewed paper arguing that these verbs are NOT equivalent confirms that the distinction is recognized within PRC academic discourse itself.

#### §2.2 — Normalization Communiqué (1979-01-01): the lexical shift to 承认

The 1979 communiqué introduces a **dual-verb structure** and a **lexical shift** from the 1972 baseline:

**Recognition clause:**
- EN: "The United States of America **recognizes** the Government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal Government of China."
- ZH: "美利坚合众国**承认**中华人民共和国政府是中国的唯一合法政府"

**Taiwan clause:**
- EN: "The Government of the United States of America **acknowledges** the Chinese position that there is but one China and Taiwan is part of China."
- ZH: "美利坚合众国政府**承认**中国的立场，即只有一个中国，台湾是中国的一部分"

In 1979, BOTH "recognizes" and "acknowledges" map to **承认** in the Chinese text. This is the same collapsed-asymmetry pattern confirmed in AUS (Day-15) and UK (Day-14) Class A substrates — but here it appears *within the same substrate's own instrument set*, seven years after the 1972 communiqué used 认识到 for the same English word.

The shift from 认识到 (1972) to 承认 (1979) is flagged as a known controversy in secondary literature. The PRC-published Chinese version uses 承认 (stronger), while the US-published English version retains "acknowledges" (same word as 1972). The Wikipedia ZH article on the three communiqués notes: "中国发表的中文版为'承认'，美国发表的英文版为'acknowledge'；有关此处中译的争议…" [Source #3].

This dual-communiqué structure means the US substrate contains within itself proof that 认识到 and 承认 are semantically distinct in PRC diplomatic Chinese — because PRC chose to use them differently in two instruments with the same US-English word ("acknowledges").

#### §2.3 — August 17 Communiqué (1982-08-17): no new Taiwan-status formula

The Third Communiqué reaffirms "the principles agreed on by the two sides in the Shanghai Communiqué and the Joint Communiqué on the Establishment of Diplomatic Relations" but does not introduce a new Taiwan-status verb. Its operative content concerns arms sales reduction commitments exclusively.

One relevant appearance of 认识到 occurs in a different context: "双方认识到这一问题将会严重妨碍中美关系的发展" ("Both sides understand/recognize that this issue will seriously hinder development of US-China relations"). Here 认识到 is used descriptively (mutual understanding of a fact) rather than as a Taiwan-status formula — confirming that PRC diplomatic Chinese distinguishes between 认识到-as-cognition (descriptive) and 承认-as-endorsement (performative) even within the same document family [Source #3].

### §3 — The legislative counterweight: TRA + Six Assurances

#### §3.1 — Taiwan Relations Act (P.L. 96-8, 1979-04-10)

The TRA is US domestic law, passed by both houses of Congress and signed by President Carter two months after normalization. It creates a **legislative floor** that operates independently of — and in structural tension with — the communiqué-level diplomatic floor.

Key provisions [Source #5, #6]:

- **§2(b)(3):** "to make clear that the United States decision to establish diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China rests upon the expectation that the future of Taiwan will be determined by peaceful means"
- **§2(b)(4):** "to consider any effort to determine the future of Taiwan by other than peaceful means, including by boycotts or embargoes, a threat to the peace and security of the Western Pacific area and of grave concern to the United States"
- **§2(b)(6):** "to maintain the capacity of the United States to resist any resort to force or other forms of coercion that would jeopardize the security, or the social or economic system, of the people on Taiwan"
- **§3(a):** Arms sales authorization — "the United States will make available to Taiwan such defense articles and defense services in such quantity as may be necessary to enable Taiwan to maintain a sufficient self-defense capability"

No other tested substrate has a domestic-law mechanism that:
1. Creates binding security commitments toward Taiwan
2. Authorizes arms sales by statute (not just by executive discretion)
3. Establishes an implementing institution (AIT) by law
4. Explicitly conditions the recognition of PRC on a "peaceful means" expectation

The TRA's legislative floor **exceeds** the diplomatic floor set by the communiqués. Where the communiqués' weakest layer (1972 认识到) merely notes a cognitive understanding without endorsement, the TRA's §2(b)(4) declares that non-peaceful resolution would be "a threat to the peace and security of the Western Pacific area and of grave concern to the United States" — language that implies an obligation to act, not merely to understand.

#### §3.2 — Six Assurances (Reagan, 1982; declassified 2020)

The Six Assurances were private commitments from President Reagan to Taiwan, made contemporaneously with the Third Communiqué (1982) as a counterweight to its arms-sales-reduction language:

1. No set date for ending arms sales to Taiwan
2. No agreement to consult with PRC on arms sales to Taiwan
3. No mediation role between PRC and Taiwan
4. No revision of the Taiwan Relations Act
5. No alteration of US position on Taiwan sovereignty
6. No pressure on Taiwan to negotiate with PRC

These were passed as non-binding concurrent resolutions by both houses of Congress in 2016 (H.Con.Res.88, S.Con.Res.38) and declassified in 2020 [Source #7].

Assurance #5 ("no alteration of US position on Taiwan sovereignty") is structurally significant: it explicitly freezes the US sovereignty-status position at the 1972/1979 level and declares that the 1982 communiqué does not modify it. This means the 1972 认识到 formula — the weakest verb in the sample — is the ANCHORED US position by private diplomatic commitment, even though the 1979 承认 formula exists in a later instrument.
