## Executive summary (3 sentences)

Day-17 extends the P28 substrate-test to the United States as the sixth and structurally most complex substrate, completing the five-power state-extension arc begun at Day-13 (EU+JPN+PHL). The primary-source pull at 14:00 confirmed the core finding: the 1972 Shanghai Communiqué's Chinese text renders "acknowledges" as **认识到** (rènshí dào, "comes to understand") — a verb lexically distinct from both 承认 (AUS/UK Class A) and 注意到 (CAN Class B) — while the 1979 Normalization Communiqué shifts the *same English word* to **承认** (chéngrèn, "recognize/admit"), creating a dual-communiqué lexical architecture found in no other tested substrate [Source #1, #2, #3]. The US is classified as **Class C**: a compound substrate defined by the weakest diplomatic-floor verb in the sample (1972 认识到) combined with the strongest legislative-floor instrument (Taiwan Relations Act 1979), producing the widest diplomatic-to-legislative gap of any substrate tested.

## Today's thesis

If P28(c) holds across a substrate that is structurally unique on three axes — (i) a dual-communiqué lexical architecture where the same English word maps to different Chinese verbs across two foundational instruments, (ii) a domestic-law legislative floor (TRA) with no equivalent in any other substrate, and (iii) a private-commitment counterweight (Six Assurances) that explicitly constrains the interpretive range of a third communiqué — then the predicate is no longer merely portable across Westminster-derived parliamentary systems; it survives the substrate with the most complex instrument-set architecture in the sample, where the diplomatic floor and the legislative floor operate in structurally opposite directions.

## Research findings

### §1 — Substrate-selection rationale (US as sixth and final in the five-power arc)

The five-power state-extension arc (Days 13-16: EU, JPN, PHL, UK, AUS, CAN) was designed to test P28 portability across substrates with increasing institutional distance from each other. The US was reserved for last because its instrument-set architecture is structurally unique:

- **Three communiqués** (1972, 1979, 1982) rather than a single recognition instrument
- **A domestic-law counterweight** (TRA, 1979) with no parallel in any other substrate
- **Private diplomatic assurances** (Six Assurances, 1982/declassified 2020) operating as interpretive constraints on the Third Communiqué
- **The most legislatively active Taiwan-related register** in any substrate (Taiwan Travel Act 2018, TAIPEI Act 2020, Taiwan Assurance Act 2021, NDAA provisions FY22-26)

Testing the US last allows the classification framework refined across six prior substrates (Class A collapsed, Class B preserved, architectural-absence) to be applied to a substrate that may require extending the framework itself.

### §2 — The Three Communiqués: lexical floor architecture

#### §2.1 — Shanghai Communiqué (1972-02-28): 认识到 — the weakest floor in the sample

The 1972 Shanghai Communiqué established the foundational Taiwan-status formula in the US-PRC bilateral relationship. The full EN and ZH texts are verified from Wikisource primary archives [Source #1, #2]:

**EN (para 12):**
> "The US side declared: The United States **acknowledges** that all Chinese on either side of the Taiwan Strait maintain there is but one China and that Taiwan is a part of China. The United States Government does not challenge that position."

**ZH:**
> "美国方面声明：美国**认识到**，在台湾海峡两边的所有中国人都认为只有一个中国，台湾是中国的一部分。美国政府对这一立场不提出异议。"

The Chinese verb **认识到** (rènshí dào) means "comes to understand" or "comes to realize." In the diplomatic-register hierarchy established across Days 13-16, it sits below both tested alternatives:

| Verb | Pinyin | Meaning | Substrates | Class |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 承认 | chéngrèn | "recognize/admit" (performative) | AUS 1972, UK 1972, US 1979 | A (collapsed) |
| 注意到 | zhùyì dào | "take note of" (epistemic, minimal) | CAN 1970 | B (preserved) |
| **认识到** | **rènshí dào** | **"come to understand" (cognitive, non-endorsing)** | **US 1972** | **C (unique)** |

认识到 is weaker than 注意到 because it frames the US position as a *cognitive act* ("we have come to understand that Chinese on both sides hold this view") rather than even the minimal epistemic acknowledgment of 注意到 ("we note this position"). The US did not "take note of" PRC's claim; it noted that *both sides' Chinese people* hold this view — a structurally different claim that locates the one-China position in the political views of Chinese people rather than in PRC's sovereign assertion.

The scholarly controversy over this translation is itself load-bearing evidence. 黄安年's 2002 academic paper "究竟是'承认'还是'认识到'?" (Exactly "recognize" or "come to understand"?) in 学术界 (Academia) directly debates whether 认识到 in the 1972 text should be read as equivalent to 承认 [Source #4]. The fact that a Chinese academic journal published a peer-reviewed paper arguing that these verbs are NOT equivalent confirms that the distinction is recognized within PRC academic discourse itself.

#### §2.2 — Normalization Communiqué (1979-01-01): the lexical shift to 承认

The 1979 communiqué introduces a **dual-verb structure** and a **lexical shift** from the 1972 baseline:

**Recognition clause:**
- EN: "The United States of America **recognizes** the Government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal Government of China."
- ZH: "美利坚合众国**承认**中华人民共和国政府是中国的唯一合法政府"

**Taiwan clause:**
- EN: "The Government of the United States of America **acknowledges** the Chinese position that there is but one China and Taiwan is part of China."
- ZH: "美利坚合众国政府**承认**中国的立场，即只有一个中国，台湾是中国的一部分"

In 1979, BOTH "recognizes" and "acknowledges" map to **承认** in the Chinese text. This is the same collapsed-asymmetry pattern confirmed in AUS (Day-15) and UK (Day-14) Class A substrates — but here it appears *within the same substrate's own instrument set*, seven years after the 1972 communiqué used 认识到 for the same English word.

The shift from 认识到 (1972) to 承认 (1979) is flagged as a known controversy in secondary literature. The PRC-published Chinese version uses 承认 (stronger), while the US-published English version retains "acknowledges" (same word as 1972). The Wikipedia ZH article on the three communiqués notes: "中国发表的中文版为'承认'，美国发表的英文版为'acknowledge'；有关此处中译的争议…" [Source #3].

This dual-communiqué structure means the US substrate contains within itself proof that 认识到 and 承认 are semantically distinct in PRC diplomatic Chinese — because PRC chose to use them differently in two instruments with the same US-English word ("acknowledges").

#### §2.3 — August 17 Communiqué (1982-08-17): no new Taiwan-status formula

The Third Communiqué reaffirms "the principles agreed on by the two sides in the Shanghai Communiqué and the Joint Communiqué on the Establishment of Diplomatic Relations" but does not introduce a new Taiwan-status verb. Its operative content concerns arms sales reduction commitments exclusively.

One relevant appearance of 认识到 occurs in a different context: "双方认识到这一问题将会严重妨碍中美关系的发展" ("Both sides understand/recognize that this issue will seriously hinder development of US-China relations"). Here 认识到 is used descriptively (mutual understanding of a fact) rather than as a Taiwan-status formula — confirming that PRC diplomatic Chinese distinguishes between 认识到-as-cognition (descriptive) and 承认-as-endorsement (performative) even within the same document family [Source #3].

### §3 — The legislative counterweight: TRA + Six Assurances

#### §3.1 — Taiwan Relations Act (P.L. 96-8, 1979-04-10)

The TRA is US domestic law, passed by both houses of Congress and signed by President Carter two months after normalization. It creates a **legislative floor** that operates independently of — and in structural tension with — the communiqué-level diplomatic floor.

Key provisions [Source #5, #6]:

- **§2(b)(3):** "to make clear that the United States decision to establish diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China rests upon the expectation that the future of Taiwan will be determined by peaceful means"
- **§2(b)(4):** "to consider any effort to determine the future of Taiwan by other than peaceful means, including by boycotts or embargoes, a threat to the peace and security of the Western Pacific area and of grave concern to the United States"
- **§2(b)(6):** "to maintain the capacity of the United States to resist any resort to force or other forms of coercion that would jeopardize the security, or the social or economic system, of the people on Taiwan"
- **§3(a):** Arms sales authorization — "the United States will make available to Taiwan such defense articles and defense services in such quantity as may be necessary to enable Taiwan to maintain a sufficient self-defense capability"

No other tested substrate has a domestic-law mechanism that:
1. Creates binding security commitments toward Taiwan
2. Authorizes arms sales by statute (not just by executive discretion)
3. Establishes an implementing institution (AIT) by law
4. Explicitly conditions the recognition of PRC on a "peaceful means" expectation

The TRA's legislative floor **exceeds** the diplomatic floor set by the communiqués. Where the communiqués' weakest layer (1972 认识到) merely notes a cognitive understanding without endorsement, the TRA's §2(b)(4) declares that non-peaceful resolution would be "a threat to the peace and security of the Western Pacific area and of grave concern to the United States" — language that implies an obligation to act, not merely to understand.

#### §3.2 — Six Assurances (Reagan, 1982; declassified 2020)

The Six Assurances were private commitments from President Reagan to Taiwan, made contemporaneously with the Third Communiqué (1982) as a counterweight to its arms-sales-reduction language:

1. No set date for ending arms sales to Taiwan
2. No agreement to consult with PRC on arms sales to Taiwan
3. No mediation role between PRC and Taiwan
4. No revision of the Taiwan Relations Act
5. No alteration of US position on Taiwan sovereignty
6. No pressure on Taiwan to negotiate with PRC

These were passed as non-binding concurrent resolutions by both houses of Congress in 2016 (H.Con.Res.88, S.Con.Res.38) and declassified in 2020 [Source #7].

Assurance #5 ("no alteration of US position on Taiwan sovereignty") is structurally significant: it explicitly freezes the US sovereignty-status position at the 1972/1979 level and declares that the 1982 communiqué does not modify it. This means the 1972 认识到 formula — the weakest verb in the sample — is the ANCHORED US position by private diplomatic commitment, even though the 1979 承认 formula exists in a later instrument.

### §4 — P28(c) test result on US substrate

**P28(c, t=3) on US**: PASSES — strongest PASS in the sample.

The US substrate produces Tier-3 legislative instruments that are not merely proposed but *enacted into law*, a depth of advancement unmatched by any other tested substrate:

1. **Taiwan Relations Act** (P.L. 96-8, 1979) — the foundational Tier-2 legislative floor. Sections §2(b)(3-6) are now verified verbatim from the US Code (22 USC §3301) [Source #8]:
   - §2(b)(3): "to make clear that the United States decision to establish diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China rests upon the expectation that the future of Taiwan will be determined by peaceful means"
   - §2(b)(4): "to consider any effort to determine the future of Taiwan by other than peaceful means, including by boycotts or embargoes, a threat to the peace and security of the Western Pacific area and of grave concern to the United States"
   - §2(b)(6): "to maintain the capacity of the United States to resist any resort to force or other forms of coercion that would jeopardize the security, or the social or economic system, of the people on Taiwan"

2. **Taiwan Travel Act** (P.L. 115-135, 2018) — permits high-level official travel between the US and Taiwan. First enacted statute to explicitly sanction diplomatic contact since 1979 normalization [Source #9, secondary — primary verification deferred].

3. **TAIPEI Act** (P.L. 116-135, 2020) — directs the US government to support Taiwan's participation in international organizations and strengthen bilateral economic ties [Source #10, secondary — primary verification deferred].

4. **Taiwan Assurance Act of 2020** (P.L. 116-260, div. FF, subtitle B, §§311-315) — codified in the Consolidated Appropriations Act 2021. Requires regular arms sales reviews and reaffirms the TRA and Six Assurances as "cornerstones" of US-Taiwan relations [Source #8, statutory notes].

5. **BOLSTER Act** (P.L. 118-159, div. E, title LI, §5121, 2024) — "Building Options for the Lasting Security of Taiwan through European Resolve." Mandates US-European consultations on coordinated sanctions against PRC "if the PRC is found to be involved in" threats to Taiwan. This is the first US statute explicitly conditioning *multilateral* sanctions coordination on a Taiwan contingency [Source #8, statutory notes, verbatim verified].

6. **Taiwan Assurance Implementation Act** (P.L. 119-45, 2025) — amends the 2020 Taiwan Assurance Act [Source #8, statutory notes].

**Floor-stability assessment (54 years, 1972-2026):**

The 1972 认识到 formula has survived 54 years of interpretive pressure without formal displacement. The 1979 承认 shift exists within the same substrate's instrument set, creating the dual-communiqué tension described in §2. But the 1972 formula has HELD as the anchored US position for three structural reasons:

(i) The Six Assurances (1982) explicitly freeze the US sovereignty-status position and declare that the 1982 communiqué does not modify it. Since the 1979 承认 formula and the 1982 communiqué form a continuous interpretive chain, and the Six Assurances sever the ratchet mechanism at 1982, the 1972 认识到 floor is preserved by private diplomatic commitment.

(ii) Every subsequent legislative instrument (TRA 1979, TTA 2018, TAIPEI Act 2020, TAA 2020, BOLSTER Act 2024, TAIA 2025) builds *above* the 1972 cognitive-acknowledgment floor without requiring or implying 承认-level endorsement of PRC's sovereignty claim. The legislative floor exceeds the diplomatic floor in the opposite direction — strengthening ties with Taiwan rather than conceding sovereignty claims.

(iii) The executive practice of "strategic ambiguity" is itself a *product* of the 认识到/承认 gap. If the US position had collapsed to 承认 (as AUS/UK Class A did), there would be no ambiguity to maintain. The persistence of the ambiguity doctrine confirms that the 认识到 floor is the operative baseline, not the 承认 variant.

**GWW3 predicate formulation:**

P28(c, t=3, λ=C, κ=absent) where:
- c = US substrate
- t = 3 (Tier-3 testing: multiple enacted statutes, not merely committee activity)
- λ = C (dual-communiqué: 认识到 layer + 承认 layer + legislative counterweight)
- κ = absent (no commission-of-inquiry equivalent; the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission [USCC] is a standing congressional monitoring body, not an event-triggered judicial commission as in CAN)

The λ=C encoding requires a dual-layer predicate that the λ=A and λ=B substrates did not need. Where λ=A substrates (UK, AUS) have a single diplomatic-floor verb and λ=B substrates (CAN) have a single preserved verb, λ=C substrates contain *both* a weak floor (认识到) and a strong floor (承认) within the same bilateral relationship, plus a legislative floor (TRA) that exceeds both. The predicate must capture this three-layer architecture:

- **Layer D₁** (diplomatic-weak): 1972 认识到 — cognitive acknowledgment, non-endorsing
- **Layer D₂** (diplomatic-strong): 1979 承认 — performative recognition, collapsed
- **Layer L** (legislative): TRA + subsequent statutes — binding domestic law, exceeds D₁ and D₂

P28(c) PASSES because Tier-3 instruments proliferate above all three layers without any being withdrawn under PRC pressure. The no-crisis condition is satisfied at the legislative level: none of the six enacted statutes (TRA through TAIA 2025) has been repealed, suspended, or substantively modified in response to PRC diplomatic protests. PRC has protested each instrument — most notably through military exercises (August 2022 following Speaker Pelosi's Taiwan visit) and diplomatic downgrades — but the legislative floor has only *risen* in the 47 years since the TRA, never retreated.

### §5 — Substrate-discriminating refinement: Class C and the updated predicate-status table

**Class C formal definition:**

Class C = a substrate exhibiting dual-communiqué lexical architecture, defined by the conjunction of:
1. A **weak diplomatic floor** (认识到 or cognate: cognitive, non-endorsing) in the foundational instrument
2. A **strong diplomatic floor** (承认 or cognate: performative, collapsed) in a subsequent instrument within the same bilateral relationship
3. A **legislative floor** (domestic law creating binding security commitments toward Taiwan) that exceeds both diplomatic floors in institutional weight
4. The structural property that the same English word ("acknowledges") maps to *different* Chinese verbs across the substrate's own instrument set

Currently, only the US satisfies all four conditions. No other tested substrate has a dual-communiqué verb structure or a domestic-law legislative floor.

**Observations on the complete table:**

1. **Advancement ceiling is the key discriminant.** UK/AUS reach select-committee level. CAN reaches first-reading (House) / second-reading (Senate). The US reaches *enacted law* — the highest possible advancement in a legislative register. The advancement-ceiling axis now spans five tiers: committee report → EDM/debate → first reading → second reading → enacted law.

2. **λ-class distribution**: A=2 (UK, AUS), B=1 (CAN), C=1 (US), n/a=3 (EU, JPN, PHL). The three lexical classes each map to a different region of the advancement-ceiling axis: Class A substrates (collapsed) reach committee level; Class B (preserved) reaches first/second reading; Class C (dual-communiqué + legislative floor) reaches enacted law. This correlation between lexical-class and advancement-ceiling requires testing whether it is causal or coincidental — the sample size (4 substrates with λ values) is insufficient to determine.

3. **κ remains single-instantiation.** Only CAN has κ=present. The US's USCC is a standing advisory body, not a triggered commission. The κ question flagged in Day-16 §6.4 remains open.

**§5.1 — Hassaleh-Nexus implications (US substrate)**

The US substrate carries the heaviest Nexus implications of any tested substrate, across three registers:

1. **Semiconductor supply-chain nexus**: The CHIPS and Science Act (P.L. 117-167, 2022) allocates ~$52.7B for domestic semiconductor manufacturing, of which TSMC's Phoenix, Arizona fab complex is the largest single recipient category. The US substrate's P28 PASS means the legislative floor supporting Taiwan-adjacent semiconductor investment is reinforced by the same institutional architecture that sustains the TRA. For Hassaleh-Nexus, the CHIPS Act is a supply-chain-reshoring signal that compounds the P28 floor: if Tier-3 instruments (including CHIPS) survive PRC pressure, the reshoring investment thesis is more durable than if it rested on executive action alone. [CHIPS Act specifics: primary verification deferred to Day-18 research fire.]

2. **Arms-sales corridor**: The TRA §3(a) authorizes arms sales by statute. The US is the only substrate where Taiwan-directed defence supply is a *statutory obligation* rather than an executive-discretion decision. For Hassaleh-Nexus, this means the arms-sales corridor is legislatively insulated from executive-level diplomatic concessions — a structural property that no other substrate's defence relationship with Taiwan possesses.

3. **ITAR and export-control architecture**: US International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) and Export Administration Regulations (EAR) create a jurisdiction-risk layer for any entity handling dual-use technology in the US-Taiwan-PRC triangle. The BOLSTER Act's mandated US-European sanctions consultation adds a multilateral dimension to this jurisdiction-risk: European entities with US nexus now face coordinated sanctions risk under a Taiwan contingency, not just US-unilateral ITAR exposure.
