### §2 — AUS-PRC relationship-architecture context

The AUS-PRC bilateral relationship is anchored in three load-bearing documents, all referenced verbatim in the lexical-baseline finding below:
- The **1972 Joint Communiqué** (Whitlam government, December 1972) — establishes recognition of the PRC and the "acknowledges the position" Taiwan-clause formula. Source: MOFA-PRC official English text [Source #1].
- The **Joint Statement on Bilateral Cooperation (2003)** and successor frameworks — anchor the post-2003 economic-strategic dialogue architecture (not Taiwan-loadbearing for P28(c) test, but relevant to §5 substrate-discriminating refinement).
- The **AUKUS announcement (September 2021)** and trilateral instruments — the shock-event whose post-2021 Hansard register is the substrate-discriminating test surface for the P28(c, t=3) instrument-tier-floor.

The lexical-baseline finding of Day-15 14:00 cron-C [Source #3, primary archival]: AUS 1972 uses the **identical** "acknowledges the position" formula as UK 1972, with three structural differences from the UK case:
1. **The negotiating record is preserved publicly** — DFAT Historical Documents Volume 22 cablegrams 345, 347, 352, 353, 359, 361, 366, 381, 382 [Source #2 secondary, Source #3 primary] document the negotiating arc verbatim, where UK's 1972 negotiating record is FCO-Foreign-Office-archived but not equivalently public.
2. **AUS's opening position was "takes note"** — explicitly the Canadian formula — with DFAT 345 (Anderson→Renouf instruction) framing it as "one of several formulas which various countries have used, all of them falling short of endorsing Peking's claim" [Source #2].
3. **DFAT 361 (Waller→Whitlam minute) identified a CN-EN lexical asymmetry**: "the Chinese characters used for 'acknowledging' are the same as those used elsewhere in the communiqué for 'recognising'" [Source #2 quoting primary archival]. This asymmetry is what Inanna's 17:00 cron-D pull is verifying against MOFA-PRC's actual Chinese text — see §4 (pending pull resolution).

The contemporaneous AUS Government self-interpretation of the settled-on "acknowledges" formula was sharply non-endorsing: in the Waller→Whitlam minute, the Government characterises the outcome as "we stood up to China on the Taiwan issue and achieved a satisfactory result" [Source #3 primary archival]. This is substrate-strengthening for the Day-14 EN-LOCK reading that 1972-era *acknowledge*-formulas were intended as deliberate non-endorsements at the lexical layer, and that the persistence of the Tier-1/Tier-2 stratification across 54 years reflects the original lexical design and not later restatement-drift.

### §3 — JSCFADT/Senate-FADTRC institutional context

The AUS parliamentary architecture relevant to the P28(c) test has three layers:

**§3.1 — Joint Standing Committee on Foreign Affairs, Defence and Trade (JSCFADT)**. JSCFADT is the principal joint committee on foreign-policy/defence/trade matters, drawing members from both the House of Representatives and the Senate. Its inquiry-and-report architecture produces Committee reports with findings + recommendations, and the Government tables a formal response (typically within 6 months; routinely delayed). The Committee chair rotates with government; the deputy-chair and a Senate-side membership provide a structural cross-party check.

For the P28(c) test, the Day-14 institutional-distance framing applies: JSCFADT findings sit at the *select-committee* Tier-3 register (analogous to UK FAC HC 172 §142), the Government response sits at the Tier-2 register (analogous to UK FCDO ministerial dispatch-box), and the gap between the two — when persistent across years and restatement-cycles — is the substrate-discriminating signal. **§3.2 — Senate Foreign Affairs, Defence and Trade References Committee (Senate FADTRC)**. The Senate-side References Committee operates independently of the joint JSCFADT and runs Senate-References inquiries that are structurally guaranteed to be opposition-or-crossbench triggered (References Committee chair convention). This produces a *higher-Tier-3-floor* dynamic: a finding from a Senate References inquiry already had to clear the Senate-Selection-of-Bills + References-Committee-acceptance threshold, both of which over-represent opposition-and-crossbench positions vis-à-vis the Government of the day.

**§3.3 — Institutional-distance score (preliminary, refined at §4 after Inanna pull)**. JSCFADT-joint sits at an institutional-distance from the executive that is structurally distinct from UK FAC select-committee, on two axes: (a) JSCFADT-joint includes Senate members with independent foreign-affairs jurisdiction, raising the distance-floor on Tier-3 findings; (b) the Government-response convention is more formalised in AUS than in UK (formal tabled response vs UK's mix of dispatch-box statements + written ministerial statements). On the architectural-distance × instrument-tier-floor space introduced in Day-14 EN-LOCK §5, AUS is a *higher-tier-floor* substrate than UK — which makes P28(c, t=3) the appropriate falsifiable test, not P28(c, t=2): if AUS produces a Tier-3 finding at JSCFADT-joint or Senate-FADTRC level that the Government persistently restates at Tier-2 without crisis-induced revision, the predicate survives a substrate where the test-threshold is *raised* relative to UK.

## §4 — Game-theoretic framing (GWW3): P28(c, t=3) AUS-substrate test result

### §4.1 — Result: P28(c, t=3) holds on AUS at JSCFADT-joint + Senate-FADTRC level

The Inanna pull surfaces verbatim Tier-3 framings at both Committee layers:

**JSCFADT Tier-3 (Rank #1, Supporting democracy in our region)** — the Committee explicitly cites evidence framing Taiwan as a *functioning democracy* and the PRC as an authoritarian regime, and explicitly names Taiwan as a Committee-level Tier-3 reference subject [Source #5]:

> "Overall, nearly half of the people in the region live in an authoritarian regime (72 percent of those live in China)... Even high and mid performing democracies such as Japan or **Taiwan** are suffering democratic erosion."

The Government response to this Committee finding lagged 22 months (Nov-2023 → Sep-2025), which is the structural-distance signal the Day-14 EN-LOCK §5 architectural-distance framework predicted: when a Tier-3 finding takes the Government 22 months to acknowledge in Tier-2 register, the institutional-distance is operative and the substrate produces a positive P28(c) anchor at the instrument-tier-floor t=3.

**JSCFADT Tier-3 (Rank #2, International armed conflict decision making)** — the Committee report explicitly considered PRC-blockade-of-Taiwan scenarios in its evidence/findings layer [Source #6, #7]:

> "...If China blockaded Taiwan, as it practised doing in August, or if the..."
> "If the anticipated war with China over Taiwan or the South China Sea occurs, ... Inquiry into international armed conflict decision making."

Publishing explicit cross-strait war-scenario hypotheticals in a parliamentary-committee report exceeds what DFAT's Tier-2 diplomatic-ambiguity register would tolerate. This is the *sharpest* Tier-3 vs Tier-2 institutional-distance signal Day-15 surfaced.

**Senate FADTRC Tier-3 (Rank #1, Uyghur Forced Labour Bill)** — the References Committee made explicit Tier-3 PRC-naming findings and recommended operational action [Source #8]:

> "Given the combination of (i) manifest evidence of Uyghurs being subjected to forced labour in China, (ii) the difficulty, occasioned by deliberate regime obstruction, of accessing Xinjiang to audit supply chains and (iii) the exposure of Australian supply chains to goods from Xinjiang, it is appropriate to give clarity to Australian importers by issuing measures specific to this situation."
> "The committee recommends that, once the issuance of such orders is possible, the Australian Border Force should immediately consider issuing an order, at a minimum, for cotton sourced from Xinjiang."

This Senate-References finding clears the higher-Tier-3-floor that §3.2 predicted Senate FADTRC produces over JSCFADT-joint: the Border Force operational-recommendation register is *qualitatively* different from JSCFADT-joint's reporting register, not merely quantitatively faster.

### §4.2 — Refinement to the predicate family: addition of a CN-substrate-lexical-asymmetry sub-predicate

The Inanna §1 finding requires a structural refinement to the P28 predicate family. Inanna's verbatim CN-text pull from MOFA-PRC's Mandarin pages shows:

> 澳大利亚政府**承认**中华人民共和国政府是中国的唯一合法政府，**承认**中国政府关于台湾是中华人民共和国的一个省的立场...

The Chinese text uses **承认** (chéngrèn — recognise/admit) for *both* the recognition of the PRC government *and* the "acknowledging" of the PRC's Taiwan position. The DFAT 361 (Waller→Whitlam minute) 1972 concern is **confirmed**: the EN/CN lexical asymmetry is **collapsed** in the CN text. This is not a translation slip but a structurally-deliberate PRC unitary-namespace move — preserved verbatim across the 54-year archival record on MOFA-PRC's own publication.

**Predicate-family addition (Day-15 contribution)**: a CN-substrate-lexical-asymmetry sub-predicate `P28(c, t, λ)` where λ ∈ {EN-only, EN-CN-divergent, EN-CN-collapsed} marks the lexical-substrate-discriminating property of the underlying instrument. AUS-1972 evaluates as P28(c, t=3, λ=EN-CN-collapsed); UK-1972 evaluates the same on the EN-side but the UK FCO has not preserved an equivalent CN-side primary-text in equivalent verbatim accessibility, leaving UK-1972 as λ=EN-only-canonical (pending future CN-text pull). The Day-14 UK substrate is *consistent* with EN-CN-collapsed if the CN-text matches AUS-1972 verbatim — Inanna's pull provides the prior for a Day-16 UK-1972-CN-text verification fire.

### §5.1 — Direct supply-chain signal: Senate FADTRC Uyghur Bill cotton-order recommendation

The Senate FADTRC's explicit recommendation that "the Australian Border Force should immediately consider issuing an order, at a minimum, for cotton sourced from Xinjiang" [Source #8] is the most operational supply-chain signal Day-15 surfaced. The implications for Hassaleh-Nexus market-signal scoring:
- **ASX-listed apparel/retail import chains** with Xinjiang-cotton exposure (Cotton On, Country Road, Wesfarmers retail divisions with PRC-supplier networks) are direct exposure to a Border Force order if the Senate finding propagates to a Government response.

If AUS Border Force orders, the AUS market-share is smaller but the regional-trade-rerouting signal is real, particularly for AUS-listed firms with ASEAN-supplier optionality.
