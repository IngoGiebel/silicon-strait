## Executive summary

The PRC coercion toolkit is systemic policy, not ad-hoc retaliation: a deliberate 2010→2026 architecture spanning trade weaponization, supply-chain license-as-veto, third-country leverage, lawfare, gray-zone operational tempo, narrative ops, and diplomatic blockage. Two anchor cases — Lithuania 2021–2026 (single-vector intensity, ~4.5y sustained resistance) and Australia 2020–2024 (multi-vector layering, ~3y political reset 2022–2023 / nearly 4y to wine-tariff final resolution 2024-03) — reveal that target-state resistance is endogenous to two structural variables, *bloc-backstop presence* and *GDP-vulnerability ratio*, rather than to the duration or intensity of PRC pressure. The same-day MOFCOM Notice #21 of 2026-05-02 — first public activation of the 2021 阻断办法 framework — forces a sharper reading: PRC is now simultaneously coercion-actor and coercion-defendant, and the coercion-toolkit category is converging across major-power statecraft as a shared institutional language.

## Today's thesis

**The PRC coercion toolkit is a systemic, multi-stage construction — not a sequence of ad-hoc reactions.** The legal-architecture timeline alone proves the systemic claim: Unreliable Entity List 2019-05 announcement → 2020-09 formal publication → 反外国制裁法 (Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law) 2021-06 → State Council enforcement regulations 2025-03 → Blocking Order #21 against named U.S. EOs 2026-05-02. Five stages over seven years, in PRC's own legal voice [Source 8, 9, 10].

**Resistance is not a function of pressure.** The cross-case finding from Lithuania and Australia is counter-intuitive: a small EU member with low PRC trade-share (Lithuania, below ~1% of broad export base, denominator-dependent) is harder to coerce than a medium Pacific democracy with high PRC trade-share (Australia, ~30–35% of exports, denominator-dependent) and no equivalent bloc-backstop. **GDP-vulnerability ratio + bloc-backstop presence** explain the divergence better than population, ideology, or duration of pressure [Source 2, 3].

**The toolkit category itself is converging.** PRC's 2026-05-02 blocking order is structurally identical to the EU Anti-Coercion Instrument (Reg 2023/2675) [Source 1]: a Schelling commitment that responds to coercion *as defined by the state issuing the response*. Same architecture, opposed political directions. Day-5's honest reading: the coercion-toolkit category is the *competitive equilibrium* of major-power statecraft circa 2020–2026, not a PRC-exclusive deviation.

**Implication for the GWW3 model:** a Taiwan simulation that treats coercion as a PRC-side parameter misspecifies the problem. The correct model has *coercion-architecture mirroring* across actors and *target-state structural exposure* (GDP-vulnerability ratio + bloc-backstop presence) as the resistance-side load-bearing variables. The war-or-peace boundary is not bilateral force-ratio — it is the convergence of coercion-architecture choices across actors and the structural-exposure profile of the target-state coalition.

## Research findings

### Toolkit categories — the eight-fold structure

The Day-5 scoping fire (2026-05-02 11:00) enumerated eight coercion-toolkit categories that recur across the 2010–2026 PRC case literature:

1. **Trade weaponization** — blanket import bans, country-of-origin delisting, tariffs (Lithuania 2021, Australia 2020, Norway 2010, S. Korea 2017).
2. **Supply-chain chokes / license-as-veto** — rare earths 2010 + 2023, gallium/germanium 2023–2024, magnesium 2024, US-targeted export bans 2024-12 [Source 4].
3. **Tourism and student-flow** — S. Korea THAAD 2017, Australia 2020, Taiwan group-tour bans 2019–2025.
4. **FDI and BRI-leverage** — bidirectional decoupling (AU Chinese-investment value down ~61% in 2020 on KPMG/CIS investment-flow basis; FIRB approval-count series may differ — methodology not interchangeable); Hambantota / Mombasa / Mattala precedent set as bargaining-chip examples in coercion analysis.
5. **Lawfare** — UNCLOS reinterpretation (2016 SCS award disregard), NSL extraterritoriality, Hong Kong arbitration framework, the 2019→2026 PRC counter-coercion legal architecture covered below [Source 8, 9, 10].
6. **Gray-zone military** — operational tempo as substitute for narrative dominance (Day-4 framing); CCG presence in Scarborough/Senkaku/Taiwan ADIZ [Day 4 cross-reference].
7. **Narrative ops / Wolf Warrior diplomacy** — peak 2019–2021, Xi recalibration 2021-05, Zhao Lijian reassigned 2023-01, "belligerent answers declined to 2018 levels" by 2025 per *The Economist*; non-monotonic retreat (Osaka 2025-11 anomaly) [Source 6].
8. **Diplomatic pressure** — UN-system blocking (WHO/ICAO/Interpol Taiwan exclusion), Wolf-Warrior phase, named hostage diplomacy (Two Michaels), ambassador summoning.

These are not analytically independent: most cases deploy three or more vectors simultaneously. The diagnostic question is not which vector but *how many concurrently* — concurrent multi-vector deployment is the higher-cost-to-back-down equilibrium and is therefore PRC's audience-cost commitment-magnitude signal.

### Anchor case 1 — Lithuania 2021–2026: single-vector intensity, sustained resistance

The trigger was nominal, not policy-substantive: Vilnius opened a "Taiwanese Representative Office" in 2021-08 — first under that name in Europe (rather than the typical "Taipei" formulation) [Source 2].

PRC response was rapid and concentrated. 2021-11-21: diplomatic downgrade to chargé d'affaires, ambassador recall, demand for reciprocation. 2021-12-03: country-of-origin delisting and full import block. December 2021 onwards: pressure on multinationals to drop Lithuanian components from supply chains — a third-country lever the EU Chamber of Commerce called "unprecedented." This is the high-leverage move, multiplying effective coercion 10–30x by extending to firms with PRC-market exposure (German manufacturing footprint in Lithuania).

The target-state path was not capitulation. President Nausėda in 2022-01 acknowledged the office decision was a "mistake" — but proposed no policy reversal. By 2024-10 a new coalition promised to restore full diplomatic relations, while in 2024-11 Lithuania *escalated* on the infrastructure axis (restricted Chinese access to renewable energy, expelled three Chinese diplomats). This concession-on-diplomacy / escalation-on-infrastructure divergence is the case's signature move. By 2026-02, PM Inga Ruginienė: Lithuania "really jumped in front of a train and lost"; office could be renamed to "Taipei" — partial concession at the framing level, not yet enacted [Source 2].

**Two structural facts produced the hold.** First, the bloc backstop: the 2023-08 EU WTO case against PRC (DS610) reversed leverage direction; the EU Anti-Coercion Instrument (Reg 2023/2675) was *catalyzed by* the Lithuania case (named in the legislative history) [Source 1]. Second, the GDP-vulnerability ratio: Lithuania-PRC direct export exposure was small — roughly hundreds of millions of euros annually, below ~1% of Lithuania's broad export base depending on denominator (goods vs goods+services) [Nisaba: primary citation still needed]. The coercion *quantity* was high; the coercion *target's structural exposure* was low.

### Anchor case 2 — Australia 2020–2024: multi-vector layering, eventual break

The trigger envelope was broader: PM Morrison's 2020-04 call for COVID-origin investigation against an accumulating 2017–2019 friction (foreign-interference law, Huawei 5G ban, S. China Sea posture). PRC response was the densest commodity dataset in the case literature [Source 3].

Within seven months PRC deployed eight commodity vectors concurrently: 2020-05-12 beef ban (4 AU processors accounting for about 35% of Australia's beef exports to China — a destination-share figure, not a share of all AU beef exports); 2020-05-19 80.5% barley tariff; 2020-06-19 state-attributed cyber-attacks; 2020-08 wine "anti-dumping" investigation; 2020-10 cotton informal blockade; 2020-11 lobster customs delays; 2020-11-27 wine tariffs at 107.1%–212.1% interim, later up to about 218.4% combined anti-dumping/countervailing final duties; 2020-12 full coal blockade and Victoria/NSW/SA timber blocks. AU counter-measures: 2021-04 cancel Victoria's BRI agreement; 2021-06 WTO complaint on wine tariffs (DS602).

The lifting cascade was slow and economically rational from PRC's side. Coal lifted first informally in 2023-04 — *opaque*, no announcement, because PRC's own coal supply gap forced de-facto re-import. Then formal recoveries: barley 2023-08, wine 2024-03 (AU drops WTO case in parallel). Lifting sequence ranks PRC-side substitution pain, not target-state damage. The target-state political driver was 2022-05 Albanese government taking office; coalition turnover, not policy reversal under continuous coalition.

**Two structural facts produced the break.** First, no equivalent bloc backstop: Five Eyes coordinates intelligence, not trade; CPTPP did not respond as a bloc to PRC measures. Second, GDP-vulnerability: AU-PRC trade absorbed roughly one-third of Australian exports at peak (~30–35%, denominator-dependent on goods-only vs goods+services). The AU economy had asymmetric structural exposure that Lithuania did not.

### Cross-case comparison — the bloc-backstop and GDP-vulnerability variables

| Axis | Lithuania | Australia | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vector concentration | Single-vector intensity | Multi-vector layering (8 commodities + cyber) | Concentration is not effectiveness; LT held longer |
| Bloc backstop | EU (ACI-creating) | None equivalent | Bloc backstop = decisive variable for hold |
| Resolution path | Holding ≥4.5y; partial concession Feb 2026 | Broke 2023–2024 under coalition turnover | Coalition turnover trigger > pressure-duration |
| Pre-coercion trade share of target exports | <1% (denominator-dependent) | ~30–35% (denominator-dependent) | Inverse correlation with hold-duration |

The decisive variables are *structural*, not *behavioral*. **A small EU member with low PRC trade-share is harder to coerce than a medium Pacific democracy with high PRC trade-share and no equivalent bloc.** This is the publication's load-bearing comparative finding — pure size + economic-power matters less than *structural exposure ratio* + *bloc embedding*.

The case-attrition curve assembled from the five candidate cases (Norway, S. Korea THAAD, Australia, Lithuania, Japan rare-earths) shows duration-at-resolution ranging from 8 months (S. Korea) to 6 years 2 months (Norway), with Lithuania at 4y3m and counting. Variance across cases is dominated not by the PRC measure-mix but by the GDP-vulnerability ratio and bloc-backstop variables [research/economic_pressure_pathways/notes-2026-05-02-case-studies.md; Nisaba audit applied — Lithuania impact/GDP revised from <0.3% to ~0.45–0.55%; Australia ~1.4–1.5%; S. Korea ~0.9–1.0%; Norway ~0.26%; Japan rare-earths regime-level (n/a). Ordering remains monotonic, supporting the bloc-backstop-explained variance].

### The 2019→2026 PRC counter-coercion legal architecture

Three ZH-language primaries pulled in the 21:00 fire establish the systemic claim from PRC's own legal voice:

**Stage 1 (2019-05 → 2020-09): Unreliable Entity List 不可靠实体清单** [Source 9]. Announced 2019-05-31, formally published 2020-09-19 by MOFCOM with State Council approval. Authority: Foreign Trade Law + National Security Law. Justification phrasing: "不遵守市场规则，背离契约精神" (fail to follow market rules, violate contractual principles); "非商业目的" (non-commercial — i.e. politically motivated) blockades; "严重损害中国企业正当权益" (serious harm to legitimate Chinese enterprise rights). 2023 first additions: Lockheed Martin, Raytheon Missiles & Defense, Boeing Defense. 2024–2026: 72+ U.S. entities added, several Korean and Japanese entities in 2026.

**Stage 2 (2021-06): Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law 反外国制裁法** [Source 8]. Enacted 2021-06-10, 13th NPC Standing Committee 29th session, Presidential Order No. 90. Three primary countermeasure categories — travel restrictions, asset freezing, commercial restrictions — plus "其他必要措施" (other necessary measures), an open-ended residual. Sanction targets through 2024: Lockheed Martin, Raytheon, General Dynamics; U.S. officials and legislators; think tanks (Hudson Institute, Reagan Library); Canadian institutions.

**Stage 3 (2025-03-24): State Council enforcement regulations.** Operationalized the 2021 law across government departments. Converted the 2021 framework from a statutory shell into a functional administrative regime [Source 8].

**Stage 4 (2026-05-02): Blocking Order #21** [Source 10]. MOFCOM 商务部公告2026年第21号 — first public activation of the 阻断办法 (Rules on Counteracting Unjustified Extraterritorial Application of Foreign Legislation, 2021) framework, against named U.S. Executive Orders 13902 and 13846 and five named PRC firms (恒力石化, 山东寿光鲁清石化, 山东金诚石化, 河北鑫海化工, 山东胜星化工). Verbatim load-bearing clause: "不得承认、不得执行、不得遵守美国依据第13902号行政令、第13846号行政令等规定" — Chinese persons and entities must not recognize, must not enforce, must not comply with U.S. sanctions issued under those EOs.

The legal-architecture timeline — five stages over seven years — proves the systemic claim. PRC coercion-and-counter-coercion is not ad-hoc retaliation; it is a multi-year deliberate construction of a coercion-architecture in the country's own legal voice.

## Bias-balance — the inverted-mirror frame

The skill's bias-balance rule requires citing both perspectives on a disputed claim. The disputed claim is the coercion-typology label itself: *does PRC engage in economic coercion as a systemic policy?* PRC denies the framing entirely as applied to itself, while simultaneously adopting the framing with high specificity when applied to U.S. measures against PRC firms.

The PRC inverted-mirror frame is verbatim in [Source 7], a Global Times report on U.S. Department of Commerce orders halting chip-equipment shipments to Huahong (China's second-largest chipmaker), 2026-04-29. MFA Spokesperson Lin Jian: U.S. should "keep the global supply chains stable and unimpeded." Zhou Mi (CAITEC, Chinese Academy of International Trade and Economic Cooperation): U.S. measures reflect "a recurring trade protectionist mindset"; semiconductor industry requires "win-win cooperation"; restrictions unlikely to succeed given China's rapid growth. Ma Jihua (tech analyst): restrictions are "blatant unfair commercial competition" exemplifying "the politicization of trade"; create "a cycle of pressure and breakthrough" with diminishing effectiveness.

The inverted-mirror frame structure: PRC denies the coercion-frame entirely as applied to itself (re: Australia/Lithuania/rare earths). PRC adopts the coercion-frame with high specificity when applied to U.S. measures against PRC firms. The two framings co-exist in the official discourse without internal acknowledgment.

The honest move is not to adjudicate the framing. It is to cite the GT framing as PRC's own characterization and let the reader see the asymmetry. The reader sees the same toolkit-category being deployed by both sides while each side's narrative reserves the term "coercion" for the other side's deployment. This is the meta-level finding: coercion-as-a-statecraft-category is operationally bidirectional even when narratively claimed as unidirectional.

## Game-theoretic framing (GWW3)

Day-5 promotes seven framing seeds for the GWW3 model. Five were anticipated in the 11:06 scoping; two more were promoted from the 17:00 case-study consolidation.

1. **Audience-cost signaling** (PRC side). Beijing's visible commitment to coerce a target raises the domestic-credibility cost of backing down — especially after CCP rhetoric frames the trigger event in stark terms ("secessionist," "splittist"). Equilibrium implication: partial concession from the target (Ruginienė's 2026-02 renaming proposal) is *enough* — PRC can claim victory narratively without requiring full target capitulation. Audience-cost asymmetry in democratic targets: capitulation is electorally costly; the equilibrium is *attritional ambiguity*, not clean resolution.

2. **Two-level game (Putnam).** Target government negotiates with PRC and with its own electorate in parallel. The Lithuanian Nausėda 2022 → Ruginienė 2026 progression and the Australian Morrison → Albanese 2022 transition both show that the load-bearing variable is *target-state coalition shift*, not PRC measure-mix. Hypothesis: PRC coercion campaigns *outlast* the target-state coalition that produced the trigger decision; resolution requires coalition turnover, not policy reversal under continuous coalition.

3. **Case-attrition curve.** Resistance duration varies from 8 months (S. Korea) to 6 years 2 months (Norway). Variance is dominated by structural exposure variables, not measure-mix. Empirically, the curve fits no simple parametric model — the load-bearing predictor is the *interaction* of GDP-vulnerability ratio with bloc-backstop presence.

4. **Lawfare-as-substrate.** China learned from the 2012 WTO defeat on rare earths: blanket export bans fail at WTO. The 2023–2025 license-as-veto regime is per-shipment approval, evading WTO general-complaint architecture. The 2026-05-02 Blocking Order #21 mirrors EU ACI in form. Lawfare is the substrate on which all other coercion vectors rest: the legal architecture must be in place before specific measures can be deployed at scale.

5. **Tail-risk asymmetry.** PRC absorbs reputation cost across many small coercion deployments; one target absorbs full pressure of one large deployment. Cumulative costs across the relationship are not symmetric. This is the strategic-aggregation logic: PRC's coercion budget is bounded only by the *aggregate* deterioration of its commercial reputation, while each individual target's resistance budget is bounded by its *individual* commodity / channel exposure.

6. **(NEW) Bloc-backstop variable.** Whether the target is embedded in a bloc that responds to single-state coercion as a bloc-level event determines hold duration more than target-state internal cohesion. EU bloc multiplied Lithuania's effective resistance via WTO DS610 + ACI Reg 2023/2675. Five Eyes did not perform this function for Australia. CPTPP did not. Implication for GWW3: bloc-backstop presence is not a binary; it has *quality* (trade-domain bloc backstop differs from intelligence-domain bloc backstop).

7. **(NEW) GDP-vulnerability ratio.** Pre-coercion bilateral trade as a fraction of target-state GDP / target-state total trade is the strongest single predictor of break vs. hold — but as a *relative-exposure* signal, not a clean threshold model. Across the five-case dataset (Norway ~0.26%, Lithuania ~0.45–0.55%, S. Korea ~0.9–1.0%, Australia ~1.4–1.5%, Japan rare-earths regime-level/n/a), lower direct GDP-impact exposure paired with bloc-backstop sustains hold; higher exposure without trade-domain bloc-backstop degrades hold. The dataset is too small for parametric thresholds, but the *ordering* is monotonic and the *interaction* with bloc-backstop is the load-bearing predictor.

These seven seeds are the Day-5 contribution to the GWW3 Belief Subgraph. They are not yet stochastic predicates — translation into the GSL grammar is deferred to a later GWW3-modeling phase fire.

## Hassaleh-Nexus implications

The financial-market signals that move under the Day-5 framing are:

**Commodity-shock signals.** Rare-earth ETF tracking; gallium/germanium spot-price feeds; magnesium futures. 2025-10 PRC tightening on rare-earth controls (subsequently SUSPENDED for one year, a de-escalation signal) is the most recent volatility event. The pricing tail (gallium ~$110–130/kg pre-2023-07 → $687/kg by 2025-05 [UNVERIFIED — both endpoints pending Argus/Asian Metal/USGS monthly methodology] — a 5.3–6.2x ratio depending on baseline endpoint) is the canonical license-as-veto signal. Hassaleh-Nexus should track per-commodity license-grant rates as a leading indicator separable from price.

**Coercion-resistance sovereign-risk premia.** Do bond markets price ACI-style instruments? Hypothesis: EU member-state credit-spread divergence relative to non-EU democracies under PRC-coercion exposure is testable. Lithuania CDS spread divergence 2021–2024 versus a non-EU control (e.g. Australia) is the natural baseline test. The bloc-backstop variable should map to credit-spread compression for embedded states relative to unembedded equivalents.

**FDI-decoupling indicators.** The Australia 2020 ~61% Chinese-investment-value drop (KPMG/CIS investment-flow basis, not FIRB approval-count basis) is the canonical bidirectional-decoupling signal. Hassaleh-Nexus should track quarterly investment-value flows AND FIRB approval counts as separate series for the coercion-exposed-state set — methodology-mixing reduces leading-indicator quality.

**Counter-coercion architecture activation.** PRC blocking-order activations (2026-05-02 was the first) and EU ACI activations (still zero as of 2026-05) are the structural-state-shift signals. Each activation event will redraw the institutional-substrate map; the timing of the *next* activation on either side is a non-trivial Hassaleh-Nexus event-prediction problem.

**CNY-USD hedge ladders.** Coercion-exposed firms' CNY-denominated revenue concentration is a vulnerability metric the Day-5 framework formalizes. Hassaleh-Nexus product implication: a coercion-exposure-weighted hedge index distinct from a generic FX-volatility hedge.
