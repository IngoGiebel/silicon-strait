## Today's thesis

**Japan is the first state-extension actor in the GWW3 China-Taiwan model whose game-theoretic role is determined less by what it *can* do and more by what its diplomatic substrate *will not let it say* — and the gap between those two has now widened to a publicly-asserted Diet-of-record commitment whose rhetorical pre-commitment outpaces its legal pre-commitment by exactly one Cabinet Decision.** Through 2021, the Japanese posture on a Taiwan contingency was *「做了但不公开说」* ("did it but did not say it publicly") — the operational frame moved (collective self-defense 2014, "situations" tiers 2015, counterstrike capability 2022, JJOC 2025), but the rhetorical frame stayed at the level of extra-parliamentary precursor statements by past PMs and deputy PMs. The Takaichi 2025-11-07 statement crosses the threshold: a *sitting* PM, on the *record*, in the *Diet*, naming the *precise statutory trigger* (存立危機事態) under which Japan would exercise collective self-defense in a Taiwan contingency, and refusing to retract under PRC pressure. This does not give Japan any new legal authority — the 2014 Cabinet Decision and 2015 Peace and Security Legislation already conferred it — but it forces every Taiwan-contingency game-tree branch to carry a **PRC belief-update term** about how seriously to take a Japanese rhetorical pre-commitment, in addition to the existing material-capability terms. The deterministic skeleton can eight-prune one branch (Japan formally re-recognises Taiwan) but cannot eight-prune the Takaichi linkage-doctrine itself, because the doctrine is now a fact-of-record and its operational consequences are stochastic by construction.

### §3.1 — Formal diplomatic frame: constancy as constraint

The Japanese government has held a single formal position on Taiwan since 1972, and that position is restated essentially verbatim across every contemporary MOFA reference reviewed for this publication. The constancy itself is load-bearing: Japan's legal posture *forbids* treating Taiwan as a state, but *does not commit* Japan to any particular substantive interpretation of cross-strait peace.

The anchor is **Article 3 of the 1972 Japan-PRC Joint Communiqué** [Source #1]. The PRC side states that Taiwan is an inalienable part of PRC territory; the Japanese side "**fully understands and respects** this stand" — Japanese verbs **理解 (rikai)** and **尊重 (sonchō)**, explicitly *not* **承認 (shōnin, "recognises")**. The understands-respects-not-recognises formula is the canonical Japanese vocabulary and is the reason every subsequent MOFA Taiwan reference defaults to "non-governmental working-level relations" rather than any state-to-state language.

The **MOFA Taiwan Basic Data page** (last updated 2023-10-25) restates the frame in current institutional terms: "In line with the 1972 Japan-China Joint Communique, Japan-Taiwan Relations has been maintained as working relations on a non-governmental basis" [Source #2]. The institutional consequences enumerated by the same page are concrete: there is no Japanese embassy in Taipei and no Taiwanese embassy in Tokyo; bilateral relations route through the Japan-Taiwan Exchange Association (Tokyo HQ + Taipei and Kaohsiung offices) and the Taiwan-Japan Relations Association on the ROC side. Trade volume in 2022 was USD 54.6 B (Japan exports) + USD 33.6 B (Japan imports) [Source #2] — large enough that the "non-governmental" frame absorbs substantial real-world coordination while the framing itself is preserved unchanged.

The **MOFA Press Secretary statement of 2025-12-31** on late-2025 PLA exercises around Taiwan compresses the operative components of the formal frame into three sentences [Source #3]:

> "1. The recent military exercises conducted by the Chinese military around Taiwan constitute actions that increase tensions across the Taiwan Strait, and the Government of Japan has conveyed its concerns to the Chinese side.
> 2. It has been the consistent position of the Government of Japan that it expects that the issue surrounding Taiwan will be resolved peacefully through dialogue.
> 3. Peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait are important for the international community as a whole. We will continue to monitor related developments with strong interest." [Source #3]

Three structural features of this statement matter for game-theoretic instantiation:

1. **"Increase tensions"** is a calibrated upward-pressure verb, not an escalation verb. Japan reserves the harder vocabulary for actions it would prepare a kinetic response to. The verb itself is part of the frame.
2. **"Resolved peacefully through dialogue"** stakes a *procedural* preference (means: dialogue) without staking a *substantive* preference (outcome: any particular cross-strait political settlement). This is the diplomatic equivalent of refusing to be a co-determining player on the political-integration branch — Japan is explicitly *not* an outcome-claimant on cross-strait reunification or independence.
3. **"International community as a whole"** collectivises the stake. Japan asserts that Taiwan-Strait peace is a global public good rather than a bilateral PRC-ROC matter. This is the discursive lever Japan uses to legitimise its own operational build-up *without* having to argue the cross-strait question on its own terms.

The frame is therefore **constant on substance** and **modulated on tempo**. Japan does not move the diplomatic frame in response to PRC pressure; it modulates the operational frame and the East China Sea tempo channel. This pattern itself produces the first eight-pruning candidate of Day 10 (carried forward to §4): the branch in which Japan formally re-recognises Taiwan as a state is canonically inaccessible on the same logic Days 7–9 used for US-Exec unilateral repeals — substrate-protection at the diplomatic-frame layer, fifty-three years of unbroken anchoring, and incommensurable cost of breakage relative to any in-crisis-window gain.

### §3.2 — Operational defense frame: drift through four inflection points

Japan's operational frame moved more between 2014 and 2025 than in any decade since the Self-Defense Forces were founded in 1954. The drift is anchored by four discrete inflection points that the game-tree's Japanese-player namespace must encode separately because each opens a distinct subspace of moves.

**Inflection 1 — 2014-07-01 Cabinet Decision on collective self-defense.** The Abe Cabinet Decision of 2014-07-01 reinterpreted Article 9 of the Constitution to permit collective self-defense under three conditions (**三要件**): an armed attack against a country in close relationship with Japan that threatens Japan's survival; absence of other appropriate means; use of force limited to the minimum necessary [Source #4]. The 2014 decision is the *legal* permission for Japan to act in a Taiwan contingency that does not directly attack Japanese territory — a permission that did not exist in pre-2014 doctrine.

**Inflection 2 — 2015 Legislation for Peace and Security (effective 2016-03-29).** The 2015 PSL operationalised the 2014 decision by codifying a tiered "situations" framework [Source #5]:

- **重要影響事態 (jūyō-eikyō jitai)** — "important influence situations": situations significantly affecting Japan's peace and security, in which the SDF may provide rear-area logistics/support to US or other partner forces without itself engaging in combat.
- **存立危機事態 (sonritsu-kiki jitai)** — "survival-threatening situations": situations in which a country in close relationship with Japan is under armed attack, the attack threatens Japan's survival, and no other means exist — in which the SDF may exercise collective self-defense, i.e. use force.

A Taiwan contingency does *not* automatically fall into either tier; the Cabinet must designate it. This designation-gate is the most important single feature of the Japanese operational frame for game-theoretic purposes, because it converts a continuous spectrum of PRC kinetic actions into a discrete binary decision (Cabinet designates / does not designate) whose probability is empirically thin and whose downstream consequences (force authorisation) are large.

**Inflection 3 — 2021 senior-official precursor statements.** Two statements pre-staged the Takaichi inflection without yet being made in the Diet [Sources #6, #7]:

- **Aso Tarō**, Deputy PM and Finance Minister, fundraising-party speech 2021-07-05: a major Taiwan incident *"could be related to a situation threatening Japan's survival"* (存立危機事態に関係する), implying SDF collective self-defense authorisation in such a case [Source #6]. The highest-ranking explicit *public* linkage of the *jutī* tier to a Taiwan contingency by a *sitting* official before Takaichi — but at a *fundraising* venue, not the Diet.
- **Abe Shinzō**, former PM, online video address to Taiwan's Institute for National Policy Research 2021-12-01: *「台湾有事は日本有事であり、日米同盟の有事でもある」* ("A Taiwan emergency is a Japan emergency, and is also a Japan-US Alliance emergency") [Source #7]. Although Abe was out of office, the formulation became politically operative inside the LDP and is now routinely invoked.

These two statements form the rhetorical bridge between the legal permission of 2014–2015 and the parliamentary assertion of 2025-11-07. They do not produce a Diet-of-record commitment but they establish the precedent vocabulary.

**Inflection 4 — 2022 three-document set (Cabinet Decisions 2022-12-16).** The National Security Strategy, National Defense Strategy, and Defense Buildup Program — the so-called *three strategic documents* — together commit Japan to [Source #8]:

- a defense budget rising to **2% of GDP** by FY2027 (a doubling from the long-standing ~1% ceiling that prevailed from 1976 to 2022);
- the acquisition of **counterstrike capabilities** (反撃能力, *hangeki nōryoku*) — the ability to strike adversary missile launch facilities, formally breaking with the post-1956 exclusive-defense (専守防衛) interpretation that had prevailed for sixty-six years.

The three-document set is the *materiel* permission for what 2014 conferred as *legal* permission. By the time Takaichi addressed the Diet in November 2025, the legal authority, the doctrinal tier, the budget trajectory, and the weapons-acquisition pathway were all in place. What remained absent was the parliamentary on-the-record commitment.

**Operational restatement — Defense of Japan 2025 (DOJ 2025).** The 2025 Defense White Paper, foreword by Defense Minister Nakatani Gen, opens with "*The international community is now facing its greatest trial since the end of World War II… Japan finds itself in the most severe and complex security environment of the post-war era*" [Source #9]. It frames China's role: *"China has been swiftly increasing its national defense expenditures, thereby extensively and rapidly enhancing its military capability… and intensifying its activities in the East China Sea, including around the Senkaku Islands, and the Pacific"* [Source #9]. And it announces two new structural operational changes:

- The **JSDF Joint Operations Command (JJOC)** was established on **2025-03-24**, centralising peacetime-through-contingency command authority over Ground/Maritime/Air SDF plus space/cyber-domain units under a single Commander-JJOC reporting directly to the Defense Minister [Source #9]. This compresses the Cabinet → SDF chain by one rung in any contingency.
- The **US Forces Japan upgrade to a joint force headquarters** is underway in step with JJOC, and discussions are advancing on *"expansion of bilateral joint presence in the southwestern region of Japan"* [Source #9] — the southwest-islands framing that operationally means Ryukyu / Senkaku / Yonaguni reinforcement.

Each of these expands the *Japanese-player action space* in a Taiwan-contingency game tree beyond what was available a decade ago. The cumulative effect across the four inflections is that, by mid-2025, Japan had assembled every operational pre-condition for a credible collective-self-defense response to a Taiwan contingency *except* an unambiguous parliamentary statement of intent. The Takaichi 2025-11-07 statement is the closing of exactly that gap.

### §3.3 — The Takaichi inflection (2025-11-07)

In the Lower House (衆議院) Security Committee questioning on 2025-11-07, Prime Minister Takaichi Sanae stated, in answer to opposition questioning on Japan's posture in a Taiwan contingency [Source #14]:

> *(BBC Chinese paraphrase of the Diet exchange:)* "If 'Taiwan emergency' — beyond cognitive warfare — involves China's use of force, such as naval blockade, this could constitute a 'survival-crisis situation' under the Security Legislation, under which Japan would be able to exercise collective self-defense — though she stressed it would depend on the specific case as it actually arose." [Source #14]
>
> 「如果'台湾有事'是除了认知作战之外，伴随中国使用武力的情况，例如海上封锁，就有可能构成'安全保障法制'中，日本能够行使集体自卫权的'存亡危机事态'，不过她强调仍要看实际发生的个别具体情况。」 [Source #14]

Two structural features make this statement qualitatively different from Aso-2021 and Abe-2021:

1. **Speaker × venue.** Aso was Deputy PM at a private fundraising-party speech; Abe was *former* PM speaking via online video to a Taipei think-tank. Takaichi is the **sitting Prime Minister speaking on the record in the National Diet**. CHEN Yu-hua of Akita International University frames the shift precisely: prior Japanese governments practised *「做了但不公开说」* ("did it but did not say it publicly"); the Takaichi posture is *「做了就公开说」* ("did it and now says it publicly") [Source #14]. This is the more analytically load-bearing shift than the *content* of the linkage, which has been doctrinally stable since at least the 2015 PSL.
2. **Legal mechanism named.** Takaichi invoked *存立危機事態* — the precise statutory trigger under the 2015 Peace and Security Legislation that authorises collective self-defense. The substrate-notes (§3.2 above) establish what that trigger is and how it gates `J.counterstrike_authorised` in the game-tree namespace. The parliamentary statement collapses what was previously empirically thin — one historical near-trigger, no actual trigger — into *empirically asserted as applicable* by the head of government in the venue where Japan's collective-self-defense designations actually originate.

The statement has not been retracted as of today (2026-05-17), six months and ten days after delivery, through six identified PRC escalation events (§3.4 below) and at least two kinetic incidents implicating the JSDF (§3.5 below).

A textual caveat: the substrate available for this section is BBC Chinese's textual paraphrase rather than the Diet's own Japanese-language record (国会会議録). Closing that single-language gap with the JPN-side primary is the highest-priority q-J5+ open item filed by today's research notes (see §6 Open questions). This caveat does not change the analytical claim — both the date and the substantive content of the statement are corroborated across BBC Chinese, PRC MOFA's response statements (which quote and protest the statement), and JPN-side coverage referenced in the BBC article — but it is flagged here as a citation-discipline matter to be closed before Day-10 EN-LOCK.

### §3.5 — ECS conflict-tempo baseline and new kinetic data

The East China Sea / Senkaku theatre is the *tempo channel* Japan and the PRC use to communicate without using the Taiwan channel. It would be inherited intact into any Taiwan crisis and would almost certainly escalate first.

The **Japan Coast Guard official page** states the canonical pattern in one line: *「月に数回の頻度で領海侵入を繰り返している」* — "Repeating territorial-water incursions several times monthly" [Source #13]. The page anchors the pattern's start at September 2012, when Japan nationalised three of the Senkaku islands, and notes (i) the September 2010 deliberate collision of a PRC fishing vessel with a JCG patrol ship and (ii) the January 2021 enactment of the Chinese **海警法** (Maritime Police Law / Coast Guard Law), which the Japanese government considers problematic with respect to international-law compliance [Source #13]. JCG publishes monthly tabular data through May 2025 (令和8年5月); computing the monthly time-series is a Nisaba 🌾 numerical-audit task best executed at q-J3 Day-10 audit time and is filed as a §6 open item.

Three implications of this baseline for the game tree, carried over from the substrate notes:

1. **Pre-positioning is already paid for.** Japan does not need to introduce a new "raise tempo in Senkaku" move into the game tree; it inherits one from a thirteen-year-old baseline.
