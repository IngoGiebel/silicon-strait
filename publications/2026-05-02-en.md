## Executive summary

The cross-strait military balance is structurally asymmetric: the PLA outnumbers Taiwan's active forces by nearly 13:1 and outspends it by roughly 12:1 using the CSIS official 2025 PLA budget against Taiwan's 2026 budget (alternative headline estimates — SIPRI, IISS, PPP-adjusted — produce 16:1 to 24:1) — but the strategically load-bearing question is not who would win a symmetric contest, because Taiwan cannot and does not attempt one. Taiwan's deterrence is a cost-imposition game: the objective is to raise the PLA's expected cost of a Taiwan operation above the political and economic threshold Xi Jinping's calculus implies, not to match force for force. Three empirical trends from 2025 data sharpen this framing: the PLA has shifted its operational posture from inside-the-bubble pressure to outside-the-bubble reach (+81% carrier days outside the First Island Chain), the ADIZ baseline jumped from ~156 to ~319 sorties per month at Lai's inauguration in May 2024 — a Schelling commitment rather than a capacity ramp — and the PRC's own information-domain operations reveal that operational tempo is being used as a substitute for narrative-domain dominance Beijing cannot achieve outside its own information environment.

## Today's thesis

**Military balance is a cost-imposition game, not a symmetric contest.** Taiwan's armed forces (~160,000 active; $20.1 B defense budget 2026) face a PLA (~2,035,000 active; $244.8 B official 2025 / ~$471 B PPP-adjusted estimate) that is not ~12× stronger in a one-dimensional sense but rather operates a fundamentally different kind of power — precision missile reach, carrier-strike projection, and informational saturation — whose logic is *deterrence by cost* rather than *deterrence by denial*.

The implication for the GWW3 model is exact: **a Taiwan simulation that collapses to a force-ratio comparison misspecifies the problem.** The correct model has a *cost-threshold parameter* for PRC decision-making and a *cost-imposition capability curve* for Taiwan (and its backers), and the war-or-peace boundary is the intersection of those two curves across a set of scenario types — not a headcount ratio.

This matters analytically, not just rhetorically. Taiwan's defense modernization (F-16V Block 70 upgrades, Harpoon Block II+ coastal defense, indigenous submarines, HIMARS) [individual program magnitudes UNVERIFIED in this fire's source set — see Sources note] is comprehensible only as cost-imposition investment, not parity-seeking. Evaluating it by whether it closes the force-ratio gap produces exactly the wrong analysis.

## Research findings

### PLA force structure and spending opacity

The PLA deploys approximately 2,035,000 active personnel, supported by 510,000 reserves [Source 1]. Its publicly stated 2025 defense budget is 1.78 trillion RMB ($244.8 B, +7% YoY), with 2026 guidance of 1.91 trillion RMB ($276.7 B) [Source 5]. The PLA has kept official spending "at or below 2 percent of GDP since 2000" [Source 5] — a figure that diverges sharply from outside-analyst estimates:

| Estimate source | 2024 figure | Premium over PRC official |
|---|---|---|
| PRC official | $231 B | — |
| SIPRI 2024 | $313.7 B | +36% |
| IISS 2024 | $325 B | +41% |
| PPP-adjusted (2024 study) | ~$471 B | +104% |
| US DoD range | official × 1.32–1.63 | +32–63% |

**What drives the gap:** The official budget excludes military R&D, space programs, People's Armed Police ($21.5 B in 2024 alone), defense mobilization funds, and some recruitment costs [Source 5]. The CSIS analysis notes that PLA equipment spending constitutes roughly 41% of total reported spend (2017 white paper figure), suggesting the R&D and procurement categories carry substantial off-book content.

For the GWW3 model, this numerical opacity is not a data-quality problem to be averaged out — it is itself a strategic asset. Every adversary planning against the PLA must plan against the upper bound. The spread between official ($244.8 B, 2025) and PPP-adjusted ($471 B, 2024 study) represents approximately $226 B of "as-if" deterrence capacity generated at zero marginal cost through budget opacity [Source 5] — note this combines a 2025 official figure with a 2024 PPP estimate; the same-year 2024 spread is $471 B / $231 B = 2.04× ≈ $240 B [Source 5]. **A model that uses one point estimate is wrong by construction; the Belief Subgraph must carry a probability distribution over true spend.**

### PLARF: missile geometry and the Taiwan-specific engagement problem

The People's Liberation Army Rocket Force holds the kinetic load-bearing instruments for any Taiwan contingency. Three systems are Taiwan-specific in their geometry [Source 2]:

**DF-21D (MRBM anti-ship, 1,500 km range):** The "carrier-killer" — the first operational ballistic missile system designed for anti-surface strikes against maneuvering naval vessels at sea. Its primary intended effect is not to hit every carrier but to force US carrier strike groups to operate at distance, degrading strike-aircraft sortie rates and compressing the engagement window for US air intervention. The DF-21D's impact on US naval doctrine (the "salvo competition" problem) is more significant than its kill probability on any individual carrier.

**DF-17 (MRBM + hypersonic glide vehicle, 1,600 km, Mach 10):** The DF-17 paired with the DF-ZF HGV stresses Patriot PAC-3 and THAAD intercept geometry in ways a standard ballistic re-entry trajectory does not. A hypersonic glide vehicle has a depressed, maneuvering terminal phase that shrinks the engagement window available to a ballistic missile defense system. Taiwan's Tien Kung III / PAC-3 upgrades are real investments in cost-imposition (making kinetic attacks more expensive), but the DF-17 was specifically developed to erode the intercept equation these upgrades address. The missile arms race is not static.

**DF-26 (IRBM, dual-capable, 5,000 km, ~250 launchers across Bases 61-62-64-66):** The DF-26's range puts Guam well within its conventional strike envelope. US reinforcement to a Taiwan contingency runs through Guam. The A2/AD zone is therefore not a defensive perimeter around Taiwan — it is a threat radius that reaches the forward-staging US base for Indo-Pacific operations. This is the time-compression variable for any force-deployment model: the question is whether US forces can mass inside the DF-26 and DF-21D engagement envelopes before a PLA operation achieves a fait accompli.

DoD estimates the PLARF conventional arsenal at approximately 900 SRBMs, ~1,300 conventional MRBMs, ~500 IRBMs, ~400 cruise missiles, and ~400 ICBMs [Source 2, via DoD secondary data in PLARF article].

### PLAN: carrier capability and the erosion of the A2/AD perimeter

China operates three aircraft carriers:

- **Liaoning** (Kuznetsov-class STOBAR derivative, commissioned 2012) — training/limited combat role
- **Shandong** (domestically built STOBAR, commissioned 2019)
- **Fujian** (domestically built CATOBAR, sea trials ongoing since 2024) — the load-bearing platform for future contingency analysis

The Fujian's CATOBAR configuration (electromagnetic catapult, structurally analogous to USS Ford class) is significant not for its current operational status but for what it signals about PLAN doctrine. STOBAR carriers launch aircraft at reduced fuel/weapons loads due to ski-jump physics; CATOBAR removes this constraint. The Fujian's operational-ready date — which open sources place in 2026–2027, though sea-trial timelines have slipped repeatedly — is the single most consequential near-term platform milestone for any joint-operations scenario model.

PLAN also deploys 62 destroyers (including 8 × Type 055, currently the world's second-largest destroyer class by displacement) and 68 submarines (12 nuclear-powered; 6 × Type 094 SSBN carrying 72 warheads; Type 093 SSN) [Source 4].

The **key 2025 operational data point** from CSIS [Source 6]: PLAN carriers operated **58 days outside the First Island Chain in 2025, versus 32 days in 2024 — a +81% increase in days and +35% in sorties (1,680 vs 1,240).** This is the structural shift that makes the "A2/AD bubble" framing obsolete. The bubble was always a defensive description — a zone within which PLA forces denied US access. The 2025 data shows PLAN exiting the bubble with growing confidence. The A2/AD zone is no longer just a perimeter; it is a base from which forward presence is projected.

### ROC armed forces: the asymmetric deterrence logic in practice

Taiwan's active force stands at approximately 160,000 personnel [Source 3], with a reserve establishment of 1.66–2.5 million, significantly expanded by the Tsai-era reserve reforms (2022–2024: 14-day annual training cycles for part of the reserve, up from the criticised 5-day refresher). Defense budget 2026: $20.1 B, crossing 3% of GDP for the first time since 2009 [Source 3]. President Lai's 8-year defense plan totals $39.89 B in additional investment.

Taiwan's core modernization programs are comprehensible only as cost-imposition investments:

- **F-16V Block 70 upgrades** [program-magnitude UNVERIFIED in this fire's source set; ROC has ~384 F-16s legacy fleet per Wikipedia scaffold, with a fleet-wide upgrade program publicly announced — exact in-fleet count pending DSCA/MND primary in Day-5 fire]: extends air-combat range and electronic warfare capability. The asymmetric value is not "can Taiwan dominate PLA air?" but "can Taiwan impose enough attrition on PLA air sorties that the sortie-count advantage shrinks faster than planned?"
- **Harpoon Block II+ coastal defense missiles** [DSCA approved 2020; magnitude UNVERIFIED in this fire's source set, DSCA pull blocked HTTP 403 in 14:00 + 17:00 fires]: shore-launched anti-ship. The DF-21D targets US carriers; the Harpoon targets PLAN amphibious and surface vessels in the strait. It is specifically a cost-imposition weapon against the ground-force-delivery layer.
- **Indigenous submarine program (IDS):** first hull (Narwhal) launched September 2023 at CSBC Corp., Kaohsiung. Sea trials ongoing. Cost-imposition value: a credible submarine threat raises the cost of a naval blockade by requiring PLAN to dedicate ASW resources to strait surveillance, reducing the forces available for the surface and air aspects of a joint operation.

The reserve-quality question is the most consequential open empirical issue. The 2.5 M reserve figure cannot be treated as a force multiplier at face value; it is a probability distribution over how many show up, trained enough to fight, within the first 72–96 hours. **This distribution, not the headcount, is the war-gaming variable.** The GWW3 model must carry it as a distribution, not a point.

### PRC operational tempo: the 2025 data pattern

CSIS's 2025 tracking data [Source 6] reveals three structural patterns beyond the headline 3,764 ADIZ incursions (+22.4% YoY):

**1. May 2024 step-change, not gradual ramp.** ADIZ aircraft sorties averaged ~156/month from January 2022 to April 2024. Beginning in May 2024 — coincident with Lai Ching-te's inauguration on May 20 — the baseline jumped to ~319/month, sustained through 2025. This is not capacity growth; the PLA did not double its available aircraft in spring 2024. It is a **posture signal** — a Schelling commitment that the new Lai government's election would be met with a structurally higher harassment floor. The Belief Subgraph read: PRC moved the expected-harassment parameter as a public, irreversible communication of political cost.

**2. Quality migration in late 2025.** The year-total ADIZ count is +22.4%, but the May–December 2025 window shows about -5% vs the same 2024 window (2,479 vs 2,613; arithmetic on the rounded figures yields -5.1%, CSIS reports -5.4% from underlying daily data). Meanwhile, major exercises grew in scale: Strait Thunder-2025A (April 2025: 135 sorties, 38 vessels) and Justice Mission-2025 (December 29-30, 2025: 8 exercise zones, 27 rockets launched). CSIS analysis suggests PRC is shifting from sustained-pressure mode to **rehearsal mode** — the mix matters more than the headline count.

**3. China-Russia joint exercises declined 57%** (14 in 2024 → 6 in 2025). This is an anomaly. The "China-Russia axis" that dominated Western strategic commentary in 2022–2023 shows a structural decoupling in exercise frequency. Possible explanations: Russia's Ukraine-theater commitments reduce its Pacific exercise capacity; PRC calculates that China-Russia exercises carry reputational costs that exceed the tactical benefit in a period of active economic diplomacy with Europe. Either way, the **Taiwan deterrence calculation cannot assume a China-Russia combined front** on the basis of 2025 data.

### PRC framing: operational activities read from two registers

The same operational events that CSIS describes in aggregate (3,764 ADIZ incursions; 163 SCS operations; CCG presence at Scarborough doubled) appear in PRC-sourced material through a fundamentally different lens.

**Global Times, 2026-04-30 [Source 8]:** Reporting the PLA Southern Theater Command's combat-readiness patrol in the territorial sea and airspace of Huangyan Dao (Scarborough Shoal) and surrounding areas, the GT article frames the operation squarely in sovereignty terms: *"Huangyan Dao is an inherent part of China's territory."* No fleet counts, no engagement details — the doctrinal claim is the entire content. The STC's official WeChat account is the cited dissemination channel, meaning the primary release was Chinese-domestic-first; GT English is the international reflection.

**Global Times, 2026-04-17 [Source 9] (Senior Colonel Xu Chenghua, PLA ETC spokesperson):** Reporting the tracking of Japanese destroyer JS Ikazuchi transiting the Taiwan Strait (04:02–17:50, April 17), ETC spokesperson Xu Chenghua provides the doctrinal frame directly: *"wrong signals to 'Taiwan independence' separatist forces."* The use of Japan's vessel name (unusually specific for GT coverage) is itself a signal: PRC is publicly treating Japan's FONOPs through the Strait as substantively supportive of Lai-government sovereignty claims, not as innocent passage.

**CNR (央广军事), 2026-05-01 [Source 10]:** The Chinese-language original on the same Huangyan Dao patrol presents a structured three-information-payload release (in Chinese: 三个信息):

> ① 黄岩岛周边态势尽在掌握，菲律宾的任何动向都在解放军的掌控之中 [The situation around Huangyan Dao is fully within our grasp; any movement by the Philippines is under PLA control.]
> ② 解放军对黄岩岛的掌控固若金汤，任何势力都不可能从解放军的手中把它夺走 [The PLA's grip on Huangyan Dao is rock-solid; no force could possibly seize it from the PLA's hands.]
> ③ 解放军的常态战备警巡，将使黄岩岛的安全和生态环境越来越好 [The PLA's normalized combat-readiness patrol will make Huangyan Dao's security and ecology better and better.]

The structure here is analytically significant. This is not journalism — it is **structured information-payload delivery**: dominance (① situational awareness), irreversibility (② grip framing), and positive normalization (③ ecology and security). The patrol itself is persistent posture, not mobilization. The informational work is done at zero marginal cost by the ongoing operational tempo. This is the mechanism by which the PRC operationalizes information-domain cost-imposition: the operation is indistinguishable from the narrative asset it generates.

## Game-theoretic framing (GWW3)

Five predicates for the Belief Subgraph, developed across the four research fires:

### Predicate 1 — Cost-imposition asymmetry

The strategic variable for Taiwan is not the force-ratio but the **cost-imposition threshold**: what level of cost can Taiwan impose (in blood, materiel, timeline, international legitimacy, and economic disruption) such that the expected political cost to Xi Jinping of proceeding exceeds the expected political benefit of successful unification? Taiwan's deterrence works if and only if it can credibly move PLA planners' expected-cost calculation above this threshold.

The GWW3 model needs:
- A `cost_imposition_capacity(taiwan, scenario)` distribution (not a point)
- A `political_threshold(xi, scenario)` distribution (not a point; depends heavily on domestic legitimacy conditions, economic state, succession considerations)
- The war-or-peace boundary as the region where these two distributions do not overlap

### Predicate 2 — A2/AD bubble dissolution

The +81% carrier-days-outside-FIC datum (2025 vs 2024) is not just an operational metric — it is a qualitative phase transition. The A2/AD zone was doctrinally conceived as a denial layer: PLA forces within, US forces outside. The 2025 data pattern suggests PLAN is probing power-projection posture beyond the bubble. The Belief Subgraph implication: the "PLA operates defensively within its bubble" assumption, which many deterrence models carry as a fixed prior, is no longer valid as of 2025. The model must update this prior or it will systematically underestimate PRC capacity for non-denial scenarios.

### Predicate 3 — Budget opacity as deterrence multiplier

The 1.92× spread between official ($244.8 B) and PPP-adjusted ($471 B) military spending estimates generates ~$226 B of "as-if" deterrence capacity at zero additional cost, because every adversary must plan against the upper bound [Source 5]. The Belief Subgraph representation: each player node holds a `prc_military_spend` belief probability distribution; the spread of this distribution (not its mean) is the load-bearing parameter for adversary planning. PRC derives benefit from *not* reducing this spread. Transparency would be a concession.

### Predicate 4 — May 2024 Schelling commitment

The ADIZ step-change at Lai's inauguration (156 → 319 sorties/month, sustained for 20+ months) is the clearest example in recent history of a **Schelling commitment move** — a costly, public, visible, and self-binding signal that the new status quo is structurally different from the prior one. The value of the signal comes from its irreversibility: sustaining 319 sorties/month is expensive, which is precisely what makes it credible as a commitment. GWW3 predicate: `PRC.commitment_credibility ∝ sustainability_cost × duration × visibility`.

### Predicate 5 — Operational tempo as information-domain substitute

The CNR structured-payload article (Source 10) is an instance of a general PRC mechanism: because PRC cannot dominate the narrative environment outside its information-sphere (GT English reaches some international audiences; Xinhua gets limited traction in Western discourse), it uses **operational tempo as a renewable information-domain asset**. Every patrol generates a domestically-targeted information payload at near-zero marginal cost. The 319/month aircraft baseline is simultaneously a deterrence posture and a narrative-production engine for domestic consumption.

GWW3 predicate: `PRC.information_domain_cost_imposition = f(operational_tempo, domestic_narrative_audience)`. Players who model PRC responses as purely military-deterrent signals will systematically underpredict PRC reaction intensity to apparently-routine third-party actions (FONOPS, arms sales, diplomatic visits), because those actions are read in Beijing as belief-update events, not military provocations.

## Hassaleh-Nexus implications

**Nexus binding — preliminary, requires Day 5+ numerical-audit pass:**

1. **Defense-sector equity signals:** Taiwan's $39.89 B Lai 8-year plan and the crossing of the 3% GDP threshold for the first time since 2009 are load-bearing signals for the defense-sector equities that service the ROC (Raytheon/RTX for Harpoon and PAC-3; Lockheed for F-16V; CSBC as a domestic shipbuilder for IDS). These are trackable signals in Hassaleh nodes.

2. **Cross-strait risk premium in semiconductor pricing:** Day 3 (chip supply chain) established TSMC's 70%/90% foundry concentration [Day-3 source set]. Day 4's military data completes the risk equation: the PLARF can reach TSMC fabs at hypersonic-glide flight times from eastern China launch positions [precise DF-17-to-TSMC flight-time figure UNVERIFIED in this fire's source set; physically a hypersonic glide vehicle launched at ~250 km strait-traverse distance reaches its target in single-digit minutes, but a publishable estimate requires a launch-coordinate / target-coordinate / glide-trajectory primary not yet pulled]. The cross-strait risk premium should therefore track **not** headline incident count but **capability proximity** (DF-17 deployment depth relative to TSMC fab coordinates) and **posture signals** (Strait Thunder exercise frequency as rehearsal-mode indicator). The ADIZ step-change in May 2024 was not priced into semiconductor supply-chain risk assessments in real time — a Hassaleh signal-detection latency problem worth modeling.

3. **Operational-tempo time series as leading indicator:** if information-domain cost-imposition (Predicate 5) is correct, then **PRC press-release volume on Taiwan/SCS operations** should be trackable as a leading indicator alongside ADIZ incursion counts. The hypothesis: PRC press-release volume leads kinetic incident volume, because the information operation is prepared before the kinetic operation and sometimes substitutes for it. If this correlation holds in historical data, it is a Hassaleh signal node with ~1–4 week forward-looking capacity.

4. **China-Russia exercise decoupling:** the -57% joint-exercise decline in 2025 is not yet priced into any European threat-model that still bundles China+Russia as a combined adversarial axis. If this decoupling persists into 2026, it has implications for European defense spending calculus (less pressure to over-provide for the East flank against a combined Russia-China scenario) and for USD-CNY pricing (PRC has less near-term incentive to maintain a high-tension Russia relationship, which marginally reduces the probability that US secondary sanctions bite hard on China-Russia trade).
