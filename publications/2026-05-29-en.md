### §2.3 — Ceiling-pinning argument for ROK at Tier-1 / Low-Tier-2

**Why not Tier-1 plain (UK / AUS-like)?** ROK has *formal resolution submissions* — parliamentary instruments designed by procedure to be voted on. UK EDMs and AUS committee reports do not progress through a resolution-pipeline; they are status-communicative acts at origination. The formal-procedure character lifts ROK above plain Tier-1.

**Why not Tier-2 plain (EU-like)?** EU EP non-binding resolutions are routinely *passed plenary*. ROK's 4 resolutions over 9 years are: 1 expired, 3 pending — zero plenary passages to date. The *form* matches Tier-2; the *function* (plenary endorsement) does not.

**Effective placement: Tier-1 / Low-Tier-2 hybrid.** Inanna's classification recommendation is correct and we preserve her wording in the cross-substrate table.

**Architectural reason for the hybrid.** ROK Constitution Art. 60 limits National Assembly consent requirements to specific treaty categories; foreign-status declarations sit outside that framework, removing the institutional vehicle for plenary-grade endorsement. Combined with the inter-Korean confound (§4 of Day-19, retained here as a background constraint), this leaves resolutions as a *signalling vehicle* rather than a *binding instrument*, which structurally drives the "submit-but-not-pass" pattern. The Day-19 P28 prediction that recognition-language anchor-stability comes with high operational drift is empirically confirmed — anchor (zero recognition-status revisions) + drift (20 informal-channel instruments) co-exist by design.

## §5 — Coupling-Property Operationalisation (Day-19 §5.4 prediction)

**Day-19 §5.4 finding (novel).** ROK is the sole positive instance in the 8-substrate corpus of *textual coupling* between the 1992 communiqué's recognition clause (clause 3) and a third-party substrate-relevant commitment (clauses 4–5 on inter-Korean dynamics).

**Day-19 §5.4 prediction.** Coupling-property predicts higher *anchor-stability* + higher *operational drift* than uncoupled Class-A.

**Operationalisation test against NA catalogue:**

| Property | Predicted behaviour | Catalogue evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anchor stability (no formal recognition-status revision) | Stable | Zero Tier-3 instruments, zero formal revisions; 4 of 4 resolutions expire or remain pending | **Confirmed** |
| Operational drift (informal-vehicle activity) | High | 20 instruments over 9 years; mean ≈ 2.2 instruments/year; concentrated post-2021 (15 of 20 = 75% in 2021–2026 = 60% of the period) | **Confirmed (and quantified)** |
| Drift channel | Informal | 18 of 20 instruments (90%) are informal channels (excludes 2 Trade Committee acts that are formal-of-record but not formal-of-policy) | **Confirmed** |

**Refined coupling-property statement.**

> *ROK substrate exhibits high formal anchor-stability (zero recognition-status revisions over 34 years 1992–2026) combined with high operational drift through informal vehicles (~20 NA instruments over 9 years, 90% through parliamentary-diplomacy / FAUC / floor-speech channels rather than statutory channels). The coupling clause (1992 §3 ↔ §4–5 inter-Korean) acts as a substrate-level structural anchor that channels Taiwan-related political signal into informal vehicles, where it can be expressed without violating the formal recognition position. ROK demonstrates that coupling does not suppress activity; it redirects activity from formal to informal channels.*

**Cross-substrate comparison.** Does the coupling-property predict only ROK behaviour, or is it generalisable?

- **UK / AUS / PHL** (uncoupled Class-A): also exhibit high informal-channel activity but with *lower anchor-stability* (UK FAC reports more directly engage status questions; AUS standing-committee reports occasionally cross into status-narrowing register).
- **EU** (uncoupled, Tier-2): exhibits *both* high formal-channel and high informal-channel activity, consistent with absence of coupling allowing formal-channel expression.
- **CAN** (uncoupled, Tier-3): exhibits formal-channel activity (Bill C-343, Bill S-277) that ROK does *not* exhibit despite a similar Class-A starting position.

**Generalisation.** Coupling-property appears to be **necessary-but-not-sufficient** for the anchor-stability-with-informal-drift pattern. Coupling redirects activity from formal to informal; uncoupled substrates *can* exhibit similar patterns (UK / AUS) but are not structurally constrained to do so. The Day-19 §5.4 prediction is upheld and operationalises. Day-21's H21.1 (pre-registered in `5388040` §3) lifts this from substrate-classification to *financial-market signal*: substrates with coupling-property are predicted to show elevated equity cross-correlation between primary-exposure ticker and TSMC under cross-strait stress events, relative to comparable-magnitude non-coupling substrates.
