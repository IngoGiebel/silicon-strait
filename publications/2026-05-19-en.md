### §3.1 — PHL formal diplomatic frame: One-China-Policy constancy under Marcos Jr.

The Philippine government has held a single formal position on Taiwan since the **1975-06-09 Joint Communiqué on the Establishment of Diplomatic Relations between the Republic of the Philippines and the People's Republic of China** (the "1975 Joint Communiqué") and the parallel termination of formal recognition of the Republic of China. The 1975 Joint Communiqué structure — Manila acknowledges the PRC government as "the sole legal government of China" and the PRC position that "Taiwan is an integral part of the territory of the People's Republic of China" — has been restated in essentially unchanged form by every subsequent Philippine administration through Marcos Jr. [Source #2]. A *structural* point worth flagging at the outset: the 1975 Communiqué's verb is **"recognises"** ("the Government of the Republic of the Philippines recognises the Government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal Government of China") — *one tier stronger* than Japan's 1972 Joint Communiqué verb **"understands and respects"** (理解し、尊重する). The PHL substrate therefore stands on a stronger doctrinal floor than the JPN substrate; the institutional consequences are concrete: the Manila Economic and Cultural Office (MECO) in Taipei and the Taipei Economic and Cultural Office (TECO) in Manila handle de-facto bilateral relations, mirroring the JPN Japan-Taiwan Exchange Association model but at a higher reversal cost.

The Marcos Jr. administration's formal frame was tested on **2025-08-11 → 2025-08-13** when President Marcos described Taiwan in **"physical geographic"** terms that the PRC side read as departing from the strict one-China formulation. The DFA (Department of Foreign Affairs) under Secretary Enrique Manalo and then Secretary Theresa Lazaro re-stated the formal frame as **"clear and unwavering"** adherence to the One China Policy [Source #1, #3], creating a **register-split** between the executive (Marcos) and the DFA (Manalo/Lazaro) within a forty-eight-hour window. The PRC response chain (§3.5) addressed Marcos and not Lazaro, behaviourally respecting the register split it was responding to. That asymmetry is the substrate for predicate candidate **P24** (executive-vs-DFA register split — every player has at least two registers; the inter-state response respects the register split), which the predicate-decision table in §4 promotes to **active** on the strength of the same PRC behavioural evidence rather than on Manila-side claims.

### §3.2 — PHL operational frame: MDT + EDCA + Balikatan + Cagayan-Isabela geography

The Philippine operational frame on a Taiwan contingency rests on three architectural layers structurally different from the JPN substrate. **Layer 1: the 1951 Mutual Defense Treaty (MDT)** — a binding mutual-defense commitment whose Article IV covers attacks on either party's "metropolitan territory" and "Armed Forces, public vessels or aircraft in the Pacific." The Pompeo 2019 reinterpretation and Blinken 2021 + 2023 affirmations extend the Article IV coverage explicitly to PHL armed forces in the South China Sea, including Scarborough and the Second Thomas Shoal contingencies. The MDT's *mutual-defense* clause is *symmetric* on its face (each party defends the other), where the Japan-US Treaty of Mutual Cooperation and Security (1960) assigns defense-of-Japan obligations to the US without a symmetric Japanese commitment to defend US metropolitan territory; the symmetric form gives the MDT a stronger covering-claim against US executive-branch retraction than the asymmetric Japan-US instrument carries.

**Layer 2: the 2014 Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement (EDCA)** as expanded by the **2023-04-03 announcement of four additional EDCA sites**, two of which — **Naval Base Camilo Osias in Sta. Ana, Cagayan** and **Lal-lo Airport in Cagayan** — face the Bashi Channel and lie roughly 480–600 km from southern Taiwan, within tactical-lift range for a Taiwan-contingency air-and-sea operation. Marcos's own characterisation of EDCA's Taiwan relevance — calling a future Strait conflict a **"terrible occurrence"** for which the Philippines must prepare — is unusual for a treaty principal at the executive register and acknowledges the Taiwan-contingency coupling that EDCA's Cagayan tier was selected to enable.

**Layer 3: Balikatan 2026** (41st iteration, 20 April → mid-May), which integrated three new structural elements simultaneously: (i) ~1,000 JGSDF ground troops deploying for the first time on Philippine soil under the Reciprocal Access Agreement signed Manila-Tokyo (RAA, in force September 2025), (ii) an Integrated Air and Missile Defense (IAMD) live-fire serial at Naval Stations Leovigildo Gantioqui and San Miguel (26–29 April) coupling US Marine MADIS + G/ATOR, US Army Avenger + VAMPIRE + FS-LIDS, JSDF Type-11 Tan-SAM Kai II, and the Philippine Air Force's SPYDER medium-range air-defense battery, with Philippine Navy frigates BRP *Antonio Luna* (FFG-15) and BRP *Miguel Malvar* (FFG-6) joining the air-defense simulation; (iii) JMSDF destroyer *Ikazuchi* transiting **southbound** Yokosuka → Balikatan via a Taiwan-Strait routing (Day-10 [Source #17] Reuters), the first JMSDF major-surface-unit transit on a structurally three-way US-PHL-JPN kinetic-trainer end-state rather than the bilateral US-PHL pattern of pre-2023 Balikatan iterations. AFP Chief Brawner described the IAMD serials as Balikatan's first-ever **"common areas"** anti-cruise / anti-ballistic missile training surface — a formulation that parallels Takaichi's 存立危機事態 by making Taiwan-contingency engagement legible under a domestic legal frame *without naming Taiwan*, a recurring structural feature now observed on both the JPN and PHL substrates.

The cumulative effect is that PHL — like JPN — has assembled every operational pre-condition for a contingency-relevant role without staking a parliamentary commitment to a Taiwan-contingency trigger doctrine. The Marcos administration has *not* offered an analogue to Takaichi's 2025-11-07 Diet-of-record *存立危機事態* statement; instead, the PHL pattern is **operational-pre-positioning ahead of rhetorical-pre-commitment** — the inverse temporal sequence of the JPN pattern Day 10 documented. The asymmetric sequence has its own informational signature for the GWW3 frame (§4): it raises the **observability** of contingency-relevant capability without yet activating the *commitment* layer that would convert capability into deterrence credibility.

Audit findings (verbatim from Nisaba):

- **CCG claim passes.** Aug-Dec-2024 mean = 48.0 ship-days (SD 12.90, n=5); Jan-May-2025 mean = 94.6 ship-days (SD 18.35, n=5); mean difference **+46.6 ship-days**, lift **+97.1%** (`94.6/48.0 − 1`), Welch t = 4.645, df = 7.18, **p = 0.00221**, 95% CI for the mean difference **[+22.99, +70.21]** — wholly positive. Mann-Kendall on the same 10-month CCG series: S = 35, τ = 0.778, **p = 0.00236** — statistically clear upward monotonic trend. Pettitt regime-change test: maximal change statistic K = 25, estimated split **after 2024-12 / before 2025-01**, **p ≈ 0.0661** — suggestive of the expected January-2025 regime shift, at-the-edge of conventional significance for n=10.

- **PCG/BFAR claim needs correction.** The supplied rows do **not** reproduce the AMTI-stated +47% lift. Treating November-2024 as a genuine operational zero (Indonesia port visit + domestic disaster relief + Second Thomas Shoal resupply + combat drills per AMTI's own attribution): Aug-Dec-2024 mean = 15.4 ship-days (SD 9.10, n=5), Jan-May-2025 mean = 30.2 ship-days (SD 12.44, n=5), mean difference +14.8, lift **+96.1%**, Welch t = 2.147, **p = 0.0671**, 95% CI **[-1.35, +30.95]** — directionally positive but CI crosses zero. Treating November-2024 as missing-non-comparable: lift drops to **+56.9%**, Welch t = 1.882, **p = 0.122**, CI **[-4.26, +26.16]** — still positive direction but weaker evidence. The AMTI-stated +47% would correspond to `30.2 / 20.5 − 1`, but the supplied late-2024 rows sum to 77 across five months (mean 15.4) or 77 across four nonzero months (mean 19.25), neither of which produces a 20.5 baseline.

- **Interactions linkage is plausible, not proven.** CCG vs PCG/BFAR monthly Pearson r = 0.569 (p = 0.0858, CI [-0.094, 0.883]); total ship-days vs interaction days r = 0.580 (p = 0.0785, CI [-0.078, 0.886]). Both compatible with the qualitative claim that denser law-enforcement presence increases encounters, but n=10 is too short for stable effect estimates.

- **2025-09-16 USNI primary [Source #8]** is *event-level evidence*, not a continuation row in the monthly series. The BRP Datu Gumbay Piang (MMOV 3014) bridge-damage water-cannon incident with PLAN-054 *Ma'anshan* "prompt live fire exercise" radio broadcast is suitable as a register-elevation marker for §3.5, not as a monthly patrol-days statistical observation.
