## §3 — λ-class taxonomy: formal definition

The λ-class taxonomy emerged incrementally across Days 14–17 as each new substrate revealed a distinct relationship between the diplomatic-lexical floor (the Chinese-text verb used to render the English "acknowledges" in the foundational bilateral communiqué) and the legislative-advancement ceiling. Day 18 formalizes the taxonomy with precise definitions, membership criteria, and structural properties.

### §3.1 — Definition: λ (lexical-class)

λ is a substrate-level property that classifies the relationship between the English and Chinese texts of the foundational bilateral communiqué's one-China clause, specifically the verb used to render the English formula "acknowledges the position of the [Chinese Government / PRC] that..." in the Chinese text.

**Domain:** {A, B, C, n/a}

**Classification rule:** A substrate is assigned a λ value if and only if it possesses a bilateral communiqué with an explicit one-China clause containing an English verb in the "acknowledge/take note of/recognise" semantic field whose Chinese rendering can be compared to the PRC's preferred verb 承认 (chéngrèn, "recognise/admit").

### §3.2 — Class A: collapsed (承认 = 承认)

**Definition:** A substrate belongs to Class A if the Chinese text of its foundational communiqué renders the English formula with the verb 承认 (chéngrèn), which is *the same verb* the PRC uses to express full recognition. The English text's careful distinction between "acknowledges" and "recognises" is **collapsed** in the Chinese rendering — both map to the same character.

**Membership:** UK (1972), AUS (1972)

**Structural properties:**
- The diplomatic-lexical floor is indistinguishable in Chinese from full recognition, even though the English text preserves the asymmetry
- The negotiating record (especially AUS, where DFAT Historical Documents Vol. 22 cablegrams survive [Day-15 Source #3]) shows this was a negotiated compromise — AUS sought "takes note" (the Canadian formula), PRC pushed "recognises", and "acknowledges" (承认) was the settlement
- Both Class A substrates reach a committee-level advancement ceiling but no further
- The collapsed asymmetry may structurally constrain legislative advancement: if the Chinese text already reads as "recognises", there is less diplomatic space for a legislative body to push past the executive position without appearing to contradict the communiqué's Chinese text — a text the PRC treats as authoritative

**Chinese-text evidence:**
- UK: "英国政府**承认**中华人民共和国政府是中国的唯一合法政府" [Day-14 Source, 1972 Joint Communiqué]
- AUS: "澳大利亚政府**承认**中华人民共和国政府是中国的唯一合法政府" [Day-15 Source #3, DFAT cablegrams]

### §3.3 — Class B: preserved (注意到 ≠ 承认)

**Definition:** A substrate belongs to Class B if the Chinese text of its foundational communiqué renders the English formula with a verb that is *lexically distinct from* 承认 — specifically, 注意到 (zhùyì dào, "takes note of") — thereby **preserving** the asymmetry between the English text's "takes note of" and the PRC's preferred "recognises." The EN-ZH distinction is maintained in both directions.

**Membership:** CAN (1970)

**Structural properties:**
- The diplomatic-lexical floor preserves in Chinese the carefully negotiated English distinction: Canada "takes note of" (注意到) the PRC position rather than recognising (承认) it
- CAN's 1970 communiqué is the earliest in the sample — it established the "takes note" formula that UK/AUS subsequently adapted into "acknowledges" with the opposite (collapsed) Chinese rendering
- Class B produces a higher advancement ceiling than Class A: CAN reaches 1st reading (House) / 2nd reading (Senate), versus UK/AUS at committee level
- The preserved distinction may enable deeper Tier-3 formulations: Bill C-343 §4's "Taiwan is a foreign state" [Day-16 Source #5] is the deepest Tier-3 instrument in the sample, but its advancement stalls — producing the widest Tier-2-to-Tier-3 depth gap
- κ=present is unique to Class B (Hogue Commission), though whether this is a Class B structural consequence or a CAN-specific institutional accident cannot be determined from N=1

**Chinese-text evidence:**
- CAN: "加拿大政府**注意到**中华人民共和国政府的如下立场" [Day-16 Source #2, #10]

### §3.4 — Class C: dual (认识到 → 承认, cross-communiqué shift)

**Definition:** A substrate belongs to Class C if it possesses *two or more* foundational communiqués whose Chinese texts use *different verbs* from the "acknowledge/recognise" semantic field, creating a **dual-verb architecture** where the same English word ("acknowledges") maps to different Chinese verbs across different documents.

**Membership:** US (1972 Shanghai Communiqué + 1979 Normalization Communiqué)

**Structural properties:**
- The Shanghai Communiqué (1972) uses 认识到 (rènshí dào, "comes to understand / realises") — the weakest verb in the entire sample, weaker than both 注意到 (CAN Class B) and 承认 (UK/AUS Class A)
- The Normalization Communiqué (1979) shifts to 承认 (chéngrèn) — the same verb as Class A substrates
- This cross-communiqué lexical shift creates a structurally unique diplomatic floor: the US position is bracketed by its weakest (1972) and strongest (1979) diplomatic verb, with the Six Assurances (1982, private → codified 2016/2020) explicitly constraining the interpretive range of the 1982 August 17 Communiqué
- Class C produces the highest advancement ceiling in the sample: enacted law (multiple statutes — TRA, TTA, TAIPEI Act, TAA, BOLSTER Act, TAIA)
- The dual-verb architecture + domestic-law legislative floor (TRA 1979) produces the widest diplomatic-to-legislative gap of any substrate: the weakest diplomatic floor (1972 认识到) coexists with the strongest legislative floor (enacted law), a structural impossibility in any Class A or Class B substrate where a single communiqué verb sets both the diplomatic floor and the implicit constraint on legislative advancement

**Chinese-text evidence:**
- Shanghai (1972): "美国方面**认识到**...台湾是中国的一部分" [Day-17 Source #1, #2, Wikisource bilingual]
- Normalization (1979): "美利坚合众国政府**承认**中华人民共和国政府是中国的唯一合法政府" [Day-17 Source #1, #2, Wikisource bilingual]

### §3.5 — Class n/a: unclassifiable substrates

Three substrates cannot be assigned a λ value:

| Substrate | Reason | Structural note |
|---|---|---|
| EU | Multilateral entity; one-China *policy* (EU Council) not grounded in a bilateral communiqué | EU operates through institutional policy + EP resolutions; no diplomatic-verb asymmetry to classify |
| JPN | 1972 Joint Communiqué uses a structurally different formula: "fully understands and respects" (十分理解し、尊重する), not "acknowledges" | The JP formula is not in the "acknowledge/take note/recognise" semantic field — it is a distinct diplomatic construction that does not translate into a 承认/注意到/认识到 comparison |
| PHL | Recognition-state: 1975 Joint Communiqué grants full diplomatic recognition (承认) | As a recognition-state, PHL has no asymmetry to classify — the diplomatic floor is at its maximum (full recognition), and no Tier-3 status-narrowing register exists (ARCH-ABSENCE) |

### §3.6 — λ-class verb hierarchy

The three classified verbs form a strict hierarchy ordered by diplomatic commitment strength:

```
认识到  <  注意到  <  承认
(C-weak)   (B)       (A = PRC preferred)
```

Where:
- 认识到 (rènshí dào) = "comes to understand / realises" — epistemic, non-committal; acknowledges awareness without endorsing the position
- 注意到 (zhùyì dào) = "takes note of" — performative noting; stronger than mere awareness but short of endorsement
- 承认 (chéngrèn) = "recognises / admits" — the PRC's preferred verb, functionally equivalent to recognition in Chinese diplomatic usage

The hierarchy runs *inversely* to the advancement-ceiling axis: substrates with the weakest diplomatic verb (认识到, US) reach the highest legislative advancement (enacted law), while substrates with the strongest diplomatic verb (承认, UK/AUS) reach only committee level. This inverse correlation is the λ-ceiling mechanism formalized in §4.

## §4 — λ-ceiling correlation: causal mechanism analysis

### §4.1 — The observed correlation

Among the four substrates with classifiable λ values, the diplomatic-verb strength (§3.6 hierarchy) runs inversely to the legislative-advancement ceiling:

| λ-class | Verb | Verb strength | Substrates | Advancement ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (collapsed) | 承认 | strongest (PRC-preferred) | UK, AUS | Select/joint standing committee |
| B (preserved) | 注意到 | middle | CAN | 1st reading (House) / 2nd reading (Senate) |
| C-weak layer | 认识到 | weakest in sample | US (1972) | Enacted law (multiple statutes) |

The correlation is monotone: weaker diplomatic verb → higher legislative ceiling. The question is whether this is causal or coincidental — confounded by country size, democratic tradition, institutional capacity, or unrelated constitutional features.

### §4.2 — Candidate causal mechanism: diplomatic-space theory

The strongest candidate mechanism is **diplomatic-space**: the weaker the diplomatic-verb floor, the more interpretive room exists between the executive's acknowledged diplomatic position and a legislature's Taiwan-status-narrowing assertion, and therefore the lower the political cost of legislative advancement.

The logic runs as follows:

(i) **Class A (collapsed, 承认):** The Chinese text reads the same as full recognition. If a UK or Australian legislator proposes a bill framing Taiwan as an independent state, the executive can point to the communiqué's Chinese text — which uses *the same verb* (承认) that PRC uses for full recognition — and argue that any Tier-3 legislative instrument directly contradicts the diplomatic foundation of the bilateral relationship as read in the PRC's authoritative text. The political cost of deviation is high because the communiqué leaves no interpretive space between "what we acknowledged" and "what PRC claims we recognised." Legislative Tier-3 framing therefore surfaces only where the political-accountability cost is structurally low: select committees (where individual committee members face no whole-of-House vote on the framing) and backbench speeches (where the speech-act carries no binding institutional weight).

(ii) **Class B (preserved, 注意到):** Canada's "takes note of" formula preserves in both languages the distinction between noting PRC's position and endorsing it. A Canadian legislator proposing a Taiwan-status-narrowing bill can argue that the 1970 communiqué *never* committed Canada to recognising PRC's claim — only to *noting* it. The political cost of legislative advancement is lower than in Class A because the communiqué's own text supports the interpretive gap. Legislative Tier-3 framing advances past committee level (Bill C-343, Senate S-277) but stalls at first/second reading — the advancement is deeper than Class A but still encounters a ceiling where whole-of-Parliament adoption would force a direct confrontation between the enacted law and the diplomatic formula.

(iii) **Class C (dual, 认识到 → 承认):** The US possesses the widest diplomatic-space of any substrate. The 1972 Shanghai Communiqué's 认识到 is the weakest verb in the entire sample — it is epistemic ("comes to understand"), not even performative ("takes note of"). Combined with the Six Assurances' explicit freeze of the US sovereignty-status position at the 1972 level, the space between the diplomatic floor and a legislature's Taiwan-status assertion is structurally maximal. The TRA (1979) was enacted exactly *because* this diplomatic space existed: Congress could legislate a binding security commitment to Taiwan without contradicting the diplomatic foundation, because the diplomatic foundation (认识到) committed the US to understanding PRC's position, not to endorsing it. Every subsequent statute (TTA, TAIPEI Act, TAA, BOLSTER Act, TAIA) fits within the same space.

### §4.3 — Mechanism test: within-substrate evidence

The causal hypothesis makes a within-substrate prediction: if diplomatic-space is the mechanism, then the *timing* of legislative advancement should correlate with periods when the diplomatic-space was politically articulated or tested.

**US substrate (strongest evidence):** The TRA was enacted in 1979 immediately following normalization — the legislative floor was built at the moment the diplomatic space was widest (the gap between 认识到 and full diplomatic relations with PRC created urgent political demand for a legislative counterweight). The Six Assurances (1982) were articulated at the next moment of diplomatic-space compression (the August 17 Communiqué), explicitly to prevent the space from collapsing. Legislative advancement resumed in 2018 (TTA) when the diplomatic space was again politically salient (PRC's increased pressure on Taiwan's international participation under Xi). The temporal pattern supports the diplomatic-space mechanism.

**CAN substrate (partial evidence):** Bill C-343's "Taiwan is a foreign state" formulation (2023) emerged during a period of increased attention to Canada-PRC relations (the Hogue Commission on foreign interference). The legislative advancement coincided with a political context in which the diplomatic-space between Canada's "takes note of" formula and PRC's preferred reading was actively being discussed in the context of PRC influence operations. The temporal pattern is consistent with but does not independently confirm the mechanism (N=1 legislative-advancement event).

**UK/AUS substrates (no within-substrate temporal test):** The committee-level ceiling in both substrates has been stable since 2017 (the earliest catalogued period in Inanna's Hansard and JSCFADT surveys). The absence of advancement *beyond* committee level is consistent with the collapsed-space hypothesis but does not test it dynamically.

### §4.4 — Confounders and limitations

Three confounders cannot be ruled out from the current sample:

1. **Country size and global influence.** The US is the world's largest military and economic power. Its capacity for legislative advancement on Taiwan may reflect institutional confidence in managing PRC retaliation rather than diplomatic-space per se. The UK (Class A) also possesses substantial global influence but reaches only committee level — this argues against a pure size-confound, but N=2 is not dispositive.

2. **Constitutional structure.** The US has a separation-of-powers system where Congress legislates independently of the executive's diplomatic commitments; Westminster systems have stronger executive-legislative fusion. Class A and B substrates are all Westminster-derived; Class C is the only presidential system. The advancement-ceiling difference may partially reflect the constitutional-separation effect rather than the diplomatic-space effect.

3. **Historical contingency.** The TRA's enactment was a specific response to the normalization shock of 1979 — a political moment that may not generalize. Without the normalization-driven urgency, the diplomatic-space theory predicts the US would *eventually* reach enacted-law ceiling but cannot predict *when*.

### §4.5 — Verdict: suggestive causal mechanism, insufficient sample for confirmation

The diplomatic-space theory provides a structurally coherent explanation for the observed inverse correlation between diplomatic-verb strength and advancement ceiling. The within-substrate temporal evidence from the US supports the mechanism. The confounders (size, constitutional structure, historical contingency) weaken but do not defeat the causal claim.

**For GWW3 purposes:** The correlation is encoded as a *structural relationship* (§5 below), not as a confirmed causal law. The model treats λ-class as a *conditioning variable* on advancement-ceiling distributions, not as a deterministic cause. If future substrates (e.g. India, South Korea) provide additional λ-classified data points, the correlation can be tested for robustness. Until then, the diplomatic-space mechanism is the working hypothesis, with the confounders explicitly noted in the model documentation.

### §5.3 — Encoding notes

**Ceiling ordinal.** The five-tier ordinal (0-4) is an analytical convenience, not a measurement scale. The intervals between tiers are unequal: the gap from committee (1) to plenary vote (2) is institutionally narrower than the gap from reading (3) to enacted law (4). The ordinal is used for ordering and conditional lookup, not for arithmetic.

**Class C three-layer architecture.** The US substrate's unique D₁/D₂/L architecture (Day-17 §4) is encoded via three additional `game_state` properties (`d1_verb`, `d2_verb`, `legislative_floor`) rather than a nested structure. This preserves the gsl_ops.lark v1.2 compatibility requirement. If the grammar extends to support struct kwargs (q8 option B, tracked as a Hassaleh-engine issue), the encoding can be refactored.

**Consolidated counters.** The `pass_count`, `fail_count`, and `arch_absence_count` properties are running tallies that the GWW3 model can query to determine whether the P28 sample is sufficient for closure. Current values: pass=6, fail=0, arch_absence=1.
