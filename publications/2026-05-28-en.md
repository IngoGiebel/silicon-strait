### §1.2 — Why ROK is the structurally cleanest test

Four features make ROK the cleanest available addition to the corpus:

1. **Era-isolation.** The PRC-ROK Joint Communiqué was signed 1992-08-24 — 20 years after JPN 1972, 22 years after CAN 1970, 13 years after US 1979. Unlike the 1970s normalisation wave (UK, AUS, CAN, US all signed within 9 years of each other), ROK's communiqué is the only post-Cold-War instrument in the corpus. If λ-class is era-dependent (templates evolved), ROK should exhibit either a clear continuity with the 1970s template or a clear deviation. The pre-registration (§2 below) predicted continuity at high confidence; this publication tests that prediction.

2. **East Asian lexicon-family.** ROK shares the East Asian "respects" lexicon-family with JPN — both render the position-verb as 尊重 (Chinese pinyin: zūnzhòng; Korean Hangul: 존중) rather than the "acknowledges/takes note/recognises" Western set. This forces a structural choice: is JPN's apparent unclassifiability in Day-18 §3 a property of JPN-specific architecture (non-comparable verb-pair) or a property of the East Asian lexicon-family more generally? ROK's classification answers this directly.

3. **Inter-Korean confound textually coupled to one-China.** Clauses 4 and 5 of the 1992 communiqué commit PRC to *respect Korean-nation aspirations for peaceful peninsula unification* and *support inter-Korean dialogue led by Koreans themselves* — in the same document that commits ROK to the one-China clause. No other substrate in the corpus has a clause structure where the diplomatic floor is textually coupled to a security-strategic concession from PRC on a third-party question (the DPRK). This creates a substrate-specific asymmetric exit-cost on recognition-language drift that is itself a P28 prediction: ROK should be unusually stable at λ-class A *because* drift triggers reciprocal PRC re-evaluation of clauses 4–5.

4. **Semiconductor-supply-chain exposure.** Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix together represent the second-largest national semiconductor exposure to PRC after Taiwan itself (Day-21 Nexus deep-dive territory). This makes ROK the substrate where the diplomatic floor / economic ratchet gap is most acute: economic alignment with the US (CHIPS Act extension, IPEF participation, KORUS 2.0) ratchets continuously toward US Indo-Pacific framing while diplomatic recognition language remains pinned to the 1992 formula. The gap is exactly what P28 names; ROK is the empirical instance where it is widest.

### §1.3 — What ROK is NOT (architectural exclusions)

To avoid scope creep, three ROK-substrate questions are explicitly out of scope for Day-19/20:

- **DPRK as adjacent substrate.** The DPRK does not recognise PRC's one-China position in the same way (DPRK independently recognises ROC-as-PRC), and the question of DPRK's own substrate-position is methodologically distinct. Day-19/20 holds DPRK as a confound variable only.
- **ROC (Taiwan)-as-counterparty trade.** ROK has substantial ROC-Taiwan trade and is the second-largest source of foreign investment in Taiwan after the US/Japan in certain semiconductor sub-sectors. This is Day-21 Nexus material, not λ-class material.
- **Post-2024 ROK political volatility.** The Yoon administration's December 2024 martial-law declaration and subsequent impeachment trajectory introduces government-stability noise. P28 is a structural-property test, not a current-administration-policy test; the publication uses MOFA-ROK 2024-11-01 joint statements (Yoon-era) as the most recent stable primary source and does not project beyond.

## §2 — 1992 PRC-ROK Joint Communiqué: clause-3 verb analysis

### §2.1 — The pre-registered prediction (blind, filed 2026-05-28 11:21 CEST)

The pre-registration document (`silicon-strait/research/kr_state_extension/p28-pre-registration-2026-05-28.md`, commit `2b6b81f`, SHA-256 `6f541463...41105b`) made three predictions before any ZH-text source-pull:

| Prediction | Class | Confidence | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | A (collapsed, 承认) | 55–60% | Post-Tiananmen PRC negotiating strength + ROK *Nordpolitik* incentives + canonical 1990s drafting templates |
| Secondary | B (preserved, 注意到) | 20–25% | Possible lexical trade against PRC's DPRK-clause concession |
| Tertiary | Fourth-class (尊重 / unclassifiable) | 15–20% | East Asian lexicon-family neighbour effect with JPN |

**Strict-falsification rule:** Unambiguous 承认 → Class A. Unambiguous 注意到 / 注意 / 留意 → Class B. Any verb outside {承认, 注意到, 认识到} → unclassifiable, forcing a Day-18 §3 taxonomy reframe.

### §2.2 — The post-pull evidence (ZH primary text, MFA-PRC archive)

The full 6-article verbatim ZH text was pulled at 14:10 CEST from the MFA-PRC archive (publication record archived 2000-11-07; original signing 1992-08-24 in Beijing) [Source #1]. The clause-3 verbatim text is:

> 三、大韩民国政府承认中华人民共和国政府为中国的唯一合法政府，并尊重中方只有一个中国、台湾是中国的一部分之立场。

**Verb extraction and translation:**

| Verb | Pinyin | Function | Mapping | English equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **承认** | chéngrèn | applies to "PRC government as sole legal Government of China" | recognition-state floor | "recognises" / "acknowledges as" |
| **尊重** | zūnzhòng | applies to "PRC's position that there is only one China and Taiwan is part of China" | position-handling floor | "respects" |

The Korean clause-3 (per Ewha Daily editorial board quoting MOFA-ROK official text [Source #2]) reads:

> '대한민국 정부는 중화인민공화국 정부를 중국의 유일 합법 정부로 승인하며, 오직 하나의 중국만이 있고 대만은 중국의 일부분이라는 중국의 입장을 존중한다'

The Korean verbs **승인** (seungin, "recognise / approve") and **존중** (jonjung, "respect") map straightforwardly to the Chinese 承认 and 尊重 respectively. Both languages express the same two-verb structure.

The Dione-direct English working translation (cross-checked against the KO):

> "The Government of the Republic of Korea recognises the Government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal Government of China, and respects China's position that there is only one China and that Taiwan is a part of China."

(The official ROK English translation, if any, remains owed to a later Inanna dispatch; the working translation above is sufficient for the λ-class classification but should be replaced with the official text in Day-21 archival.)

### §2.3 — λ-class verb-confirmation

The position-verb is **尊重** ("respects"), not 承认, 注意到, or 认识到. By the strict-falsification rule of the pre-registration:

- The verb is *not* unambiguously 承认 → Class A primary prediction is **technically falsified at strict-rule level**.
- The verb is *not* unambiguously 注意到 or synonym → Class B is falsified.
- The verb is **尊重**, outside the {承认, 注意到, 认识到} set → tertiary prediction (unclassifiable / fourth class) is the surface-level rule-applying outcome.

**However**, the substantive content of the clause requires careful interpretation. The recognition-state verb (PRC government as sole legal government) IS 承认 — the canonical λ-class A collapsed-recognition verb. What 尊重 attaches to is the *position* — that there is only one China and Taiwan is part of China. This is a structurally different sentence position from the Western communiqués: the US 1972 Shanghai formula has the US "acknowledging" both that "all Chinese on either side of the Taiwan Strait maintain there is but one China" and that "the United States Government does not challenge that position". The ROK formula collapses the recognition (PRC as sole legal government) into 承认 *and* uses a stronger position-handling verb (尊重) than the US, JPN, AUS, UK, or CAN formulas.

**Refined λ-class assessment:** ROK 1992 is **λ-class A with stricter position-handling than the canonical A class**. The substrate sits at the *strict end* of A, not in a separate fourth class. The Day-18 §3 taxonomy survives intact, but Day-19 adds an internal-strictness gradient *within* Class A:

| Sub-class within Class A | Position verb | Substrates |
|---|---|---|
| A.1 (canonical) | "acknowledges" / 注意到 [conceptually — in practice all collapsed-to-承认 substrates collapse the position too] | UK 1972 (sole 承认 covers both), AUS 1972 (same) |
| A.2 (strict) | "respects" / 尊重 | **ROK 1992**, JPN 1972 (with verb-pair 理解し尊重する) |

This refinement also resolves the Day-18 §3 JPN unclassifiability question: JPN is not *unclassifiable* — JPN is λ-class A.2 (strict), with the verb-pair "fully understands and respects" expressing the position-handling step as a doubled commitment (understanding *and* respect). The "understands" verb is approximately the Western "acknowledges"; the "respects" verb is the strict-end strengthener. JPN therefore belongs in the same sub-class as ROK.

**Pre-registration verdict:** Primary prediction (Class A) is **confirmed at the recognition-verb level** (承认). The pre-registration's tertiary prediction (fourth class with 尊重) was technically the more accurate prediction at the *position-verb* level, but the resolution is that 尊重 is a **strictness gradient within Class A**, not a separate class. Net: pre-registration is *substantially* confirmed; taxonomy receives a minor refinement (A → A.1 / A.2) rather than a reframe.

### §2.4 — Why the strict position-handling matters

The 尊重 register is the **strict end** of the Tier-1 one-China gradient. A ROK posture-statement that retained 尊重 while adding qualification — e.g. "respects China's position as expressed by China", "respects the historical position", "respects the position consistent with peaceful resolution" — would be λ-class A.2 → A.1 drift (still Class A, weaker sub-class). To date, no MOFA-ROK official communication has documented such drift (per the companion `dione-mofa-rok-korus-corpus-2026-05-28.md`, §3, covering 2017–2024-11 primary statements).

What HAS happened in 2024 MOFA-ROK joint statements (ROK-Canada 2+2, US-ROK 2+2) is a different kind of movement: rhetorical-register elevation in *operational* statements (Taiwan-Strait peace described as "indispensable", first-time joint-statement criticism of PRC military activity around Taiwan) **while the 1992 anchor formula remains literally unchanged**. This is the gap P28 names: operational rhetoric drifts toward US Indo-Pacific framing; anchor formula stays pinned. The substrate-asymmetric exit cost from anchor revision (the inter-Korean confound, §3.2 below) explains *why* the pinning holds.

## §3 — Substrate-architectural context: treaty corpus + inter-Korean confound

### §3.1 — 1953 US-ROK Mutual Defense Treaty (MDT) — clean Taiwan-Strait off-ramp

The MDT (Yale Avalon Project [Source #3]) is doubly scoped down from a generic mutual-defense framing:

**Article III (geographic scope):** Each Party recognises that "an armed attack in the Pacific area on either of the Parties in territories now under their respective administrative control, or hereafter recognised by one of the Parties as lawfully brought under the administrative control of the other, would be dangerous to its own peace and safety". Taiwan was under ROC administrative control in 1953 and is not a Party to the treaty. A PRC armed attack on Taiwan is therefore, by treaty text, **outside Article III scope for both Parties**.

**Senate Understanding (further narrowing):** The US Senate's understanding of ratification specifies that "neither party is obligated, under Article III of the above Treaty, to come to the aid of the other except in case of an external armed attack against such party". This narrows the trigger condition explicitly to attacks *against the Party itself*, not against third parties even within the Pacific area.

**P28-relevant implication:** The MDT gives ROK a *clean legal off-ramp* from any Taiwan-Strait kinetic commitment. If a Taiwan-Strait conflict occurred, ROK could cite Article III's territorial limit and the Senate Understanding to decline kinetic involvement *while still honouring the alliance for Korean-Peninsula defence*. This is exactly the substrate-architectural condition that lets a Tier-1 ally remain at λ-class A.2 (strict collapsed acknowledgment with respect) without contradicting its US-alliance obligations. The "indefinite" duration with one-year notice (Article VI) means ROK has structural inertia *against* repositioning — alliance-termination exit costs are high, so revisionism would have to be very strong to override.

### §3.2 — Inter-Korean confound: clauses 4 and 5 of the 1992 communiqué

The 1992 communiqué's clauses 4 and 5 are the **inter-Korean confound clauses** — they textually couple PRC and ROK on the DPRK-unification question in the *same document* that pins ROK to one-China:

- **Clause 4:** Both governments believe diplomatic relations will help ease and stabilise the Korean peninsula situation, and contribute to peace and stability in Asia.
- **Clause 5:** The PRC government respects the Korean nation's wish for early peaceful unification of the peninsula, and supports the Korean nation realising peaceful unification by itself.

This was the trade: ROK got PRC support for inter-Korean unification language; PRC got ROK's strict one-China commitment under 尊重. The two are textually coupled in the founding document of the relationship.

**P28-relevant implication:** Any scenario where ROK considers revising its one-China commitment must reckon with the *coupled* DPRK clauses. PRC could plausibly argue (and has, in lower-register diplomatic exchanges) that ROK weakening clause 3 retroactively voids PRC obligations under clauses 4–5 — UN sanctions cooperation, opposition to DPRK-Russia military cooperation, inter-Korean dialogue support. This is a **structural deterrent** against ROK recognition-language drift that has no parallel in any other substrate in the corpus. EU has no inter-Korean stake to couple to; US has no DPRK-engagement commitment from PRC textually coupled to its 1979 communiqué; UK/AUS/CAN have no third-party question textually coupled to their 1970s communiqués. ROK is the only substrate where recognition-language revision triggers a substrate-specific reciprocal concession-revision risk.

This coupling is one reason why P28 predicts λ-class A.2 stability at high confidence even under significant external pressure: the cost of moving is **asymmetrically high** for ROK because clause-3 revision triggers clause-4/5 risk on a question (DPRK) that is *existentially* load-bearing for ROK security. The structural strength of the anchor is, in ROK's case, partly the structural weakness of the substrate (its security depending on PRC continuing to honour clauses 4–5).

### §3.3 — KORUS economic ratchet (2007 baseline, 2018 renegotiation)

The 2007 KORUS FTA (USTR Final Text [Source #4], 2018 renegotiated to "KORUS 2.0" under the first Trump administration) is silent on Taiwan, China, or one-China — it is a bilateral trade agreement, not a security instrument. Its load-bearing relevance to ROK's Taiwan posture is path-dependent: KORUS locked in deep US-ROK supply-chain integration in semiconductors, autos, and pharmaceuticals, and the 2018 bipartisan renegotiation indicates US commitment to the structure regardless of administration.

The economic ratchet is significant for the Day-21 Nexus deep-dive (semiconductor supply-chain), but for Day-19's λ-class analysis the relevance is simpler: KORUS creates path-dependent economic alignment that makes ROK behaviourally close to the US position on Indo-Pacific supply-chain decoupling from China (CHIPS Act extension, IPEF participation), even though the diplomatic position formally remains anchored to the 1992 communiqué's strict one-China formulation. **The two can co-exist for years; they cannot co-exist under acute Taiwan-Strait stress.** P28's prediction is that under stress, ROK absorbs more behavioural drift (toward US alignment) than recognition-language drift (away from 1992 anchor) — *because* the substrate-architectural structure (MDT off-ramp + inter-Korean confound) supports asymmetric drift in exactly that direction.

### §3.4 — 2024 MOFA-ROK posture (joint statements, Yoon-era)

Two recent stable primary sources document the operational-rhetoric / anchor-formula gap [Sources #5, #6]:

**ROK-Canada 2+2 Ministerial Joint Statement (2024-11-01, Ottawa).** Taiwan-Strait clause: "*The Ministers underscored the importance of peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait as indispensable to global security and prosperity. They expressed concerns over recent military drills around Taiwan that escalate tension*". This is the first ROK joint-statement instance of explicit criticism of PRC military activity around Taiwan (PRC behavioural criticism in a ministerial joint statement). The recognition-language anchor is *not* mentioned — the 1992 formula is preserved by silence (operative rhetoric does not invoke "respects China's position" or rebut it; the omission preserves the anchor while operative behaviour ratchets).

**US-ROK 2+2 Ministerial Joint Statement (2024 cycle).** Comparable structure: "indispensable" Taiwan-Strait language, no recognition-language re-statement. The 1992 anchor's status is "preserved by silence in operative statements; explicit only in PRC-relations posture documents".

**P28-relevant interpretation:** The 2024 movement is *rhetorical-register elevation in operational statements* (PRC military activity criticised by name) while *anchor formula preserved unchanged* (1992 clause 3 not invoked, not modified, not qualified). This is the precise pattern P28 predicts: anchor stability + operational drift, with the asymmetric exit costs (§3.1 + §3.2) explaining why the drift goes one direction only (toward US alignment) and not the other (away from 1992 anchor).
