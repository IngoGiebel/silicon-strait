### §3.1 — UK Tier-1+Tier-2 architecture: 1972 Joint Communiqué constitutional stratification (q-day14-1 §2.1 + Inanna 1972 verbatim deliverable)

The UK established diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China on 1972-03-13 via an Exchange of Ambassadors announcement to the House of Commons, with the operative diplomatic instrument being the Anglo-Chinese Joint Communiqué of the same date. The 1972 Communiqué's two operative passages on China-Taiwan questions are now verbatim-confirmed from primary Hansard [Source 8]:

> **Tier-1 anchor (UK → PRC, sole-legal-Government):** "The Government of the United Kingdom recognise the Government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal Government of China."

> **Tier-2 instantiation (UK → PRC's Taiwan claim, with deliberate asymmetry):** "The Government of the United Kingdom, *acknowledging the position of the Chinese Government that Taiwan is a province of the People's Republic of China*, have decided to remove their official representation in Taiwan on 13th March, 1972."

The structural design — Tier-1 "*recognise*" on Government-recognition + Tier-2 "*acknowledging*" on Taiwan-status — establishes the UK-PRC opening lexicon as **constitutionally stratified** at the diplomatic opening, not late-arriving in response to subsequent political pressure. The "acknowledge"-not-"recognise" asymmetry parallels the US 1972 Shanghai Communiqué's "*acknowledges* that all Chinese on either side of the Taiwan Strait maintain there is but one China" formulation in deliberate diplomatic ambiguation, but the UK construction is slightly tighter because it explicitly names PRC *position* rather than Chinese *people*-on-both-sides as the acknowledged object. This is the load-bearing distinction between the Anglo-American Tier-2 lexicon and the EU "policy" Tier-2 lexicon (Day-12 §4): both are policy-positions, but the Anglo-American formulation makes the policy-position explicitly *about the PRC's claim* (an acknowledgement-of-position) rather than *about the UK's own policy* (a self-described "One China policy" register).

The 1972 mixed-register opening has been sustained verbatim across 54 years and across multiple government-changes. The FCDO's standing position on Taiwan, as restated by the Sunak Government to FAC in November 2023 (Tier-2 self-restatement) and by the Starmer Government to Commons in November 2024 (Tier-2 lexical continuity under a different party + Minister-of-State), continues to deploy "longstanding position has not changed" + "no diplomatic relations / unofficial relationship" + "status quo" framing that is recognisably the 1972 Joint Communiqué Tier-2 line. This **constitutional-stratification + 54-year-lexical-continuity-across-government-change** is a strong indicator of FCDO institutional ownership rather than ministerial-party-specific framing, and is structurally informative for P28 testing: the UK substrate's Tier-1+Tier-2 register architecture is institutionally entrenched at a level that makes deviation from the Tier-2 register the *active* register-event requiring institutional justification, while restatement of Tier-2 is the *default* register-event requiring no justification.

### §3.2 — FAC HC 172 §142 (2023) as Tier-3 candidate at the select-committee register (q-day14-1 §3)

The House of Commons Foreign Affairs Committee, in its Eighth Report of Session 2022-23 *Tilting horizons: the Integrated Review and the Indo-Pacific* (HC 172, published 2023-08-30 [Source 1]), paragraph 142, deploys a textbook Tier-3 framing on Taiwan:

> "Taiwan is already an independent country, under the name Republic of China (ROC). **Taiwan possesses all the qualifications for statehood, including a permanent population, a defined territory, government, and the capacity to enter into relations with other states**—it is only lacking greater international recognition."

The framing crosses the Tier-3 floor by EP-Day-12 standards on four structural axes:

- **Declarative-fact register**: "Taiwan is" — not "Taiwan should be", not "we recognise Taiwan as", not "the UK acknowledges that Taiwan is". The verb is unmodalised present-tense declarative.
- **Montevideo Convention 1933 statehood criteria invocation**: "permanent population, defined territory, government, capacity to enter into relations with other states" — the international-law definition of statehood, bypassing the UN-recognition register entirely.
- **Formal parliamentary instrument**: authored by a UK-Parliament select committee in formal session, published as House of Commons paper HC 172, not as a journalistic op-ed, APPG informal report, or backbench Early Day Motion.
- **Inconsistent-with-executive-Tier-2-register**: the FAC's framing recognises Taiwan as a state in international-law substance even while disclaiming proclamation-of-independence, which is inconsistent with the FCDO's "acknowledge"-not-"recognise" Tier-2 register.

A structurally informative observation: the FAC report's *recommendations* (paras 47-54 in the recommendations summary, including Rec 49: "The UK's 'One China' policy is not the same as China's 'One China' principle. Rather than agreeing to China's claim to Taiwan, the UK, like other liberal democracies, merely acknowledges Beijing's position") are largely Tier-2-consistent and do not extend the §142 Tier-3 framing into operative recommendations. The Tier-3 framing is concentrated at §142 in the report's *body* (the analytical chapter on China's threat to Taiwan), not extended into the *recommendations*. This is the FAC's deliberate institutional positioning: the Tier-3 register is **asserted-as-finding** but not **prescribed-as-action**. The structure is analogous to EP plenary resolutions where the *whereas* clauses can contain Tier-3 framings that the *operative* paragraphs do not require the Council/Commission to act upon — the doctrinal-narrowness register-work is finding-located rather than recommendation-located.

The 2023-08-30 FAC publication was reported globally as "first time the British parliament has referred to Taiwan as an 'independent country' in an official document" [Source 4, Source 5], confirming that the political-reception of HC 172 §142 in the international press recognised the Tier-3 framing as a register-event distinct from prior UK-Parliamentary Taiwan-attention. The Cleverly Government's PRC engagement continued through the publication week (Foreign Secretary visit to Beijing overlapping HC 172 publication) without visible disruption from the FAC framing — a structurally significant *non-event* for P28(c) testing (§3.3 below).

### §3.3 — FCDO-FAC institutional-distance + P28 three-condition test on the UK substrate (q-day14-1 §4)

The Government Response to FAC report (HC 630, published 2023-11-21 [Source 2]), paragraph 135, performs a textbook **diplomatic-distance restatement** of the FCDO Tier-2 position:

> "**The UK's longstanding position on Taiwan has not changed.** The UK has no diplomatic relations with Taiwan but a strong, unofficial relationship, based on deep and growing ties in a wide range of areas and underpinned by shared democratic values. The UK has a clear interest in peace and stability in the Taiwan Strait. We consider the Taiwan issue one to be settled peacefully by the people on both sides of the Taiwan Strait through constructive dialogue, without the threat or use of force or coercion. **We do not support any unilateral attempts to change the status quo.** This includes increased Chinese assertiveness towards Taiwan."

Three load-bearing structural features make this an exemplary P28(c) test-instance: (1) **explicit unchanged-position assertion** ("longstanding position has not changed") addresses the FAC §142 framing *without naming it* — the Government will not adopt the FAC's framing as Government policy; (2) **no direct engagement with §142 text** — nowhere does the Government quote, paraphrase, or directly counter the "qualifications for statehood" claim — the Government simply restates its own Tier-2 position without engaging the Tier-3 framing on its merits; (3) **no institutional sanction against the FAC** — no censure motion, no Committee Chair replacement demand, no Speaker referral, no Crown Law Office opinion challenging the report's competence, no Whitehall communication suppressing further FAC work on Taiwan.

The three P28 conditions test on the UK substrate as follows:

**(a) Constitutional-grounding of legislative agency.** UK Parliament is sovereign (Crown-in-Parliament). Select committees are creatures of standing orders with full powers of summons and report-publication, and the Government has a standing duty to respond formally to select committee reports (typically within 6 months) — a duty honored for FAC HC 172 by HC 630. Select committees have no power to *bind* Government foreign policy, but they have constitutional standing to publish findings inconsistent with Government policy as a formal parliamentary instrument. **TEST RESULT: POSITIVE** at the select-committee register.

**(b) Executive-independence from foreign-policy executive register.** The FAC operated independently of FCDO during HC 172 inquiry (August 2022-August 2023). FCDO submitted evidence; FAC was free to disagree with FCDO framing in its findings. The Government did not (publicly) pre-clear, veto, or amend the §142 text before publication. The FAC Chair (Alicia Kearns, 2023) was a Government-party (Conservative) MP at the time of HC 172 publication — yet still published the Tier-3 framing. This is stronger executive-independence than the EU case (where EP-Council partisan composition routinely diverges); EP independence is *enabled by* its institutional distance from the Council, whereas UK select-committee independence is *demonstrated despite* same-party chair. **TEST RESULT: POSITIVE** — arguably stronger than EP case at the same-party-chair calibration.

**(c) Non-crisis-producing-deviation-capacity.** FAC §142 was published 2023-08-30; the Government Response was published 12 weeks later (2023-11-21); the Government Response §135 restated the Tier-2 position without naming, suppressing, or sanctioning the FAC. No constitutional crisis ensued: no minister resignation, no FAC reconstitution, no PRC-induced UK diplomatic crisis that the Government attributed to the FAC report (Cleverly's August 2023 Beijing visit actually overlapped the FAC publication week with no visible disruption). The Government's "longstanding position has not changed" was *both* a restatement of policy *and* a public admission that the FAC's framing did not bind the Government — which is itself the constitutional-architecture statement that allows (c) to test positive. **TEST RESULT: POSITIVE** — FAC §142 + Gov-Response §135 is a textbook restatement-without-crisis cycle.

A structurally informative comparison: UK select-committee Tier-3 framing is *not* operationally binding on the Government, whereas EP plenary-resolution Tier-3 framing is non-binding on the Council; both substrates exhibit P28(c) positive results at their respective instrument-tier-floors, but the floors are at qualitatively different institutional levels. The UK constitutional-architecture is qualitatively different from the EU institutional-architecture (UK Parliament is sovereign with Government drawn from Parliament; EU has separation between EP, Council, Commission), which makes the UK P28 test structurally a *harder* test than the EP case because UK Government MPs sit on FAC and same-party-chair conditions are routine. The UK substrate's positive P28 result *despite* this harder test makes the predicate more robust than the EP-only evidence would have suggested.

### §4.2 — Substrate-discriminating Tier-3-floor refinement

The Day-14 finding refines P28's original formulation. P28 in Day-12 was stated as:

> P28(c): A doctrinal-narrowness Tier-3 framing instance is admissible in the substrate's parliamentary or institutional record without producing a Government crisis (institutional dissolution / minister resignation / PRC-induced diplomatic incident / reversal of the framing instance by the institution that issued it).

The refinement following Day-14:

> P28(c) [refined]: A doctrinal-narrowness Tier-3 framing instance is admissible at *some* parliamentary or institutional instrument-tier of the substrate without producing a Government crisis. The instrument-tier at which Tier-3 framing is admitted is itself a substrate-property and varies across substrates. The instrument-tier ceiling is set by political-accountability-cost, which is monotone in (commitment-weight × reversibility-cost). Tier-3 framing surfaces at instrument-tiers below the substrate-specific cost-product threshold; restatement-without-crisis happens at all timescales relevant to that substrate's accountability architecture.

This refinement is **load-bearing for GWW3 framing**:

- The original P28(c) was binary-substrate-test (does the substrate admit Tier-3 framing?).
- The refined P28(c) is instrument-tier-discriminating (at what cost-product threshold does the substrate admit Tier-3 framing?).
- The discriminator is itself a substrate-property: each substrate has its own (commitment-weight × reversibility-cost) function, and the threshold encodes the substrate's particular accountability architecture.

For GWW3 stochastic-logic encoding, this means P28(c) is not a single predicate but a **family of predicates** indexed by instrument-tier; the substrate-as-substrate evaluates `∃ instrument-tier t: P28(c, t)` — at least one tier admits Tier-3 framing — and the *value* of t is itself an observable.
