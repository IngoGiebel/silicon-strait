## Executive summary

Both sides' currently-published doctrines on cross-strait political integration are *narrowing*, not negotiation-ready — and the narrowing is **asymmetric**. The PRC side is doing the load-bearing doctrinal contraction (Xi 2019 omits the KMT's *一中各表 / "respective interpretations"* carveout from the 1992 Consensus [Source 2]; the 2022 SCIO/TAO white paper silently drops the 1993 white paper's explicit promise that the PRC "will not send troops or administrative personnel to be based in Taiwan" [Source 3]). The ROC side under Lai is responding rather than initiating: Lai 2024 introduces no new sovereignty claims, only refuses subordination ("the Republic of China and the People's Republic of China are not subordinate to one another") [Source 1]. Against this contracting doctrinal frame, the empirical 1C2S precedent — Hong Kong 1984–2024 — was first substantively walked back at year 23 of the 50-year clock with the 2020 NSL, then consolidated by electoral reform in year 24 (2021) and Article 23 in year 27 (2024), with 23 years still remaining before 2047 [Source 7, 9, 10], and the ROC public-opinion mandate against 1C2S is steady-state at ≈84 % rejection on a methodologically conventional MAC quarterly poll (n=1,099, ±2.96 % at 95 % CI) [Source 4, 5]. The Day-6 GWW3 implication is structural: the bargaining range for cross-strait political integration is not just narrow — under both sides' currently-published positions, it is **at risk of being empty**, with the residual disagreement carried entirely by force-reservation language and by the open question of who bears the cost of converting that range from empty back to non-empty.

## Today's thesis

**Asymmetric doctrinal narrowing produces an empty bargaining range under current published positions.** Three primary sources, three doctrinal positions, one shared structural finding:

1. **Lai 2024** [Source 1] — DPP-doctrine consolidation: explicit non-subordination claim; refusal to engage 1992 Consensus as either object or instrument; democratic-pluralist framing across naming options ("ROC / ROC Taiwan / Taiwan").
2. **Xi 2019** [Source 2] — PRC-doctrine consolidation: 1992 Consensus repurposed as the PRC-side singular reading without "respective interpretations"; force reserved against external interference and "very small number of 'Taiwan independence' separatists."
3. **TAO 2022** [Source 3] — PRC institutional codification: 1C2S guarantees softened linguistically ("may continue", "subordinate to One Country") and silently drops the 1993-white-paper troop/administrative-personnel non-stationing reassurance.

**The contraction is not symmetric.** The PRC side initiates the doctrinal narrowing (Xi 2019 omission of *一中各表*, TAO 2022 omission of troop-non-stationing); the ROC side under Lai responds with a defensive moderation that the PRC nonetheless reads as separatism. The ROC contribution to the contraction is its refusal to accept 1992-Consensus framing as a negotiation precondition — but that refusal is *reactive* to a PRC-side formula whose meaning the PRC itself unilaterally narrowed.

**The empirical record undermines the assurance language.** Whatever guarantees the TAO 2022 white paper extends to a hypothetical post-reunification Taiwan ("high degree of autonomy in accordance with the law"), the only live test case of analogous guarantees — Hong Kong under the 1984 Sino-British Joint Declaration — has been re-litigated 23 years into a 50-year horizon. The UK has formally declared China to be in a "state of ongoing non-compliance" since 2020 [Source 7, 8]. The median Taiwanese voter is not opposing a hypothetical formula; they are opposing a formula whose most prominent live test has been retracted by the offering party at the halfway mark.

**The bargain that no longer exists.** Even a hypothetical KMT government revival of the 1992 Consensus cannot deliver the same domestic political product it delivered in 2008–2016. The PRC's 2019 doctrine [Source 2] has locked the formula to a singular reading; the KMT's *一中各表* carveout is no longer accommodated by the counterparty. A KMT pragmatist who runs on "we'll restore the Consensus" is now offering Taiwan an asymmetric deal — the Consensus on the terms the PRC has unilaterally refined since 2019 — rather than the 2008-vintage symmetric ambiguity. Restoration on PRC terms is not restoration; it is acceptance of unilaterally-narrowed terms under a recycled label.

**Implication for the GWW3 model.** Treating the cross-strait political-integration scenario set as having a non-trivial "negotiated peaceful unification" branch is mis-specified at the doctrinal layer. The scenario set under current published positions is bounded by three corner cases:

- **PRC-side doctrinal expansion** (re-introducing *一中各表* accommodation, re-introducing troop-non-stationing language) — historically possible (the formulas existed pre-2019), politically unlikely under Xi-era doctrine consolidation.
- **ROC-side acceptance of unilaterally-narrowed PRC formula** — politically blocked by ≈84 % public rejection of 1C2S [Source 4, 5] and by the empirical HK precedent [Source 7, 8, 9, 10].
- **Forcible conversion of the range** — the residual mechanism reserved by both sides' use-of-force language [Source 2, 3], with all the strategic-stability implications of a PRC strategic doctrine that has narrowed both its peace-formula assurances and retained its force-reservation in the same period.

## Research findings

### Thread 1 — Doctrinal narrowing in three primary documents

**Lai Ching-te, Inaugural Address, 2024-05-20 [Source 1].** Three load-bearing formulations:

> "The Republic of China and the People's Republic of China are not subordinate to one another." (Section VI)

> "Our government will uphold the Four Commitments, neither yield nor provoke, and maintain the status quo." (Section III)

> "Some call this land the Republic of China, some call it the Republic of China Taiwan, and some, Taiwan; but whichever of these names we ourselves or our international friends choose to call our nation, we will resonate and shine all the same." (Section VI)

**Notable absence**: the 1992 Consensus is not mentioned. Lai inherits the Tsai-era positioning of refusing to ratify the Consensus as a precondition for cross-strait dialogue and goes one step further by also declining to negotiate against it as an explicit object. The "not subordinate" formulation explicitly rejects PRC-side "internal affair" framing; the three-name-equivalence is a deliberate refusal to litigate naming politics. This is reactive moderation, not initiating narrowing — but it is non-negotiable as long as it is constitutionally consistent with the ROC's own legal order.

**Xi Jinping, 40th anniversary speech, 2019-01-02 [Source 2].** The Xinhua translation includes:

> "We solemnly propose that political parties and all sectors on both sides of the Strait may recommend representatives to conduct extensive and in-depth democratic consultation on cross-Strait relations and the future of the nation, and establish institutional arrangement for peaceful development of cross-Strait relations, **on the basis of the common political foundation of upholding the 1992 Consensus and opposing 'Taiwan independence.'**" (emphasis added)

> "We make no promise to renounce the use of force and reserve the option of taking all necessary means."

**Critical structural finding**: Xi's 2019 speech does *not* include the formula *"different interpretations" / 一中各表 / 各自表述* that the KMT historically attached to its endorsement of the 1992 Consensus. The Xinhua highlights frame the Consensus as a singular "common political foundation" centered on the one-China principle plus rejection of independence — without the parallel KMT carveout. This is the authoritative primary-source confirmation of the doctrinal narrowing: even *if* a future KMT government revives the Consensus, the PRC's 2019-locked reading no longer leaves room for the asymmetric reading the KMT used to defend domestically.

**SCIO/TAO white paper** *The Taiwan Question and China's Reunification in the New Era* **(2022-08-10) [Source 3].** Three load-bearing passages:

> "We maintain that after peaceful reunification, Taiwan **may continue** its current social system and enjoy a high degree of autonomy in accordance with the law." (emphasis added)

> "**One Country is the precondition and foundation of Two Systems; Two Systems is subordinate to and derives from One Country.**"

> "We will work with the greatest sincerity and exert our utmost efforts to achieve peaceful reunification. But we will not renounce the use of force, and we reserve the option of taking all necessary measures."

**Critical structural finding — the omission**: the 2022 white paper does *not* contain the 1993 white paper's explicit promise that the PRC "will not send troops or administrative personnel to be based in Taiwan." This silent removal is the most load-bearing data point for the Day-6 1C2S-credibility-erosion argument: the PRC's own doctrine has retracted the strongest reassurance it once offered, in the same period where the Hong Kong precedent (NSL 2020, LegCo overhaul 2021) demonstrated walk-back of 1C2S guarantees in practice. The 2022 paper's "high degree of autonomy" phrasing is now operating in a hierarchical-not-parallel reading explicitly anchored in "Two Systems is subordinate to and derives from One Country" — a rhetorical narrowing distinct from the 1993 paper's framing.

**The cross-positional pattern**: all three documents represent narrowing positions. The narrowing is asymmetric — the PRC side carries the load-bearing contractions (Xi 2019 omission of *一中各表*, TAO 2022 omission of troop-non-stationing); the ROC side under Lai is responding rather than initiating.

### Thread 2 — ROC public opinion as the binding constraint

**Mainland Affairs Council quarterly survey, April 2025 [Source 4, 5, 6].** Three load-bearing numbers:

- **84.4 %** of respondents oppose Beijing's "one country, two systems" formula for cross-strait relations [Source 5]. The April 2025 MAC release [Source 4] is the directly-cited primary; a subsequent August 2025 MAC quarterly is reported in research notes to put rejection at 83.7 %, but the August release URL was not directly verified at publication time [UNVERIFIED pending direct August MAC URL]. The April 84.4 % is therefore the load-bearing figure; the August 83.7 % is offered only as corroboration that the figure does not appear to be a one-quarter outlier.
- **>85 %** support some form of "status quo": 36 % for keeping the status quo permanently, 25.9 % for deciding Taiwan's future at a later time, 19.9 % for status quo now but ultimately favoring independence. The three sub-categories sum to **81.8 %** by themselves — i.e. ROC public preference is *asymptotically away* from PRC-defined "peaceful reunification," not undecided about it [Source 5].
- **73.7 %** believe Beijing is intensifying its infiltration of Taiwanese society; **70.9 %** support requiring all elected officials, including lawmakers, to obtain government approval before engaging in exchanges with China [Source 5].

**Methodology**: ESC/NCCU telephone survey, n=1,099 valid samples, sampling error ±2.96 % at 95 % confidence level. ESC/NCCU is the standard contractor for ROC government-commissioned cross-strait polling. The methodology is conventional ±2.96 % at 95 % CI on n=1,099 — appropriate for population-level claims [Source 5].

**Day-6 implication**: public opinion is not a swing variable on this question. The ≈84 % rejection of 1C2S is stable across the 2025 quarters and predates 2025 by years. Any GWW3 model that treats ROC acceptance of 1C2S as a non-trivial probability is mis-specified at the population layer. The remaining live political-mechanism question is whether *elite* PRC-leaning factions (KMT pragmatists, business cross-strait interests) can route around the public preference via institutional channels — but a 73.7 %/70.9 % public mandate for *defensive* posture against PRC infiltration substantially constrains the institutional-routing path as well.

This is the empirical context for Lai 2024's "neither yield nor provoke" framing [Source 1]: the ROC public is *supplying* the political mandate for the executive's defensive posture, not being pulled into it by the executive.

### Thread 3 — The Hong Kong arc and what the median ROC voter is reading

**The original promise** [Source 7]:

> "The Sino-British Joint Declaration was a treaty signed in 1984 between the governments of China and the United Kingdom which set the conditions in which Hong Kong was transferred to Chinese control and for the governance of the territory after 1 July 1997. The central government's policies for the territory were to remain unchanged for a period of 50 years after 1997."

The 50-year horizon (1997–2047) is the literal ground of every "fifty years unchanged" framing the PRC offered Taiwan in the 1990s and 2000s when it presented 1C2S as Taiwan-ready. The TAO 2022 white paper [Source 3] continues to describe the formula in similar language — "high degree of autonomy in accordance with the law" — *without* acknowledging that the most prominent test case has been re-litigated 23 years before the 50-year horizon expires.

**The retraction starting at year 23 (NSL 2020), consolidating through year 27 (Article 23, 2024)** [Source 7, 9, 10]:

| Year | Event | What it walked back |
|---|---|---|
| 1984 | Sino-British Joint Declaration signed | Baseline: 50-year status-quo promise; "high degree of autonomy"; "current way of life" |
| 1990 | Basic Law promulgated | Codifies 50-year horizon at Article 5; codifies eventual universal suffrage at Article 45 (timeline left to Beijing) |
| 1997 | Handover | 50-year clock starts |
| 2003 | Article 23 attempt 1 | Public protest; legislation withdrawn |
| 2014 | Umbrella Movement | NPCSC restricted Chief Executive candidate vetting; mass protests over electoral reform |
| 2019 | Anti-extradition protests | Triggering event for Beijing's escalation pattern |
| 2020 | National Security Law (NSL) imposed by NPCSC bypassing LegCo | UK formally declares Joint Declaration breach; 27-country condemnation |
| 2021 | Electoral reform reduces directly elected LegCo seats | "Patriots only" framework; UK declares "ongoing non-compliance" |
| 2024 | Article 23 (Safeguarding National Security Ordinance) passes locally | Domestic security legislation completes the 2020 NSL framework |

**The UK's formal position** [Source 7]:

> "Following China's 2020 imposition of national security legislation on Hong Kong and a 2021 National People's Congress decision to approve a rework of local election laws that reduces the number of regional legislature seats elected by the public, the UK has declared China as being in a 'state of ongoing non-compliance' with the Joint Declaration."

The "ongoing non-compliance" formula is the operative diplomatic-legal phrasing. It is not a single-event treaty-violation claim; it is a *continuing-state* claim — the UK reads each subsequent encroachment (Article 23 in 2024, electoral reform 2021, NSL 2020) as an additive episode of the same breach.

**Day-6 implication**: the 1C2S empirical record has 23 years still to run on the original 50-year horizon (handover 1997-07-01 → end-of-horizon 2047-07-01). The substantive walk-back, however, arrived at year 23 of the elapsed period, with consolidation through year 27 — i.e. roughly the halfway mark of the 50-year clock, not its tail. This is the load-bearing data for ROC public-opinion intransigence on 1C2S: the median Taiwanese voter is not opposing a hypothetical formula. They are opposing a formula whose most prominent live test was substantively retracted by the offering party at the halfway mark. The TAO 2022 white paper's softening of guarantees [Source 3] runs in the same direction (assured guarantees → "may continue", "subordinate to One Country") and supplies institutional confirmation that the walk-back is doctrine, not improvisation.

### Thread 4 — The 1992 Consensus and the bargain that no longer exists

**The term is a 2000-vintage retrofit** [Source 11, 12]:

> "The term '1992 Consensus' was coined in April 2000 by Su Chi, a former National Security Council secretary-general." [Source 12]

> "Su said he made up the term '1992 consensus' as a replacement for the expression 'each side with its own interpretation' in order to benefit cross-strait development." [Source 11, paraphrasing Su's 2006 admission]

The doctrinally important point is not whether the 1992 Koo-Wang correspondence happened (it did) or whether some understanding was reached (something was) — it is that **the label "1992 Consensus" is a 2000-vintage retrofit by an ROC official trying to lock in continuity *across* the KMT→DPP transition, not a 1992-vintage agreed-to-by-both-sides treaty term**. The CCP has used the label since but never coined it.

**The KMT version with 一中各表** [Source 12]:

> "The KMT understanding of the consensus is 'one China, different interpretations' (一中各表, 一個中國各自表述), that the ROC and PRC 'agree' that there is One China, but disagree about what that means."

The KMT carveout is the entire reason the formula was domestically defensible in Taiwan from 2000 onwards: it allowed KMT politicians to maintain that *their* "one China" was the Republic of China (per the ROC Constitution which still claims the territory of mainland China), while permitting cross-strait interlocutors to read PRC into "one China." This is not formal duplicity — it is constitutional pragmatism trading interpretive ambiguity for negotiation access.

**The PRC has never accepted the "respective interpretations" half** [Source 12]:

> "The Chinese Communist Party, although acknowledging the existence of the consensus, does not formally acknowledge the term 'with differing interpretations,' repeatedly omitting the phrase in official documents."

This is independently confirmed by the Xi 2019 primary [Source 2]: the speech presents the Consensus as "the common political foundation of upholding the 1992 Consensus and opposing 'Taiwan independence'" — without *一中各表*, without "respective interpretations," without any KMT-side carveout. The omission is documented, not interpretive.

**Day-6 implication**: even a hypothetical KMT government revival of the 1992 Consensus cannot deliver the same domestic political product it delivered in 2008–2016. The PRC's 2019 doctrine has locked the formula to a singular reading; the KMT's *一中各表* carveout is no longer accommodated by the counterparty. A KMT pragmatist who runs on "we'll restore the Consensus" is now offering Taiwan an asymmetric deal: restoration of the Consensus on the terms the PRC has unilaterally refined since 2019 [Source 13]. The post-2019 1992-Consensus revival, on the PRC's published terms, is not the 2008 1992-Consensus revival.

## Game-theoretic framing (GWW3)

Day-6 motivates a specific structural extension to the GWW3 modeling layer for cross-strait political integration.

**Bargaining range as a set, not a parameter.** Standard bargaining-range models (Fearon 1995 and successors) treat the range as a continuous interval `[BATNA_PRC, BATNA_ROC]` with both sides having a most-preferred outcome and a minimum acceptable. The Day-6 finding suggests that under both sides' currently *published* doctrinal positions, this interval is at risk of being **empty** — `BATNA_PRC > BATNA_ROC` in formula-space, because the PRC's published minimum (1C2S without *一中各表*, with eroded autonomy guarantees, with retained force-reservation) is below the ROC public's published maximum acceptable (≈84 % rejecting 1C2S in any form).

**Doctrinal contraction as a stochastic process.** The 2019 → 2022 PRC narrowing (Xi 2019 omits *一中各表*; TAO 2022 omits 1993 troop-non-stationing) is a sequence of one-sided revisions to the PRC's published position. Modeled as a sequence: at each fire of doctrinal restatement, the PRC has the option to reaffirm prior framing, narrow it, or expand it. The empirical pattern 2019–2022 is two consecutive narrowings without compensating expansions. A GWW3 stochastic-logic encoding should treat each new doctrinal restatement as a Bayesian update on the trajectory, weighted by recency.

**Asymmetry as a load-bearing model property.** Symmetric models (both sides equally narrowing) miss the actual structure: the PRC side initiates, the ROC side responds. This matters for the model's prediction layer because the *responsive* side under Lai 2024 [Source 1] cannot expand the range without abandoning the constitutional-democratic frame that mandates the response in the first place. The asymmetry locks in a path-dependent contraction.

**Empty-range conversion mechanisms.** A GWW3 simulation that finds an empty bargaining range under current published positions must specify which exogenous mechanism could convert empty → non-empty. The candidates:

1. **PRC-side doctrinal expansion** — re-introducing *一中各表* accommodation, re-introducing troop-non-stationing language. Historically possible (the formulas existed pre-2019). Politically unlikely under Xi-era doctrine consolidation. Estimable counter-evidence: a future PRC restatement that explicitly re-pairs the Consensus with "respective interpretations" or re-introduces troop-non-stationing language would falsify the locked-narrowing thesis.
2. **ROC-side acceptance of unilaterally-narrowed PRC formula** — politically blocked by the empirical layer (HK 1997–2024) and by the public-opinion layer (≈84 % rejection of 1C2S, ≥85 % status-quo preference). Estimable counter-evidence: a single MAC quarterly poll showing 1C2S support above ~25 % would substantially change the model.
3. **Exogenous shock that re-prices both sides' BATNA** — a major shift in the US security guarantee, a major shift in PRC economic capacity, a major shift in ROC's regional security architecture (Japan, Philippines, AUKUS-equivalent inclusion). Each of these is its own research thread.
4. **Forcible conversion** — the mechanism explicitly reserved by both sides' use-of-force language [Source 2, 3]. The Day-6 thesis does not predict this, but it does observe that as the published-position bargaining range contracts toward empty, the *relative* weight of the force-reservation mechanism in both sides' doctrine increases mechanically. This is the strategic-stability hazard the Day-6 publication wants to put on the record.

**Coupling to Day-5's coercion-toolkit finding.** Day 5 argued that the PRC coercion toolkit is systemic policy, not ad-hoc retaliation, and that the toolkit category is converging across major-power statecraft as a shared institutional language. Day 6 adds: when the published-position bargaining range contracts toward empty, coercion is the substitute the doctrinal contraction generates. It is not coincidence that the same period (2019–2026) that saw the PRC's doctrinal narrowing (Xi 2019, TAO 2022) also saw the maturation of the PRC counter-coercion legal architecture (Unreliable Entity List 2019, *反外国制裁法* 2021, MOFCOM Notice #21 of 2026). The two trajectories are mutually reinforcing.

## Hassaleh-Nexus implications

Day-6's empty-range thesis predicts a specific shape of financial-market sensitivity to cross-strait political-doctrine restatements. The signal classes Hassaleh-Nexus should monitor:

- **TWD volatility around PRC doctrinal restatements**, not just around military exercises. The market currently prices Taiwan-strait risk primarily on observable kinetic events (PLA exercises, US carrier movements, ADIZ incursions). Day-6 implies that *doctrinal* events (a new PRC white paper, a Politburo Standing Committee statement, a TAO press conference) carry information about the bargaining range that should price in independently of kinetic events. Hassaleh-Nexus should add a doctrinal-event tape and back-test TWD response sensitivity to it across 2019–2026.
- **TSMC and Taiwan-AI-supply-chain spreads as a sentiment proxy for "convertibility-of-empty-range."** The spread between TSMC ADR (US-listed) and TSMC Taiwan-listed equities, and between Taiwan-listed semis and AAPL/NVDA composite, encodes a market-implied probability that the range stays empty without forcible conversion. A widening spread on doctrinal-narrowing news without kinetic events would indicate market re-pricing of the doctrinal-narrowing channel.
- **KMT election prospects as a tradeable signal that changes meaning post-Day-6.** Pre-2019, a KMT polling rise could be priced as cross-strait de-escalation (1992-Consensus revival). Post-2019, per the Day-6 finding, a KMT polling rise is no longer unambiguously de-escalatory because the PRC-side formula the KMT would revive is no longer the formula it accommodated in 2008–2016. Hassaleh-Nexus should re-specify any KMT-polling signal as conditioned on PRC doctrinal restatement state since the last KMT government, not as unconditional.
- **HK-CNY / HK political-risk premium as a leading indicator for ROC public-opinion stability on 1C2S.** Day-6 argues the empirical HK precedent is what makes the ROC public-opinion baseline non-swing. A change in the HK political-risk premium (e.g. unexpected PRC concession to UK on Joint Declaration compliance, or unexpected escalation under Article 23) is a leading indicator for whether the ROC baseline can shift. The signal direction is asymmetric: HK *escalation* hardens the ROC baseline (already at ≈84 % rejection); HK *de-escalation* of any plausible magnitude is unlikely to soften it because the precedent damage is durable.

The unifying observation: Hassaleh-Nexus needs a *doctrinal-state* node in the cross-strait subgraph, distinct from the *military-state* node and the *economic-coupling* node. The Day-5 coercion-toolkit publication motivated the economic-coupling node; Day-6 motivates the doctrinal-state node; Day-7 (us_strategic_options) will motivate the security-guarantee node.

## Open questions for tomorrow (Day-7 us_strategic_options)

1. **The strategic-ambiguity dial under empty-range conditions.** US strategic ambiguity on Taiwan defense was designed for a non-empty bargaining range. What does it become structurally when the published-position range is empty? Hypothesis to test: ambiguity becomes *meta*-ambiguity (ambiguous about ambiguity), and the dial loses calibration as a deterrence instrument.
2. **Bloc-backstop formation in the Asia-Pacific.** Day-5 found bloc-backstop presence load-bearing for target-state coercion-resistance. Day-6's empty-range finding raises the question of whether the absence of an explicit Asia-Pacific bloc-backstop for Taiwan (no NATO-equivalent, AUKUS scope-limited, Quad informal) is a binding constraint on US strategic options or a deliberate feature of the architecture.
3. **Japan and the Senkaku linkage.** The Senkaku/Diaoyu dispute is the only other live PRC territorial-revisionist case with a US treaty-ally counterparty. How tightly does the Japan layer couple to the Taiwan layer in PRC strategic doctrine, and at what point does coupling become explicit (joint exercise, joint declaration, joint planning)?
4. **The 2027 PLA modernization milestone vs. the 2049 great rejuvenation horizon.** Both dates appear in PRC strategic doctrine but at different scales. Day-7 should disentangle which date is operational (capabilities milestone) and which is rhetorical (national-rejuvenation horizon), because the GWW3 model treats them very differently.
