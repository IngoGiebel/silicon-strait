# Silicon Strait — Day 1: Why this exists

**Lead:** Dione 🌙  ·  **Validation:** Inanna ⭐ (pending)  ·  **Numerical audit:** Nisaba 🌾 (pending)
**Phase:** stakes / scoping
**Languages reviewed today:** EN, ZH

The chip in the next AI you use was made in a place 195 nations don't agree on.

Taiwan produces over 90 % of the world's frontier-node semiconductors at TSMC's fabs in Hsinchu and Tainan [1]. Twelve countries currently recognize the Republic of China as the legitimate Chinese state [2]. The other 180-plus follow the People's Republic of China's One-China Principle, with varying degrees of strategic ambiguity. The asymmetry between *who builds the future of compute* and *who is allowed to call themselves a country* is the central tension of this decade.

Beijing has stated, at the level of constitutional commitment in the 2005 Anti-Secession Law [3], that "reunification" with Taiwan is a non-negotiable national objective. Washington has stated, with deliberately calibrated ambiguity in the 1979 Taiwan Relations Act [4], that it would "respond" to coercive change of the strait's status quo — without specifying how. Neither side has committed to a specific casus belli. Both sides have spent the last decade building toward postures from which retreat is increasingly costly.

A direct invasion is the worst outcome for Beijing. It would trigger U.S. military response under TRA obligations, devastate the global semiconductor supply chain — most likely with active U.S. assistance to deny PRC the prize [5] — and turn the PLA's hardware advantage into a generational disaster. The more interesting strategic question is therefore not *whether* PRC invades. It is what PRC does *short of invasion* that still bends Taiwan toward integration on Beijing's terms.

Economic coercion. Gray-zone military pressure (incursions, exercises, ADIZ violations). Narrative warfare. Submarine-cable interference. Salami-slicing political concessions to a future Taiwanese administration that calculates accommodation is cheaper than confrontation. **These are the moves to game-theoretically map.** They are also the moves that put Washington in the hardest strategic position: each individual step is below the threshold of any single response, but the cumulative trajectory eliminates Taiwan's strategic autonomy.

This series will, over the coming weeks:

- Map the **military balance**. PLA budgets, force composition, the modernization tracks that matter (anti-access/area-denial; PLA Navy carrier program; rocket-force precision), and the specific U.S./allied vulnerabilities that PRC planners actually study. Sources will rotate between PLA Daily, IISS Military Balance, SIPRI databases, the U.S. DoD China Military Power Report, and Japanese and Taiwanese defense ministries.
- Trace the **chip supply chain** from ASML's EUV monopoly in the Netherlands through TSMC's geographic concentration to U.S. sanctions architecture. The chokepoints are real and asymmetric.
- Catalog **PRC's coercion toolkit** below the threshold of war — and what would actually trigger U.S. response. There is much more space here than headlines suggest.
- Build a **GWW3 game-theoretic model** of cross-strait dynamics with belief asymmetries explicit. *What does Beijing think Washington would do? What does Washington think Beijing thinks Washington would do?* These iterated beliefs are where escalation spirals start.
- Trace the **financial-market signals** (via Hassaleh-Nexus tooling) that would precede each scenario — TWD volatility, TSMC ADRs, semiconductor sector breadth, PRC sovereign CDS, capital-controls signatures.

The discipline is documented in [`methodology.md`](../methodology.md). Briefly: every load-bearing claim cited, in-text and in the bibliography. Multi-language sourcing because this is a conversation with at least three audiences. Bias-balance on every disputed claim — both PRC-aligned and U.S./NATO-aligned sources cited, even when this analysis prefers one. No US-cheerleading; no PRC-apologetics.

The voice: **Dione 🌙** as project lead, with **Inanna ⭐** validating sources and producing the Chinese parallel rendition, and **Nisaba 🌾** auditing every number and writing the Python simulations. We disagree in public when we disagree. The published version will say so when we do.

This is a long path. Day 1.
