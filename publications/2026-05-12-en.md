## Executive summary

The Lai Ching-te administration enters 2026 with a coherent doctrinal stack — "peace through strength" anchored on the inaugural's "not subordinate to each other" formulation, escalated in the 2026 New Year's Address to "alarming military ambitions," and operationalized in the 2025 National Day Address's pledge of **3% GDP defense spending in 2026, 5% by 2030**, plus the NT$1.25 trillion / 8-year special defense package. The same documentary record repeatedly exposes the **executive-legislative gating mechanism** that defines the ROC player in the four-player game (US-Exec, US-Leg, PRC, ROC): DPP is minority in the Legislative Yuan, so every load-bearing executive package — defense budget, US tariff deal — requires KMT and TPP cooperation. The S1 prose-lock therefore yields three GWW3 ROC-player predicates derivable from Lai's own speech-acts: a deterministic "not subordinate" assertion (since 2024-05-20), a deterministic legislative-constraint gate on defense + tariff packages, and a 2026-onset stochastic predicate for ROC threat-perception of PRC military escalation.

## Today's thesis

**Taiwan's executive coherence and legislative incoherence are simultaneously the strongest and weakest signals in the four-player game.** The Lai administration's strategic posture is unusually well-anchored across three speech-act layers (inaugural → New Year's → National Day) that mutually reinforce: "peace through strength" is not a slogan, it is a documented sequence with budget commitments attached. But the same speech-acts repeatedly plead for opposition-party cooperation on the very budget that gives the doctrine teeth — meaning the doctrine's substrate (legislative ratification) is structurally weaker than the doctrine's surface (presidential rhetoric). For GWW3 modeling, the ROC player is therefore **not a unitary actor**: it is a two-sub-player composition (ROC-Exec, ROC-Leg) with a gating relation between them, analogous to the US-Exec / US-Leg composition unpacked in Day-7 S7. The S2 sub-thread (next research-fire) will document the opposition side of this gate; S3 will probe whether public opinion stabilizes or destabilizes it; S4 will quantify the defense-package terms that have to pass through it; and S6 will encode the gate itself as a GSL predicate in `gww3/predicates.gww3`.

## Research findings

### S1 — Lai Ching-te (William Lai) administration cross-strait positioning **[PROSE-LOCKED]**

#### S1.a — The doctrinal stack: inaugural → New Year's → National Day

The Lai administration's cross-strait positioning is documented across three speech-acts that progressively tighten an initially open framing into an operational defense posture.

The **2024-05-20 inaugural address** [Source #1] established the constitutional sovereignty frame in two sentences that have become standing references for the post-2024 ROC executive position:

> "The sovereignty of the Republic of China shall reside in the whole body of citizens." [Source #1]
>
> "The Republic of China and the People's Republic of China are not subordinate to each other." [Source #1]

The "not subordinate" formulation is not a Lai innovation — it continues Tsai Ing-wen's 2021 National Day phrasing — but its retention at the moment of presidential transition makes it the durable executive-branch standing position rather than a single-president stylistic choice. The inaugural also coded the deterministic argument-from-PRC-revealed-preferences that anchors the deterrence-realist reading of Lai:

> "So long as China refuses to renounce the use of force against Taiwan, all of us in Taiwan ought to understand, that even if we accept the entirety of China's position and give up our sovereignty, China's ambition to annex Taiwan will not simply disappear." [Source #1]

This passage functions as a logical proof, not an emotional appeal: it forecloses the appeasement-equilibrium pathway by showing it does not deliver the security good it would purchase. For GWW3 purposes, it is a **deterministic predicate**, not a stochastic one — Lai is not modeling probability of PRC concession, he is asserting the impossibility of the appeasement payoff. The address packaged the operational response as the **Four Pillars of Peace** (strengthened national defense, improved economic security, stable cross-strait leadership, values-based diplomacy), aggregating under the tag "peace through strength" — a deliberate Reagan-era US-Republican defense slogan whose adoption signals ideational alignment with the post-2024 Trump-2 administration's preferred rhetorical register.

The **2026-01-01 New Year's Address** [Source #2] escalated the PRC threat-coding sharply. Where the inaugural urged Beijing to "renounce the use of force," the New Year's Address moves to:

> "China's rising expansionary ambitions" [Source #2]
>
> "China's alarming military ambitions" [Source #2]
>
> "Facing China's alarming military ambitions, Taiwan has no time to wait and certainly no time to consume ourselves with internal strife." [Source #2]

The rhetorical baseline has moved: Lai is no longer requesting Chinese de-escalation, he is coding it as out-of-reach and framing the response as accelerated defense buildup. The address introduced the **single most-cited 2026 ROC defense number**:

> "eight-year, NT$1.25 trillion special defense budget to comprehensively upgrade our combat capabilities and strengthen our defense industry" [Source #2]

At NT$156.25B/year average and stacked on top of Taiwan's 2025 baseline defense budget of approximately NT$647B (~2.45% GDP), the package — if additive rather than absorbed — pushes Taiwan toward the 3% GDP threshold the Trump-2 administration has publicly demanded. The address also embedded the **structural plea** that recurs across Lai's 2026 speech-acts:

> "I hope the ruling and opposition parties can work together to swiftly pass this crucial national defense budget." [Source #2]

The same address repeats the bipartisanship plea a second time within paragraphs, confirming it is a load-bearing rhetorical move rather than a stylistic flourish. The economic resilience anchors are 2025 actuals: 7.37% growth (15-year high, driven by AI/semiconductor capex), unemployment averaging ~3% (employment best in 25 years), Taiwan Stock Index at new records [Source #2]. These numbers are the foundation of the S5 economic-resilience case to be unpacked in a later research-fire.

The **2025-10-10 National Day Address** [Source #4, via [egress-blocked-summary] — Inanna egress-fallback delivery 2026-05-11] sits chronologically between the inaugural and the New Year's, and announced two operational commitments that the New Year's Address then assumed as background:

- **"T-Dome" (台灣之盾) multi-layered air defense system** acceleration. [Source #4]
- **Defense spending pledge: exceed 3% of GDP in 2026 (NATO threshold), reach 5% by 2030.** [Source #4]

The address's framing tag — "Aggression fails, unity prevails, and peace is won through strength" (侵略必敗，團結必勝，和平必須靠實力) [Source #4] — recycled the inaugural's "peace through strength" anchor in explicit 80th-anniversary-of-WWII framing, calling on PRC to "cease its distortion of UN General Assembly Resolution 2758 and historical WWII documents." [Source #4] The "Taiwan" / "Republic of China" word-count ratio in the address was 51:2 [Source #4], emphasizing Taiwan-subjectivity over ROC-constitutional framing — a measurable rhetorical shift compared to the inaugural's heavier ROC-sovereignty emphasis.

#### S1.b — The Trump-1 continuity signal: EPPD as stability anchor

The **2026-02-03 press conference on the Taiwan-US Economic Prosperity Partnership Dialogue (EPPD)** [Source #3] is the most diagnostic Lai-administration speech-act for the US-coalition-management dimension. Lai opens by anchoring the mechanism's lineage:

> "Last week, the sixth annual Taiwan-US EPPD was held in Washington, DC. This mechanism was established during US President Donald Trump's first term." [Source #3]

This is **deliberate coalition-management signaling**: Lai is citing the EPPD's Trump-1 origin to reduce the discount US Republicans might apply to a DPP-administered Taiwan policy in the Trump-2 era. The mechanism survived Trump-1 → Biden-1 → Trump-2, making it the most stable durable structure in the bilateral economic-security stack — and Lai's citation is the public confirmation that he intends to lean on this stability rather than fight against the Trump-2 partisan signal. The 2026 round's four pillars (strategic alignment on supply chain security, cooperation on critical minerals, cooperation in third countries, bilateral cooperation) [Source #3] introduce **"critical minerals" as a new 2026 pillar** — a substantive expansion beyond the pre-2026 chip-centric framing, almost certainly oriented toward third-country processing partnerships (Australia, Indonesia) since Taiwan has no major critical-minerals reserves of its own. The press conference closes with the second instance of the legislative plea:

> "I sincerely hope that, in their deliberation on the Taiwan-US tariff agreement, members of the Legislative Yuan will uphold professionalism, proceed in accordance with the law." [Source #3]

The plea is structurally identical to the New Year's Address's defense-budget plea: executive negotiates with US, legislature must ratify, opposition controls legislature. This **doubles** the S2 KMT-opposition gating mechanism — the gate operates on the entire US-Taiwan economic-security policy stack (defense + tariff), not on defense alone.

The **MAC (Mainland Affairs Council) quarterly briefings under spokesperson Liang Wen-chieh** [Source #5, via [egress-blocked-summary] — Inanna egress-fallback delivery 2026-05-11] code the executive-branch's cross-strait operational posture in three diagnostic moments:

- **2025-10-02 briefing:** MAC announced the early lifting of travel bans on overseas pro-democracy influencers (Leonard, Sydney Daddy, Wen Zhao), framing them as possessing "special skills and experience that assist Taiwan in countering CCP cognitive warfare." [Source #5] This is a deliberate cognitive-warfare counter-posture: rather than passive defense, MAC is actively repatriating diaspora influence-operators into the domestic information environment.
- **2026-01-08 first briefing of 2026:** MAC assessed PRC pressure across three vectors — narrative control via the "three 80th anniversaries," intensified "punishment of Taiwan independence" (bounty issuances), and continued military intimidation / gray-zone operations. Issued **early warning regarding potential CCP interference in the November 2026 local elections.** [Source #5]
- **2026-05-07 briefing:** MAC drew a sharp distinction between "not supporting Taiwan independence" and "anti-Taiwan independence," explicitly warning that conflating the two — as the KMT Chair had recently done in US remarks — sends the wrong signal to the international community. [Source #5] This is the most direct documentary evidence of the S1↔S2 boundary: MAC (executive) calling out KMT (opposition) by name on PRC-positioning rhetoric, in a public briefing.

### S2 — KMT opposition stance **[PROSE-LOCKED]**

The KMT under Chairwoman Cheng Li-wun (鄭麗文) is not a single point in cross-strait posture space. It is a **two-axis composite**: Axis A — cross-strait posture (PRC-facing) — is doctrinally aligned with the "1992 consensus" and now institutionally re-anchored to Beijing through the Feb-2026 KMT-CCP think-tank forum. Axis B — special-defense-budget posture (US-facing) — is fractured along the Huang Fuhsing veteran wing vs. Speaker Han Kuo-yu's faction. The two axes are not collapsible: reading the KMT as "pro-PRC" or "pro-US" both fail. The S2 prose-lock unpacks both axes, the documentary record that distinguishes them, and the ROC executive's institutional rebuttal.

**Correction to S1 §c.** The 11:00 S1 prose-lock characterised MAC's 2026-05-07 briefing as a rebuke of "the KMT Chair's anti-Taiwan-independence position in US remarks." Direct-pull primaries in this fire correct three elements: the institutional event was the **Feb 3, 2026** KMT-CCP think-tank forum in **Beijing** (not a US-side appearance); the KMT speaker on record was **Vice-Chair Hsiao Hsu-tsen (蕭旭岑)**, not the Chair (Cheng Li-wun); and the document under MAC rebuke was the **KMT-CCP Joint Declarations** issued at the forum, not solo remarks. S1's prose stays as the record-of-process; the corrected institutional anchor lives here.

#### S2.a — Axis A: Doctrinal alignment at the Feb-2026 Beijing forum

The Feb 3, 2026 forum at the China World Hotel in Beijing was co-hosted by the Research Center for Cross-Strait Relations of the CPC Central Committee Taiwan Work Office and a KMT-affiliated policy foundation. PRC-side reporting frames it as **"the resumption of the interparty exchange mechanism after nearly a decade"** [Source #7]. The institutional resumption matters as much as the content: this is the doctrinal channel re-opened, not an episodic coalition-management move.

Song Tao (CPC Taiwan Work Office Director) anchored the forum on **five pillars**: upholding the "1992 consensus"; deepening integrated development; people-to-people and familial bonds; opposing Taiwanese independence and safeguarding peace in the Strait; supporting the joint cause of "national rejuvenation" [Source #6, #7]. The framing is paradigmatic PRC cross-strait doctrine.

Hsiao Hsu-tsen, operating as institutional voice of the Chair, delivered the KMT's reciprocal load-bearing language [Source #6]:

> "The two sides of the Taiwan Strait should cooperate rather than confronting each other and allowing other countries to exploit Taiwan."
> Cross-strait relations should "ensure people's well-being, uphold the '1992 consensus,' oppose Taiwanese independence, create mutually beneficial outcomes and pursue peaceful development and 'harmonious integration,' working hand in hand to rejuvenate the Chinese nation."

Hsiao explicitly anchored Cheng's institutional buy-in: "KMT Chairwoman Cheng Li-wun cares about the forum and hopes to address urgent issues affecting Taiwan's industrial development" [Source #6]. The Chair's authority lives behind the Vice-Chair's quotes. The Xi-Cheng exchange of October 2025 — Xi congratulating Cheng on her election as KMT chairwoman [Source #7] — is the predecessor signal that the Feb-2026 forum operationalises; the sequence Oct-2025 message → Feb-2026 forum → Joint Declarations is institutionally coherent, not episodic.

**Bias-balance on "1992 consensus" provenance.** The same Taipei Times article carrying the verbatim Hsiao/Song quotes carries an editorial provenance flag: former MAC chairman Su Chi (蘇起) admitted in 2006 to coining the term "to break a cross-strait deadlock and alleviate tension" [Source #6]. The PRC-aligned source [Source #7] presents the same term as established political foundation without provenance flagging. Same term, different epistemic status — an asymmetry the GWW3 predicate set must encode, not flatten.

#### S2.b — ROC executive response: MAC's institutional rebuttal

The Mainland Affairs Council issued a press release directly answering the Feb 3 Joint Declarations under the verbatim title "The CCP's Extreme Pressure Against Taiwan Is the Greatest Obstacle to Cross-Strait People-to-People Exchanges; the KMT-CCP 'Joint Declarations' Misattribute Responsibility for the Suspension of Exchanges" [Source #8]. Verbatim body recovered via Inanna egress-fallback (`mac.gov.tw/en` was host-blocked from the runtime gateway, the same signature seen on Day-8 S1):

> "The Kuomintang (KMT) … has turned a blind eye to the CCP's intent to eliminate the Republic of China and annex Taiwan, and has insisted on co-hosting a 'KMT-CCP Think Tank Forum' with the CCP." [Source #8]

> "The two sides further released 'Joint Declarations' under the political premise of the '1992 Consensus' and opposition to 'Taiwan independence,' falsely claiming that they would promote the lifting of restrictions imposed by Taiwan's government on cross-Strait people-to-people exchanges. This narrative completely misattributes responsibility for the suspension of exchanges and is categorically rejected by the government." [Source #8]

> "The MAC emphasized that the KMT-CCP 'Joint Declarations' align with the CCP's policies of integrating Taiwan into its system and conducting united front operations, and represent a reversion to the old approach of westward orientation and reliance on the CCP." [Source #8]

MAC's framing — "intent to eliminate the ROC and annex Taiwan," "united front operations," "reversion to the old approach of westward orientation" — characterises the KMT-CCP institutional channel as a national-security issue, not an electoral one. S1 §c was therefore directionally right (MAC's executive criticism of KMT does exist); the corrective bridge is that the criticism is anchored in this institutional rebuttal, not in remarks delivered in the US.

#### S2.c — Axis B: Defense-budget posture and the inner-KMT fracture

The KMT's doctrinal unity on Axis A does not extend to Axis B. The Lai administration's NT$1.25 trillion / 8-year special defense package (S1 [Source #4]) is gated by Legislative Yuan ratification, which the KMT caucus controls. Three competing positions are documented [Source #9]:

- **Cheng Li-wun (Chair) + Fu Kun-chi (caucus whip) + Chi Lin-lien (Deputy Chair, Huang Fuhsing veteran wing):** NT$380 billion plus N, with N reserved for funds allocated after receipt of US arms-sale letters of acceptance. Chi calls the official caucus plan "completely reasonable."
- **Speaker Han Kuo-yu:** reportedly supports the NT$800 billion plan authored by KMT Legislator Hsu Chiao-hsin.
- **Hsu Chiao-hsin (KMT Legislator):** NT$800 billion competing plan; self-positions as "never do anything to pain our friends and please our enemies" and would "always abide by the legislative caucus' resolutions."

The factional dispute became institutionally visible on 2026-04-30 when Deputy Chair Chi Lin-lien threatened the Huang Fuhsing veteran wing "would stand up, even at the cost of family ties, and urge brother Kuo-yu be expelled from the party" if Han backed the NT$800B plan [Source #9]. Cheng de-escalated, calling Chi's words "a bit harsh" while reaffirming the NT$380B+N plan as "the most responsible approach to complete US arms sales, protect Taiwan, safeguard people's wallets and uphold fiscal discipline" [Source #9]. Han was scheduled to lead the cross-party negotiation on **2026-05-06**; the outcome of that negotiation is the next research target — S2 prose-lock stops at the documented split, since the negotiation outcome may reset (or further entrench) the factional weights before the LY ratification vote.

The Axis-A / Axis-B asymmetry is the operating insight of S2: **the KMT is unified at the doctrinal level (PRC-facing) and fractured at the procurement level (US-facing).** The two axes are not collapsible into a single posture.

### S3 — Taiwan public opinion **[PROSE-LOCKED]**

The S1 hypothesis was that Lai's "no time for internal strife" framing implies public opinion **backs him on PRC threat-perception but fractures on partisan execution** — high threat-perception agreement, high partisan-execution disagreement on the same survey wave. The polling record from NCCU ESC (the gold-standard time-series), TPOF (the monthly tracker), and Pew (EN-language cross-validation) confirms the hypothesis in a stronger form than S1 anticipated: identity and unification preferences are not merely stable, they are **structurally locked** within a narrow drift band, while executive approval has been the most volatile variable in 2025. The ROC-Exec sub-player operates on a foundation that is *broadly* mandated and *thinly* approved — a configuration that compresses the executive's effective authority window even when its doctrinal positioning has majority public support.

#### S3.a — Identity stability: NCCU ESC, TPOF, Pew triangulation

The NCCU Election Study Center identity time-series — methodologically the most rigorous of the three — shows a flat post-2022 plateau [Source #10]:

| Year | Taiwanese only (%) | Both (%) | Chinese only (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2022 | 63.3 | 30.6 | 2.8 |
| 2023 | 61.7 | 32.0 | 2.4 |
| 2024 | 63.4 | 31.0 | 2.4 |
| 2025 | 62.0 | 31.7 | 2.5 |

The "Taiwanese only" share oscillates within ±0.85 pp of a 62.6% mean; the "Both" share within ±0.7 pp of 31.3%; the "Chinese only" share within ±0.2 pp of 2.5%. Reading this as a noisy stationary process — not as a trend with signal — is the disciplined conclusion. **The pivot to a Taiwanese-majority identity was completed before this window opened.**

TPOF's parallel series reads higher in absolute terms: 76.1% Taiwanese / 9.0% Both / 10.1% Chinese in Jan-2025, 77.4% / 10.4% / 6.4% in Jul-2025 [Source #11]. Pew (Jun–Sept 2023 fielding, released Jan-2024) reads in between: 67% Taiwanese / 28% Both / 3% Chinese [Source #12]. The ~14 pp Taiwanese-only gap between NCCU/Pew (62–67%) and TPOF (76–77%) is the largest visible cross-source disagreement and is best read as a **methodology artifact rather than a measurement disagreement**: TPOF's questionnaire likely pushes "Both" respondents into the "Taiwanese" column via forced-choice phrasing or sequencing, while NCCU/Pew preserve the "Both" middle option more cleanly. The load-bearing signal is the *drift rate*, which is essentially zero across all three sources — the identity floor is locked regardless of which absolute baseline a researcher prefers.

The unification-preference time-series anchors the second deterministic finding [Source #10]:

| Year | Indep. ASAP | Status-quo → Indep. | Status-quo → Decide later | Status-quo → Indefinitely | Status-quo → Unif. | Unif. ASAP |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2022 | 6.0% | 25.1% | 28.4% | 27.3% | 5.2% | 1.4% |
| 2025 | 6.6% | 21.9% | 26.3% | 33.5% | 4.4% | 1.1% |

"Unification ASAP" never breaches 1.5% in the four-year window. Even the "status-quo → eventually unification" pathway floats at 4.4–5.2% — well below the "status-quo → eventually independence" pathway (21.9–25.1%) and an order of magnitude below the modal "status-quo indefinitely / decide later" cluster (~60% combined). The 2025 print shows an uptick in the indefinite-status-quo column (27.3% → 33.5%) at the expense of the "eventually independence" column — directionally consistent with a Taiwan-public hedging against rather than toward kinetic escalation, not with any softening of unification-floor preference. **The public is not the constraint on the executive's "not subordinate" assertion; the public is its substrate.**

#### S3.b — Executive approval volatility: TPOF Lai-approval as the high-noise variable

Against the flat identity baseline, TPOF's Lai-approval time-series is the highest-variance Taiwan-domestic signal in 2025 [Source #11]:

| Wave | Approve (%) | Disapprove (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| May 2024 (inaugural month) | 58.0 | 25.5 |
| Nov 2024 | 43.0 | 42.8 |
| Jan 2025 | 48.6 | 37.4 |
| May 2025 | 45.7 | 45.7 |
| Aug 2025 | 54.4 | 33.3 |
| Oct 2025 | **34.9** | **52.5** |
| Nov 2025 | 37.9 | 50.2 |
| Dec 2025 | 43.4 | 48.6 |

The trough — 34.9% approve / 52.5% disapprove in Oct-2025 — is structurally important. It is the wave immediately preceding the 2025-10-10 National Day Address [Source #4], and it is followed by the largest single-wave recovery in the series (Oct → Dec: +8.5 pp approval, −3.9 pp disapproval). The address's "T-Dome" and 3% / 5% GDP-of-defense pledges land into the most fragile executive-approval moment of the term, and the immediate-post-address rebound is consistent with the doctrinal moves having real political-substrate effect — though confounders (Trump-2 administration messaging, KMT factional events) cannot be cleanly excluded with the published series alone.

The ratio of identity-baseline volatility to approval-baseline volatility is the operating metric: NCCU "Taiwanese only" σ ≈ 0.85 pp over four years against TPOF Lai-approval σ ≈ 7.4 pp over eight 2024–2025 waves. The executive operates on a foundation that is **roughly 9× more volatile in mandate than in identity**.

#### S3.c — Threat-perception anchor and the US-reliability fracture

The most directly load-bearing S3 findings for the four-player frame come from TPOF's Oct-2025 attribution and US-reliability questions, recovered as discrete data points where the full 12-month war-likelihood table was image-embedded and not extractable as a coherent series [Source #11; image-embedding gap acknowledged as residual data gap for next research-fire]:

- **44.8% blame the PRC for cross-strait tensions** (Oct-2025 wave) [Source #11]. This is the highest single attribution category and constitutes the public-opinion anchor under Lai's "alarming military ambitions" framing in the 2026-01-01 New Year's Address (S1.a [Source #2]). The doctrinal escalation has a public-opinion floor.
- **45.3% believe Trump is capable of preventing an invasion, but 44.8% believe he is unwilling to do so** [Source #11]. The split is the most diagnostic single number in the S3 dataset for the US-coalition-management dimension. The Taiwan public has not collapsed into either a confidence-in-US or a distrust-of-US block — it holds *capability* and *will* as separate variables, and reads them in roughly opposite directions. This is exactly the configuration Lai's EPPD-citation strategy (S1.b [Source #3]) is calibrated for: the public sees a US that *can* but *might not* — so the executive's job is to make the *will* component salient via institutional continuity (EPPD as Trump-1 mechanism) rather than rhetorical confidence-building.

Pew's January 2024 release adds the longer-arc emotional-attachment finding: **40% of Taiwan adults report emotional attachment to the mainland** (with 11% "very emotionally attached") [Source #12]. The number is in apparent tension with the unification-floor finding from NCCU until it is read correctly: emotional attachment is an *affective* variable, unification preference is a *political* variable, and the two have decoupled in the post-2022 cohort. Pew's correlation finding — those primarily Taiwanese align with DPP, those holding dual or Chinese identity align with KMT [Source #12] — is the political-affect mapping that the S2 KMT-CCP forum anchored on (Hsiao's "rejuvenate the Chinese nation" framing is calibrated to the 28% dual-identity bloc and the 11% strongly-affectively-attached subset, not to the 3% primary-Chinese floor which is too small to be a political audience).

The S1 hypothesis is therefore validated: **public opinion backs the executive on PRC-threat anchoring** (44.8% blame attribution; identity floor locked; unification ASAP at 1.1%) **and fractures on executive execution** (Lai approval 34.9–58.0% range over 19 months; near 50/50 on Trump willingness). The validation is asymmetric in the executive's favor on the *doctrinal* axis and asymmetric *against* the executive on the *delivery* axis — which compresses Lai's effective authority to drive packages through the LY gate even where the doctrinal mandate is broad.

#### S3.d — Three GWW3 predicates from S3

The S3 record supports three additional ROC-player predicates beyond the S1.d and S2.d sets:

### S4 — Defense reform: budget architecture, doctrinal substrate, and the NT$780B political ratification **[PROSE-LOCKED]**

The S1 hypothesis on S4 was that Lai's NT$1.25 trillion / 8-year special defense package combined with a 3% GDP 2026 → 5% GDP 2030 trajectory would deliver an integrated, executive-driven defense ramp — that the budget envelope and the doctrinal envelope would arrive as a single Lai-administration package. The primary-source record under S4 modifies the hypothesis in a specific direction: **the budget is real and large, but the executive's effective authorship of its top-end is bounded above by KMT-TPP legislative ratification, and that ratification is itself bounded by Washington's Foreign Military Sales cadence.** What lands in the ROC defense-spend column is the joint output of three gates — MND doctrinal authoring, LY political ratification, and US-Exec procurement approval — and the 2026-05-08 passage of the NT$780 billion compromise is the cleanest measurement of the three-gate equilibrium to date. S4 also documents the doctrinal substrate (QDR 2025) on which the ratified-and-pending spend will be deployed, because the budget-level GWW3 predicates are not interpretable without the doctrine they fund.

#### S4.a — Three-layer budget architecture: NT$949.5B baseline + NT$1.1T resilience supplemental + NT$780B special

Taiwan's 2026 (民國115) defense allocation is structured in three concurrently-flowing tranches that S1's NT$1.25T anchor consolidated into a single number. KMT Legislator Ma Wen-chun, on the public record via UDN 2026-05-13, decomposes the three layers explicitly [Source #17]:

> "到了賴清德總統任內，2025年度已提高到6470億元，今年度 [2026/115年度] 更一口氣編列高達9495億元，現在的國防支出，不只是史上最高，而且還在快速擴張。"
> "今年國防相關預算，甚至一次編3大塊，包括年度公務預算9495億元、強化韌性特別預算中國防約1100億元、軍購特別預算最後通過版本是7800億元。"
> — Ma Wen-chun (KMT), quoted in UDN 2026-05-13 [Source #17]

The three layers, **as ratified or pending as of 2026-05-13**: (a) **MND baseline FY26 (公務預算)** at NT$949.5 billion — up ~46.8% from the NT$647.0 billion FY25 baseline under Lai's term; (b) **resilience supplemental (強化韌性特別預算 — defense portion)** at approximately NT$110.0 billion; (c) **special-budget arms procurement (軍購特別預算)** at NT$780 billion, ratified 2026-05-08 in the LY's third-reading [Sources #19, #20]. The sum across the three layers — approximately NT$1.84 trillion of defense-related allocation flowing in 2026 — exceeds S1's NT$1.25 trillion anchor in nominal terms, but the comparison is structurally apples-to-oranges: S1's NT$1.25T was the special-budget proposal alone, framed as an 8-year ramp; the FY26 NT$1.84T figure includes the annual baseline that would have continued without any special package, plus a one-year supplemental flow. **The cleanest like-for-like measurement is at the special-budget layer alone: NT$780 billion ratified against NT$1.25 trillion proposed — 62.4% of the original DPP envelope delivered, with the remainder neither dead nor live but conditional**. The implication is not that Lai's defense ramp failed; it is that **the GDP-share trajectory toward 5% by 2030 is now a state-contingent variable rather than a Taipei-executive commitment**, with the contingency parameter sitting in Washington.

#### S4.b — The doctrinal substrate: QDR 2025 asymmetric-warfare institutionalization

The MND's 2025 Quadrennial Defense Review — the doctrinal document the NT$780B and the baseline NT$949.5B fund — formally institutionalizes the asymmetric-warfare turn that S1's "T-Dome" framing pointed to but did not specify [Source #16]:

> "兵力整建置重點於成熟科技快速導入、無人載具、人工智慧及網通資電情監偵作戰等項目，並配合新式武器裝備獲得期程，滾動調修主戰、守備與後備部隊之編裝武器數量與兵力結構。"
> "整體規劃籌獲監偵型與攻擊型無人機，並針對未來作戰需求，進行模組化升級，以確保持續獲得最新裝備性能。"
> — MND QDR 2025 [Source #16]

Three structural moves in QDR 2025 are load-bearing for the GWW3 frame. **First**, force structure shifts toward decentralized operations: the document establishes a Navy **Littoral Strike Group (濱海打擊群)** as a dedicated force element, with explicit doctrinal text on "machine-mobile, agile, lethal, cost-effective, stealth" strike-and-combat capabilities ("強化機動、敏捷、致命、成本效益、隱匿之打擊與作戰能力") [Source #16]. **Second**, drone capability is institutionalized at the training-pipeline level rather than the procurement-line level: QDR 2025 mandates establishment of a **drone training center (無人機訓練中心)** for cultivating instructor cadres, and integrates drone operation alongside man-portable air defense and anti-armor weapons into the conscription training curriculum [Source #16]. **Third**, the document subordinates major-weapons-platform procurement timing to the asymmetric force-structure shift — force structure is described as **rolling-adjusted ("滾動調修")** against weapons-acquisition schedules rather than the inverse. The doctrinal posture funded by FY26 is one where the conscription cohort exits training with drone-operation competency, the Navy operationalizes a coastal-defense strike group separate from the blue-water surface fleet, and force-structure planning indexes against acquisition cadence rather than driving it. **This is the doctrine the political-ratification layer (S4.c) negotiated over**, not the doctrine the political ratification authored.

NCSIST's public product catalog confirms the indigenous-systems substrate that the doctrinal turn funds at the platform level — the Sky Bow III air-defense system, Sea Sword II / Sea Oryx surface-launched air-defense, and the Hsiung Feng series anti-ship suite [Source #18]. The Sea Oryx is described in the catalog as "新一代影像式紅外線(IIR)導引短程防空飛彈，具備慣性導航及資料鏈更新能力，可攔截攻艦飛彈、戰機、直升機及無人機等主要空中威脅" [Source #18] — a short-range air-defense designed specifically to intercept anti-ship missiles, aircraft, helicopters, and drones, which is the threat-spectrum a PRC gray-zone-to-quarantine campaign would saturate. **Production rates, FY26 procurement targets, and the indigenous-vs-foreign-buy split for NCSIST line items are not disclosed on the public-facing catalog** [Source #18]; the breakdown into the NT$949.5B baseline and the NT$300B/NT$480B special-budget tranches is a residual research gap acknowledged in the open questions block (q3).

#### S4.c — The 2026-05-08 political ratification: Cheng-LOA frame, KMT-internal arithmetic, MND kill-chain rebuttal

The NT$780B compromise that the LY ratified on 2026-05-08 is structured as a NT$300 billion first tranche disbursed up front, plus a NT$480 billion second tranche disbursed only after the US Foreign Military Sales pipeline issues additional Letters of Acceptance [Sources #15, #19]. KMT Chair Cheng Li-wun, on the public record via FocusTaiwan/CNA 2026-04-28, framed the LOA-contingency explicitly [Source #14]:

> "Cheng said the KMT supports passing an initial NT$380 billion defense budget, which would be sufficient to cover the US$11.1 billion in arms packages approved for sale to Taiwan by the U.S. in December 2025. An additional NT$400 billion could be considered if a second round of LOAs is issued. Without such documentation, she said, there is a risk the extra funding could be misused by the ruling Democratic Progressive Party (DPP)."
> — Cheng Li-wun (KMT Chair), quoted in FocusTaiwan/CNA 2026-04-28 [Source #14]

The KMT/TPP-side rationale for the cut, in the opposition's own framing as recorded by Liberty Times 2026-05-10, runs on three claims: (i) the special-budget envelope should cover government-to-government FMS purchases only, not commercial purchases (商購) which the opposition argues are vulnerable to price-inflation and oversight failure; (ii) domestic commissioned production (委製) is non-urgent and should return to baseline-budget review under standard LY fiscal-discipline procedure; (iii) bundling commercial and commissioned items into a special budget is a mechanism for circumventing fiscal oversight [Source #20]:

> "在野黨觀點：立院國民黨團、民眾黨團只通過政府對政府的「軍購」預算，因藍白擔憂「商購」有價格浮報與監督困難等問題，另外「委製」並非迫切需求，應回歸「年度預算」接受立法院審查，而非納入特別預算，規避財政紀律監督。"
> — Liberty Times 2026-05-10 [Source #20]

The MND's same-day rebuttal recasts the commercial-and-commissioned carve-out as a kill-chain rupture [Source #20]:

> "完全排除商購及委製案項，有損國防建軍規劃之完整性，極易造成戰力缺口... 若將國外商售（如TAK手機網路）或國內委製（如強弓飛彈）剔除，即便擁有海馬士火箭也會因「沒眼睛沒大腦」而無法發揮戰力。"
> — Ministry of National Defense, 2026-05-08 evening statement, quoted in Liberty Times 2026-05-10 [Source #20]

MND's "no eyes, no brain" framing is structurally telling: HIMARS rocket-launchers (the marquee FMS shooter) without TAK-Tactical-Awareness-Kit data-link integration (commercial purchase) and without Strong Bow / Hsiung Feng indigenous sensor-and-effector integration (commissioned production) produces shooters without targeting — a force that can fire but cannot aim. The opposition's fiscal-discipline frame and the MND's kill-chain frame are not arguing about the same object: the opposition is arguing about *who authorizes which spend through which procurement channel*, the MND is arguing about *whether the resulting integrated system functions as a deterrent*. Both positions can be internally consistent and analytically incompatible simultaneously — the negotiation that produced the NT$780B figure papered over the analytical incompatibility by holding the baseline (NT$949.5B) and the resilience supplemental (NT$110B) as the channels through which the commercial/commissioned items can be re-introduced, and the special-budget channel as government-to-government-FMS-only.

President Lai's response, on the public record via UDN 2026-05-13 from the DPP Central Executive Committee meeting, framed the larger geopolitical stakes [Source #21]:

> "面對地緣政治的快速變化，若台灣未能強化自我防衛能力，恐將削弱自身安全，以及民主陣營整體嚇阻力量，進一步影響印太區域和平穩定。"
> — Lai Ching-te (ROC President), DPP Central Executive Committee 2026-05-13, quoted in UDN [Source #21]

A separate claim circulating from TPP Chair Huang Kuo-chang's Threads account — that the DPP's private negotiating position was NT$860 billion (NT$800B for arms purchases plus NT$60B additional), substantially below the public NT$1.25 trillion figure — is **flagged as unverified**: Donovan's TaipeiTimes piece records the claim but explicitly notes the `huang.guochang` Threads account is not authenticated despite high follower count [Source #15]. For S4 prose, the alleged private position is held as a possible-but-uncorroborated provenance; the verifiable public claims — KMT's public NT$380B+N proposal, the LY-ratified NT$780B outcome, the DPP's public NT$1.25T anchor — are the load-bearing record.
