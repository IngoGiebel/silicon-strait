## §2 — What the FCDO Tier-2 lexicon already tells us

### §2.1 — Canonical UK Tier-1+Tier-2 architecture (1972 Joint Communiqué → present)

The UK established diplomatic relations with the PRC on 1972-03-13. The Anglo-Chinese Joint Communiqué (1972-03-13) is the canonical Tier-1+Tier-2 instrument. The two operative passages, paraphrased in the silicon-strait corpus and to be confirmed verbatim by Inanna's first dispatch deliverable (`research/cross_substrate/inanna-uk-1972-communique-text-2026-05-23.md`):

- **Tier-1 anchor (UK → PRC):** "The Government of the United Kingdom recognise the Government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal Government of China." — establishes PRC as sole legal China for UK diplomatic purposes.
- **Tier-2 instantiation (UK → PRC's Taiwan claim):** "The Government of the United Kingdom acknowledge the position of the Government of the People's Republic of China that Taiwan is a Province of the People's Republic of China." — note the **deliberate "acknowledge"-not-"recognise" asymmetry**. UK *recognises* the PRC as legal government of China but only *acknowledges* the PRC's *position* on Taiwan. This is the canonical Tier-2 register.

This 1972 wording closely parallels the US 1972 Shanghai Communiqué's "acknowledges that all Chinese on either side of the Taiwan Strait maintain there is but one China" — both deploy the *acknowledge*-not-*recognise* construction as a deliberate diplomatic ambiguation. This is the **load-bearing distinction** between the Anglo-American Tier-2 lexicon and the EU "policy" Tier-2 lexicon (Day-12 §4): both are policy-positions, but the Anglo-American formulation makes the policy-position explicitly *about acknowledging the PRC's claim* rather than the EU's *about the UK's-own-policy*.

### §2.2 — FAC 2023 §62-63 self-quote of UK Tier-2 lexicon (primary)

Foreign Affairs Committee (2023), *Tilting horizons*, HC 172, paragraph 62 ([Source #1]):

> "Despite protestations that it is not, China is seeking to project its power worldwide by exerting pressure, economic as well as diplomatic, on countries to recognise its sovereignty over Taiwan. The CCP requires countries to adhere to its 'One China' policy and opposes what it calls the 'two Chinas' or 'one China, one Taiwan' policy. **The UK's official policy is to 'acknowledge' China's 'One China' policy, without declaring adherence to it.**"

This is the FAC's self-description of the UK Tier-2 instantiation — confirming (a) "acknowledge"-not-"recognise" asymmetry, (b) "without declaring adherence" qualifier that exceeds the US Shanghai-Communiqué formulation in deliberate distance from PRC framing.

### §2.3 — Government Response confirmation of Tier-2 (2023)

UK Government Response to FAC report (2023-11), *Tilting horizons: the Integrated Review and the Indo Pacific — Government Response*, HC 630, paragraph 135 ([Source #2]):

> "**The UK's longstanding position on Taiwan has not changed.** The UK has no diplomatic relations with Taiwan but a strong, unofficial relationship, based on deep and growing ties in a wide range of areas and underpinned by shared democratic values. The UK has a clear interest in peace and stability in the Taiwan Strait. We consider the Taiwan issue one to be settled peacefully by the people on both sides of the Taiwan Strait through constructive dialogue, without the threat or use of force or coercion. **We do not support any unilateral attempts to change the status quo.** This includes increased Chinese assertiveness towards Taiwan."

Tier-2 self-restatement structure: "longstanding position has not changed" + "no diplomatic relations / unofficial relationship" + "status quo" framing + "peace and stability" register. This is the canonical FCDO Tier-2 lexicon, restated in November 2023 *as a response to* the FAC's Tier-3 framing at §142 (next section).

### §2.4 — 2024 ministerial restatement (Hansard, Commons Chamber)

Hansard, 28 November 2024, "Taiwan: International Status" debate, Minister's response 2:01pm ([Source #3]):

> "As two thriving democracies, the UK and Taiwan share a unique relationship which is rooted in our shared democratic values, cultural links and deep ties. **Despite not having formal diplomatic relations with Taiwan, we have strong unofficial links across a range of issues such as trade, education, science and cultural exchange.**"

Same Tier-2 register as 2023 Government Response, ~14 months later, under a different government (Labour, Starmer, Catherine West Minister of State for the Indo-Pacific). **Tier-2 lexical continuity across government-change** — a strong indicator of FCDO institutional ownership rather than ministerial-party-specific framing.

## §3 — FAC 2023 Tier-3 candidate evidence (the smoking gun)

### §3.1 — FAC §142 verbatim (the textbook Tier-3 framing)

Foreign Affairs Committee (2023), *Tilting horizons*, HC 172, paragraph 142 ([Source #1]):

> "Taiwan is already an independent country, under the name Republic of China (ROC). **Taiwan possesses all the qualifications for statehood, including a permanent population, a defined territory, government, and the capacity to enter into relations with other states**—it is only lacking greater international recognition. Taiwan's government has not made an official proclamation of independence—because China regards that as a *casus belli*—but President Tsai Ing-wen states that this is because Taiwan is 'an independent country already' so has no need to declare itself an independent state, although we noted that during our visit the vast majority of political interlocutors wanted to uphold the status quo, not declare full independence or become a province of the PRC."

This is **the textbook Tier-3 narrowest-tier framing** for the cross-substrate portability series. Specifically:
- It does not say "Taiwan should be" or "we recognise Taiwan as" or "the UK acknowledges that Taiwan is" — it says simply **"Taiwan is"** in declarative-fact register.
- It explicitly invokes the **Montevideo Convention 1933 statehood criteria** ("permanent population, defined territory, government, capacity to enter into relations with other states"), which is the international-law definition of statehood — bypassing the UN-recognition register entirely.
- The framing was authored by a **UK-Parliament select committee in formal session**, published as **House of Commons paper HC 172**, not as a journalistic op-ed or APPG informal report.
- The text was reported globally as "first time the British parliament has referred to Taiwan as an 'independent country' in an official document" (Politico 2023-08-30, SCMP 2023-08-30 [Source #4]).

This crosses the Tier-3 floor by EP-Day-12 standards: it is a **formal parliamentary instrument** containing a **declarative-status-narrowing** claim on Taiwan that is **inconsistent with the executive Tier-2 register** ("acknowledge"-not-"recognise" — the FAC's framing recognises Taiwan as a state in international-law substance even while disclaiming proclamation-of-independence).

### §3.2 — FAC Tier-2-consistent surrounding recommendations

The FAC report's *recommendations* (paras 47-54 in the recommendations summary) are largely Tier-2-consistent and do not extend the §142 Tier-3 framing into operative recommendations:
- Rec 47: support visits by trade/science/education ministers (Tier-2 substantive-relationship)
- Rec 48: campaign for Taiwan's CPTPP admission (Tier-2 substantive-relationship)
- Rec 49: "The UK's 'One China' policy is not the same as China's 'One China' principle. Rather than agreeing to China's claim to Taiwan, the UK, like other liberal democracies, merely acknowledges Beijing's position." (Tier-2 self-restatement, explicitly using "acknowledges")
- Rec 50-54: cooperation, deterrence-diplomacy, semiconductor supply chains, international-body participation, language teaching (all Tier-2 substantive)

**Reading:** the Tier-3 framing is concentrated at §142 in the report's *body* (the analytical chapter on China's threat to Taiwan), not extended into the *recommendations*. This is the FAC's deliberate institutional positioning: the Tier-3 register is *asserted-as-finding* but not *prescribed-as-action*. This is structurally analogous to EP plenary resolutions where the *whereas* clauses can contain Tier-3 framings that the *operative* paragraphs do not require the Council/Commission to act upon.

## §4 — FCDO-FAC institutional-distance: the P28(c) test on UK

### §4.1 — The Government Response §135 partial-repudiation pattern

The Government Response (November 2023) §135 ([Source #2]), quoted in full above at §2.3, performs a textbook **diplomatic-distance restatement** with three load-bearing structural features:

1. **Explicit unchanged-position assertion**: "The UK's longstanding position on Taiwan has not changed" — addresses the FAC §142 framing without naming it. This is the *substance* of the partial-repudiation: the Government will not adopt the FAC's "Taiwan is already an independent country" framing into Government policy.

2. **No direct engagement with §142 text**: nowhere in §135 (or §136-141) does the Government quote, paraphrase, or directly counter the FAC's "qualifications for statehood" claim. The Government simply **restates its own Tier-2 position** without engaging the Tier-3 framing on its merits.

3. **No institutional sanction against the FAC**: no censure motion, no Committee Chair replacement demand, no Speaker referral, no Crown Law Office opinion challenging the report's competence, no Whitehall communication suppressing further FAC work on Taiwan. The FAC continues its Indo-Pacific oversight role.

### §4.2 — The three P28 conditions tested on UK substrate

**(a) Constitutional-grounding of legislative agency:**
- UK Parliament is sovereign (Crown-in-Parliament). Select committees are creatures of standing orders with full powers of summons and report-publication.
- The Government has a **standing duty to respond formally** to select committee reports (typically within 6 months) — this duty was honored for FAC HC 172 by the publication of HC 630.
- Select committees have no power to *bind* Government foreign policy, but they have **constitutional standing to publish findings inconsistent with Government policy** as a formal parliamentary instrument.
- **TEST RESULT: POSITIVE.** Constitutional-grounding clearly present at the select-committee level. (At the plenary-resolution level, see Inanna dispatch §5 — Commons motions on Taiwan have less institutional weight than committee reports in this constitutional architecture, the inverse of EP's plenary-vs-committee weighting.)

**(b) Executive-independence from foreign-policy executive register:**
- The FAC operated independently of FCDO during HC 172 inquiry (Aug 2022-Aug 2023). FCDO submitted evidence; FAC was free to disagree with FCDO framing in its findings.
- The Government did not (publicly) pre-clear, veto, or amend the §142 text before publication.
- FAC Chair (Alicia Kearns, 2023; subsequent Chairs) was a Government-party (Conservative) MP at time of HC 172 publication — yet still published the Tier-3 framing. This is stronger executive-independence than the EU case (where EP-Council partisan composition routinely diverges); EP independence is *enabled by* its institutional distance from the Council, whereas UK select-committee independence is *demonstrated despite* same-party chair, which is a stricter form of (b).
- **TEST RESULT: POSITIVE.** Demonstrated executive-independence; arguably stronger than EP case at the same-party-chair calibration.

**(c) Non-crisis-producing-deviation-capacity:**
- FAC §142 was published 2023-08-30. The Government Response was published ~12 weeks later (November 2023). The Government Response §135 restated the Tier-2 position without naming, suppressing, or sanctioning the FAC.
- No constitutional crisis ensued: no minister resignation, no FAC reconstitution, no PRC-induced UK diplomatic crisis that the Government attributed to the FAC report (the Government's PRC engagement continued through Cleverly's August 2023 Beijing visit — which actually overlapped the FAC publication week, with no visible disruption).
- The Government's "longstanding position has not changed" was *both* a restatement of policy *and* a public admission that the FAC's framing did not bind the Government — which is itself the constitutional-architecture statement that allows (c) to test positive.
- **TEST RESULT: POSITIVE.** Tier-3 framing was tolerated without crisis; FCDO simply restated Tier-2 and continued operations.

## §7 — Methodology + limitations

### §7.1 — Source-discipline notes

- **Primary sources first**: FAC HC 172 + Gov-Response HC 630 + Hansard 28 Nov 2024 are all official UK Parliament publications, accessed via publications.parliament.uk and hansard.parliament.uk. The 1972 Joint Communiqué wording at §2.1 is paraphrased; Inanna's q-day14-2 deliverable will provide verbatim from a primary-source citation.
- **Bias-balance**: §2.2 quotes the FAC quoting itself ("UK's official policy is to 'acknowledge'") — a single-side reading. The PRC-MOFA reading of UK policy (typically reading the 1972 Communiqué as Tier-1 "recognise Taiwan as part of PRC" rather than Tier-2 "acknowledge PRC position") is documented in the Day-13 cross-substrate corpus and not re-litigated here.
- **Date-stamps**: FAC HC 172 published 2023-08-30; Gov-Response HC 630 published 2023-11-21; Hansard debate 2024-11-28. All within the 2023-2026 cycle relevant to current UK foreign-policy register.

### §7.2 — Limitations of this fire's preliminary finding

1. **Single-report Tier-3 framing.** FAC HC 172 is the only formal UK-parliamentary-report Tier-3 framing on Taiwan in the silicon-strait corpus as of this fire. A single instance is *one observation*, not a *register*. The Inanna dispatch tests whether the register is institutionally available or whether HC 172 is an outlier.

2. **Government Response is one data-point.** The Government Response §135 is *one* restatement-without-crisis observation. A robust P28(c) test would catalogue multiple such Tier-3-Tier-2-restatement cycles. (PHL had multiple Marcos/DFA Tier-2-restatement cycles surveyed in Day-11; UK would need similar surveying for full confidence.)

3. **Government-change confound.** FAC HC 172 was a Conservative-majority Parliament committee report (Sunak Government); Gov-Response was the Sunak Government's response; 2024-11-28 ministerial restatement was under Labour Government (Starmer). The Tier-2 lexical continuity *across government-change* is supportive of P28's substrate-discriminating reading, but does not rule out the possibility that a different ministerial response style (e.g., a hypothetical Labour-Government response to a hypothetical further Tier-3 FAC report) might exhibit different restatement structure.

4. **Constitutional-architecture asymmetry with EP.** UK Parliament constitutional-architecture is qualitatively different from EU institutional-architecture: UK Parliament is sovereign with Government drawn from Parliament; EU has separation between EP, Council, Commission. Testing P28 on UK is structurally a *harder* test than on EP because UK Government MPs sit on FAC. If P28 tests positive on UK despite this *harder* test, the predicate is more robust than the EP-only evidence would suggest.
