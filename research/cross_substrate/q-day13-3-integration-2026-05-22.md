### §4.2 — New candidate predicate: P28 (substrate-discriminating Tier-3 floor)

The PHL reading (1) confirmation reveals that **a substrate can have a complete substantive layer (Tier 1 + Tier 2) without ever instantiating Tier 3**. This is a portability-floor phenomenon distinct from a coverage gap: it means Tier 3 (status-narrowing) is *not* a necessary consequence of having Tier 1 + Tier 2; it requires *additional substrate conditions* (constitutional foundation depth, parliamentary independence from executive doctrine, supranational-vs-national governance level, etc.).

Candidate predicate **P28** (to be developed for Day-13 §4 or Day-14): the Tier-3 status-narrowing register requires (a) a constitutionally-grounded legislative body that (b) is institutionally distant enough from executive foreign policy to (c) issue resolutions that contradict the executive Tier-2 register without producing a constitutional crisis. The EP satisfies all three (EU has no foreign-policy-executive in the Westphalian sense; the EP can challenge the Council without dissolving anything). PHL fails (c): the Senate respects DFA "One China Policy" as a binding executive register; deviation would force a foreign-policy crisis. JPN's Diet is an untested case (TAO 2025-09-10 individual-act register touched Tier-3 territory but the Diet collectively did not produce a TA-9-2021-0431 equivalent).

P28 is a candidate predicate that the Day-13 publication should *flag* but not *establish* — Day-14 or beyond should test it against a third substrate (CAN, AUS, UK parliaments are plausible test substrates).
