## 2. Conclusion and Tier-3 Disambiguation

**Finding:** The PHL Senate possesses multiple *substantive-relationship* instruments (addressing OFWs, investment, cultural exchange) and *institutional-relationship* instruments (supporting WHO observer status and MECO operations). However, it lacks any *Taiwan-status-narrowing* instrument. The closest instrument to addressing Taiwan's political status—Senate Resolution No. 302 (17th Congress)—specifically aims to "PREVENT POSSIBLE VIOLATIONS OF THE ONE CHINA POLICY", functionally reinforcing the Executive Tier-2 register rather than escalating to a Tier-3 narrowest-tier position (such as the EP's "neither subordinate to the other").

**Tier-3 Answer:** **No, the PHL Senate does not have a Taiwan-status-narrowing instrument.** This supports q-day13-1 §3.3 **reading (1)**: there is a real architectural absence at the status-narrowing axis. The PHL legislative branch engages heavily in substantive but not status-narrowing legislative acts, meaning the PHL Tier-3 slot is effectively empty compared to the EP TA-9-2021-0431 equivalent.
