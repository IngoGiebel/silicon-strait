## Why this thread, and why now

Chip supply chain (Day 3) established the *material* layer — what makes the cross-strait conflict globally consequential. Military balance is the *capacity* layer — who can actually do what, and on what timeline. Without it, the scenarios in Day 5+ (coercion toolkit, integration scenarios) have no force-structure grounding. The military balance also feeds the Silicon Shield analysis directly: if TSMC and ASML fabs can be credibly neutralized without kinetic conflict (blockade, missile demonstration, cyber), the deterrence calculus in Day 3's Framing 2 changes shape. This thread and the chip thread are therefore the two load-bearing quantitative pillars for everything that follows.

**GWW3 / Taiwan-specific implication:** DF-21D (1,500 km) and DF-17 (1,600 km) can cover the entire Taiwan island and approaches from multiple inland launch positions well outside the range of Taiwan's own anti-ship systems. Any force-structure model for a Taiwan contingency must carry separate intercept-probability columns for ballistic vs hypersonic incoming.

The Fujian's sea trials have been ongoing; its operational-ready date is the single most consequential near-term platform milestone for any joint-operations scenario.

Taiwan's strategic calculus cannot be symmetric deterrence; it can only be asymmetric deterrence ("porcupine strategy") that raises the cost/time of an operation above the PLA's acceptable threshold, not matching force-for-force.

## Preliminary GWW3 framing seeds

### Framing 1 — The asymmetric deterrence equilibrium

Taiwan cannot win a symmetric military contest. The GWW3 model should not frame this as a binary win/lose game; it should frame it as a **cost-imposition game**: Taiwan's objective is to raise the PLA's expected cost of a Taiwan operation above the political and economic cost threshold Xi Jinping's calculation implies. The deterrence parameter here is not "can Taiwan defeat a PLA landing" but "can Taiwan impose costs high enough that PLA planners forecast a political cost to Xi that exceeds the political benefit of successful reunification."

This is a very different model than a conventional military-balance spreadsheet, and it has a consequential implication: Taiwan's military modernization program (the F-16V upgrades, the Harpoon Block II+ purchases, the Tien Kung III PAC-3 upgrades, the new indigenous submarine program) should be evaluated not by whether it closes the force-ratio gap but by whether it changes the expected cost curve beyond the decision threshold.

### Framing 2 — The time-compression problem

Any US response to a Taiwan contingency requires deployment from Guam (~2,700 km from Taipei), Japan (carrier-strike-group access), and Hawaii (~7,400 km). The PLARF DF-21D and DF-17 can target the Guam naval facilities and carrier approaches from western China. The A2/AD zone (anti-access / area-denial) created by the PLARF arsenal means that US reinforcement speed is a decisive parameter in any scenario model: if the PLA can achieve a *fait accompli* before US forces can mass inside the DF-21D engagement envelope, the post-event equilibrium is structurally different from one in which US forces arrive in time to contest the operation.

The GWW3 model needs a **time-to-fait-accompli** parameter alongside the **time-to-US-intervention** parameter, and a probability distribution over each. The spread between these two distributions is the war/peace boundary, not a static military-balance ratio.

### Framing 3 — Reserve quality and the mobilization wild card

Taiwan's reserve force (1.66–2.5 M) dwarfs its active force (~160,000). However, Taiwan's reserve system has long been criticized as paper-heavy and training-light — the 2022–2024 reserve reform program under President Tsai extended the training cycle from 5-day refresher to 14-day annual rotations for part of the force. The quality of reserve mobilization is therefore a contested empirical question, not a parametric one: a model must carry a probability distribution over "how much of the reserve force shows up, trained enough to fight, on Day 3" rather than treating the 2.5 M figure as a force-multiplier at face value.

1. **Fujian carrier operational-ready date** — critical for scenario timing. Wikipedia and open sources have conflicting claims on sea-trial progress. TechInsights (naval equivalent) or CSIS China Power project would be the better source.

3. **TSMC fab + military target overlap** — what is the actual distance between Hsinchu Science Park (TSMC HQ fabs) and the nearest PLARF missile brigade? Maps exist; need to cite.
4. **PLA exercise frequency and ADIZ incursion data** — Taiwan MND publishes daily ADIZ incursion counts. Trend 2020–2025. Wikipedia has partial data; Taiwan MND or project ADIZ tracker (MND primary) is the right source.
5. **US arms sales 2017–2024 to Taiwan** — DSCA (Defense Security Cooperation Agency) official announcements for: Harpoon Block II+, F-16V upgrade, Abrams, Stinger MANPADS, HIMARS. These are all public record.
