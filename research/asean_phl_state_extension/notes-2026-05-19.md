## q-A1 — Formal PHL diplomatic frame on cross-strait

The Philippines's formal frame on the cross-strait question rests on a single load-bearing instrument — the **1975 Joint Communiqué between the Republic of the Philippines and the People's Republic of China** — and a long sequence of DFA reaffirmations that hold the One-China-Policy line constant across administrations. The August 2025 episode is the most recent and most informative reaffirmation, because it crystallises the *register split* between the executive (Marcos Jr.) and the diplomatic apparatus (DFA + Secretary Tess Lazaro) that is itself a Day-11 predicate-test candidate.

### Anchor — 1975 PHL-PRC Joint Communiqué

The 1975 Communiqué is the cornerstone of PHL-PRC diplomatic relations and the legal-doctrinal floor of the PHL "One China Policy" formulation [Source #A1, #A3]. The text predates the 1979 US-PRC normalisation by four years; the PHL recognised the PRC as the sole legal government of China and broke formal diplomatic relations with the ROC in the same instrument.

Two features matter for Day-11:

- The PHL formula uses **"recognises"** (not Japan's "understands and respects" from the 1972 JPN-PRC Communiqué). PHL is therefore on a *stronger* doctrinal commitment to the PRC's territorial frame than Japan is.
- The Communiqué establishes the **MECO/TECO model** for PHL-ROC unofficial relations: the Manila Economic and Cultural Office (MECO, PHL side) and the Taipei Economic and Cultural Office (TECO, ROC side) handle economic, cultural, and consular functions in lieu of formal state-to-state contact. This is structurally similar to Japan's Interchange Association → Japan-Taiwan Exchange Association mechanism, but rests on a stronger underlying doctrinal commitment (recognition, not just understanding).

### Reaffirmation — August 2025 DFA statement triggered by Marcos remarks

In August 2025, President Marcos Jr. made a Taiwan-contingency comment that the DFA judged required formal clarification:

> "To be very practical about it, if there is confrontation over Taiwan between China and the United States, there is no way that the Philippines can stay out of it simply because of our physical geographic location." [Source #A2, #A4 — Marcos quote as reported in Rappler and Tribune.net.ph]

The PRC framing read this as a deviation from the One-China Policy. The DFA responded with a formal statement and a Senate-hearing reaffirmation by DFA Secretary Tess Lazaro:

> "We do not recognize Taiwan as a sovereign state." [Source #A2 — Tess Lazaro at Senate hearing, August 2025]

The DFA's full formulation [Source #A3 — DFA Statement on One China Policy, dfa.gov.ph]:

- The 1975 Joint Communiqué "remains a cornerstone" of PHL-PRC relations.
- The Philippines "consistently upholds the One China Policy" and "does not recognize Taiwan as a sovereign state".
- The policy is "clear and unwavering".
- The PHL maintains economic, people-to-people, trade, investment, and tourism engagements with Taiwan "within the bounds of the One China Policy".
- The ~200,000 Filipino nationals working and residing in Taiwan give the PHL a "direct interest in peace and stability in the region" — the **legitimate-interest formulation** that Day-11 must encode as a predicate.

### EDCA expansion — operational floor under the diplomatic frame

The Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement (EDCA, 2014) governs US rotational access to designated PHL military facilities. The Marcos Jr. administration expanded the EDCA footprint from 5 to 9 sites on **2023-04-03** [Source #A5 — USINDOPACOM PR; #A6 — Asian Military Review]. Of the four new sites, **three face Taiwan from north Luzon** and one is on Palawan facing the South China Sea:

- **Lal-lo Airport, Cagayan** — north Luzon, ~600 km from southern Taiwan
- **Naval Base Camilo Osias, Santa Ana, Cagayan** — north Luzon, ~480 km from southern Taiwan
- **Camilo Osias and Lal-lo are the Taiwan-adjacent pair**; their proximity to the Bashi Channel makes them load-bearing for any US Taiwan-contingency posture
- A third north-Luzon site in Isabela province
- Balabac Island, Palawan — SCS-facing

Marcos's own framing of the EDCA-Taiwan linkage, on the record [Source #A6 — Asian Military Review, 2024-06]:

> "[U.S. access to the new EDCA bases will] prove to be useful for us should that terrible occurrence come about."

The "terrible occurrence" referent is a Chinese invasion of Taiwan. This is an *executive-register* acknowledgment that the EDCA expansion is Taiwan-contingency-coupled, distinct from the DFA-register One-China-Policy reaffirmation. The two registers do not contradict each other doctrinally — the PHL can simultaneously recognise the PRC as the sole legal government of China AND prepare its territory for use in a Taiwan contingency that the PHL frames as a US-PRC matter — but they speak in different vocabularies to different audiences.

### Financial-coupling angle — Nov 2025 US congressional panel proposal

A US congressional panel in November 2025 recommended that Taiwan (the ROC, not the PRC) **fund EDCA-base upgrades in the Philippines** [Source #A7 — Naval News, Philstar]. The proposal is unofficial and has not been adopted, but it represents a *cross-strait financial-coupling* that would, if executed, materially complicate the PHL's "within the bounds of the One China Policy" formulation — since ROC funds flowing to PHL infrastructure on EDCA-designated sites would be hard to reconcile with non-recognition of Taiwan as a sovereign state. The PHL has not formally responded. Worth tracking as a Day-11 open thread.

### Day-11 predicate-test relevance

The PHL exhibits an **Executive-vs-DFA register split** that mirrors the Day-10 finding of an **MoD-vs-MOFA register split** on the PRC side (P23). This is a candidate *mirror predicate*: where the PRC has its own intra-state register discriminator, so does the PHL. If the mirror holds across Day-11+12+13 substrates (PHL, then EU, then ROK), it generalises into a structural finding about *all* second-tier players in the GWW3 frame: every state in the cross-strait game has at least two registers, and the discriminator distinguishes the formal-treaty-anchored frame from the operational-contingency frame.

This will be developed in q-A5 (PRC framing on PHL-Taiwan linkage), where I expect the MOFA-PRC register to address the PHL One-China-Policy reaffirmation while the MoD-PRC register addresses EDCA-base expansion.

## q-A2 — Balikatan 2026 operational frame

Balikatan 2026 is the **41st iteration** of the AFP-US bilateral exercise (which has grown to multilateral). It runs **April 20 through mid-May 2026** [Source #A8 — IPDF Forum]. Three operational facts dominate the Day-11 predicate test:

1. **First-time JGSDF ground-force deployment** (~1,000 troops) into a Balikatan as exercise participants — not observers, not HADR support [Source #A8]. The prior JGSDF participation history was: observer status → HADR roles → 2025 maritime drill → 2026 first ground deployment.
2. **Reciprocal Access Agreement (RAA) Manila-Tokyo took effect in September 2025** [Source #A8]. This is the *legal floor* that enables (1) and parallels the JPN-AUS and JPN-UK RAAs. The RAA explicitly contemplates exercise-and-mission deployment by either party into the other's territory.
3. **Integrated Air and Missile Defense (IAMD) multilateral integration** at Naval Stations Leovogildo Gantioqui and San Miguel, Apr 26-29 [Source #A9 — USINDOPACOM 2026-04-29].

### Day-10 → Day-11 kinetic-record bridge

Day-10 §3.5 [Source #A10 — Reuters via Day-10 publication 2026-05-17, in-text [Source #17]] anchored a JMSDF *Ikazuchi* Taiwan-Strait southbound transit on 2026-04-17 from Yokosuka *en route to Balikatan 2026*. That transit is therefore the *kinetic linkage* between the Day-10 Japan substrate and the Day-11 PHL substrate: Japan's JMSDF participation in Balikatan is itself the operational instantiation of the JPN-PHL RAA, and the transit through the Strait was justified by the exercise destination.

This is structurally important: the Day-10 finding that *PRC selectively acknowledges JMSDF Taiwan-Strait transits and elevates individual GSDF acts to state-signal language in MoD register but not MOFA register* (P22) can now be tested on a transit whose **stated purpose is PHL-bound, not Taiwan-directed**. The PRC's framing of the *Ikazuchi* transit — whether it was named as Balikatan-bound or framed as cross-strait-provocative or both — is a predicate test for P22 generalisation.

### Balikatan 2026 IAMD details — multilateral integration depth

The USINDOPACOM 2026-04-29 readout [Source #A9] documents an Integrated Air and Missile Defense exercise spanning Apr 26-29 with the following force composition:

**US Marine Corps**: Marine Air-Ground Task Force (commanded by Brig. Gen. Omar Randall), Marine Air Control Group 38, 3rd Littoral Anti-Air Battalion, 12th LAAB, 3rd Marine Littoral Regiment.

**US Army**: 1st Battalion 51st Air Defense Artillery Regiment, 6th Battalion 52nd ADA. Equipment included AN/TWQ-1 Avenger, VAMPIRE (Vehicle-Agnostic Modular Palletized ISR Rocket Equipment), FS-LIDS (Fixed Site Low Slow Small UAS Integrated Defeat System).

**Japan Self-Defense Force**: Type 11 Tan-SAM Kai II surface-to-air missile system, integrated into the multilateral air-defense network alongside US and PHL assets.

**Philippine Air Force**: SPYDER (Surface-to-Air Python and Derby) surface-to-air missile system, 960th Air and Missile Defense Wing.

**Philippine Army**: 1st Air Defense Artillery.

**Philippine Navy**: Frigates **BRP Antonio Luna (FFG 15)** and **BRP Miguel Malvar (FFG 6)** participated via simulated air-defense engagements that extended the IAMD network into maritime space.

The exercise statement of intent, attributed to **Col. Dennis Hernandez, BK26 AFP spokesperson**: "Integrating the 960th Air and Missile Defense Wing, the 1st Air Defense Artillery, and our Navy ships alongside U.S. and Japanese units allows us to build a truly unified tactical picture."

The US Army officer commentary, attributed to **Lt. Col. Kenneth Collins (1-51 ADA)**: "By bringing rapid, palletized capabilities like VAMPIRE to the shorelines, we provide a decisive, precision-strike capability against small UAS threats."

The exercise is operationally significant for two reasons: (a) it is the first time US-PHL-JPN IAMD assets have been integrated into a **single unified tactical picture**, which is the operational precondition for a coordinated regional air-defense umbrella in a Taiwan contingency; (b) it normalises the JSDF Type 11 Tan-SAM as a deployable asset on PHL territory under RAA cover, which extends Japan's de-facto first-island-chain defense perimeter southward.

### AFP CoS framing

**Gen. Romeo Brawner Jr.**, AFP Chief of Staff, on the JGSDF Balikatan participation [Source #A8]: "With the participation of Japan, this will expand the scope of our defensive operations. We are now working not only with the United States and Australia in preparing to defend the Philippine archipelago, but also our common areas."

The "common areas" phrasing is significant: it does not name Taiwan, but does establish a JPN-PHL shared defensive responsibility extending beyond either country's territorial waters. This is the JPN-PHL parallel to the Day-10 finding on Takaichi's "存立危機事態" (sonritsu-kiki-jitai, "survival-threatening situation") framework — neither names Taiwan, both make Taiwan-contingency engagement legible under domestic legal frames.

### Surrounding exercises (context for Day-11 §4 framing if scope permits)

- **Salaknib 2026**: ~420 JGSDF + 4,400 PHL + 2,800 US + smaller AUS/NZ contingents. Phase 1: Apr 6-17. Phase 2: May-June (post-Balikatan) [Source #A8].
- **Kamandag 2026**: JPN+PHL+US Marines, scheduled June-July [Source #A8 via Japan Times secondary]. Verifies the *cadence*: Balikatan → Salaknib → Kamandag is a roughly continuous JPN-PHL-US training tempo from April through July, not a single annual event.

### Day-11 predicate-test relevance

Balikatan 2026's IAMD integration and the JGSDF first-deployment together constitute a **kinetic-tempo elevation** on the PHL substrate that runs parallel to the JCG-Senkaku contiguous-zone tempo elevation on the JPN substrate (Day-10 Nisaba audit, +52% post-2021-海警法 level shift). The Day-11 equivalent — the PRC Coast Guard tempo on Scarborough Shoal and Second Thomas Shoal — is q-A4 (deferred to 14:00 or 17:00 fire) and should be Nisaba-audited if a clean monthly timeseries is reachable.
