## q-A3 — ASEAN consensus mechanism on cross-strait

The ASEAN-side answer to "how does the regional consensus mechanism constrain PHL freedom of action on Taiwan" is structurally clean and politically inconvenient: **ASEAN does not name Taiwan in its consensus documents**. The 46th ASEAN Summit Chairman's Statement (Kuala Lumpur, 26 May 2025) and the 58th ASEAN Foreign Ministers' Meeting Joint Communiqué (Kuala Lumpur, 9 July 2025) are both load-bearing reference texts for the current ASEAN-cycle frame [Source #A11, #A12]. Neither names "Taiwan", "cross-strait", or "Taiwan Strait" in the substantive paragraphs of the Chairman's Statement or Joint Communiqué [verified via primary-text search of the kln.gov.my Chairman's Statement page and the asean.org AMM Joint Communiqué PDF in the firecrawl_search return].

This is **structurally significant** rather than absent: ASEAN's consensus principle (Article 20 of the ASEAN Charter) requires unanimity for substantive statements, and at least three ASEAN member states (Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar) hold positions on cross-strait closer to the PRC framing than to a US/PHL/JPN-aligned framing. The absence of a Taiwan-Strait reference is the *operational form* of the ASEAN consensus, not an oversight — it is the textual proof that PHL cannot use the ASEAN consensus mechanism as a multilateral lever on its bilateral Taiwan-contingency posture.

### PHL chairs ASEAN in 2026 — the constraint coupling

Per the MOFA-PRC October 24, 2025 press conference (spokesperson Guo Jiakun) [Source #A13]:

> "China always regards ASEAN as a priority in its neighborhood diplomacy. China supports the theme of 'Inclusivity and Sustainability' set by ASEAN's rotating chair Malaysia. … We will work to build the China-ASEAN Free Trade Area 3.0 and ensure the high-quality implementation of the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership."

The October 24 statement, made on the eve of Premier Li Qiang's attendance at the 28th China-ASEAN Summit (October 27-28, KL), confirms **PRC's institutional framing of ASEAN as an economic-cooperation forum, not a security-consensus forum**. Critically: **Taiwan is not named** in the East-Asia-cooperation paragraph either, despite the high-density political moment (Takaichi swearing-in, ASEAN summit week). The MOFA register treats ASEAN-as-bloc as below-threshold for cross-strait framing.

This sets up a **2026 stress test**: when the PHL chairs ASEAN in 2026, the PHL has a structural incentive to use the chairmanship to entrench the no-Taiwan-reference status quo (to avoid forcing a consensus collapse), while simultaneously building its bilateral Taiwan-contingency posture (EDCA, Balikatan, RAA-JPN) outside the ASEAN frame. The bifurcation — "ASEAN-silent on Taiwan + bilateral-loud on Taiwan-contingency" — is itself a Day-11 predicate candidate (working name **P25: chairmanship-as-status-quo-anchor**).

### Five-Point Consensus on Myanmar as precedent

The Five-Point Consensus on Myanmar (April 2021) is the only recent precedent where ASEAN explicitly named a *member-vs-PRC-aligned-position* tension and reached a consensus statement. It is also the precedent that **demonstrably failed**: the Myanmar junta accepted the consensus but did not implement it, and successive chairs (Cambodia 2022, Indonesia 2023, Laos 2024, Malaysia 2025) progressively narrowed the active-engagement scope. The Myanmar case is therefore *negative evidence* for a hypothetical cross-strait consensus: ASEAN's revealed capacity to enforce a consensus that even names a contested party is bounded by the lowest-common-denominator member.

### Day-11 predicate-test relevance — P21 dimension

If ASEAN-as-bloc does not appear in the PRC.opponent_set even when the PHL chairs (q-A3 finding), this is *evidence for* P21's venue-conditional formulation: PRC selects opponents at the bilateral or trilateral level (US, JPN, ROC, sometimes PHL) and treats multilateral institutions where consensus structurally excludes naming (ASEAN, UN General Assembly) as below-threshold. The MOFA-PRC October 24 transcript [Source #A13] is the load-bearing anchor: a high-density political moment (Takaichi, ASEAN summit) yields a single Takaichi-naming + zero ASEAN-Taiwan-naming. Venue conditionality holds.

## q-A4 — PRC-PHL kinetic baseline (AMTI primary timeseries)

The CSIS Asia Maritime Transparency Initiative (AMTI) published "Holding the Line: China's Expanding Patrols around Scarborough Shoal" on **2025-06-16** [Source #A14], based on automatic identification system (AIS) data from August 2024 through May 2025. The article provides the Day-11 *equivalent* of the Day-10 JCG-Senkaku contiguous-zone timeseries that Nisaba audited at +52% post-2021-海警法 level shift. The Scarborough series is comparably clean and dispatch-ready.

**AMTI-computed binned means** [Source #A14]:
- CCG: Aug-Dec 2024 mean = 48.0 ship-days/month; Jan-May 2025 mean = 94.6 ship-days/month → **+97% level shift** in the first five months of 2025 relative to the previous five months.
- PCG/BFAR: Aug-Dec 2024 mean = 20.5; Jan-May 2025 mean = 30.2 → **+47% level shift** (Philippine side increase smaller in absolute and relative terms; AMTI attributes the Nov-2024 zero to PCG redirection to disaster relief + Indonesia port visit + Second Thomas Shoal resupply, plus combat-drills, i.e. a capacity-allocation constraint rather than a policy step-back).

### Extension into Sept 2025 — first serious-injury incident

USNI News (2025-09-16, by Aaron-Matthew Lariosa) reports [Source #A15] that on **16 September 2025**, the Philippine Coast Guard vessel **BRP Datu Gumbay Piang (MMOV 3014)** sustained water-cannon attacks from **China Coast Guard Zhaojun-class large patrol ship 5201** and **Type 301-class coastal patrol boat 21562** approximately 14 nautical miles east of Scarborough Shoal, in a 30-minute encounter. The CCG-5201 water cannon **tore through Datu Gumbay Piang's bridge aft window**, injuring one PCG sailor with lacerations to the right ear from flying glass and damaging five air-conditioning units and the captain's cabin. The incident is the first reported PHL personnel injury since the June 17, 2024 Second-Thomas-Shoal incident (PHL Navy SEAL lost a thumb).

The September 16 incident was accompanied by a **PLA Navy Type 054 frigate Ma'anshan** radio broadcast warning of a "prompt live fire exercise" at four coordinates directly east of Scarborough Shoal. This is the **first PLAN frigate (not CCG) live-fire announcement** in the Scarborough sequence per the USNI account [Source #A15] — a register-elevation from CCG-administrative to PLAN-military.

Contextual surrounding events per USNI:
- Mid-2025: collision between a Chinese cutter and a Chinese destroyer during interdiction of a PHL patrol boat (Chinese-side intra-fleet collision, not cross-flag) — first publicly known instance.
- Aug 2025: US Navy held a Freedom-of-Navigation operation at Scarborough Shoal.
- 2025-09-15: PRC announced the "Huangyan Island National Nature Reserve" at Scarborough — sovereignty-escalation through nature-reserve declaration, similar in shape to the 2012 PRC sovereignty consolidation pattern.

### Day-11 P22 + Nisaba audit relevance

The AMTI primary series is **Nisaba-audit-ready**: 10 clean monthly observations on two series (CCG, PCG/BFAR) plus a derived series (interactions). The pre-2025 mean = 48.0 CCG ship-days/month; post-Jan-2025 mean = 94.6. The AMTI-self-reported +97% lift is testable via t-test on the 5-vs-5 binned series, and the **regime-change date hypothesis** is Jan 2025 (CCG-side) coinciding with Marcos Kadiwa-mission expansion and pre-Balikatan-2026 posture-building.

P22 test (kinetic-tempo acknowledgment): the AMTI series gives the **Philippine-side observation**; the missing dimension is **whether PRC selectively acknowledges the level shift in MOFA register vs. MoD register vs. CCG-administrative register**. q-A5 carries that test.

P22 sub-test (individual-act-to-state-signal elevation): the 2025-09-16 Datu Gumbay Piang incident gives one **individual act** (CCG-5201 ship-name + Type 301-21562 specific hull number + named PLAN-Ma'anshan frigate); the PRC response framed it as "illegal activities" requiring "regulatory measures in accordance with the law" [Source #A15 quoting CCG release]. PRC did *not* elevate the incident to "state-signal" language in the CCG release — the register stayed at administrative/regulatory rather than military/political. This is **inconsistent with Day-10's Murata-case finding** where PRC MoD elevated an individual GSDF officer's act to state-signal language. q-A5 mirror test (whether MOFA-PRC or TAO-PRC elevates the Sept-16 incident) is therefore critical for P22 generalisation.

## q-A5 — PRC framing on PHL-Taiwan linkage (Aug-2025 MOFA-PRC episode)

The natural P21/P23-mirror anchor on the PRC side is the **August 11-13, 2025 MOFA-PRC press-conference sequence** in response to Marcos's "physical geographic location" remarks (Day-11 q-A1 anchor) and to the DFA reaffirmation of the One-China Policy.

### The August 2025 episode — what is in the public record

Spokesperson **Guo Jiakun** at MOFA-PRC press conferences on or around August 11-13, 2025, used the formulation [Source #A16 — Newsweek + CGTN Europe transcripts via Facebook posts confirming the Foreign Ministry statement; Source #A17 — Wikipedia "Mao Ning" diplomat entry citing AP/Reuters wire reporting]:

> "Those who play with fire will get burned. … China urges the Philippines to stop playing with fire on the Taiwan question."

And the auxiliary formulation: "President Marcos needs to read more about the Taiwan question."

These are **MOFA-register** statements (Guo Jiakun is a MOFA spokesperson, not a TAO or MoD spokesperson). The "play with fire" formulation has documented PRC usage on Taiwan-question issues since the Xi-Biden November 2022 meeting [Source #A17]; its application to a non-US state directly on Taiwan-coupled remarks is a **register-elevation for the bilateral-PHL channel** — PHL is being addressed in the same lexical register that PRC reserves for US-on-Taiwan.

### Key features of the MOFA-PRC Aug-2025 framing

1. **The "play with fire" lexicon is borrowed from US-on-Taiwan precedent.** This is a *Day-10 P21 mirror inversion*: where the Day-10 finding was PRC.opponent_set venue-conditional with MOFA→{JPN}+TAO→{JPN, ROC}, the Aug-2025 episode shows MOFA→{PHL} explicitly in the Taiwan-question register. P21's PHL membership in the MOFA opponent_set is therefore **confirmed for Taiwan-coupled venue** (Marcos remarks on Taiwan) but **not confirmed for Balikatan/EDCA venue** (which would be MoD-register).
2. **The DFA reaffirmation (Source #A1/A3 from 11:00 notes) is acknowledged, not contested.** The MOFA response does not dispute the DFA's "clear and unwavering" One-China-Policy formulation; it disputes Marcos's "physical geographic location" framing as a separate executive-register transgression. This is **direct evidence for P24** (executive-vs-DFA register split discovered in q-A1): PRC's response *itself* operates on the register-split, addressing Marcos and not Lazaro.
3. **PRC does not deploy the "stop playing with fire" formulation against Japan in the same sequence.** The Day-10 record shows MOFA-PRC criticism of Takaichi and Murata, but the lexical register is "deeply concerned" / "strong dissatisfaction" / "grave provocation" rather than "play with fire". The lexical-register discriminator therefore **separates PHL-on-Taiwan from JPN-on-Taiwan** in MOFA register: PHL gets the US-class formulation, JPN gets the regional-protest formulation. This is a *new* Day-11 finding (working name **P26: lexical-register country-class discriminator**) and warrants ZH-original verification (Inanna dispatch).
