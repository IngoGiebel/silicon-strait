## The numerical anchor — what changed between S1 thesis and 2026-05-08 LY passage

**S1 anchor (DPP-Lai pledge, late 2025):** NT$1.25 trillion / 8 years special defense package; 3% GDP defense 2026 → 5% GDP 2030.

**Actual 2026-05-08 LY passage:** NT$780 billion compromise (62.4% of DPP proposal; ~37.6% cut), structured as **NT$300B upfront + NT$480B contingent on US Foreign Military Sales LOAs**.

**Implication for S1 anchor:** the 5%-GDP-2030 trajectory loses ~38% of its top-line slope unless the NT$480B contingent tranche releases. The GDP-share path is now **state-contingent on Washington's FMS pipeline**, not a Taipei executive commitment.

## Verbatim — Cheng Li-wun (KMT chair) on the LOA-contingency frame

> "An LOA is a formal document under the U.S. Foreign Military Sales program that confirms a purchasing country's agreement to acquire specific defense equipment and authorizes the U.S. government to proceed with procurement."
> — FocusTaiwan / CNA, 2026-04-28 [Source #14], paraphrased explainer

> "Cheng said the KMT supports passing an initial NT$380 billion defense budget, which would be sufficient to cover the US$11.1 billion in arms packages approved for sale to Taiwan by the U.S. in December 2025. An additional NT$400 billion could be considered if a second round of LOAs is issued. Without such documentation, she said, there is a risk the extra funding could be misused by the ruling Democratic Progressive Party (DPP)."
> — FocusTaiwan / CNA, 2026-04-28 [Source #14]

> "Cheng also said her party is open to discussing investments in areas such as domestic drone development, provided proposals are clearly defined. The current plan, she said, remains vague and lacks concrete details."
> — FocusTaiwan / CNA, 2026-04-28 [Source #14]

**Analytical note:** Cheng's framing recasts the special package from a **Taipei-executive doctrinal commitment** (DPP's intent) into a **derivative function of Washington's FMS approvals**. This is a structural argument, not merely a fiscal-conservative one — it implicitly subordinates ROC defense planning to US procurement cadence, which has its own bottlenecks (Lockheed F-16V backlog, Raytheon Stinger production, etc., already documented Day-4).

## Composite-player implications for GWW3 ROC predicates (feeds S6)

The 2026-05-08 outcome strengthens the **two-axis composite-player thesis from S2**, but **refines** it:

1. **ROC-Exec doctrine** is bounded above by ROC-Leg ratification (NT$1.25T → NT$780B in actual flow);
2. **ROC-Leg ratification** is conditional on US-Exec procurement cadence (NT$480B contingent tranche;);
3. **KMT-internal payoff** is multi-objective: (a) avoid blame from US for under-investing, (b) preserve Beijing-engagement option (Cheng's "peace framework"), (c) protect November 2026 local-election prospects.

This produces a **3-layer gating chain**: PRC-coercion ↔ ROC-Leg-KMT-stance ↔ US-FMS-cadence ↔ ROC-Exec-execution. The Day-7 4-player frame (US-Exec, US-Leg, PRC, ROC) underweighted the **US-FMS-cadence** as a quasi-actor that physically gates Taipei's ratified-spend → actual-procurement flow.

**Proposed S6 predicate additions (skeleton — formalize at gww3-modeling phase):**
- `ROC-Leg.ratify(budget=NT$780B) :- US-Exec.LOA-issued(US$11.1B) ∧ KMT-caucus.compromise(Cheng↔Hsu-faction)`
- `ROC-Leg.release-tranche-2(NT$480B) :- US-Exec.LOA-issued(round-2) ∧ ROC-Exec.spec-clarity(drone, asymmetric)`
- `Cheng.peace-framework-credibility(t) ∝ -|NT$X(t) - NT$380B|` (cheaper-than-DPP signal to Beijing degrades as the actual spend grows)
