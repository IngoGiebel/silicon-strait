## q-J1 — Formal diplomatic frame

The Japanese government has held a constant formal position since 1972, and that position is restated essentially verbatim in every contemporary MOFA reference. The constancy itself is a load-bearing constraint on the game tree: Japan's legal posture forbids treating Taiwan as a state, but does not commit Japan to any particular interpretation of the *substance* of cross-strait peace.

### Anchor — 1972 Japan-PRC Joint Communiqué (Sep 29, 1972)

Article 2 of the Communiqué states that Japan "recognises the Government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal Government of China" [Source #1]. Article 3 — the load-bearing clause for the Taiwan question — reads in the official JPN-MOFA English text:

> "The Government of the People's Republic of China reiterates that Taiwan is an inalienable part of the territory of the People's Republic of China. The Government of Japan fully understands and respects this stand of the Government of the People's Republic of China, and it firmly maintains its stand under Article 8 of the Potsdam Proclamation." [Source #1]

The Japanese verb is **理解** (rikai, "understands") **and 尊重** (sonchō, "respects"), explicitly *not* **承認** (shōnin, "recognises"). This wording is the canonical Japanese formula and is the reason every subsequent MOFA Taiwan reference uses "non-governmental working-level relations" rather than any state-to-state vocabulary.

### Reaffirmation — MOFA Taiwan Basic Data page (last updated 2023-10-25)

The current canonical statement of the frame:

> "In line with the 1972 Japan-China Joint Communique, Japan-Taiwan Relations has been maintained as working relations on a non-governmental basis." [Source #2]

Concrete institutional consequences enumerated by the same page: bilateral relations are conducted through the *Japan-Taiwan Exchange Association* (Tokyo HQ + Taipei + Kaohsiung offices) on the Japan side and the *Taiwan-Japan Relations Association* on the Taiwan side. There is no Japanese embassy in Taipei and no Taiwanese embassy in Tokyo. Trade in 2022 was USD 54.6 B (Japan exports) + USD 33.6 B (Japan imports) [Source #2] — large enough that the "non-governmental" frame absorbs substantial real-world coordination, but the framing itself is preserved.

### Current-events restatement — MOFA Press Secretary statement on PRC exercises around Taiwan (2025-12-31)

The Press Secretary KITAMURA Toshihiro statement of 2025-12-31 — issued in response to the late-2025 PLA exercises around Taiwan — restates the three load-bearing items of the formal frame in the most compact form available:

> "1. The recent military exercises conducted by the Chinese military around Taiwan constitute actions that increase tensions across the Taiwan Strait, and the Government of Japan has conveyed its concerns to the Chinese side.
> 2. It has been the consistent position of the Government of Japan that it expects that the issue surrounding Taiwan will be resolved peacefully through dialogue.
> 3. Peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait are important for the international community as a whole. We will continue to monitor related developments with strong interest." [Source #3]

Three components worth noting for GWW3 instantiation:

1. **"Increase tensions" framing** — a calibrated upward-pressure verb, not an escalation verb. Japan reserves the harder vocabulary for actions it would prepare a kinetic response to.
2. **"Resolved peacefully through dialogue"** — Japan stakes a procedural preference (means: dialogue) without staking a substantive preference (outcome: any particular cross-strait political settlement). This is the diplomatic equivalent of refusing to be a co-determining player on the *political-integration* branch.
3. **"International community as a whole"** — collectivising the stake. Japan asserts that Taiwan-Strait peace is a global public good, not a bilateral PRC-ROC matter. This is the discursive lever Japan uses to legitimise its own military build-up *without* having to argue the cross-strait question on its own.

The frame is therefore **constant on substance** and **modulated on tempo**. Japan does not move the diplomatic frame in response to Chinese pressure; it moves the operational frame (see q-J2).

## q-J2 — Operational defense frame

Japan's operational frame moved more in the 2014–2025 window than in any decade since the SDF was founded in 1954. The key inflection points:

### Anchor — 2014-07-01 Cabinet Decision on collective self-defense

The Abe Cabinet Decision of 2014-07-01 reinterpreted Article 9 to permit collective self-defense in **三要件** (three conditions): an armed attack against a foreign country in a close relationship with Japan that threatens Japan's survival; no other appropriate means; and the use of force limited to the minimum necessary [Source #4]. The 2014 decision is the *legal* permission for Japan to act in a Taiwan contingency that does not directly attack Japanese territory.

### Operationalisation — 2015 Legislation for Peace and Security (effective 2016-03-29)

The 2015 PSL operationalised the 2014 decision by codifying a tiered "situations" framework:

- **重要影響事態 (jūyō-eikyō jitai)** — "important influence situations": situations that would significantly affect Japan's peace and security, in which the SDF may provide rear-area logistics/support to US or other partner forces without itself being engaged in combat [Source #5].
- **存立危機事態 (sonritsu-kiki jitai)** — "survival-threatening situations": situations in which a country in a close relationship with Japan is under armed attack, the attack threatens Japan's survival, and there is no other means — in which the SDF may exercise collective self-defense, i.e. use force [Source #5].

A Taiwan contingency does not automatically fall into either tier; the Cabinet must designate it. Public statements by senior officials (see Aso 2021, below) have explicitly contemplated that such a contingency *could* be designated as a "survival-threatening situation" — which is the highest legal tier Japan possesses outside a direct attack on Japan.

### Political-statement primaries — 2021

Two senior-official statements from 2021 are durable references for the operational frame:

- **Aso Tarō**, Deputy Prime Minister and Finance Minister, fundraising speech 2021-07-05: stated that a major Taiwan incident "could be related to a situation threatening Japan's survival" (存立危機事態に関係する), implying that the SDF could be authorised to exercise collective self-defense in such a case [Source #6]. This is the highest-ranking explicit public linkage of the *jutī tier* to a Taiwan contingency.
- **Abe Shinzō**, former Prime Minister, online address to a Taiwan think-tank 2021-12-01: stated that **「台湾有事は日本有事であり、日米同盟の有事でもある」** ("A Taiwan emergency is a Japan emergency, and is also a Japan-US Alliance emergency") [Source #7]. Although Abe was out of office, the formulation became politically operative inside the LDP and is now routinely invoked.

### 2022 three-document set — National Security Strategy, National Defense Strategy, Defense Buildup Program (Cabinet Decisions, 2022-12-16)

The three-document set commits Japan to:

- a defense budget rising to **2% of GDP** by FY2027 (a doubling from the long-standing ~1% ceiling);
- the acquisition of **counterstrike capabilities** (反撃能力, *hangeki nōryoku*) — i.e. the ability to strike adversary missile launch facilities, breaking with the post-1956 exclusive-defense (専守防衛) interpretation that had prevailed for sixty-six years [Source #8].

The three-document set is the *materiel* permission for Japan to do what the 2014 Cabinet Decision gave the *legal* permission for.

### Current operational restatement — Defense of Japan 2025 (MOD Annual White Paper)

The 2025 Defense White Paper, foreword by Defense Minister Nakatani Gen, opens with: "The international community is now facing its greatest trial since the end of World War II… Japan finds itself in the most severe and complex security environment of the post-war era" [Source #9].

It frames China's position in this environment as follows:

> "China has been swiftly increasing its national defense expenditures, thereby extensively and rapidly enhancing its military capability in a qualitative and quantitative manner and intensifying its activities in the East China Sea, including around the Senkaku Islands, and the Pacific." [Source #9]

And it announces two structural operational changes:

- The **JSDF Joint Operations Command (JJOC)** was established on **2025-03-24**, centralising peacetime-through-contingency command authority over Ground/Maritime/Air SDF plus space/cyber-domain units under a single C-JJOC reporting to the Defense Minister [Source #9].
- The **US Forces Japan upgrade to a joint force headquarters** is underway in step with JJOC, and discussions are advancing on "expansion of bilateral joint presence in the southwestern region of Japan" [Source #9] — the southwest-islands framing that operationally means Ryukyu / Senkaku / Yonaguni reinforcement.

Each of these moves expands the **Japanese-player action space** in a Taiwan-contingency game tree well beyond what was available a decade ago.

## q-J3 — Conflict-tempo baseline (East China Sea / Senkaku)

The Senkaku theatre is the *tempo channel* Japan and the PRC use to communicate without using the Taiwan channel. It would be inherited intact into any Taiwan crisis and would almost certainly escalate first.

### Pattern statement — Japan Coast Guard official page (海上保安庁: 尖閣諸島周辺海域における中国海警局に所属する船舶等の動向と我が国の対処)

The JCG's official Senkaku-incursion page states the canonical pattern in one line:

> 「月に数回の頻度で領海侵入を繰り返している」 — "Repeating territorial-water incursions several times monthly." [Source #10]

The page anchors the pattern's start at September 2012, when Japan nationalised three of the Senkaku islands, and notes (i) the September 2010 deliberate collision of a PRC fishing vessel with a JCG patrol ship and (ii) the January 2021 enactment of the Chinese **海警法** (Maritime Police Law / Coast Guard Law), which the Japanese government considers problematic with respect to international-law compliance [Source #10].

The JCG publishes monthly tabular data through May 2025 (令和8年5月) [Source #10]; pulling those tables and computing the monthly time series is a Nisaba 🌾 numerical-audit task best done at q-J3 Day-10 EN-DRAFT time, not in scoping.

### Why this matters for the game tree

The Senkaku-channel tempo is the most reliable signal Japan has of PRC escalation appetite, and it has been used as such by both Tokyo and Beijing for over a decade. Three implications:

1. **Pre-positioning is already paid for.** Japan does not need to introduce a new "raise tempo in Senkaku" move into the game tree on Day-10; it inherits one from a thirteen-year-old baseline.
2. **Crisis-coupling risk.** A Taiwan contingency would arrive coupled to an already-warm Senkaku channel. The probability of pure Taiwan-Strait-only kinetic events without ECS spillover is low. This is *not* a small effect: the partition between the eight pruned branches and the six stochastic branches in the Day-9 framework may need a Japan-side adjustment so that ECS-and-Taiwan-coupled scenarios are explicitly modelled.
3. **Diplomatic-frame stability + tempo modulation = Japan's signature move.** Japan does *not* move the formal diplomatic frame in response to Chinese pressure (see q-J1). It modulates the Senkaku tempo and the operational frame. A Day-10 EN-DRAFT prediction is therefore: in a Taiwan crisis, Japan's first move will be a step-up in JCG/MSDF presence around the Senkakus, *not* a diplomatic statement re-framing Taiwan.

Three concrete artefacts:

2. **Two predicates that may need to enter `gww3/predicates.gww3`** — `coupled_ecs_taiwan_escalation` (binary; gates whether Senkaku-only and Taiwan-only sub-branches are mergeable), and `j_collective_self_defense_threshold` (function from PRC kinetic-act type to Cabinet designation probability). The second is empirically thin (one historical near-trigger, no actual trigger) and should be modelled as a stochastic branch, not a deterministic prune.
3. **One pruning candidate** — the "Japan formally re-recognises Taiwan as a state" branch can be eight-pruned, on the same canonical-enumeration logic Day-8 / Day-9 used: every primary above re-anchors the 1972 frame, and the cost of breaking it is incommensurable with anything Japan would gain by doing so in a crisis window.
