- **URL:** https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/taiwan/who-we-are_en?s=242
- **Outlet:** EEAS (European External Action Service) — official EU institutional self-rendering
- **Source class:** EU-official-institutional self-description, Tier-1
- **Date accessed:** 2026-05-21
- **Verbatim load-bearing quotes:**
  - **Founding statement:** "The European Economic and Trade Office, established in 2003 as the EU Office in Taiwan, is the official representation of the European Union in Taiwan."
  - **Mandate:** "Represent, promote and advance the interests and values of the European Union in Taiwan" and "Develop and strengthen bilateral relations in trade and investment, policy cooperation, culture, education, research and innovation."
  - **Internal structure:** "Under the direction of the Head of Office, the Office is comprised of three sections: Political, Press and Information Section. Trade Section. Administration Section."
- **What this primary establishes:**
  - **CRITICAL self-contradiction in EEAS's own rendering:** the page says "established in 2003 *as the EU Office in Taiwan*" — i.e. EEAS implies the office was *originally* named "EU Office in Taiwan" and then renamed to "European Economic and Trade Office" at some later point. This is the inverse of the EP-2021-resolution narrative that "EETO has always been the name" and the EP wants it changed *to* "EU Office in Taiwan." The EEAS sentence either: (a) is wrong (most likely — EETO has been the name since 2003), (b) reflects an internal-rename that EEAS has not publicised, or (c) refers to the office's URL-slug "EU Office in Taiwan" being the internal directory name while the public-facing name remained EETO. **Inanna source-validation candidate: cross-check against EEAS organigramme + COM legal acts 2003-2005 for the actual original naming.**
  - **Three-section structure mirrors a Delegation's standard layout** (Political / Trade / Admin) — i.e. the office is functionally configured like a Delegation but not legally categorised as one. This is the visible footprint of the "diplomatic internally, non-diplomatic externally" double-coding.
  - **The verbatim phrase "official representation"** — note that EU member-state diplomatic missions abroad are formally categorised as "Delegations" since Lisbon 2010 (Council Decision 2010/427/EU Art. 5 establishing EEAS organisation); the EETO is referenced in the EEAS URL-tree as `/delegations/taiwan_en` but the human-readable name avoids the term "delegation."

This is the legal-status workaround that lets EU institutions interact with Taiwan without violating one-China policy.
  - **The 7-dialogue institutional architecture** (bilateral trade consultations / mid-term review / industrial-policy / digital-economy / 4+ sectoral working groups) — this is the actual substantive EU-Taiwan engagement. Note: no political dialogue, no security dialogue, no human-rights dialogue named in this primary (though EPRS P-B5#4 confirms annual human-rights consultations exist separately).

## Cross-reference findings (institutional architecture synthesis)

### F-B5-S1 — EETO institutional double-coding
The full picture of EETO's institutional status across all 5 primaries:

| Dimension | Public-facing rendering | Internal-systems rendering | Structural significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Office name** | "European Economic and Trade Office" | URL-tree `/delegations/taiwan_en` | External: trade-only | Internal: Delegation-class |
| **EEAS email** | n/a (public) | `DELEGATION-TAIWAN@eeas.europa.eu` | Internal Delegation-class lexical adoption |
| **Section structure** | Trade-focused name | Political / Trade / Admin (3 sections, same as standard Delegation) | Functionally configured as Delegation |
| **MOFA-Taiwan category** | "Foreign Institutions in Taiwan" (non-diplomatic) | n/a (Taiwan does not get internal EEAS access) | Taiwan accepts the unofficial public framing |
| **EP push** | "Rename to EU Office in Taiwan" (2021 resolution, 580-26) | n/a | EP rejects the double-coding ambiguity |
| **Council/HR-VP** | Silent on rename (no Council Decision since 2021) | n/a | Council preserves the ambiguity |

**This double-coding is the institutional ground-truth of EU-Taiwan relations: the EU operates like-it-is-a-Delegation in actual function while maintaining like-it-is-not-a-Delegation in public-facing terminology. PRC tolerates this because the public-facing rendering preserves the one-China policy formulation; EU tolerates this because the internal functioning provides Delegation-equivalent capacity.**

### F-B5-S2 — The doctrinal-gap mechanism (why EU-Taiwan ties can deepen without recognition crisis)

Three nested doctrinal formulations, with each EU institution choosing a different one:

1. **PRC "one China principle"** (most expansive): PRC is sole legal government + Taiwan is part of China + no third state may have ANY official/unofficial ties suggesting otherwise. **Used by:** PRC MFA (q-B4 P-B4#1 primary verbatim).
2. **EU "one China policy"** (medium): PRC is sole legal government + no official ties with Taiwan. **DOES NOT include** "Taiwan is part of China" territorial-integrity claim. **Used by:** EEAS, Commission, Council, HR-VP. **EU formulation = PRC formulation minus territorial-integrity clause + minus unofficial-ties prohibition.**
3. **EP positions** (narrowest): EP Feb-2024 resolution explicitly states "neither Taiwan nor China is subordinate to the other" + "China's territorial claims have no basis in international law" — i.e. EP rejects even the territorial-integrity framing PRC uses. **EP formulation = EU formulation minus PRC-recognition-as-legal-government clause (de facto).**

**Mechanism:** Taiwan attacks the PRC formulation publicly (P-B5#5 MOFA: "refute China's false claim") while accepting the EU formulation. EP advances even further than the EU consensus. PRC retaliates against EP positions (q-B4 P-B4#1: 严正交涉 for Czech-EP-class engagement) but cannot retaliate against the EU consensus (because the EU consensus already concedes PRC-as-sole-legal-government). **This gap-mechanism is what lets sectoral dialogues, the trade-and-investment-dialogue upgrade (2021), the liaison-officer post on disinformation (2024), and the institutional double-coding all exist without producing recognition-crisis.**

### F-B5-S3 — Liaison-officer post on disinformation as the institutional probe

The liaison-officer post is the smallest visible upgrade to EETO since the 2021 trade-and-investment dialogue. Key facts:
- **Recommended:** EP September 2022 resolution on Taiwan Strait situation (RC-B9-0389/2022).
- **Implemented:** Between 2022 and 2024 (exact announcement date pending Inanna source-validation pass).
- **Welcomed:** EP RC-B10-0134/2024 (UN Res 2758 misinterpretation resolution) — "welcomes the posting of a liaison officer at the European Economic and Trade Office in Taiwan to coordinate joint efforts to tackle disinformation and interference as a first important step towards deeper EU-Taiwan cooperation."
- **Rhetorical-domain choice — disinformation, not security or defence:** The liaison-officer post operates in the "countering disinformation" domain (one of Taiwan MOFA's 6 publicly-declared cooperation domains; P-B5#5). This domain choice is deliberate — disinformation cooperation has plausible-civil-purpose framing (information integrity, civil protection) and avoids the security/military framings that would trigger PRC kinetic-tier response.
- **What this establishes:** The liaison-officer post is the **probe variable** for whether EU-Taiwan engagement-deepening can proceed without PRC retaliation. As of Day-12 corpus: no PRC retaliation observable on this specific upgrade (PRC retaliation has remained focused on country-level Tsai-call/HoS-rapprochement events, not on EU-institutional sectoral upgrades). **This is consistent with D12.1 deterministic-floor finding: PRC retaliation infrastructure is calibrated for country-level individual-act provocations, not for EU-institutional sectoral upgrades within the existing one-China policy framework.**
