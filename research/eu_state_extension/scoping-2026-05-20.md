## Why EU is the right Day-12 thread

The 2026-05-20 06:00 cron-A fire transitioned Day-11 to `publications_made` and seeded Day-12 EU scoping into `state.fire_log_2026_05_20[0].day12_scoping_seeds`. The justification chain:

1. **Day-11 spillover predicates carry to a non-isomorphic substrate.** P21 (PRC.opponent_set venue-conditional) instantiated cleanly for PHL via the TAO 2025-09-10 Peng Qing'en PHL-naming on Taiwan-coupled substrate; it now needs a test on a substrate that **shares the Taiwan-coupling vector but breaks the kinetic-proximity vector**. The EU is that substrate: it engages PRC on Taiwan via diplomatic and parliamentary instruments, but has zero kinetic surface in the Western Pacific.
2. **D11.2 weaker-substrate hypothesis explicitly delegated to Day-12.** Day-11 deferred the `D11.x — formal recognition of ROC under weaker unofficial-relations substrate` test because PHL's MECO/TECO substrate is structurally similar to JPN's Interchange Association; running PHL and EU as parallel weaker-substrate tests would muddy the methodology. EU's European Economic and Trade Office (EETO, Taipei, founded 2003) is the substrate-fit test: shape similar to MECO/TECO but multilateral and consensus-bound.
3. **GWW3 player-set extension logic — multilateral first time.** Day-10 promoted JPN as a single top-level node; Day-11 promoted PHL as a single top-level node; Day-12 promotes the EU as a **constraint-bloc node** with member-state sub-nodes for Lithuania (LT), Czechia (CZ), and Hungary (HU) as test cases. This is the first Day in the series where the new player is not a single state — directly answers the latent question from Day-10/Day-11 of how the GWW3 frame handles non-state and supra-state actors.
4. **Substrate non-isomorphism is the methodological signal.** A predicate that survives JPN → PHL → EU has earned the *PRC-side structural* label. A predicate that fails at EU but passed JPN+PHL has earned the *kinetic-proximity dependency* label. That diagnostic is the Day-12 publication value, not the EU framing per se.

## Player-tag decision

The thread is called **`eu_state_extension`** (single name, not `eu_lithuania_czech_state_extension`) because:
- The Day-10/Day-11 player extensions treated each new player as a *single top-level node* (JPN, PHL).
- For Day-12 the right top-level node is **EU-as-bloc**, with Lithuania and Czechia appearing as *member-state sub-nodes inside the bloc's constraint surface*, and Hungary appearing as the *deterministic veto floor* that locks the bloc's consensus-bound posture.
- The model promotion is *one new compound player (EU-bloc with member-state sub-nodes)*; the member states do not become top-level nodes themselves. This is structurally different from JPN (single state) and PHL (single state with ASEAN constraint envelope) — the EU is the first case where the constraint envelope IS the player.

If at the end of Day-12 the Lithuania or Czechia sub-nodes require their own top-level predicate sets, that is a Day-13+ rescope (most likely a per-member-state thread carved out of `eu_state_extension`), not a Day-12 mid-stream pivot.

## Eight-pruning candidate (Day-12 D12.x)

- **D12.1 EU formally recognises the ROC at bloc level.** Deterministically blocked by:
  - **Council unanimity requirement on CFSP foreign-recognition decisions** (TEU Art. 31(1)) — recognition decisions are CFSP, not QMV-eligible. Hungary's pro-PRC alignment under Orbán makes consensus structurally impossible through at least 2027 (Hungary's 2026-H2 Council presidency cadence + Hungary's vetoes of 2022-2024 EU statements on China/HK as established pattern).
  - **TFEU Art. 4(3) loyal cooperation duty** — even if QMV applied, member states owe consistency with EU positions. The 1975 EU-PRC framework + the 2003 "One China policy" reaffirmation in EU-PRC Joint Communiqué establishes the bloc's recognition floor.
  - **Treaty-level amendment to override unanimity** — requires Convention + IGC + ratification by all 27 → multi-decade lift; not addressable on a Davidson-window timescale.
  - Conclusion: D12.1 is **deterministically blocked at bloc level** for the analysis window. Pruning candidate retained.

- **D12.2 candidate (subject to research) — EU member state formally recognises the ROC (Lithuania-or-Czechia case).** This is the *direct parallel* to Day-11 D11.2 (PHL formally recognises ROC) on EU member-state substrate. Verification needed:
  - Lithuania's 2021 "Taiwanese Representative Office Vilnius" name change is the **closest existing approach** to formal recognition — but it was explicitly *not* a recognition act (the office remains an unofficial-relations vehicle, not an embassy). The Lithuanian Foreign Ministry and Seimas were explicit on this.
  - Czechia's Pavel-Tsai 2023 phone call + Senate visits are *state-signal acts* short of recognition.
  - The **TFEU Art. 4(3) loyal cooperation duty** binds member states to EU CFSP positions. A unilateral recognition by a member state would be a treaty-violation event with EU infringement-procedure exposure.
  - The Lithuanian and Czech political-economy substrates (EU funds dependency, EU trade exposure, NATO-Article-5 stake) make the cost of a formal-recognition step structurally prohibitive — not just politically.
  - Conclusion: pending verification, D12.2 at member-state level is also deterministically blocked, but for **bloc-loyalty-cost** reasons rather than the bloc-level unanimity reasons. If verified, that gives a two-distinct-deterministic-floor result on the same eight-prune-candidate shape — methodologically informative.

The Day-11 D11.1 (PHL revokes EDCA) had a single-pathway deterministic floor (legal+political+economic-exposure stacking). Day-12 D12.1 has a unanimity-floor that is structurally different. Day-12 D12.2 (if verified) adds a third deterministic-floor type (bloc-loyalty-cost). The pruning catalogue grows in *floor-type* diversity, not just in count.
