## Findings from primary 1 — TA-9-2021-0431

The 2021 EP recommendation is the bloc-level register-setter on which subsequent EU-Taiwan elaboration builds. Six load-bearing structural features:

**F1.1 — "Like-minded partners that share common values."** Recital A: "the EU and Taiwan are like-minded partners that share common values of freedom, democracy, human rights and the rule of law." This is the EP register's *highest-tier framing* of the relationship: the same "like-minded partners + shared values" formula EU uses for Japan, Korea, Australia, and Canada. The verb is *partners*, not *partner-states* — the formal recognition floor stays intact.

**F1.2 — "Under the guidance of the EU's One China Policy."** Operative paragraph (a): "intensify EU-Taiwan political relations and to pursue a comprehensive and enhanced partnership *under the guidance of the EU's One China Policy*; consider Taiwan a key partner and democratic ally in the Indo-Pacific on its own merit." The clause is doctrinally load-bearing: it threads partnership-deepening through One-China-Policy framing rather than around it. This is the structural pattern that Day-12 §4 P24 (executive-vs-DFA register split) test will examine — EP holds Taiwan-as-partner *under* the bloc's One-China commitment, in a single sentence.

**F1.3 — Bilateral Investment Agreement (BIA) urging.** Operative paragraph (b) urges the Commission and HR/VP to "urgently begin an impact assessment, public consultation and scoping exercise on a Bilateral Investment Agreement (BIA) with the Taiwanese authorities." This is the q-B5 institutional-substrate continuity test: the BIA-impact-assessment process is the institutional vehicle EP wants for EETO-mandate-expansion-by-stealth. Day-12 carries this forward to q-B5.

**F1.4 — Explicit named-individual condemnation (Vystrčil case) — Day-11 P22-individual-act-elevation candidate for EU substrate.** Operative paragraph (k): "condemn the threats of reprisals and pressure on Miloš Vystrčil, the President of the Czech Senate, by the PRC because of his visit to Taiwan in August 2020, and note that the Czech Republic and all other sovereign countries have the right to develop economic and cultural cooperation with Taiwan." This is exactly the *individual-act anomaly* Day-11 found structurally absent on PHL substrate — and on the EU substrate, the EP register *names the individual official* (Vystrčil), *names the substrate state* (Czech Republic), *names the act* (visit to Taiwan in August 2020), *names the responding PRC behaviour* (threats of reprisals and pressure), and *generalises the response* ("all other sovereign countries have the right"). The Vystrčil case is therefore the **first confirmed EU-substrate carrier for P22-individual-act-to-state-signal-elevation** — but in the *reversed* direction relative to Day-10's MoD-PRC elevation of the Murata case: here the EP register elevates the individual act to bloc-signal *defensively* on behalf of the member-state official, not *offensively* against an opposing state's official. The asymmetry is itself the predicate refinement: P22-individual-act has a *direction-of-elevation* dimension that PHL and JPN substrates did not surface.

**F1.5 — Semiconductor frame.** Operative paragraph (r): "Taiwan's central role in strategic industries such as the fifth generation of communication infrastructure (5G), as it is home to the world's largest foundry and the leading producer of semiconductors; … in the future microchips will play a central role in shaping the global order." This is the Day-12 §5 Hassaleh-Nexus binding-point — EP's semiconductor-strategic framing is doctrinally aligned with the EU Chips Act trajectory and provides the EU-namespace signal class for chip-supply-chain-disruption pricing.

**F1.6 — Indo-Pacific Strategy alignment + Council-Conclusions coupling.** Operative paragraph (l): "continue to support the inclusion of Taiwan in the EU's Indo-Pacific Strategy" — referring to JOIN(2021)0024 (the 2021-09-16 Joint Communication on the EU Indo-Pacific strategy) + the 2021-04-16 Council Conclusions on the same. This is the Council-Conclusions anchor for q-B1: the 2021 EP recommendation explicitly threads itself through the Council-and-Commission Indo-Pacific Strategy framework, which is the only EU instrument with formal-Council buy-in on Taiwan-Strait positioning (EP resolutions are not CFSP-binding; Council Conclusions are the binding-floor reference).

## Findings from primary 2 — P10_TA(2024)0030

The 2024 EP resolution is the **doctrinally most explicit EU bloc-level statement on Taiwan in the 2021–2026 window**. Seven load-bearing structural features:

**F2.1 — The EU "One China policy" / PRC "One China principle" distinction — DOCTRINAL LINE.** Recital F: "the EU continues to maintain its own 'One China' policy, *which is different from the PRC's 'One China' principle*; whereas the EU's long-standing position has been to support the status quo and a peaceful resolution of differences across the Taiwan Strait." This is the most important sentence in the EU corpus for the Day-12 publication. It explicitly bifurcates the EU and PRC doctrines: *policy* (EU; permits dialogue, status-quo support, peaceful-resolution framing) vs *principle* (PRC; non-derogable, sovereignty-claiming). The bifurcation is repeated in operative paragraph 6: "Reiterates the EU's commitment to its 'One China' policy as the political foundation of EU-China relations; recalls that the EU's China strategy emphasises that *constructive cross-strait relations are part of promoting peace and security*." The structural finding: the EU corpus does not treat One-China as a single doctrine shared with the PRC — it treats it as a *bilateral diplomatic floor* whose content the EU sets unilaterally. This is the EU's **architectural escape valve** for partnership-deepening with Taiwan without formal-recognition exposure, and is the foundation for the Day-12 §4 P24 four-pillar test (does HR/VP and Commission and Council all respect the same "policy not principle" distinction, or does the register fracture across pillars?).

**F2.2 — "UN Resolution 2758 takes no position on Taiwan."** Operative paragraph 5: "Underlines that UN Resolution 2758 takes no position on Taiwan; strongly rejects and refutes the PRC's attempts to distort history and international rules." This is a direct EP-register contradiction of the PRC's "Taiwan is part of China per UN-2758" framing. Recital E expands: "UN Resolution 2758 addresses the status of the PRC, but does not determine that the PRC enjoys sovereignty over Taiwan, nor does it make any judgement on the future inclusion of Taiwan in the UN or any other international organisation." The 2024 EP resolution is therefore the EU bloc-level *legal argument* against the PRC's UN-2758 framing — a substrate that PHL/JPN bloc-level rhetoric did not produce (the JPN substrate uses 1972-Communiqué "understands and respects" without the UN-2758 doctrinal contestation; the PHL substrate uses 1975-Communiqué "recognises" without the UN-2758 doctrinal contestation). EU is the **first substrate in the series with active doctrinal challenge to PRC's UN-2758 reading**.

**F2.3 — Operative-paragraph 1: Taiwan-as-partner.** "Reiterates that Taiwan is a key EU partner and a like-minded democratic friend in the Indo-Pacific region." Same "like-minded partners" register as the 2021 recommendation; the 2024 resolution carries the 2021 register forward unchanged. Stability of the EP register across the 2021–2024 window is itself a finding (no register-erosion or register-amplification observable; the bloc register is structurally constant).

**F2.4 — Joint Sword-2024B detail.** Recital N: "on 14 October 2024 the PRC launched a large-scale military drill, named Joint Sword-2024B, that simulated a blockade of Taiwan; whereas during this exercise a record number of 153 PRC aircraft, 18 warships and 17 PRC coastguard ships were detected around Taiwan." Recital O: median-line violations and coastguard "law enforcement" framing as PRC's *erosion-of-status-quo* tool. This is the substrate-fit detail for q-B4 (PRC framing test) — every PRC kinetic event in 2024 carries an EU-bloc-level acknowledgment that is structurally absent from the ASEAN bloc-level register (Day-11 found ASEAN-as-bloc not in PRC.opponent_set on Taiwan-Strait).

**F2.5 — Czechia cyber-campaign attribution — load-bearing for q-B3.** Recital U: "the PRC has long been pursuing a sophisticated strategy of targeting Taiwan with foreign information manipulation and interference (FIMI), including hybrid and cyberattacks." The Czechia-specific incident is *not* in the 2024 EP resolution recital chain (it is named in the Kallas 2025-05-31 Shangri-La speech below) — this is a Day-12 cross-substrate timing finding: the EP resolution from October 2024 anticipates the PRC-state-backed cyber-campaign against Czechia that Kallas would publicly attribute in May 2025. q-B3 (Czechia substrate) carries this forward.

**F2.6 — EETO mandate upgrade signal.** Operative paragraph 9: "welcomes the posting of a liaison officer at the European Economic and Trade Office in Taiwan to coordinate joint efforts to tackle disinformation and interference as a first important step towards deeper EU-Taiwan cooperation." The EP register frames the EETO liaison-officer post as the *institutional* signal of EU-Taiwan deepening — substrate continuity with the 2021 BIA-urging (F1.3) but with EETO as the carrier rather than a BIA. q-B5 (EETO institutional substrate) carries this forward — the EETO is now the active institutional vehicle for EU-Taiwan deepening, replacing the long-stalled BIA pathway.

**F2.7 — Bloc-of-allies signal — Australia + Netherlands UN-2758 motions.** Recital AE: "members of the Australian Senate and of the Dutch House of Representatives have recently adopted motions concerning the distortion of UN Resolution 2758 by the PRC." This is the EP register *positioning the EU's UN-2758 doctrinal challenge inside a wider democratic-bloc move* — the Australian Senate motion of 2024-08-21 and the Dutch House motion of 2024-09-12 are referenced as precedent for the 2024-10-24 EP resolution. Day-12 §4 spillover: this is **bloc-of-blocs signalling** that PHL substrate produced (Day-11 found PHL's bilateral-loud + ASEAN-silent bifurcation), but here the EU register *aligns with bloc-of-democracies* rather than bifurcating; a predicate-classification difference between Day-11 and Day-12 substrates worth tracking through §4.

## Findings from primary 3 — Kallas Shangri-La 2025-05-31

The Kallas Shangri-La speech is the **first major substantive HR/VP register on Taiwan-Strait in the Kallas tenure (December 2024–)**. Six load-bearing structural features:

**F3.1 — Taiwan-Strait formula — HR/VP register.** "On maritime security, we share a commitment to the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea. And the EU always speaks out when countries challenge this, including in the South China Sea. *We also speak out when tensions escalate in the Taiwan Strait and we oppose any unilateral attempt to change the status quo, including by force or coercion.*" The HR/VP register formula is **Strait-stability + unilateral-status-quo-change opposition + force-or-coercion clause** — structurally identical to the G7 formula used by all G7 foreign-ministers' statements 2022–2024. Notably *does not* name Taiwan-as-partner (that register is reserved for EP); does not name the PRC explicitly (the antecedent for "we oppose any unilateral attempt" is left structurally open); does not name specific PRC kinetic events (Joint Sword-2024B unnamed). The HR/VP register is therefore **Strait-focused** (one tier *under* the EP register on Taiwan-as-partner; one tier *over* the Council-Conclusions register on cross-strait dialogue).

**F3.2 — Kishida-coupling — Russia–Ukraine ↔ Taiwan-Strait analogy.** "Back in 2022, former Japanese Prime Minister Kishida warned from this stage that *Ukraine today may be East Asia tomorrow*. We are over three years into this war now. We speak in Europe about Ukraine's defence as our defence. But it is the defence of our common security." This is the EU's first **explicit cross-theatre coupling** at HR/VP register: Taiwan-Strait deterrence is framed not as a separate theatre but as a *consequence* of the Ukraine-defence imperative. Kallas's quoting of Kishida's 2022 Shangri-La line is doctrinally load-bearing — it brings the Japan-MOFA register (Day-10 substrate) into the EU bloc's foreign-policy doctrine via direct citation. The Day-12 §4 P21 (PRC.opponent_set venue-conditional) test now has a cross-substrate transmission case: JPN→EU register-transmission via Kishida's 2022 Shangri-La address now embedded in Kallas's 2025-05-31 HR/VP speech.

**F3.3 — China dual-use + cyber-Czechia + Baltic-cable trio.** "And China says it's neutral, but its *dual-use exports are fuelling Russia's war*. … In 2023, a *Chinese-flagged ship dragged its anchor for over 180 km in the Baltic Sea, damaging a gas pipeline and undersea cables*. Reckless at best; sabotage at worst. … Recently, *Chinese state backed actors were behind a malicious cyber campaign against Czechia*. These attacks are unacceptable." Three PRC-attribution events in a single HR/VP register: dual-use to Russia + Baltic-cable kinetic incident + Czechia state-backed cyber campaign. The register is **moderate-condemnation tier** — "reckless at best, sabotage at worst", "unacceptable" — distinctly **below** the US-tier "play with fire" register that Day-11 retracted-as-stated on the PHL substrate. The EU's PRC-register tier is therefore the **between-tier** in the GWW3 frame: stronger than ASEAN-bloc's structural-silence (Day-11), weaker than US-tier (Day-11 P26-retracted). This is the **predicate-classification finding for P26** on the EU substrate: P26 (lexical-register country-class discriminator) regains usefulness on EU substrate as a *three-tier* discriminator (US-tier / EU-tier / ASEAN-tier), even though it failed on PHL substrate as a two-tier discriminator.

**F3.4 — 800-billion-euro defence framing.** "we are making up to 800 billion euro available for defence investments; and are fast becoming a global security partner, too. We have agreed security and defence partnerships with Japan and the Republic of Korea, for example. We would like to deepen our security relations with India and Australia." The HR/VP register frames the EU as a **security-actor**, not only an economic-actor — a register-shift that the 2021 EP recommendation anticipated (F1.6, Indo-Pacific Strategy) and that the 2024 EP resolution institutionalised (F2.6, EETO liaison-officer). Day-12 §5 Hassaleh-Nexus binding: the EU-namespace defence-investment signal class is now active at the HR/VP register and pricing-relevant for European defence-sector equities.

**F3.5 — Czechia-cyber + Baltic-cable: the EU-bloc-substrate equivalent of Day-10's individual-act anomaly.** The Czechia cyber-campaign and the Baltic Sea cable incident are EU-bloc-substrate carriers for *individual-state harm by PRC-attributed actors*. These are exactly the **substrate-fit cases** for Day-11's P22-individual-act-elevation branch that PHL structurally could not generate — *if the PRC framing chain (MOFA/TAO/MoD) elevates these individual-state events to bloc-signal*, P22-individual-act generalises beyond kinetic substrate; *if the PRC framing chain leaves them at the country-bilateral register* (PRC-vs-CZE, PRC-vs-the-Baltic-coastal-state), P22-individual-act fails the EU substrate test. q-B4 (PRC framing seed) is the test-fire.

**F3.6 — Notably-not-named asymmetries.** Kallas's speech does **not** name (i) Lithuania or the 2021 Vilnius office decision, (ii) Czechia's Pavel-Tsai 2023 phone call or Vystrčil 2020 Taiwan visit, (iii) the EETO upgrade signal, (iv) Joint Sword-2024B by name, (v) Lai Ching-te by name. The Kallas Shangri-La register is **strait-stability + dual-use + cyber + Baltic-cable** — *not* Taiwan-substrate-rich, *not* EU-member-state-Taiwan-cooperation-rich. The asymmetry is itself the predicate-test signal: HR/VP register stays bloc-and-strait-stability-focused even as the EP register names individual-state Taiwan-cooperation cases. This is the **EU four-pillar register split** Day-12 §4 P24 was designed to test, and it holds in the affirmative direction on this fire's evidence.
