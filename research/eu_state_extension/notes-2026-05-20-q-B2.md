## Findings from primary 1 — urm.lt 2021-07-20

Four load-bearing structural features in the LT-MFA announcement:

**F1.1 — Cooperation framing, NOT state-recognition framing.** Lead paragraph: "The Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Lithuania informs that it has received a notification from Taipei that a Taiwanese representative office will be opened in Lithuania." The clause structure is *notification-received* (a formal but unilateral act on Taipei's side) not *agreement-concluded* (which would imply a state-level bilateral). The second paragraph anchors the substrate in "economic relations and cultural exchanges" and "people-to-people contacts between Lithuanians and Taiwanese" — both registers that international law treats as non-state-recognition activity (e.g. EETO Taipei does the same on the EU side). This is the **doctrinal escape valve** identical in shape to Day-12 q-B1 §F2.1 finding (EU "One China policy" vs PRC "One China principle"): LT operates the policy at the *member-state-level* with the same partner-not-state distinction. The publication's §4 P24 carrier hypothesis on LT substrate is supported: LT-MFA register, like EP register, threads partnership *under* the One-China framework without breaking it.

**F1.2 — Reciprocity announcement — LT trade office in Taipei planned for autumn 2021.** Third paragraph: "Lithuania plans to open a trade office in Taipei this autumn, in line with the Government's goal of diversifying Lithuania's export markets, and to seek new partners among democratic states in the Indo-Pacific region." This is the LT-side institutional equivalent of EETO Taipei: a **trade office**, not an embassy. Substrate-fit for q-B5 EETO comparison (2026-05-21 06:00 cron-A): LT and EU both build the Taiwan-bilateral via unofficial-relations trade-office institutional form. The LT framing also embeds the Lai administration's "key partner and democratic ally in the Indo-Pacific on its own merit" register (Day-12 q-B1 §F1.2 from TA-9-2021-0431 — note the same "democratic" + "Indo-Pacific" + "diversifying export markets" lexical chain).

**F1.3 — Cross-Baltic context — accreditation chain from Riga.** Fourth paragraph: "Taiwan has representations in 74 countries around the world, including 18 in the European Union. Taiwan currently has its representative office in Riga. The representative is accredited to all three Baltic states." The LT-MFA itself situates the Vilnius office as an *upgrade* from the Riga (Latvia)-based accredited representation, NOT as a recognition-act precedent. The publication's §4 P22 test (individual-act elevation) on LT substrate must distinguish *opening Vilnius office* (LT-MFA frames as accreditation upgrade) from *naming Vilnius office "Taiwanese"* (the actual point of PRC contention — but this naming question is *not* addressed in the LT-MFA 2021-07-20 statement at all, only by Taipei).

**F1.4 — What is NOT said.** The LT-MFA 2021-07-20 statement does **not**: (i) name the office's intended title ("Taiwanese" vs "Taipei" naming is absent — this came later, from Taipei's side); (ii) reference cross-strait military or political-stability framing; (iii) reference PRC reaction expectations; (iv) reference EU policy on Taiwan beyond the partnership-deepening generic. The substrate finding: the LT-MFA built the institutional vehicle (trade office reciprocity), and Taipei made the *naming* choice that became the contention point. This is structurally important for §4 P22: if the LT side did NOT name the office and the PRC retaliation cascade was triggered by Taipei's naming choice, then the *individual-act-elevation* on LT substrate runs Taipei (act) → PRC (response) → LT-as-state (cost-bearer) — a *three-step* elevation chain qualitatively different from Day-11 ASEAN-PHL where the bilateral was direct.

## Findings from primary 2 — WTO DS610 case page (2026-02-12 snapshot)

Six load-bearing structural features:

**F2.1 — Procedural arc to silent termination.** Timeline: Consultations requested 2022-01-27 → Panel requested 2022-12-07 → DSB established panel 2023-01-27 → Director-General composed panel 2023-04-18 (since parties could not agree on the third member) → MPIA (Art. 25 DSU) arbitration framework agreed by EU+PRC 2023-07-04 → Final-report estimate 2024-H2 (per Chair's 2023-10-13 letter) → **Panel work suspended at EU request 2024-01-25** (Art. 12.12 DSU; "for an indeterminate period of time") → ... → **2025-12-01 (WT/DS610/14): EU notified the DSB that "since the key objectives behind the dispute had been met and relevant trade had resumed, the dispute is terminated"**. The publication's §3.2 carrier finding: the EU's rule-of-law-and-WTO commitment on Lithuania ended in silent bilateral normalisation, no panel ruling on the merits. This is the **single most surprising substrate finding** of the Day-12 thread so far.

**F2.2 — The unprecedented breadth of third-party joiners.** Original-proceedings third parties: Australia, Brazil, Canada, Colombia, India, Indonesia, Japan, Korea, New Zealand, Norway, Russia, Switzerland, Chinese Taipei, Thailand, Türkiye, UK, US, Viet Nam — eighteen separate states. The chinaobservers.eu corpus item notes this is the second-most third-party-rich EU-China WTO dispute on record (after the 2012 rare-earths case post-Senkaku). The substrate finding: the LT case *globally* mobilised democratic-bloc concern as a precedent on economic coercion *during* the dispute's lifecycle — but the EU walked away from the merits without a panel ruling, which means *the precedent was never adjudicated*. Day-12 §4 spillover: this is **bloc-of-blocs signalling without bloc-of-blocs enforcement** — the EU substrate produces solidarity signalling (Day-12 q-B1 §F2.7 finding) but at the institutional level does not deliver enforcement.

**F2.3 — The Art. 25 MPIA arbitration agreement that never produced an arbitration.** On 2023-07-04 the EU and PRC agreed to an MPIA arbitration framework to substitute for the dysfunctional Appellate Body — a non-trivial procedural commitment by both parties. The framework was never used because the panel work was suspended six months later (2024-01-25). Substrate finding: the EU and PRC built the *appellate review structure for the case* before the EU then suspended the *underlying panel work* — signalling that both parties expected the case to go all the way through review, until the EU's 2024-01 reversal. The 2024-01-25 suspension date is therefore the **case-turn pivot**: something in EU-PRC bilateral that month or just before changed the case's destination.

**F2.4 — The "key objectives have been met" framing.** The EU's 2025-12-01 termination notification's chosen language is bilateral-normalisation-fit, not enforcement-fit. The EU is saying *the case got us what we wanted because trade resumed*; it is *not* saying *we won and China was held to account*. Substrate-fit for §4 P24 register split: this is the **EU Commission register / DG TRADE register** taking the silent-resolution path, qualitatively different from the **EP register** (Day-12 q-B1 §F1 + §F2: continues to elevate the Lithuania defence in operative-paragraph language through 2024) and from the **HR/VP register** (Day-12 q-B1 §F3.3 — Kallas frames PRC as "reckless at best, sabotage at worst" on Czechia cyber + Baltic-cable but does not mention Lithuania at all in the 2025-05-31 Shangri-La address). The four-pillar split on LT substrate has a *temporal* dimension within each pillar: EP escalates from 2021 through 2024; Commission/DG TRADE de-escalates from 2022-01 (file complaint) through 2025-12 (terminate).

**F2.5 — Lithuania is not the complainant.** This is structurally important: the WTO DS610 complainant is the European Union, not Lithuania. Lithuania does not appear as a separate WTO member-state party to the dispute (EU member states are bound by the EU's CCP — Common Commercial Policy — and the Commission represents them in WTO disputes per TFEU Art. 207). The substrate finding: even at the height of Lithuania's bilateral conflict with the PRC, **Lithuania's defence was bloc-level, not member-state-level**. This is the *bloc-loyalty-cost* deterministic floor empirically demonstrated: a member-state cannot run its own WTO case against the PRC even when it is the named target of the PRC's measures.

**F2.6 — What the case did NOT prove on the merits.** Because there was no panel ruling, the substantive question — *whether China's measures were inconsistent with GATT Art. I:1 (MFN), III:4 (national treatment), V:6 (freedom of transit), X:3(a) (uniform/impartial/reasonable administration), XI:1 (quantitative restrictions); SPS Art. 2.2/2.3/3.1/3.2/5.1/5.2/5.6/5.8/8/Annex C; or TFA Art. 7+10* — was never adjudicated. The chinaobservers.eu corpus item anticipated this (2023-11: "the WTO does not work as it should … The dispute settlement process is, at best, slow and often ends years after it began"); the empirical outcome confirms it. Day-12 §6 (eight-pruning) implication: economic-coercion-via-WTO is *not* a pruning-floor (the WTO route does not deterministically prevent or reverse economic coercion); rather, it is a *signalling channel* that the EU used as part of a bilateral-negotiation toolkit.
