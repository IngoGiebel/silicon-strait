## §1 — Whitlam-1972 lexical baseline (q-day15-3): the foundational finding of Day-15

**The AUS 1972 communiqué uses the IDENTICAL "acknowledges the position" formula as UK 1972.** This is the primary substrate-discriminating finding of the day, and it lands cleanly with both primary-text confirmation AND a rich negotiating-record archival trail that UK 1972 does *not* have publicly available.

### §1.1 — The verbatim formula (primary, MOFA-PRC official English text)

From MOFA-PRC's [official English text of the 1972 communiqué](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/eng/gjhdq_665435/2797_665439/3377_664820/3378_664822/202406/t20240612_11429130.html), updated 2002-04-19, accessed 2026-05-24 (Source #1):

> *The Australian Government **recognizes** the Government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal Government of China, **acknowledges the position** of the Chinese Government that Taiwan is a province of the People's Republic of China, and has decided to remove its official representation from Taiwan before January 25, 1973.*

The lexical structure is identical to the UK 1972 communiqué's "acknowledges the position of the Chinese Government that Taiwan is a province of the People's Republic of China" (cited verbatim in Day-14 EN-LOCK §3). This is **not** the Canadian-1970 "takes note" formula, and **not** the US-1972 Shanghai "acknowledges that all Chinese on either side of the Taiwan Strait maintain there is but one China" formula.

### §1.2 — The negotiating record: AUS *intended* "takes note", *settled* on "acknowledges the position", and *interpreted* the result as a deliberate non-endorsement

This is the structural surprise of the day. The DFAT Historical Documents Volume 22 (the 30th-anniversary commemorative edition published 2002) reveals via primary archival cablegrams that:

1. **AUS's opening negotiating position was the Canadian "takes note" formula.** The instruction from DFAT to the negotiator (Renouf in Paris) was to "seek Chinese agreement to a formula in which Australia 'takes note' of China's position that Taiwan was 'an inalienable part of the territory of the People's Republic of China'" — explicitly framed as "one of several formulas which various countries have used, all of them falling short of endorsing Peking's claim" (Doran 2022 quoting DFAT 345 minute from Anderson to Renouf, Source #2).

2. **PRC counter-proposed the maximal "recognises" formula:** "Australia 'recognises that Taiwan is a province of China'" — DFAT had pre-emptively flagged this as the predictable PRC "extreme position" trying to exploit Whitlam's urgency (Doran 2022 quoting DFAT 353 Shann→Whitlam minute, Source #2).

3. **The AUS negotiator categorically refused** the PRC maximal formula: "Australia should not be asked … to accept explicitly the Chinese position … Some middle ground had to be found" — language signalling no-further-compromise that drew from the Chinese Ambassador a counter-proposal in the form of words that became the final formula (Doran 2022 quoting DFAT 359 cablegram to Canberra, Source #2).

4. **AUS tried to push back to "takes note"** with Whitlam's approval, arguing Zhou Enlai had agreed to that formula in his July 1971 conversation with Whitlam-as-opposition-leader; PRC denied Zhou had approved it and "insisted that there could be no compromise on Australia's 'acknowledging' the position of China in relation to Taiwan" (Doran 2022 quoting DFAT 359 + 366 cablegrams, Source #2).

5. **DFAT identified a CN-EN lexical asymmetry as the structural concern**: "the Chinese characters used for 'acknowledging' are the same as those used elsewhere in the communique for 'recognising'" — meaning PRC could later read the Chinese text as Australian endorsement, even though the English text only acknowledged the PRC position (Doran 2022 quoting DFAT 361 Waller→Whitlam minute, Source #2). **This CN-EN lexical asymmetry is itself a Day-15 substrate-discriminating finding worth surfacing in the EN-LOCK.**

6. **AUS's contemporaneous self-interpretation was deliberate non-endorsement.** The Waller-to-Whitlam minute 381 of 1972-12-21 (primary archival, accessed 2026-05-24 via DFAT historical-documents-volume-22, Source #3) records that the draft post-signing instructions to AUS posts were "deliberately quite frank, in order to impress on other governments both that we have no need or wish to be other than forthcoming, and also that **we stood up to China on the Taiwan issue and achieved a satisfactory result**" (emphasis added). Nine governments were told the "principal point at issue" during negotiations was Taiwan, that Australia had "argued hard" for "takes note", but was satisfied because "'Acknowledges' is very similar to 'takes note'" (Doran 2022 quoting DFAT 382 cablegram to posts, Source #2).

### §1.3 — Substrate-discriminating implication for the Day-15 finding

The Day-14 EN-LOCK §3 demonstrated that the UK-1972 "acknowledges the position" formula opened doctrinal space for the FCDO-Tier-2 / FAC-Tier-3 distinction that P28(c) captures. The AUS-1972 lexical baseline confirms the *same doctrinal opening exists* in the AUS substrate — same formula, same lexical room for Tier-3 framings to go beyond Tier-2 Government framings without contradicting the foundational communiqué.

But AUS adds something UK 1972 does not have publicly: a **primary archival negotiating record** that makes the doctrinal interpretation explicit. The Waller-to-Whitlam minute 381 is the AUS-Government's own statement that "acknowledges" was chosen to *avoid* endorsement and to land "very similar to 'takes note'". This means the AUS substrate evidence-base for P28(c) is *richer* than the UK substrate, because the doctrinal interpretation can be cited from the AUS-Government's own contemporaneous primary sources rather than inferred from FAC select-committee interpretive practice 50 years later.

**Day-15 substrate-finding (provisional pending parliamentary-instrument-tier evidence in §2-§3 below):** AUS parallels UK as a positive P28(c) anchor, with the additional substrate-discriminating feature that AUS's doctrinal interpretation is documented in primary archival sources rather than emerging from later select-committee practice. This is a *substrate-strengthening* finding for the predicate family, not a substrate-discriminating finding in the sense of partitioning the Westminster substrate space.
