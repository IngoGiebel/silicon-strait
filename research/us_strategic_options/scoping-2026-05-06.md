## Why this thread, why now

The Day-6 publication ("Political Integration Scenarios — The Empty Bargaining Range") closed on a structural finding: PRC's 2019/2022-locked reading of the 1992 Consensus + the post-NSL HK precedent collapse the negotiable surface to roughly zero. That conclusion is incomplete without the third actor's option set. Day-7 names that actor's options, ranks them by political viability under the Trump-2 administration's actual decision rules, and connects each to the Hassaleh-Nexus signals that move under it.

This thread is *not* a US foreign-policy review. It is a constrained-optimization map: given (a) the empty bargaining range from Day-6, (b) the chip-supply chokehold from a future thread, and (c) the legacy TRA / TAIPEI Act / NDAA accumulation, what does the US realistically *do* — and which of those moves are credible enough to shift PRC's belief-state?

## Provisional thesis (subject to source-validation revision)

**The realistic Trump-2 menu has narrowed to three live options — selective semiconductor decoupling, transactional concession, and fortress-USA withdrawal — with the historically-default fourth option (strategic ambiguity + arms sales pipeline) no longer being the median because every component of the default has been individually destabilized.**

The supporting structure:

1. **Strategic ambiguity is dead.** The 2021–2024 Biden "we will defend Taiwan" series + the 2018–2024 TRA-plus legislative accumulation produced *strategic translucency*: opaque enough for plausible deniability, dense enough that PRC and ROC both plan as-if-intervention. Trump-2's first-quarter Taiwan-tariff signaling (Q1 2026) re-introduces the prior question — but the legislative density doesn't reverse.
2. **Arms sales pipeline is supply-constrained, not will-constrained.** The HIMARS / Harpoon / F-16V / MQ-9B / MK-48 backlog (~$19B as of 2025-Q4 per CRS) functions as a pseudo-alliance through materiel velocity. Velocity is the problem: deliveries are 2-to-5 years behind authorization, which means the pseudo-alliance is conditional on US industrial capacity rather than political will. Trump-2 hasn't accelerated the pipeline.
3. **Economic statecraft is the only durable rail.** CHIPS Act + Oct-7-2022 export controls + Oct-2023 + Dec-2024 expansions + Entity List growth span the Biden→Trump-2 transition without reset. This is the "settled" component of US Taiwan posture; everything else (NDAA Taiwan funding, alliance commitments, trade preferences) is 4-year-scoped.
4. **Alliance multiplication is real but indirect.** US-Japan 2024 defense guidelines update, Camp David trilateral (US-JP-ROK) 2023, AUKUS Pillar I+II, Philippines EDCA 2023 expansion all raise the regional cost of PRC action without making formal Taiwan commitments. Read by PRC as encirclement; read by ROC as durable backstop only if US itself remains committed.

This thesis is a working hypothesis. The 17:00 primary-source fire validates or revises it. If primaries contradict (e.g. recent Trump-2 signaling restores ambiguity-as-default in a way I'm under-weighting), the thesis flips before draft-lock.

### S1. Strategic ambiguity → strategic translucency (legislative + executive trace)
- 1979 Taiwan Relations Act: text + Section 2(b)(4) "consider any effort to determine the future of Taiwan by other than peaceful means" + Section 3 arms-supply provisions.
- Six Assurances (1982): Reagan-era unilateral assurances — text reconstructed from State Department release 2020-08-31.
- Three Communiqués (1972, 1979, 1982): doctrinal counterweights from PRC perspective.
- Taiwan Travel Act (2018), TAIPEI Act (2020), Taiwan Assurance Act (2021).
- NDAA Taiwan provisions FY2022/23/24 (Pacific Deterrence Initiative; Taiwan Security Cooperation Initiative).
- Biden's four "we will defend Taiwan" statements: 2021-08-19 (ABC), 2021-10-21 (CNN town hall), 2022-05-23 (Tokyo presser), 2022-09-18 (60 Minutes); + each White House walk-back transcript.
- Trump-2 first 100 days Taiwan signaling — primary tweets/Truth Social posts + USTR statements (to be inventoried at 17:00).

### S5. Economic statecraft — the durable rail
- CHIPS and Science Act 2022 (P.L. 117-167) + implementation rules.
- Oct-7-2022 BIS export controls (advanced computing + semiconductor manufacturing equipment).
- Oct-17-2023 expansion (closing 4090 + A800/H800 loopholes).
- Dec-2-2024 expansion (HBM + 24 SME categories).
- Foreign Direct Product Rule (FDPR) extensions to PRC-Taiwan supply linkages.
- Entity List additions 2022-2026 (cumulative count by sector).
- Outbound Investment Notification rule (effective 2025-01-02) — Treasury final rule.
- USTR Section 301 review 2024 finding + tariff schedule (semis 25% → ?).

### S6. Nuclear & extended deterrence (negative-space sub-thread)
- The Taiwan-not-a-treaty-ally fact and what it means for nuclear-umbrella extension. (Critical: Taiwan does not have an explicit nuclear guarantee.)
- Tactical-nuclear posture changes in INDOPACOM 2022-26 (B61-12 deployment, NSDM-91 succession).
- PRC nuclear doctrine evolution: 2020 PRC nuclear modernization (Pentagon CMPR estimates 200 → 600 → 1500 warheads trajectory).
- Strategic Stability talks status 2024-26.
- The unstated question: under what PRC nuclear-coercion scenario does the US still intervene conventionally?

### S7. Domestic political constraints (Trump-2 era)
- Trump-2 transactional Taiwan signaling Q1 2026: "Taiwan stole our chip industry" + 100% reciprocal-tariff threat.
- Congressional bipartisanship floor: Taiwan Caucus membership 2026, House FA + Senate FR Committee Taiwan-language consensus.
- 2026 midterms preview: Indo-Pacific salience in target races.
- PRC-side counter-signaling: Taiwan Affairs Office press briefings, MOFA presser response patterns to Trump-2 statements.

### S8. Game-theoretic frame (GWW3-precursor)
- The US has a multi-period commitment problem: deterrence requires credibility, credibility requires costly signaling, every credible signal raises PRC's belief that not-moving is increasingly costly to PRC.
- Schelling-style commitment-device analysis: legislative density (TRA + TAIPEI + NDAA) is itself a tying-of-hands; Trump-2 transactionalism is a deliberate untying.
- Connection to Day-6 empty bargaining range: if PRC's bargaining range is empty *and* US's commitment is partially untied, the equilibrium that emerges is mutual mobilization without negotiation — the "deterrence without diplomacy" trap.
