## Finding 1: H1 CONFIRMED — Shanghai Communiqué 1972 uses 认识到

**EN (Wikisource, para 12):**
> "The US side declared: The United States **acknowledges** that all Chinese on either side of the Taiwan Strait maintain there is but one China and that Taiwan is a part of China. The United States Government does not challenge that position."

**ZH (Wikisource, verified against 人民日报 1972-02-28 original):**
> "美国方面声明：美国**认识到**，在台湾海峡两边的所有中国人都认为只有一个中国，台湾是中国的一部分。美国政府对这一立场不提出异议。"

**Key verb: 认识到 (rènshí dào — "comes to understand/realize")**

This is lexically distinct from ALL previously tested substrates:
- ≠ 承认 (chéngrèn — "recognize/admit"; AUS/UK Class A collapsed)
- ≠ 注意到 (zhùyì dào — "take note of"; CAN Class B preserved)

认识到 is the weakest of the three verbs in the diplomatic-register hierarchy:
- 承认 = "formally recognize/admit" (strongest)
- 注意到 = "take note of" (middle)
- 认识到 = "come to understand/realize" (weakest — implies cognition without endorsement)

Source: Wikisource EN (en.wikisource.org/wiki/Shanghai_Communiqué), Wikisource ZH (zh.wikisource.org/wiki/中华人民共和国和美利坚合众国联合公报_(1972年))

## Finding 2: H2 CONFIRMED WITH TWIST — 1979 shifts "acknowledges" from 认识到 to 承认

**1979 Normalization Communiqué — recognition clause:**
- EN: "The United States of America **recognizes** the Government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal Government of China."
- ZH: "美利坚合众国**承认**中华人民共和国政府是中国的唯一合法政府"

**1979 Normalization Communiqué — Taiwan clause:**
- EN: "The Government of the United States of America **acknowledges** the Chinese position that there is but one China and Taiwan is part of China."
- ZH: "美利坚合众国政府**承认**中国的立场，即只有一个中国，台湾是中国的一部分"

**CRITICAL: In 1979, BOTH "recognizes" and "acknowledges" map to 承认.**

The same English word "acknowledges" maps to DIFFERENT Chinese verbs across the two communiqués:
- 1972: "acknowledges" → **认识到**
- 1979: "acknowledges" → **承认**

This is not a translation error. The Wikipedia ZH article on the three communiqués explicitly flags it as a known controversy: "承认"（中国发表的中文版为"承认"，美国发表的英文版为"acknowledge"；有关此处中译的争议…）

Academic reference: 黄安年. "究竟是'承认'还是'认识到'?——就中美上海公报中的一个史实问题答罗志田先生". 学术界. 2002年, (5): 105-114.

## Finding 3: 1982 August 17 Communiqué — no new Taiwan-status language

The 1982 communiqué reaffirms "the principles agreed on by the two sides in the Shanghai Communique and the Joint Communique on the Establishment of Diplomatic Relations" but does NOT introduce a new Taiwan-status formula. Its operative content is exclusively about arms sales reduction commitments.

One relevant usage of 认识到 appears in a different context:
> "双方认识到这一问题将会严重妨碍中美关系的发展" ("Both sides recognize/understand that this issue will seriously hinder development of US-China relations")

Here 认识到 is used descriptively (mutual understanding of a fact) rather than in the Taiwan-status formula context. This confirms that PRC diplomatic Chinese distinguishes between 认识到-as-cognition (descriptive) and 承认-as-endorsement (performative).

Source: Firecrawl search (MOFA-PRC bilingual treaty text, Wikisource ZH)

## Finding 4: TRA key provisions (secondary literature; primary scrape timed out)

Congress.gov scrape timed out. TRA key provisions verified from AIT and secondary sources:

- **§2(b)(3):** "to make clear that the United States decision to establish diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China rests upon the expectation that the future of Taiwan will be determined by peaceful means"
- **§2(b)(4):** "to consider any effort to determine the future of Taiwan by other than peaceful means, including by boycotts or embargoes, a threat to the peace and security of the Western Pacific area and of grave concern to the United States"
- **§2(b)(6):** "to maintain the capacity of the United States to resist any resort to force or other forms of coercion that would jeopardize the security, or the social or economic system, of the people on Taiwan"
- **§3(a):** Arms sales authorization — "the United States will make available to Taiwan such defense articles and defense services in such quantity as may be necessary to enable Taiwan to maintain a sufficient self-defense capability"

The TRA is US domestic law (P.L. 96-8, signed April 10, 1979), passed by both houses of Congress and signed by Carter. It creates the American Institute in Taiwan (AIT) as the implementing mechanism and establishes a legislative floor that EXCEEDS all other tested substrates' institutional provisions.

## Finding 5: Six Assurances (Reagan, 1982; declassified 2020)

1. No set date for ending arms sales to Taiwan
2. No agreement to consult with PRC on arms sales
3. No mediation role between PRC and Taiwan
4. No revision of the Taiwan Relations Act
5. No alteration of US position on Taiwan sovereignty
6. No pressure on Taiwan to negotiate with PRC

Passed as non-binding concurrent resolution by both houses of Congress in 2016 (H.Con.Res.88, S.Con.Res.38). Declassified 2020.

The Six Assurances operate as a **private-commitment counterweight** to the Third Communiqué (1982) — they explicitly state that the arms sales reduction commitments of the 1982 communiqué do NOT imply the six listed concessions. This creates a private-diplomatic floor that was designed to prevent the 1982 communiqué from being interpreted as a ratchet toward 承认-level endorsement.
