## 3. UNGA Resolution 2758 — the legal-interpretation battlefield

The 2024-2025 contestation around UNGA 2758 has become the single most active recognition-diplomacy front, *more* active than formal-state switches. The reason: PRC has effectively won the formal-recognition game (only 12 holdouts) but is losing the *interpretive* war about what UN-membership exclusion entails.

**The dispute (paraphrased from Source 4):** Does Resolution 2758 (1971) determine Taiwan's *political status* and bar its UN-system participation, or does it merely settle which government represents *China* at the UN?

**Resolution text (operative, 1971-10-25):** Recognized "the People's Republic of China as the only legitimate representative of China to the United Nations, and expelled the representatives of Chiang Kai-shek." Vote: 76-35-17. [Source 4]

**PRC interpretation [Source 4]:**
- Wang Yi: "Taiwan can only be referred to as 'Taiwan, Province of China'" and the resolution "entirely ruled out the prospect of 'two Chinas' or 'one China, one Taiwan.'"
- Senior PRC diplomat: "if China's representation did not include Taiwan, there would have been no need to expel the representative of Chiang Kai-shek."

**US interpretation, April 2024 [Source 4]:**
- Deputy Assistant Secretary Mark Lambert: "Resolution 2758 does not endorse, is not equivalent to, and does not reflect a consensus for the PRC's 'one China' principle." China "mischaracterizes the resolution by falsely conflating it with China's 'one China' principle."

**Parliamentary motions rejecting PRC's interpretation, 2024 [Source 4]:**
- Australia (Aug 2024): resolution "does not establish the People's Republic of China's sovereignty over Taiwan"
- Netherlands (Sep 2024): vote of **147-3** rejecting "China's interpretation"
- Canada (Nov 2024): resolution "did not establish Chinese sovereignty over Taiwan"
- UK (Nov 2024): resolution "is silent on Taiwan's participation in UN bodies"

**Reading:** the IPAC-coordinated parliamentary cascade is a multi-state *cascade Schelling move*. Each parliament's motion is non-binding domestically but lowers the cost of the next parliament's motion — a coordination game where the equilibrium is shifting from "no parliament dares challenge PRC interpretation" to "any democracy that *fails* to challenge looks suspect." This is GWW3 Diplomatic-dimension dynamics in pure form, and it has only become possible *because* PRC overplayed its hand by demanding the broad reading.

## 4. The Lithuania case — economic-coercion modulus

Lithuania is the cleanest case study of "name-as-coercion-vector" in 2021-2024 recognition diplomacy [Source 5]:

- **2021-11-18:** "Taiwanese Representative Office in Lithuania" opens in Vilnius. Naming uses 台灣 (Taiwan), not 台北 (Taipei) — the politically explosive distinction.
- **PRC response:** Recall ambassador, downgrade embassy to chargé d'affaires, expel Lithuania's ambassador from Beijing. Customs/trade restrictions also reported widely (not in this Wikipedia source — verify in Day 2).
- **2024-05:** Lithuania's president *requests* a rename of the office. Taiwan's foreign minister refuses: "Taiwan had not received any official request for name change and would not accept it even if such request was received."
- **Post-2024-10 elections:** Incoming Social Democratic government pledges restoration of full PRC relations but does *not* reverse the office arrangement.

**Key reading:** the Lithuanian case proves that name-level recognition decisions (二字之差 — "Taiwan" vs "Taipei") have material economic consequences. PRC treats the *signifier* itself as the strategic asset, not the underlying diplomatic relationship. This is the single most important fact for modeling PRC's coercion utility function.

## 5. The recognition-diplomacy game in GWW3 framing (preliminary)

Three observations to develop further in `gww3-modeling` phase:

1. **Two parallel games, weakly coupled.** Game A: formal-recognition switches, slow attrition, high stakes per switch (~$1–10B in promised PRC investment). Game B: legal-interpretation cascade (UNGA 2758), faster, lower per-move stakes but coordinating thousands of parliamentary actors. PRC is winning Game A and losing Game B. The two games are coupled via *international-organization participation* (WHA, ICAO, Interpol) — Game B losses translate into Game A pressure releases.

2. **Stickiness asymmetry.** The 12 remaining recognizers are a *self-selected stubborn equilibrium*. Each has already faced PRC offers and refused. Their priors include non-economic premiums (Vatican: religious-freedom; Eswatini: monarchy/stability; Pacific states: US-security framework). PRC's marginal cost to flip any one of them is rising — the cheap flips are gone.

3. **Sequencing premium.** Nauru's switch *two days* after the Lai election shows PRC has internalized that the *timing* of a switch carries informational value beyond the switch itself. This means recognition diplomacy is no longer a memoryless game; the Markov property fails. GWW3 modeling will need a Belief-Subgraph layer that tracks Taiwan's electoral calendar and PRC's expected responses to it.
