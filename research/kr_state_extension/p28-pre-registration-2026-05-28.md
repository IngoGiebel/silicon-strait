## §1 — Why pre-registration is needed here

The λ-class taxonomy consolidated on Day-18 (PASS-PASS-PASS-PASS-PASS-PASS / one ARCHITECTURAL-ABSENCE across seven substrates: PHL/EU/JPN/AUS/CAN/UK/US, six P28 PASS) has now matured to the point where each new substrate is no longer a *novelty* observation — it is a *test* of the taxonomy. Specifically, the Day-18 §3 closing observations name two regularities that can be falsified by future substrates:

1. **Class A → committee ceiling; Class B → 1st/2nd reading; Class C → enacted law.** Monotone, suggestive-but-not-causal.
2. **λ-class is determined by the rendering of "acknowledge / take note / recognise" verbs in the foundational bilateral communiqué.**

ROK is the cleanest available test because:

- The PRC-ROK normalisation communiqué was signed **1992-08-24** — 20 years after JPN 1972, 22 years after CAN 1970, 12 years after US 1979 — and is therefore not a derivative of the 1970s normalisation wave but a post-Tiananmen, post-Cold-War instrument with its own lexical choices.
- ROK is **the second post-1979 normalisation in the corpus** (the only other being substrates that are not yet in the sample). Day-18 §3 explicitly named "India, South Korea" as the substrates whose addition would test taxonomy robustness.
- ROK shares an institutional architecture niche with JPN (East Asian, non-Westminster, post-WWII US-aligned, semiconductor-relevant), making it the structurally closest neighbour to a substrate that turned out to be **unclassifiable** (JPN's 十分理解し、尊重する is outside the comparison space). Whether ROK falls inside or outside the comparison space is itself a finding.
- ROK is the substrate where the **semiconductor-supply-chain Nexus implication (Day-18 §5.4.1)** binds most tightly — Samsung + SK Hynix together account for the second-largest national semiconductor exposure to PRC after Taiwan itself.

**Why pre-register and not just publish predictions inside the Day-19 publication?** Because the publication itself will *read* the 1992 ZH-text, and any prediction "made" after that read is biased by the data. The skill's bias-balance step (Step-3) requires pre-registration *before* Inanna's dispatch returns the ZH-text rendering. The Inanna dispatch goes out next (§7); this file freezes the prediction now.

## §2 — Pre-registered λ-class prediction

**Primary prediction: Class A (collapsed, 承认).**

**Confidence band: 55–60%.**

Justification (structural priors only — zero data look at the 1992 communiqué):

- The 1992 PRC-ROK normalisation context was strongly PRC-favourable: post-Tiananmen (1989) PRC was actively rebuilding international standing; ROK was incentivised to deliver a strong recognition to support Roh Tae-woo's *Nordpolitik* and de-isolate the DPRK by making PRC's traditional Pyongyang alignment more conditional.
- Unlike CAN 1970 (where CAN was the first major Western recogniser and could extract concessions in 20 rounds of Stockholm negotiations) or AUS/UK 1972 (where the post-Shanghai-Communiqué momentum gave PRC a stronger drafting hand than 1970), ROK was negotiating from a structurally weaker position: ROK needed to break the PRC-DPRK lock more than PRC needed ROK recognition.
- The 1992 PRC drafting templates by that point were canonical 承认 ("recognises") variants. The likelihood that ROK extracted a 注意到 ("takes note") concession of the CAN type is low absent strong evidence of a Stockholm-equivalent multi-round push.

**Secondary prediction: Class B (preserved, 注意到 or similar).**

**Confidence band: 20–25%.**

Justification (acknowledged uncertainty):

- The ROK-PRC normalisation also included PRC's tacit acceptance that ROK would not recognise the DPRK government and would retain its Korean War-era alliance structure with the US. This was a substantive concession from PRC. It is plausible that PRC traded lexical floor at the Taiwan clause for non-recognition floor at the DPRK clause.
- The 1992 communiqué's English text is reported in secondary sources as "respects" rather than "acknowledges" (per non-blind general knowledge of Day-7 economic statecraft research), but the ZH primary text has not been verified yet. If the EN says "respects" and the ZH says 尊重 (zūnzhòng), this would put ROK in a **fourth class** alongside JPN (尊重 is part of JPN's verb-pair) — an unclassifiable substrate from the EN→ZH "acknowledge/take note/recognise" semantic field standpoint.

**Tertiary prediction: Unclassifiable (fourth class with JPN, 尊重 or 表示尊重 register).**

**Confidence band: 15–20%.**

Justification:

- JPN's 尊重 register is post-1972 East Asian PRC-normalisation default. If ROK 1992 adopted JPN-style language as a near-neighbour template (geographic proximity, structural similarity to JPN's post-war US-alignment status), ROK joins JPN in the unclassifiable bucket.
- The implication if so: λ-class is not a substrate-universal property; it is a **lexicon-family-bound** property, and ROK + JPN together define a second lexicon-family (the East Asian "understands/respects" family) outside the Western "acknowledges/takes note/recognises" family. This would be a major structural finding that requires the Day-18 §3 taxonomy to be reframed as Western-substrate-only.

**Strict-falsification rule:** If the 1992 ZH-text verb is *unambiguously* 承认, the Class-A prediction holds. If it is *unambiguously* 注意到 or a synonym (e.g. 注意, 留意), Class B. If it is 尊重 or any verb outside the {承认, 注意到, 认识到} set, the substrate is unclassifiable under Day-18 §3 taxonomy and forces a §3 reframe.

## §3 — Pre-registered advancement-ceiling prediction

**Primary prediction: Tier-1 ceiling, executive-discretionary register only.**

**Confidence: 65–70%.**

Justification (structural priors):

- ROK has a **unicameral** National Assembly (Gukhoe / 국회) — 300 members, single chamber, four-year terms. No Senate equivalent. No second-reading-of-second-chamber instrument architecture analogous to CAN Senate S-277.
- ROK National Assembly has a Foreign Affairs and Unification Committee (외교통일위원회), structurally similar to JPN Diet Foreign Affairs Committee and UK FAC. Whether the FAUC produces report findings exceeding the Tier-2 (Government / MOFA-ROK) framing is the load-bearing empirical question.
- ROK's Constitutional treatment of foreign relations vests strong executive primacy (Constitution Article 73 — President "appoints diplomatic envoys"; Article 60 — National Assembly "consent" required only on specific treaty categories). Legislative independence on Taiwan-status framing has no clear instrument vehicle.
- The Korean-language Taiwan discourse in National Assembly proceedings is structurally different: the dominant ROK-Taiwan framing has been **trade-and-investment** (Foxconn, Samsung-PRC, SK Hynix-TW supply-chain), not **status-narrowing**. A Bill C-343 / S-277 equivalent has no obvious ROK counterpart.

**Secondary prediction: Tier-2 framing exists (MOFA-ROK position-statements), but no Tier-3 register.**

**Tier-3 falsification trigger:** If the ROK National Assembly Taiwan-related catalogue (Inanna dispatch §7) surfaces any FAUC report, committee findings, or floor resolutions 2017–2026 that explicitly exceed the MOFA-ROK Tier-2 framing on Taiwan-status, the Tier-1 prediction is falsified and ROK joins UK/AUS/CAN/US in the Tier-3-capable bucket. Estimated 25% probability based on the JPN Diet committee discussions that did appear (Day-13 finding) — committee discussion is more common than Tier-3 instrument production, but the bar for the prediction is the latter.

## §5 — Cross-cutting pre-registered predictions

### §5.1 — Semiconductor-supply-chain Nexus binding

**Prediction: ROK provides the strongest semiconductor Nexus binding of any substrate.**

Samsung + SK Hynix together hold approximately 60% of global memory-chip production (DRAM + NAND combined). PRC is the largest consumer market for both. Day-18 §5.4.1 named this binding as a Nexus implication; ROK substrate is where this implication has the highest signal-to-noise ratio.

**Specific empirical prediction:** The ROK National Assembly's Taiwan-related catalogue (Inanna dispatch) will surface more Taiwan-mentions in trade/economy committees (산업통상자원위원회 / Trade-Industry-Energy Committee, 기획재정위원회 / Strategy-Finance Committee) than in the Foreign Affairs and Unification Committee. If this prediction holds, it directly supports the §5.4.1 Nexus implication: Taiwan is structurally a *commodity-supply-chain* question in ROK Parliament, not a *diplomatic-status* question.

### §5.2 — Inter-Korean confound (DPRK lever)

**Prediction: The Inter-Korean dimension acts as a *constraint* on ROK Taiwan-status framing.**

Justification: PRC maintains diplomatic and economic leverage with DPRK that no other substrate-country can match. ROK's strategic objective of moderating DPRK behaviour through PRC mediation creates a structural incentive for ROK to *avoid* Taiwan-status-narrowing instruments that would alienate PRC. This is qualitatively distinct from the AUKUS / Five Eyes / EU institutional-architecture frame.

**Empirical test:** If the ROK National Assembly catalogue shows that Taiwan-related motions or statements 2017–2026 systematically de-escalate during periods of active DPRK provocation (nuclear tests, missile launches, joint US-ROK military exercises postponement), the Inter-Korean confound is operative. If they are independent of DPRK timeline, the confound is at most weak.

### §5.3 — KORUS treaty corpus angle

**Prediction: The KORUS FTA (2007 signed, 2012 in force) and KORUS Mutual Defense Treaty (1953) do not contain Taiwan-status language but are *indirectly* relevant** through the *non-Taiwan-status* economic and security integration with the US.
