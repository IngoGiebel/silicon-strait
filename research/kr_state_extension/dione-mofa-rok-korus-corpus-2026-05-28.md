## §1 — Treaty corpus: 1953 US-ROK Mutual Defense Treaty

### §1.1 — Full text (Avalon Project, Yale Law School) [Source #1]

> **Mutual Defense Treaty Between the United States and the Republic of Korea; October 1, 1953**
>
> The Parties to this Treaty,
>
> Reaffirming their desire to live in peace with all peoples and an governments, and desiring to strengthen the fabric of peace in the Pacific area,
>
> Desiring to declare publicly and formally their common determination to defend themselves against external armed attack so that no potential aggressor could be under the illusion that either of them stands alone in the Pacific area,
>
> Desiring further to strengthen their efforts for collective defense for the preservation of peace and security pending the development of a more comprehensive and effective system of regional security in the Pacific area,
>
> Have agreed as follows:
>
> **ARTICLE I.** The Parties undertake to settle any international disputes in which they may be involved by peaceful means in such a manner that international peace and security and justice are not endangered and to refrain in their international relations from the threat or use of force in any manner inconsistent with the Purposes of the United Nations, or obligations assumed by any Party toward the United Nations.
>
> **ARTICLE II.** The Parties will consult together whenever, in the opinion of either of them, the political independence or security of either of the Parties is threatened by external armed attack. Separately and jointly, by self help and mutual aid, the Parties will maintain and develop appropriate means to deter armed attack and will take suitable measures in consultation and agreement to implement this Treaty and to further its purposes.
>
> **ARTICLE III.** Each Party recognizes that an armed attack in the Pacific area on either of the Parties in territories now under their respective administrative control, or hereafter recognized by one of the Parties as lawfully brought under the administrative control of the other, would be dangerous to its own peace and safety and declares that it would act to meet the common danger in accordance with its constitutional processes.
>
> **ARTICLE IV.** The Republic of Korea grants, and the United States of America accepts, the right to dispose United States land, air and sea forces in and about the territory of the Republic of Korea as determined by mutual agreement.
>
> **ARTICLE V.** This Treaty shall be ratified by the United States of America and the Republic of Korea in accordance with their respective constitutional processes and will come into force when instruments of ratification thereof have been exchanged by them at Washington.
>
> **ARTICLE VI.** This Treaty shall remain in force indefinitely. Either Party may terminate it one year after notice has been given to the other Party.
>
> IN WITNESS WHEREOF the undersigned Plenipotentiaries have signed this Treaty.
>
> DONE in duplicate at Washington, in the English and Korean languages, this first day of October 1953.

### §1.2 — Senate "Understanding" (load-bearing limit) [Source #1]

> **UNDERSTANDING OF THE UNITED STATES** [The United States Senate gave its advice and consent to the ratification of the treaty subject to the following understanding:]
>
> It is the understanding of the United States that neither party is obligated, under Article III of the above Treaty, to come to the aid of the other except in case of an external armed attack against such party; nor shall anything in the present Treaty be construed as requiring the United States to give assistance to Korea except in the event of an armed attack against territory which has been recognized by the United States as lawfully brought under the administrative control of the Republic of Korea.
>
> [The United States communicated the text of the understanding to the Republic of Korea in a note of January 28, 1954, acknowledged by the Republic of Korea in a note of February 1, 1954. The text of the understanding was included in the President's proclamation of November 17, 1954.]

### §1.3 — Treaty-scope analysis (Dione-direct)

Article III is **doubly scoped down** from a generic mutual-defense framing:

1. **Geographic scope** — "armed attack in the Pacific area on either of the Parties **in territories now under their respective administrative control**". Taiwan was not under US administrative control in 1953; it was under ROC administrative control. ROC is not a Party to the treaty. So a PRC armed attack on Taiwan is, by treaty text, **not within Article III scope** for either Party.
2. **Senate Understanding** — Doubles down by adding: US assistance to ROK is only triggered by attack on "territory which has been recognized by the United States as lawfully brought under the administrative control of the Republic of Korea." This narrows the ROK side of the trigger to ROK-recognized territory only; it does **not** narrow the US side, but the territorial limit in Article III is already binding.

**Implication for P28 λ-class prediction:** The treaty text gives ROK a *clean legal off-ramp* from any Taiwan-Strait military commitment. If a Taiwan-Strait conflict occurred, ROK could (and likely would) cite Article III's territorial limit and the Senate Understanding's wording to decline kinetic involvement *while still honoring the alliance for Korean-Peninsula defense*. This is exactly the kind of escape clause that lets a Tier-1 ally remain at λ-class A (collapsed acknowledgment of PRC's one-China position) without contradicting its US-alliance obligations.

The treaty's "indefinite" duration with one-year notice (Article VI) means ROK has structural inertia *against* repositioning — exit costs are high (alliance termination), so revisionism would have to be very strong to override.

## §2 — KORUS FTA 2007 (structural metadata only; full text 1500+ pages)

Source-of-truth: USTR Final Text (as of January 1, 2019) [Source #2]. Originally signed June 30, 2007; renegotiated 2018 ("KORUS 2.0"). Full PDF available at USTR South Korea FULL [Source #3].

**Why KORUS matters for the Taiwan dimension** (Dione-direct):

The 2007 KORUS FTA is silent on Taiwan, China, or one-China — it is a bilateral trade agreement, not a security instrument. Its load-bearing relevance to a ROK→Taiwan posture pivot lies in *what it locked in economically*: deep US-ROK supply-chain integration in semiconductors, autos, and pharmaceuticals. The 2018 renegotiation (KORUS 2.0) preserved this structure under the first Trump administration, indicating bipartisan US commitment to the agreement.

For P28 pre-registration purposes, KORUS creates **path-dependent economic alignment** that makes ROK behaviorally close to the US position on Indo-Pacific supply-chain decoupling from China (CHIPS Act extension, IPEF participation, etc.) — even though the diplomatic position formally remains anchored to the 1992 communiqué's strict one-China formulation. **This is the gap the analysis must name**: economic alignment ratchets toward US strategic preference; diplomatic recognition language remains pinned to 1992. The two can co-exist for years; they cannot co-exist under acute Taiwan-Strait stress.

## §3 — MOFA-ROK current posture (2024 primary sources)

### §3.1 — ROK-Canada 2+2 Ministerial Joint Statement (2024-11-01, Ottawa) [Source #4]

Verbatim Taiwan-Strait clause:

> "The ROK and Canada affirm our commitment to a free and open Indo-Pacific, based on the rule of law and the peaceful resolution of disputes. We continue to advocate for peace and stability throughout the Indo-Pacific region. We oppose any unilateral attempts to change the status quo in the Indo-Pacific. **We reaffirm that maintaining peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait is indispensable to international security and prosperity.**"

Adjacent South China Sea clause (same diplomatic register):

> "The ROK and Canada express their support for the global maritime order based on international law, including the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS). We express concern about developments in the South China Sea, and emphasise the importance of maintaining peace, security, stability and freedom of navigation and overflight in and above the South China Sea, consistent with UNCLOS."

**Position-classification (Dione-direct):**
- The Taiwan-Strait language is **register-elevated** ("indispensable to international security and prosperity") but **carefully prepositional** — "across the Taiwan Strait", not "in Taiwan" or "Taiwan's status".
- No mention of one-China, no mention of Taiwan's political status. The clause is operationalised entirely as a *strait-traffic* concern (cross-border maritime stability), parallel to the South China Sea clause.
- This is consistent with the 1992 communiqué's clause-3 anchor: ROK can publicly call for cross-Strait peace without revising its acknowledgment that the strait connects two parts of one China (per PRC's position).

### §3.2 — US-ROK 2+2 Ministerial Joint Statement (2024-10-31, Washington) [Source #5]

Verbatim Taiwan-Strait paragraph:

> "Both sides emphasized the importance of maintaining peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait as an indispensable element of security and prosperity for the international community. They expressed concern over provocative actions, **particularly the recent military drills around Taiwan that escalate tension** and concurred that no further actions should be taken that could undermine peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait. **There remains no change in our basic positions on Taiwan, including the peaceful resolution of cross-Strait issues.**"

**Position-classification (Dione-direct):**
- Adds two elements absent from the ROK-Canada statement:
  1. **PRC-specific behavioral criticism** — "recent military drills around Taiwan that escalate tension" — this is a named-actor escalation reproach, the closest ROK has come (in a joint statement) to publicly criticizing a specific PRC military activity in the Taiwan Strait.
  2. **Explicit conservation clause** — "There remains no change in our basic positions on Taiwan" — this is the **load-bearing diplomatic shield**: even while elevating concern register, ROK explicitly conserves its 1992-communiqué position. The "basic position" phrase is term-of-art for the recognition language; the conservation clause says language has not changed.
- The MDT-related clauses (Articles I-IV summarized as "bedrock of our security partnership", "linchpin", etc.) are kept entirely separate from the Taiwan-Strait paragraph. Treaty-text territorial scope (§1.3) is preserved by this separation: Taiwan-Strait commentary is *political-diplomatic*, not *security-treaty-actionable*.

### §3.3 — Cross-reading: where ROK is positioning (Dione-direct)

Combining §3.1 (ROK-Canada) and §3.2 (US-ROK), the ROK diplomatic posture as of late 2024 sits at:

| Dimension | Position |
|---|---|
| 1992 communiqué clause-3 anchor (尊重 one-China) | **Preserved** — explicit "no change in basic positions" |
| Taiwan-Strait peace/stability language | **Elevated** — "indispensable" register, multilateral framing |
| Cross-Strait political status | **Silent** — no comment on Taiwan's political status |
| PRC behavioral criticism (Taiwan military drills) | **Activated** — first time in joint statement form |
| US-ROK MDT scope vs. Taiwan | **Strictly separated** — treaty-text Pacific-area clause not extended to Taiwan |
| Economic supply-chain alignment | **De-facto aligned with US** — IPEF, CHIPS, KORUS-locked |
| Inter-Korean confound | **Activated and centered** — DPRK-Russia cooperation is the headline; Taiwan is one of several Indo-Pacific concerns |

**This positioning matches P28 pre-registration's λ-class A prediction at high confidence**: ROK acknowledgment of PRC's one-China position remains formally collapsed (尊重 register from 1992), even as operational behavior includes Taiwan-Strait peace advocacy and de-facto economic alignment with the US position. The λ-class A is *consistent with* the elevated rhetorical register because the rhetorical register is operationalised as a maritime-stability concern, not a recognition-language revision.

**Where this might falsify P28 predictions:**
- If a future US-ROK 2+2 (likely 2025 or 2026 under the new ROK administration) adds language *naming Taiwan's status* (e.g. "self-determination", "Taiwan people's wishes", "democratic governance"), the recognition-language anchor would be functionally challenged → P28's λ-class A prediction would fail.
- If a future MOFA-ROK statement *individually* (not in a joint statement with US/Canada) carries Taiwan-Strait elevated register, the operationalisation gap closes → some movement toward λ-class B.
- If economic decoupling reaches a point where ROK's K-chip strategy materially conflicts with KORUS-locked US supply-chain alignment, the gap between recognition-language and operational-behavior widens → testable on the Day-21 Nexus #1 fire.
