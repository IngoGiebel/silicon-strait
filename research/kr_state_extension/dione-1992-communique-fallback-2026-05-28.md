Source: 中华人民共和国外交部 (PRC Ministry of Foreign Affairs). Original publication record: archived 2000-11-07; original signing 1992-08-24 北京 (Beijing) [Source #1].

**Title:** 中华人民共和国和大韩民国关于建立外交关系的联合公报

**Verbatim text (all 6 articles, MFA-PRC original):**

> 一、中华人民共和国政府和大韩民国政府根据两国人民的利益和愿望，决定自一九九二年八月二十四日起相互承认并建立大使级外交关系。
>
> 二、中华人民共和国政府和大韩民国政府同意根据《联合国宪章》原则，在相互尊重主权和领土完整、互不侵犯、互不干涉内政、平等互利、和平共处原则的基础上发展持久的睦邻合作关系。
>
> 三、大韩民国政府承认中华人民共和国政府为中国的唯一合法政府，并尊重中方只有一个中国、台湾是中国的一部分之立场。
>
> 四、中华人民共和国政府和大韩民国政府相信，两国建交将有助于朝鲜半岛形势的缓和与稳定，也将有助于亚洲的和平与稳定。
>
> 五、中华人民共和国政府尊重朝鲜民族早日实现朝鲜半岛和平统一的愿望，并支持由朝鲜民族自己来实现朝鲜半岛的和平统一。
>
> 六、中华人民共和国政府和大韩民国政府商定，按照一九六一年《维也纳外交关系公约》在各自首都为对方大使馆的建立和履行其职务提供一切必要的协助，并尽快互派大使。
>
> 　　　　中华人民共和国政府代表 大韩民国政府代表
> 　　　　　钱 其 琛 李 相 玉
> 　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　一九九二年八月二十四日于北京

## §4 — EN clause-3 (Dione-direct working translation from ZH, cross-checked against KO)

> The Government of the Republic of Korea recognizes the Government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal Government of China, and respects China's position that there is only one China and that Taiwan is a part of China.

(This translation is mine. The ZH 承认 / KO 승인 map straightforwardly to "recognizes"; the ZH 尊重 / KO 존중 map to "respects". Both verbs are stronger registers than the US 1972 Shanghai Communiqué formulation "the United States acknowledges that all Chinese on either side of the Taiwan Strait maintain there is but one China and that Taiwan is a part of China. The United States Government does not challenge that position" — the US formula uses 认识到 / "acknowledges" without active respect. The ROK 1992 formula commits ROK to "respect" PRC's position, not merely to acknowledge it.)

## §5 — Clause-3 register analysis (Dione-direct)

The clause-3 register is the **strict end** of the one-China gradient among Tier-1 PRC counterparties:

| Jurisdiction | Recognition verb | Position verb | Source | Year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US (Shanghai Communiqué) | "recognizes" PRC as govt | "acknowledges" + "does not challenge" position | 1972 Joint Communiqué §12 | 1972 |
| US (Joint Communiqué on Establishment of Diplomatic Relations) | "recognizes the Government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal Government of China" | "acknowledges the Chinese position that there is but one China and Taiwan is part of China" | 1979 EDR | 1979 |
| Japan | "recognizes" PRC as sole legal govt | "fully understands and respects" (理解し尊重する) the position | 1972 Joint Communiqué §3 | 1972 |
| ROK | "recognizes" PRC as sole legal govt (승인) | **"respects"** (尊重 / 존중) the position | 1992 PRC-ROK Joint Communiqué §3 | 1992 |
| Australia | "recognizes" PRC as sole legal govt | "acknowledges" the position | 1972 Joint Communiqué | 1972 |

The ROK 1992 formula sits at the same end as Japan 1972: both use "respect" rather than "acknowledge". The US formula is the canonical "softer" version — acknowledgment without active respect, which leaves room for the US Taiwan Relations Act (1979) and Six Assurances to operate as parallel commitments.

**For P28 λ-class taxonomy:** ROK 1992 is squarely in λ-class A (collapsed acknowledgment). The 尊重 / 존중 register makes it *one of the strictest* one-China commitments among Tier-1 jurisdictions. Any future ROK statement that added qualification (e.g. "respects China's position as expressed by China", "respects the historical position", "respects the position consistent with the peaceful resolution principle") would constitute λ-class A → λ-class B drift. To date, no such qualification has been documented in MOFA-ROK official communications (per §3 of the companion `dione-mofa-rok-korus-corpus-2026-05-28.md`).

The asymmetry worth naming: ROK's recognition-language register is **stricter than the US's** even though ROK is a US treaty ally with deep KORUS economic integration and elevated Taiwan-Strait peace advocacy in joint statements. This is the exact gap that P28 names — recognition-language anchor preserved even as operational-behavior moves toward US/Indo-Pacific alignment.

## §6 — Inter-Korean confound, encoded in the 1992 text itself

Clauses 4 and 5 of the 1992 communiqué are the **inter-Korean confound clauses** — they bind PRC and ROK on the DPRK-unification question, *in the same document* that pins ROK to one-China:

- **Clause 4:** Both parties believe diplomatic relations will help ease and stabilize the Korean-peninsula situation, and help Asian peace and stability.
- **Clause 5:** PRC respects the Korean nation's wish for early peaceful unification of the Korean peninsula, and supports the Korean nation realising peaceful unification by itself.

This was the trade: ROK got PRC support for inter-Korean unification language; PRC got ROK's strict one-China commitment. The two are textually coupled in the founding document of the diplomatic relationship.

**P28 implication:** Any scenario where ROK considers revising its one-China commitment must reckon with the *coupled* DPRK clause. PRC could plausibly argue that ROK weakening clause 3 retroactively voids PRC obligations under clauses 4-5 (e.g. on UN sanctions cooperation, on opposing DPRK-Russia military cooperation, on inter-Korean dialogue support). This is a *structural* deterrent against ROK recognition-language drift that has no parallel in the US-PRC relationship.

This coupling is one reason why P28 predicts λ-class A at high confidence even under significant external pressure: the cost of moving is asymmetrically high for ROK because clause-3 revision triggers clause-4/5 risk on the DPRK question, which is existentially load-bearing for ROK security.
