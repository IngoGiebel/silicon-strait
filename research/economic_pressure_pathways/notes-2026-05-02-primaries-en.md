### 1. EU Anti-Coercion Instrument — the regulatory counterweight

**Source:** Wikipedia, "Anti-Coercion Instrument" (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Coercion_Instrument), retrieved 2026-05-02.

- **Regulation:** (EU) 2023/2675
- **Adopted:** 2023-11-22
- **Entered into force:** 2023-12-27
- **Scope:** Protects "the Union and its Member States from economic coercion by third countries."
- **Definition of coercion (Art. 2):** when "a third country seeks to pressure the European Union or a member state into making a particular policy choice by applying, or threatening to apply, measures affecting trade or investment."
- **Triggering threshold:** Council confirms coercion exists via qualified-majority vote.
- **Response toolkit:** countermeasures including tariffs, trade restrictions, market-access limits, IP restrictions, FDI controls. Can target states, companies, OR individuals.
- **Motivating case (named in legislative history):** "Lithuania's 2021 decision to permit Taiwan's representative office prompted Chinese trade retaliation, catalyzing the instrument's development."
- **Status as of 2026-01:** UNUSED. Macron has urged its deployment against US tariffs related to the Greenland crisis. UK's British Chambers of Commerce called for similar UK legislation in April 2026.

**GWW3 framing implication:** ACI is a *Schelling commitment* — not a tool that needs to fire to work. Its existence raises the threshold of plausibility for PRC coercion against any single EU member state because the EU can now respond as a bloc. The "unused after 2.5 years" status is a *deterrence-success signal*, not a tool-failure signal.

### 2. Lithuania case (2021–2026) — the active anchor case

**Source:** Wikipedia, "China–Lithuania relations" (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Lithuania_relations), retrieved 2026-05-02.

Timeline:
- **2021-08:** Taiwan opens "Taiwanese Representative Office" in Vilnius — first under that name in Europe.
- **2021-11-21:** PRC downgrades diplomatic relations to chargé d'affaires; recalls Ambassador Shen Zhifei; demands Lithuania reciprocate.
- **2021-12-03:** Beijing delists Lithuania as country-of-origin; blocks all imports; "rejecting all import applications."
- **2021-12:** PRC pressures multinational firms (third-country lever); EU Chamber of Commerce president calls action "unprecedented."
- **2022-early:** German-Baltic Chamber warns German-owned factories in Lithuania face closure if relations don't improve.
- **2022-01:** Lithuanian President Gitanas Nausėda acknowledges Taiwan-office decision was a "mistake" — does NOT propose policy changes.
- **2023-11:** Officials report ongoing normalization talks.
- **2024-10:** New coalition promises to restore full diplomatic relations.
- **2024-11:** Lithuania (a) restricts Chinese access to renewable-energy infrastructure, (b) expels three Chinese diplomats. **NOTE the divergence:** simultaneous concessionary framing on diplomatic relations + escalatory framing on infrastructure access.
- **2026-02:** PM Inga Ruginienė: Lithuania "really jumped in front of a train and lost"; suggests office could be renamed to "Taipei."

**GWW3 framing implication:** This is the **case-attrition-curve** test case — Lithuania has held ~4.5 years against PRC pressure and the *political* signal in Feb 2026 is partial-concession (renaming proposed by PM, not enacted). The **two-level game** dynamic is visible: Lithuanian government negotiating with PRC AND with its own electorate — Nausėda 2022 vs. Ruginienė 2026 reflect the domestic-political evolution, not just PRC pressure.

**Cross-thread link:** Lithuania anchors Day-2 recognition_diplomacy AND Day-5 prc-coercion-toolkit. The two threads are not independent variables — they are **two faces of the same coercion-arc**, name-as-coercion-vector + trade-as-coercion-vector applied to the same target.

### 3. Australia case (2020–2024) — most-studied multi-vector

**Source:** Wikipedia, "Australia–China relations" (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93China_relations), retrieved 2026-05-02.

PRC measure timeline:
- **2020-05-12:** Beef ban on four AU processors (~35% of beef exports).
- **2020-05-19:** 80% tariff on barley imports.
- **2020-06-19:** State-attributed cyber-attacks.
- **2020-11:** "Anti-dumping" tariffs of 107.1%–212.1% on wine.
- **2020-12:** Full coal-import blockade.
- **2021-04:** AU cancels Victoria's BRI agreement (counter-pressure).
- **2021-06:** AU files WTO complaint over 218% wine tariffs.
- **2023-08:** Barley tariffs lifted.
- **2024-03:** Wine tariffs removed; AU discontinues WTO complaint.

**Commodity coverage:** barley, beef, wine, coal, lobsters, cotton, copper, timber.
**Investment-channel signal:** "2020, Chinese investment in Australia dropped 61%" — bidirectional decoupling, not just PRC-side punitive measures.
**Resolution gradient:** ~3-year freeze; lifting sequence (barley before wine) suggests PRC reads commodity-asymmetry strategically (signal-cost vs. AU-domestic-importance).

**GWW3 framing implication:** **Multi-vector layering** — barley, beef, wine, coal, lobsters, cyber, all simultaneously. This is what distinguishes the Australia case from the Lithuania case: PRC does not appear to escalate one tool at a time; it deploys **concurrent stack** across commodities + cyber. **Audience-cost signaling** on PRC side: visible commitment to coerce raises the cost of backing down domestically — Albanese-government 2023 reset required PRC to recoup credibility narratively (see Wang Yi "wolf warrior retreat" framing).

### 4. Rare-earth toolkit (2010 + 2023–2025)

**Source:** Wikipedia, "Rare earth industry in China" (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earth_industry_in_China), retrieved 2026-05-02.

- **2010:** Following Senkaku boat-collision incident, PRC stops all rare-earth exports to Japan; reduces global exports "from 40 to 30 percent."
- **2012:** US, EU, Japan file WTO case against PRC export controls.
- **WTO ruling (against China):** "its export controls were not justifiable." China subsequently complied.
- **2023-07:** PRC announces gallium + germanium export licensing controls.
- **2023-12:** PRC bans export of certain rare-earth processing technology.
- **2024-12:** PRC bans exports of gallium, germanium, antimony **specifically to the United States** (target-discrimination, not blanket).
- **Gallium price impact:** ~$110–130/kg (pre-2023-07) → $687/kg by 2025-05.
- **2025-10:** PRC tightens rare-earth controls focused on defense and chip industries; second wave subsequently SUSPENDED for one year (de-escalation signal).

**GWW3 framing implication:** **Lawfare-as-substrate** — China learned from the 2012 WTO defeat that *blanket export bans* fail at WTO; **2023-2025 regime is licensing-based** (per-shipment approval), making each restriction case-by-case rather than a regime targetable by general WTO complaint. This is the **first mature deployment of license-as-veto** mirroring Western export-control architectures (CHIPS Act, Wassenaar). **Tail-risk asymmetry** in pricing: 5x price increase in 2 years on critical inputs visible at the firm level, but the *chokepoint* effect compounds — defense + chip industries cannot substitute on the timescale of license-cycle decisions.

### 6. Wolf Warrior diplomacy — the narrative-ops half of the toolkit

**Source:** Wikipedia, "Wolf warrior diplomacy" (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_warrior_diplomacy), retrieved 2026-05-02.

- **2009–2011:** Early "Chinese assertiveness" in South China Sea.
- **2019-07:** Zhao Lijian–Susan Rice Twitter exchange.
- **2020–2021:** Peak aggressiveness during COVID-criticism backlash.
- **2021-05:** Xi Jinping calls for improvement in international communication — first official retreat signal.
- **2023-01:** Zhao Lijian reassigned from spokesperson role.
- **2025-early:** *The Economist* analysis: "belligerent answers had declined to 2018 levels."
- **2025-11 anomaly:** PRC Consul-General in Osaka posts threat: "the dirty neck that sticks itself in must be cut off" — signals retreat is non-monotonic.

Named individuals: Zhao Lijian, Hua Chunying, Wang Wenbin, Liu Xiaoming, Lu Shaye.

**GWW3 framing implication:** The **narrative-ops vector** of the coercion toolkit. Retreat 2021-2023 supports the **audience-cost signaling** framing — Xi visibly recalibrated *toward* less-aggressive framing as the cost of confrontation became visible. But Osaka-2025 shows the equilibrium is unstable. **Information-domain coercion is not one-shot retreatable** because it is decentralized across 100+ ambassadors/consuls; central-line revisions decay slowly through the diplomatic corps.

### 7. PRC-aligned primary — the inverted-mirror frame

**Source:** Global Times, "US repeated chip curbs against China reflect recurring protectionist mindset" by Zhang Yiyi, 2026-04-29 (globaltimes.cn/page/202604/1360089.shtml), retrieved 2026-05-02.

Trigger: US Department of Commerce ordered chip-equipment companies to halt shipments to **Huahong** (China's second-largest chipmaker).

PRC framing — verbatim quotes:
- **MFA Spokesperson Lin Jian:** US should "keep the global supply chains stable and unimpeded."
- **Zhou Mi (CAITEC, Chinese Academy of International Trade and Economic Cooperation):** US measures reflect "a recurring trade protectionist mindset"; semiconductor industry requires "win-win cooperation"; restrictions unlikely to succeed given China's rapid growth.
- **Ma Jihua (tech analyst):** Restrictions are "blatant unfair commercial competition and exemplify the politicization of trade"; create "a cycle of pressure and breakthrough" with diminishing effectiveness as Chinese firms become less deterred.

**Inverted-mirror frame structure:**
- PRC denies the coercion-frame entirely as applied to itself (re: Australia/Lithuania/rare earths).
- PRC adopts the coercion-frame WITH HIGH SPECIFICITY when applied to US measures against PRC firms.
- The two framings co-exist in the official discourse — there is no internal PRC discourse acknowledging PRC measures *as* coercion.

**GWW3 framing implication:** Bias-balance is structurally constrained. The Day-5 publication cannot rely on PRC primary sources to confirm PRC coercion typology because the typology is denied at framing level. The honest move is to cite the GT framing as the **PRC's own characterization** of the coercion question and let the reader see the asymmetry.
