## CASE 1 — Lithuania 2021–2026: single-vector intensity, sustained resistance

### Coercion arc

| Date | PRC measure | LT response | Coercion-vector classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-08 | (LT decision) Open "Taiwanese Representative Office" in Vilnius | — | (trigger) |
| 2021-11-21 | Diplomatic downgrade to chargé d'affaires; recall Amb. Shen Zhifei; demand reciprocation | None | Diplomatic-pressure vector |
| 2021-12-03 | Country-of-origin delisting; full import block | None | Trade-weaponization vector (single-shot blanket) |
| 2021-12 | Pressure on multinationals to drop LT components from supply chains | EU Chamber of Commerce: "unprecedented" | Third-country lever — extraterritorial extension of trade-weaponization |
| 2022-early | German-Baltic Chamber warning re. LT-located German factories | None policy-level | Third-country lever continued |
| 2022-01 | (no new measure) | Nausėda: Taiwan-office decision was "a mistake" — but no policy reversal | (target signaling — not concession) |
| 2023-08-09 | EU files WTO case against PRC re. LT trade restrictions (DS610) | EU acts as bloc-counter-leverage | Multilateral counter-pressure |
| 2023-11 | (normalization talks reported) | LT engages bilaterally | (de-escalation feeler) |
| 2024-10 | (no new measure) | New coalition promises full diplomatic restoration | (target signaling — partial) |
| 2024-11 | (no new measure) | LT restricts Chinese access to renewable-energy infra; expels 3 PRC diplomats | Target *escalation* on infrastructure axis; concessionary on diplomatic axis (DIVERGENCE) |
| 2026-02 | (no new measure) | PM Ruginienė: "really jumped in front of a train and lost"; suggests office could be renamed to "Taipei" | Partial concession (frame-level, not yet enacted) |

### Vector decomposition

- **Diplomatic vector:** downgrade + ambassador recall + reciprocation demand. Cost to PRC: low (no measurable economic cost). Cost to LT: bilateral diplomatic infrastructure loss.
- **Trade-weaponization vector:** customs delisting = blanket import ban. Cost to PRC: ~€300M annual LT-PRC trade pre-2021 (≈0.7% of LT total exports). Cost to LT: small *direct* exposure but symbolic + signaling effect.
- **Third-country lever:** pressure multinationals to *drop LT*. This is the high-leverage move — multiplies effective coercion 10-30x by extending to firms with PRC-market exposure (German manufacturing footprint in LT).
- **Lawfare counter-vector:** EU WTO case 2023-08 (DS610) reversed leverage direction; PRC has not won that case as of 2026-05-02.

### GWW3 framing seeds (Lithuania)

1. **Audience-cost signaling (PRC side):** Beijing visibly committed to coerce LT — backing down imposes domestic-credibility cost, especially after CCP rhetoric framed Vilnius office as "secessionist." This is why partial concession from LT (Ruginienė renaming proposal) is *enough* — it lets PRC claim victory narratively without requiring full LT capitulation. **Audience-cost asymmetry:** LT's audience cost of capitulation is high (electoral-democratic accountability); PRC's audience cost of de-escalation is also high (CCP narrative fixity); equilibrium is *attritional ambiguity*.
2. **Two-level game (Putnam):** LT government negotiates with PRC AND with LT electorate in parallel. Nausėda 2022 ("mistake" framing) → Ruginienė 2026 (renaming proposal) reflects *domestic political evolution under sustained pressure*, not gradual PRC escalation. Confirms Putnam: domestic-coalition shift is the load-bearing variable when external pressure is constant.
3. **EU bloc as Schelling commitment:** EU Anti-Coercion Instrument (Reg 2023/2675) was *catalyzed by* the LT case (named in legislative history). Its existence post-2023-12 raises the threshold of plausibility for *future* PRC single-state coercion in EU. The instrument's "unused after 2.5 years" status is a deterrence-success signal, not a tool-failure signal (cf. nuclear posture). LT's *resistance* generated the structural counter-coercion infrastructure.
4. **Cross-thread Markov-failure:** Day-2 recognition_diplomacy framed UNGA 2758 as a Markov-stuck game (the meaning of "China" frozen via decades of repetition). Day-5 LT shows the inverse: a *single* recognition-adjacent decision (office name) breaks Markov-stuck consensus and triggers coercion-arc activation. The two threads are not independent — they are *coupled state* in the same belief-graph.

## CASE 2 — Australia 2020–2024: multi-vector layering, eventual break

### Coercion arc

Trigger envelope: 2020-04 (PM Morrison call for COVID-origin investigation) + accumulating 2017-2019 friction (foreign-interference law, Huawei 5G ban, S. China Sea posture).

| Date | PRC measure | Commodity / vector | Cost to AU |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2020-05-12 | Beef ban on 4 AU processors | Beef (~35% of AU beef exports concentrated in those plants) | ~AU$3.5B/yr at risk |
| 2020-05-19 | 80.5% tariff on barley | Barley | ~AU$1.5B/yr |
| 2020-06-19 | State-attributed cyber-attacks (PM Morrison statement) | Cyber | (non-monetary) |
| 2020-08 | Wine "anti-dumping" investigation announced | Wine | (signal) |
| 2020-10 | Cotton imports informally blocked | Cotton | ~AU$900M |
| 2020-11-06 | Lobster customs delays | Lobster | ~AU$700M |
| 2020-11-27 | 107.1%–212.1% wine tariff (then 218% final) | Wine | ~AU$1.1B/yr |
| 2020-12 | Coal blockade (full halt of imports) | Coal | ~AU$14B/yr at peak |
| 2020-12 | Timber imports blocked from Victoria/NSW/SA | Timber | ~AU$600M |
| 2021-04 | (AU response) cancels Victoria's BRI agreement | (counter-pressure — investment vector) | — |
| 2021-06 | (AU response) WTO complaint on wine tariffs | (lawfare counter — aligned with LT case structurally) | — |
| 2022-05 | Albanese government takes office; tone reset | (target-domestic shift) | — |
| 2023-04 | China lifts coal ban informally | Coal | (recovery) |
| 2023-08 | Barley tariffs lifted | Barley | (recovery) |
| 2024-03 | Wine tariffs removed; AU drops WTO case | Wine | (recovery) |

### Vector decomposition

- **Trade-weaponization vector deployed concurrently across 8 commodities** within 6 months (May–Dec 2020). This is the defining feature of the AU case versus the LT case.
- **Cyber vector:** unique to AU among the five candidate cases — gives PRC plausible-deniability framing (no ministry attribution) while imposing measurable cost.
- **Investment-channel signal:** "2020, Chinese investment in Australia dropped 61%" (per primaries-en.md §3) — bidirectional decoupling, not one-sided coercion.
- **Resolution gradient:** lifting sequence barley (2023-08) → wine (2024-03) suggests PRC reads commodity-asymmetry strategically. **Coal lifted first, informally, in 2023-04** — coal's lifting was *opaque* (no announcement) because PRC's own coal supply gap forced de-facto re-import; the formal recoveries on barley/wine then *codified* what economic necessity had already broken.

### GWW3 framing seeds (Australia)

1. **Multi-vector layering signals commitment magnitude:** PRC choice to deploy 8 commodity vectors *concurrently* rather than sequentially raises the audience-cost commitment relative to single-vector cases. The signaling-game literature predicts: concurrent multi-vector deployment is a higher-cost-to-back-down equilibrium than sequential escalation. Hass (Hass-Vance JPAS 2024) calls this "saturation coercion."
2. **Asymmetric exit costs created the 3-year hold:** PRC's exit cost on barley/wine was low (substitutable on PRC side) — it lifted those first. PRC's exit cost on coal was high (PRC needs the coal more than AU needs to sell it on the margin) — that vector broke first, but informally. Pattern: tools with low PRC-exit-cost are kept longest as bargaining chips; tools with high PRC-exit-cost erode first under AU's substitution + PRC's domestic-demand pressure. **Implication for the curve:** resolution is endogenous to the *PRC-side* substitution pain, not just target-state resistance.
3. **WTO-process as Schelling clock:** AU 2021-06 WTO complaint on wine tariffs ran in parallel with LT 2023-08 EU-led WTO complaint (DS610). Both cases use the WTO multilateral-arena as a *time-binding commitment* — once filed, both sides accept that the dispute will conclude on a calendar PRC cannot fully control. WTO-arena participation is itself a *de-escalation infrastructure*: it routes the coercion contest into a procedural channel where PRC can save face by accepting a panel ruling rather than capitulating bilaterally.
4. **Albanese reset as domestic-coalition trigger:** Compare Putnam two-level game: LT case showed coalition shift as concession-trigger (2024-10 promise → 2026-02 renaming proposal). AU case showed coalition shift as reset-trigger (2022-05 Albanese government → tone reset → economic coercion lifting cascade). In *both* cases, target-state domestic-political shift is the catalyst, not PRC-side recalibration. **Hypothesis:** PRC coercion campaigns *outlast* the target-state political coalition that produced the trigger decision; resolution requires coalition turnover, not policy reversal under continuous coalition.

## Cross-case comparison — what the pairing reveals

**LT vs. AU on three axes:**

| Axis | Lithuania | Australia | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vector concentration | Single-vector intensity | Multi-vector layering | Concentration ≠ effectiveness; LT held longer |
| Bloc backstop | EU (ACI-creating) | None equivalent (Five Eyes coordinates intel, not trade) | Bloc backstop = decisive variable for hold |
| Resolution path | Holding (≥4.5y) — partial concession Feb 2026 | Broke 2023–2024 — coalition turnover | Coalition turnover trigger > pressure-duration |
| GDP-vulnerability | LT-PRC trade ~0.7% of LT exports | AU-PRC trade ~30% of AU exports | LT was less coercible on quantitative-economic axis (smaller exposure ratio) |

**The decisive variable:** LT held *despite* being smaller because (a) economic exposure to PRC was smaller in proportion, AND (b) bloc-backstop multiplied counter-leverage. AU broke *despite* being larger because (a) economic exposure was disproportionate, AND (b) no bloc trade-backstop (CPTPP did not respond as a bloc to PRC measures, Five Eyes is intelligence-only). **GDP-vulnerability ratio** + **bloc-backstop presence** explain the divergence better than population, ideology, or duration of pressure.

**Counter-intuitive finding for the publication:** pure size + economic-power matters less than *structural exposure ratio* + *bloc embedding*. A small EU member with low PRC trade-share is harder to coerce than a medium Pacific democracy with high PRC trade-share and no equivalent bloc.
