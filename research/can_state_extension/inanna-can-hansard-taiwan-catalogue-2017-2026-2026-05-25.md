## Post-Hogue Commission Register Shift Analysis

The Hogue Commission (Public Inquiry into Foreign Interference in Federal Electoral Processes) delivered reports spanning 2024-2025, heavily identifying China as an aggressive actor in foreign interference, specifically targeting diaspora communities.

**Did the PRC-interference findings lower the political cost of Tier-3 Taiwan framing?**
Yes. Following the Hogue Commission's findings, the parliamentary register exhibits a clear shift. The cost of adopting a Tier-3 maximalist or narrow defense-oriented framing of Taiwan has notably decreased.
1. **Intersection of Taiwan and Domestic Security:** Parliamentarians (e.g., Michael Chong, Jenny Kwan) increasingly reference Taiwan not just in the context of Indo-Pacific foreign policy, but as a direct parallel to Canada's own struggles with PRC foreign interference.
2. **Economic Coercion as a Shared Threat:** Debates in 2025/2026 (like Jacob Mantle's remarks) openly cite Taiwan and partners like Lithuania to illustrate PRC economic coercion, shifting from cautious diplomatic rhetoric to explicit Tier-3 defensive posturing. 
3. **Legitimacy of Pro-Taiwan Bills:** The environment fostered by the Hogue Commission has provided political cover for stronger legislative efforts, such as Bill C-343 / S-277 (Canada-Taiwan Relations Framework Act), by framing closer Taiwan ties as a mechanism for countering foreign interference and standing up for democracies.
