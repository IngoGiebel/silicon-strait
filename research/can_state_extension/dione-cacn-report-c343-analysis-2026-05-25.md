### 2.2 Tier-3 Framings in Recommendations

**Recommendation 2** (the P28(c) surface):
> "That the Government of Canada offer and declare its clear and unwavering commitment that **the future of Taiwan must only be the decision of the people of Taiwan**."

This is the committee's Tier-3 peak — an explicit self-determination framing that implies the right to choose independence. The committee *chose* this language rather than the softer alternatives available ("support peaceful resolution", "maintain status quo"). The voted adoption on 2023-03-06 makes this a collective-committee instrument, not just individual-witness testimony.

### 2.3 Tier-2 Language in Recommendations (baseline)

Recommendations 1, 3-18 operate within the Tier-2 engagement frame:
- Rec. 1: official communications consistency
- Rec. 3-4: parliamentary/diplomatic visits
- Rec. 5-7: multilateral participation, development
- Rec. 8: disinformation exchange
- Rec. 9-14: trade, investment, semiconductor, CPTPP
- Rec. 15: "peaceful status quo" (explicit Tier-2 anchor)
- Rec. 16-18: SCS, Quad/AUKUS, military de-escalation

### 2.4 Report Body Framing

The report describes Taiwan as "a democratic and progressive island" (§Section I header) — Tier-2 descriptive language that avoids "country" or "nation" at the editorial level. The committee incorporated Tier-3 witness testimony without editorially adopting or rejecting the sovereignty/country designations. This is the same committee-report-incorporates-testimony-without-editorial-stance pattern observed in UK FAC reports.

## 3. Government Response — P28(c) Restatement Test (Sub-question C)

**Source**: ourcommons.ca, Government Response 8512-441-232, signed by Minister Mélanie Joly, September 15, 2023.

### 3.1 Response to Recommendation 2 (the P28(c) signature)

Government Response: **"The Government takes note of this recommendation."**

Then the restatement:
> "For over five decades, the Government of Canada has consistently maintained its One China Policy... Consistent with this policy, Canada opposes unilateral actions by either side that seek to alter the status quo across the Taiwan Strait and **does not support Taiwan independence**. Canada supports constructive efforts that advance peace, stability and dialogue in the region. This longstanding position remains unchanged."

**Analysis**: This is the CAN P28(c) restatement-without-crisis mechanism. Structurally parallel to UK Gov-Response HC 630 §135 (restating Tier-2 after FAC HC 172 §142 "independent country"), but with three distinctive properties:

1. **Formula echo**: "The Government takes note" echoes the 1970 communiqué's "takes note" — the executive branch deploys the diplomatic formula as a domestic-policy instrument. This is absent in the UK pattern.

2. **Explicit independence rejection**: "does not support Taiwan independence" is a *harder* restatement than the UK's implicit Tier-2 restatement. The UK Government didn't explicitly say "does not support Taiwan independence" — it simply restated existing policy. The CAN Government goes further by naming and rejecting the implication.

3. **No-crisis condition satisfied**: The committee adopted Rec. 2 on 2023-03-06, the report was tabled 2023-03-30, and the Government Response was filed 2023-09-15. No PRC diplomatic crisis, no sanctions, no ambassador recall. The cost-product threshold was NOT breached by the committee's Tier-3 recommendation, because the Government's Tier-2 restatement absorbed the Tier-3 deviation.

### 3.2 Response Pattern Across All 18 Recommendations

| Tier of response | Recommendations | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **"Agrees"** | 1, 6, 7, 8, 9, 14, 16 | Within existing policy envelope |
| **"Agrees in principle"** | 3, 5, 15 | Directionally supportive, implementation discretionary |
| **"Partially accepts"** | 10 | Policy complexity (critical minerals) |
| **"Takes note"** | **2, 4, 12, 13, 17** | Exceeds current policy envelope; formula-echo of 1970 communiqué |

The "takes note" cluster marks the Government's red line: Rec. 2 (self-determination), Rec. 4 (diplomatic visits), Rec. 12 (Minister signing in Taiwan), Rec. 13 (CPTPP prioritization), Rec. 17 (Quad/AUKUS membership). These are the recommendations that would functionally elevate the CAN-Taiwan relationship beyond the unofficial frame.

## 4. Bill C-343 — Tier-3 Text Analysis (Sub-question B)

**Source**: parl.ca/DocumentViewer/en/44-1/bill/C-343/first-reading
**Introduced**: June 15, 2023, by Michael Cooper (CPC, St. Albert—Edmonton)
**Full title**: "An Act respecting a framework to strengthen Canada-Taiwan relations"
**Status**: First reading only; no second reading. Outside the Order of Precedence.

### 4.1 Tier-3 Provisions (load-bearing)

**Preamble — self-determination invocation (international-law-grounded)**:
> "the relations that Canada maintains with Taiwan should take into account **the right of all peoples to self-determination** that is recognized in the Charter of the United Nations, the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights"

This is the most legally grounded Tier-3 formulation in any substrate so far — it invokes three international instruments to support Taiwan's right to self-determination. Stronger than UK FAC HC 172 §142 (which was descriptive) because it grounds the claim in binding treaty obligations.

**Preamble — separate-governance recognition**:
> "Taiwan and the People's Republic of China have been governed separately since 1949 without interruption"

**Preamble — One China Policy ≠ One China Principle (explicit disambiguation)**:
> "the 'One China Policy' to which the Government of Canada is committed is not the same as the 'One China Principle' adopted by the People's Republic of China"

**Section 3(1)(e) — self-determination language policy**:
> "when referring to Taiwan, use language that reflects its right to self-determination"

This would *require* the Government to frame Taiwan in self-determination terms in all official communications — a standing Tier-3 policy mandate.

**Section 3(1)(f) — Taiwan Relations Act mirror**:
> "consider any effort to determine the future of Taiwan by other than peaceful means, or by boycotts or embargoes, to be a threat to the peace and security of the Indo-Pacific region and of grave concern to Canada"

Directly modeled on the US Taiwan Relations Act §2(b)(4).

**Section 4 — foreign-state deeming (strongest single provision)**:
> "A general reference in a law of Canada to **foreign states** or to their governments... **is deemed to include Taiwan**"

This would legally deem Taiwan a foreign state in Canadian law. The strongest Tier-3 provision across all substrates analyzed (UK, AUS, JPN, PHL, EU, CAN). No other substrate has a legislative instrument that would legally classify Taiwan as a state.

**Section 3(2)(d) — symbolic elevation**:
> rename TECO to "Taiwan Representative Office" and Canadian Trade Office in Taipei to "Canadian Representative Office in Taiwan"

**Section 6 — state-like legal personality**:
> "Taiwan has the capacity to sue and be sued in a court in Canada in accordance with the laws of Canada, in particular **the laws respecting the status of states**"

### 4.2 Legislative Status as P28(c) Finding

C-343 has been introduced in three parliamentary sessions:
- 43rd Parliament, 2nd session (2020): Bill C-315 — first reading only
- 44th Parliament, 1st session (2023): Bill C-343 — first reading only
- Senate parallel S-277 (2023): first reading Sept 26, 2023; **second reading debate May 21, 2024** (Senator Michael L. MacDonald, Conservative)

**H4 confirmed**: The CAN legislative register *generates* Tier-3 instruments (C-343 is deeply Tier-3) but the cost-product threshold blocks House advancement beyond first reading. The bill persists across sessions (reintroduced 2020 → 2023) without advancing — the same structural pattern as UK EDMs that never reach plenary vote.

**S-277 advance to second reading**: The Senate parallel *advanced further* than the House version. The appointed Senate (lower personal electoral cost) advanced the bill to second reading debate, while the elected House kept it outside the Order of Precedence. This confirms the §3.3 hypothesis that the appointive-Senate institutional-distance produces freer advancement on Tier-3 instruments.
