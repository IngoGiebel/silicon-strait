## 2. CAN 1970 communiqué — the "takes note" lexical floor

The joint communiqué establishing CAN-PRC diplomatic relations (October 13, 1970) reads:

> "The Canadian Government recognizes the Government of the People's Republic of China as the sole legal Government of China."

On Taiwan:

> "The Chinese Government reaffirms that Taiwan is an inalienable part of the territory of the People's Republic of China. **The Canadian Government takes note of this position of the Chinese Government.**"

This is the "Canadian formula" — lexically weaker than:
- UK 1972: "acknowledges the position of the Chinese Government that Taiwan is a province"
- AUS 1972: "acknowledges the position of the Chinese Government that Taiwan is a province"
- US 1972 Shanghai Communiqué: "acknowledges that all Chinese on either side of the Taiwan Strait maintain there is but one China"

The "takes note" formula was a deliberate negotiating achievement. The Stockholm negotiations (20 rounds, 1968–1970) saw PRC push for "recognises" and CAN hold at "takes note" — the weakest non-endorsement formula available. This formula became the model for subsequent recognitions (Italy 1970, Belgium 1971, and others used variants).

**P28 implication**: CAN's Tier-2 lexical floor is *weaker* than UK/AUS. The analytical question is whether a weaker Tier-2 baseline lowers the (commitment-weight × reversibility-cost) product threshold for Tier-3 admittance — i.e., does "takes note" make Tier-3 framing *easier* to reach at lower instrument tiers because the gap between Tier-2 and Tier-3 is narrower?

### 2.1 CN-text asymmetry question

Day-15 confirmed that AUS 1972's "acknowledges" maps to the same Chinese character (承认) used for "recognises" elsewhere in the AUS communiqué — the lexical asymmetry first identified in DFAT 361 (Waller→Whitlam minute). **Open research question for Inanna**: What Chinese character does PRC use for the CAN 1970 "takes note"? If it is 注意到 (zhùyì dào, "noted/taken note of") rather than 承认 (chéngrèn, "acknowledge/recognise"), the CN-text preserves the lexical-floor distinction; if PRC renders "takes note" as 承认, the CN-text *collapses* the CAN-UK/AUS distinction. This directly affects whether P26 Tier-2 function-class register treats CAN as a distinct lexical instantiation or as collapsed with UK/AUS.

## 3. Parliamentary committee architecture

### 3.1 CACN — Special Committee on Canada-PRC Relationship

CACN is the **primary committee vehicle** for the P28(c) test. Established by motion in the 43rd Parliament (2020), renewed in the 44th Parliament (2021–2025). Mandate: "explore all aspects of the relationship between Canada and the People's Republic of China, including diplomatic, consular, legal, security and economic relations."

**Key output: CACN Report No. 2** — "Canada and Taiwan: A Strong Relationship in Turbulent Times" (44-1, tabled ~2023). Study began September 20, 2022. Incorporated testimony from FAAE sessions. Government Response tabled.

P28(c) test surface: Does CACN Report No. 2 contain Tier-3 framings on Taiwan's status? The report title ("Strong Relationship") and introduction frame Taiwan as "a democratic and progressive island" — potentially Tier-3-adjacent but not necessarily Tier-3 in the Day-14 sense (explicit "independent country" / sovereignty status framing).

**Structural note**: CACN is a *special* (ad hoc) committee, not a standing committee. This differs from:
- UK FAC (select committee — permanent institutional architecture)
- AUS JSCFADT (joint standing committee — permanent)
- EU EP committees (standing)

The institutional-distance question: does a special committee carry *less* institutional permanence but *more* mandate-specific latitude than a standing committee? If so, the cost-product calculus may differ — a special committee's findings are more easily dismissed by the Government as "that committee's mandate ended" but harder to restrain during the committee's active period.

### 3.2 FAAE — Standing Committee on Foreign Affairs and International Development

The standing committee. Receives more routine Government appearances (confirmed: October 30, 2025 appearance with China/Taiwan briefing notes published on GAC transparency portal). FAAE testimony fed into CACN's Report No. 2.

P28(c) test surface: FAAE report or study recommendations touching Taiwan status? Lower expected yield than CACN for Tier-3 framings because the standing-committee mandate is broader and more routine.

### 3.3 Senate AEFA — Foreign Affairs and International Trade Committee

Senate-side parallel. CAN's Senate is appointed (not elected), which creates a different institutional-distance-from-electorate than AUS Senate (elected). The appointive nature may produce freer Tier-3 framing (lower personal political cost) or more restrained (less electoral mandate to claim mandate for status-shifting positions).

## 4. Legislative instrument: Bill C-343 (Canada-Taiwan Relations Framework Act)

Introduced by Conservative MP Michael Cooper (St. Albert—Edmonton) in 2021. Modeled on the US Taiwan Relations Act.

The bill:
- Clarifies Canada's "One China Policy"
- Promotes defense cooperation between Canada and Taiwan
- Supports Taiwan's participation in multilateral international organizations

**Status (as of May 2025):** First reading passed; has NOT undergone second reading. The bill has been reintroduced in subsequent sessions but remains stalled.

**P28(c) analytical significance**: This is a *legislative instrument* that would formally structure the CAN-Taiwan relationship — different from committee-report findings (CACN) or executive-branch policy (IPS). If C-343 contains Tier-3 framings and passes any reading stage, it represents a *legislative register* Tier-3 surface not present in UK/AUS/JPN/PHL/EU substrates. If it remains stalled at first reading, it is analytically similar to a UK EDM that never reaches a vote — structurally present but blocked at the cost-product threshold.

The fact that the bill has been reintroduced across sessions but never advanced past first reading is itself a P28(c) finding: the CAN Parliament has *generated* a legislative-register Tier-3 instrument but the cost-product threshold blocks it from advancing.

## 5. Shock-and-recovery substrate

### 5.1 Two Michaels (2018–2021)

Michael Kovrig (former diplomat, ICG analyst) and Michael Spavor (business consultant) detained by PRC on December 10, 2018 — 10 days after Canada arrested Meng Wanzhou (Huawei CFO) on US extradition request. Both held 1,019 days. Released September 24, 2021 — same day Meng reached deferred prosecution agreement with US DOJ.

This is **direct PRC hostage diplomacy** — qualitatively different from the AUS AUKUS shock (alliance-restructuring) or the UK FAC HC 172 publication (committee finding). The Two Michaels crisis is the substrate-disruption event that lowered the political cost of anti-PRC positioning across CAN parliamentary instruments. The question for P28(c): did Tier-3 framings on Taiwan *emerge or intensify* in the post-Two-Michaels recovery period (2021+)?

### 5.2 Hogue Commission (2023–2025)

The Public Inquiry into Foreign Interference in Federal Electoral Processes and Democratic Institutions (Justice Marie-Josée Hogue, Commissioner). Established September 7, 2023.

Key findings: PRC "clandestinely leveraged" Canadian officials; interference was "a stain on Canada's elections" (BBC reporting). CSIS reports cited in the Commission confirm PRC operational activity targeting CAN democratic institutions.

**P28(c) implication**: The Hogue Commission judicially confirms PRC interference in CAN domestic politics — a substrate fact not present at equivalent judicial/commission-of-inquiry level in UK, AUS, JPN, PHL, or EU. This may further lower the political cost of Tier-3 Taiwan framing because anti-PRC positioning is now grounded in a domestic-defence rationale (defending Canadian democracy from PRC interference) rather than purely foreign-policy rationale.

### 5.3 Indo-Pacific Strategy (2022)

Canada's IPS launched November 2022. States: "China is an increasingly disruptive global power" and "Canada will oppose unilateral actions that threaten the status quo in the Taiwan Strait." Also highlights cooperation with Taiwan and Taiwan's resilience.

The IPS is an **executive-branch policy document** (Tier-2 register, analogous to FCDO ministerial restatement in UK). It does not contain Tier-3 framings but it does establish the policy context within which parliamentary Tier-3 framings may surface.

### 5.4 Naval transits

First joint US-CAN Taiwan Strait transit in 2021. Continued in 2022+. Canadian Armed Forces maintain "routine presence in the Indo-Pacific, including in the Taiwan Strait, in full accordance with international law" (GAC FAAE briefing 2025).

## 9. Hypotheses to test

**H1 (takes-note-floor hypothesis):** CAN's weaker "takes note" Tier-2 baseline produces a *lower* P28(c) cost-product threshold than UK/AUS "acknowledges" baseline, meaning Tier-3 framings surface at *lower* instrument tiers in the CAN Parliament.

**H2 (hostage-diplomacy-shock hypothesis):** The Two Michaels crisis (direct PRC hostage diplomacy) produced a more acute parliamentary register-shift toward Tier-3 framings than the AUS AUKUS shock (alliance-restructuring) or UK FAC publication event (committee finding), because hostage diplomacy personalizes the PRC threat to individual citizens rather than abstracting it to alliance architecture.

**H3 (interference-commission-lowering hypothesis):** The Hogue Commission's judicial confirmation of PRC election-interference lowers the political cost of anti-PRC positioning (including Tier-3 Taiwan framing) by providing a domestic-defence rationale, creating a post-Hogue Tier-3 escalation signature absent in substrates without equivalent commissions of inquiry.

**H4 (legislative-stall-as-cost-product-reading hypothesis):** Bill C-343's persistence at first-reading-only across multiple sessions is the CAN-specific P28(c) cost-product finding: the legislative register *generates* Tier-3 instruments but the cost-product threshold blocks advancement beyond first reading — analogous to UK EDMs that never reach plenary vote.
