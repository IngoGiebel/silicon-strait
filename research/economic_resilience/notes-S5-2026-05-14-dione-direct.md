## 1 — Submarine cable redundancy: the 2026-04-30 cut is a real-time test

Two weeks before this fire, the **Matsu No. 3 submarine cable** (Beigan–Dongyin segment) was severed at 2.64 km off Dongyin Township on 2026-04-29 (announced 2026-04-30) [S5-A]. The fact-pattern is load-bearing for S5:

- **Attribution this time is NOT direct PRC vessel.** Ministry of Digital Affairs (MoDA) + Chunghwa Telecom preliminary assessment: "rough seas may have shifted a grounded shipwreck, fully severing the cable" [S5-A, citing CNA]. The same location had **partial damage on 2026-03-30** — one month of precursor before full severance.
- **Resilience response activated immediately:** MoDA switched voice + internet to **microwave transmission**. Mobile, voice, data operational for ~1,500 Dongyin residents under backup; cable TV unsupported via microwave (so service degradation is observable to residents but not catastrophic). Per Taiwan News reading of the CNA report, internet speeds "may be slower due to weather conditions" [S5-A].
- **Other Matsu townships (Nangan, Beigan, Juguang) NOT affected** — the redundancy architecture isolated the failure to one segment. This is materially better than the February 2023 outcome, where two cables severed within six days disconnected Matsu from internet for weeks [S5-A, referencing the 2023 baseline].
- **LEO satellite back-up track:** MoDA confirms ongoing exploration of "low Earth orbit satellite systems for remote regions" [S5-A linking moda.gov.tw/en/press/press-releases/15160]. The 2023 incident triggered the policy track; 2026-04-30 tested microwave first, not satellite, suggesting satellite is still in pilot.

**Pattern across 2023–2026:** Feb 2023 (two cables, ~weeks outage) → Jul 2025 (cable repair after subsequent incident) → Dec 2025 (Taiwan rejects China's decision in undersea cable dispute — separate diplomatic track) → Apr 2026 (No. 3 cable severed, microwave backup activated within hours). The trend is toward **shorter time-to-backup** as resilience investments accumulate, even as the cut frequency does not decline.

**Anchor quote for prose-lock (S5.a):**
> "The Ministry of Digital Affairs said Chunghwa Telecom reported a core fiber break about 2.64 km off Dongyin on Wednesday, causing a complete outage on the Beigan–Dongyin segment, per CNA. **GWW3 implication for S6:** "Substrate coercion test → response activated within hours, service degraded but not lost" is a different predicate signature than 2023's "substrate coercion test → multi-week outage." The ROC-resilience capability has improved, which loosens one of the PRC short-of-war coercion levers (cable cuts as observable bandwidth attack).

## 2 — Energy security: the 2026-03 Hormuz crisis was the modern stress test

The AmCham Taiwan Business TOPICS April 2026 cover story [S5-B] is the most comprehensive single-document framing of Taiwan's energy posture I have seen, and it is dated weeks after the March 2026 Hormuz crisis put the system under live stress. Key extracted facts:

### 2.a — Dependency baseline (MOEA data, via AmCham)

- **>97% of Taiwan's energy is imported** [S5-B, citing MOEA].
- **LNG = the load-bearing import** for power generation. Roughly **one-third of LNG ordinarily comes from Qatar via the Strait of Hormuz** [S5-B].
- **LNG storage mandate = 11 days of consumption** [S5-B; cross-checked against MOEA Energy Administration position cited in earlier search [S5-D]].
  - Government plans to elevate to **14 days** [S5-B].
  - Comparable buffers: Japan 14–20 days, South Korea 14–20 days [S5-B, "analysts say"].
- **Three LNG receiving terminals** operating close to capacity; expansion slow.
- **Datan third terminal:** NT$75 billion, 9 km² off Taoyuan coast. Environmental approval 2018; target 2025; repeatedly delayed by redesigns + algal-reef protection (terminal relocated ~1.2 km offshore). "Building LNG infrastructure can take six to eight years" — AmCham Energy Committee co-chair Randy Tsai [S5-B].

### 2.b — March 2026 Hormuz crisis: live-fire test of the response architecture

- **PRC opening move:** Taiwan Affairs Office spokesman, mid-March 2026, suggested Beijing provide Taiwan with "energy security" as part of "peaceful reunification" pitch — alignment with "a strong motherland" would shield the island from external supply shocks [S5-B].
- **Taipei rejection:** Deputy Economic Affairs Minister **Ho Chin-tsang** called the proposal **"impossible,"** framed it as "China's longstanding cognitive warfare strategy" [S5-B]. Direct quote-form: Taiwan has its own "response plans" for current energy challenges from geopolitical developments.
- **Operational reassurance:** Premier **Cho Jung-tai** + energy officials publicly stated March + April LNG supply secured; most May shipments arranged despite **QatarEnergy force majeure declaration** [S5-B].
- **Contingency stack:** MOEA actively monitoring cargoes; securing additional supply incl. **increased US LNG imports starting later 2026**; **coal-fired generation reactivation/intensification** on the table if needed [S5-B].

### 2.c — Generation mix shock: nuclear phase-out completed May 2025

This is the load-bearing structural fact for the energy-security gate.

- **2025 generation mix (Taipower):** fossil-fuel >80%; renewables just over 12%; nuclear → zero after the last reactor shut **May 2025** [S5-B].
- **Demand trajectory:** semiconductor expansion + AI data-center build-out + decarbonization → "Taiwan can't get power fast enough" (PJ Huang, Bloom Energy Taiwan country head, quoted in [S5-B]).
- **Grid constraint manifesting:** Some renewable projects facing curtailment "because the grid cannot absorb all the electricity generated" — Randy Tsai [S5-B]. The supply-side problem has crossed into a distribution problem.

### 2.d — The 2026-03 Lai nuclear pivot (load-bearing for S1↔S5 link)

**In March 2026, President Lai moved beyond conditional openness to ACTIVELY supporting the restart of existing reactors** [S5-B]. Specific procedures initiated for:

- **Kuo-sheng plant** (originally shut 2021–2023 staged retirement)
- **Maanshan plant** (last reactor shut May 2025)

Lai's gating conditions per [S5-B]: strict safety standards, viable waste management, public consensus. Framing: nuclear as "pragmatic option to address rising electricity demand, particularly from AI-driven industries, and to bolster energy security."
